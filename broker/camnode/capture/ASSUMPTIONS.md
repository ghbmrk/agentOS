# camnode/capture: assumptions

Built for CAM-node part (b2), CAM-server (#3513, `briefs/CAM-node.md`, design point 5), against NET-2 (Cameras) and DEV-3. `broker/camnode/capture` is `camnode.Source` on the board: H.264 from the hardware encoder through `rpicam-vid`'s pipe, and Lepton frames over SPI. Each row is a reading a later change could overturn.

| # | Assumption | Basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAM-capture-1 | **Access units are split by the NAL units that must open one** (H.264 §7.4.1.2.3): an AUD, SPS, PPS or SEI, or a slice whose `first_mb_in_slice` is 0, after a picture's slice. That holds for one encoder writing whole pictures in order, which is `rpicam-vid`; it is not a general H.264 parser, and the core decodes payloads only in CAM-clerk's sandbox. | Brief design point 1 | A second encoder that writes slices of two pictures interleaved needs a real slice-header parse. |
| CAM-capture-2 | **Only the newest frames are kept while nothing reads them**: a full buffer drops its oldest frame (L3 on #3617, finding 4), and the node's one reader keeps only the newest for the next stream, so a core that reconnects gets live frames, never minutes-old ones. The encoder repeats SPS and PPS before each IDR (`--inline`) and sends an IDR every two seconds (`--intra`), so a decoder joining or losing frames picks up within two seconds. | NET-2 | — |
| CAM-capture-3 | **VoSPI per the Lepton datasheet**: 164-byte packets (ID, CRC-16/XMODEM over the packet with the ID's top nibble and the CRC zeroed, 160 bytes of raw14 pixels), discard packets with `xFxx` IDs, Lepton 3 segments 1 to 4 in packet 20's ID, telemetry off, SPI mode 3 [Inference: from FLIR's public datasheet; part d confirms on the core]. A torn frame (bad CRC, lost packet, segment out of order) is dropped and the reader waits for the next packet 0. A Boson core over USB UVC is not built here (a later row). | Brief design point 5 | — |
| CAM-capture-4 | **`rpicam-vid`'s flags** (`--nopreview --timeout 0 --codec h264 --inline --flush --intra --output -`, with width, height, frame rate and bitrate from the bundle) reach the CM4's hardware encoder [Inference: libcamera-apps documentation; part d confirms on the board]. The encoder is the one child the node starts; its path is the binary's flag, fixed by the image. | Brief design point 5 | — |
| CAM-capture-5 | **An encoder or thermal reader that ends is started again after a second**, so a sensor glitch costs the core a reconnect, not the camera. `Apply` first stops the running capture and drops queued frames at the old settings, then sets IR and starts the capture the new settings ask for: nothing while the stream is off, and nothing when it refuses the settings (Security on #3617, finding 2). | NET-2 | — |
