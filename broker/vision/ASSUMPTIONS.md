# Camera vision service: assumptions

What `broker/vision`, `broker/vision/supervise`, `broker/vision/faces` and `broker/cmd/agentos-vision` rest on (briefs/VIS-core.md; part (a), VIS-svc; part (b), VIS-track; parts (c1) and (c2), VIS-faces; part (d), VIS-plates-sound; part (e1), VIS-models). Each row is a reading of the brief or spec, or a property the code relies on, that a reviewer may want to change.

| # | Assumption | Spec basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| VIS-A1 | Part (a) links no runtime, as INFER-A1: `agentos-vision` built from it answers every call Unavailable, and the tests use a fake runtime. That stays the default build. Part (e1) adds `runtime_onnx.go` behind the tag `vision_onnx`, with the models and their pins (VIS-A27); part (f) starts the service from agentosd. | brief split (a), (e), (f) | Nothing reaches the service before (f) wires it. |

| VIS-A7 | A frame is decoded pixels (rgb24, gray8, or y16 for thermal) sent raw after a JSON header line, not base64 on the line: CAM-clerk delivers decoded frames, and base64 would cost a third more bytes and an encode per frame on the N95. | VIS-A8 | The class list (person, vehicle, dog, cat, bird, deer, fox, coyote, bear, unknown-animal, drone from part b, VIS-A24, and warm from part e1, VIS-A29) and the sound list (alarm, glass-break, gunshot, scream, dog-bark, vehicle, knock, doorbell) are fixed in code; changing either is a reviewed PR. | VIS-A14 | A template is little-endian float32s, at most 1024 (`MaxTemplate`), compared by cosine similarity in the service against `-match` (0.7 by default, set for SFace in part e2, VIS-A32). The service keeps templates scaled to unit length, in memory only, and a lend replaces the whole set and zeroes the old one. Each encoding the runtime returns (`Seen.Face`, `Encode`) is zeroed before the call that made it returns, on every path, a timeout included. | DEV-7, D-207 | Part (e) may change the dimension or the measure. A wider template is a reviewed change to `MaxTemplate`. |
| VIS-A15 | Erase is by key: each template is sealed under its own random wrap key, and the wrap key is kernel content (class `vision-wrap-key`) under the person's subject. Every lend reads each wrap key again with `ReadFor` under the one lineage `svc-vision`. A lend runs at each enrolment change, on `Rewind` (agentosd's D-169 rewind of `svc-vision`, wired in part f), and every 15 minutes in `Run`. So after an erase, the service drops the template at once when the rewind runs, and within 15 minutes otherwise. The cost is one `content.read` entry per enrolled person per lend, about 96 a day each. | LOG-4, D-169; comment 6459973175 point 2 | A shorter bound costs more log entries. A kernel call that answers "erased?" without a read would remove the cost; that would be a kernel change. |

Backups carry no content yet (#3559), so after a restore every wrap key is absent and every enrolled person shows as `unknown` until re-enrolled. `faces.ConsentNotice`, which the enrolment page shows (VIS-A17), says so. | DEV-7; comment 6459973175 point 3 | None. |

That the page shows `ConsentNotice` is enforced only by the page's own test, owed with that page and part (f). | DEV-7, CH-3, KEY-4; comment 6459973175 point 1 (enrolment does not seal from `broker/localui`) | If the owner's phone passkey is to approve enrolment too, `Anchors` takes the live passkeys (`State().Passkeys()`) instead. |
| VIS-A18 | A planner's only way into VIS-core is a `vision.Ask` checked by `CheckAsk`: an op from `PlannerOps` (part b's one, `count`, VIS-A25), a camera or a zone (or both) from the enrolment-id alphabet, and a class from the closed list. An Ask has no field that can hold a crop, a frame, an encoding or free text, and an op with a word such as find, match, search, identify or follow is refused with `ErrNoMatch`; every other undeclared op with `ErrNotOffered`. The clearest-unknown-face evidence references (brief point 4) need a visit, which is a track, so part (b) makes them (VIS-A22). | DEV-7, D-207; brief points 4 and 8 | Each new op's answer type is added to `TestNoCaptureLeavesVIS`'s `carrier` list, as `Count` is. |
| VIS-A19 | A vehicle track's plate reaches an event only through `Plates` (part d; comment 6461801310 point 2): once per track, after the same read has held at least `Hold` frames (3) and at least twice as often as every other read together, quoted, and within each camera's limit of 6 plate events a minute. A track that settles while the limit is full is held back, counted once in the camera's next event's `Coalesced`, and reports on a later read once the limit allows. A track that gives more than 8 distinct reads never reports. Each camera holds at most 256 tracks, evicting its own track read least recently, so one camera's flood never pushes out another's. The track number is the client's tracker's (part b), never the service's, and the event carries no frame count, which the service would choose. So a compromised service gets about 52 bits of plate text per vehicle track and at most 6 plate events a minute per camera. Residual: a reported track evicted by 256 newer tracks on its own camera, and then read again, can report again; that stays within the camera's limit. | DEV-8; VIS-A9 | Part (b) calls `Read` for each vehicle detection on a track and `End` when the track ends. A longer hold costs plates on short tracks (VIS-A21). |
| VIS-A20 | A clip reaches the service only through `Sounds.Hear` (part d; brief point 7), which zeroes the caller's clip on every path, as the service zeroes its payload copy once the call returns (VIS-A7). The service's 4 KiB connection read buffer still holds the start of each clip, as it does of each frame, until the connection ends; clearing it is part (f)'s (comment 6462623915 point 6, which covers clips too). Only list labels come back (VIS-A8), and nothing logs, stores or forwards audio. Each label repeats from one source at most once per 30 s. A label's first event in any minute always passes, so routine labels never starve a rare one; repeats beyond 6 events a minute per source are held, and each label's held count rides on that label's own next event. A source gives at most 14 events a minute. The audio source (a node microphone) is not in CAM-node yet, so the tests use synthetic clips. | DEV-7, DEV-8; Mark 2026-10-27 (audio for sound events only, never kept) | Part (f) feeds the clips and puts the events on the bus. |
| VIS-A21 | The plate figures: on the synthetic set (1000 plates in four common layouts, tracks of 3 to 20 frames, a reader that misses a quarter of frames and gets roughly one read in eight wrong by look-alike swaps and dropped characters), at least 95% of plates on tracks of 6 or more frames are reported right, and at most 0.5% of reports are wrong (measured over five seeds: 96 to 97% right, none wrong). These figures test the per-track rule, not a reader. Part (e) holds the real runtime's reads of its rendered plate set to the same two figures under `vision_real`. | brief part (d), `TestPlateReadOnSyntheticSet` | If part (e)'s reader is noisier, it records its own figures here, and the hold is retuned against both. |
| VIS-A22 | The tracker (part b) runs in the client and numbers its own tracks per camera. It is ByteTrack-class, written here in about 200 lines of standard library because there is no Go ByteTrack and `broker/vision` links the standard library only: confident detections (50% or more) join confirmed tracks first, then the rest join the confirmed tracks left, then confident ones join tentative tracks or start new ones; matching is greedy by nearness to each track's predicted box (its last motion carried on, at most three steps). A detection joins a track only of its own class, with sides within half to twice the predicted box's, and its middle within a gate of the box's longer side times (0.5 + 3 per second elapsed). The gate is in time, not overlap, so a walker stays tracked at the 1-frame-a-second floor (VIS-A26). A tentative track is confirmed after 3 frames in a row, or after 2 frames at least 1 s apart, so a car seen twice at the floor rate is a track; it dies at its first miss. A confirmed track ends unmatched for 3 s, or on `Expire`. Detections that join no confirmed track are dropped and counted. A confident detection that joined no second frame (a tentative track that died after one hit, or one past the track bound) is inconsistent. Each camera keeps its own one-minute window, so honest short tracks on several busy cameras never add up, and a window with at least 120 confident detections of which more than half were inconsistent calls `Faulty`, which part (f) wires to `supervise.Faulty`: `infer/supervise`'s `Restart` option (comment 6461801310 point 4) ends the run and the loop restarts the service with the cause `restarted: faulty`. A confirmed track carries no face: a face only decides whether its visit leaves an evidence reference. A visit whose faces were only ever `unknown` leaves one when it ends: the segment, frame time, box and confidence of its clearest frame (largest box times confidence, until part e gives a sharpness score), for the owner's page only (brief point 4). Plates on a confirmed vehicle track go through `Plates` (VIS-A19); a tentative track's plates are never read. | DEV-7, DEV-8; brief points 2, 4 and 5 | Part (e) retunes the gate, the confirm count and the faulty figures against its real detector; a detector that flickers more lowers `DropRate`'s reach by raising `DropMin`, never by letting unconfirmed detections out. |
| VIS-A23 | The ground plane: each camera's pose (position in metres from the site origin, bearing, pitch, roll, focal lengths and image centre in pixels) is owner data that part (f) reads from the sealed store and passes to `NewSite`, never git. A track stands where its box's bottom edge meets the ground (pinhole model, no lens distortion; part e may add it). A point farther than 300 m from its camera, or a ray at or above the horizon, places nothing; such a track still counts per camera but enters no zone. Tracks of one class on different cameras within 1.5 m of each other, the object seen in the last 2 s, are one object; two tracks of one camera are never fused. An object ends with its last track. Distinct counts are of objects, so they come from tracks, never faces (`TestDistinctCountWithoutIdentity`). | brief point 5 | A flat-ground model misplaces things on slopes; part (e) or a later row adds a height map if the owner's lot needs one. |
| VIS-A24 | The drone is a class (`drone`), never fused, zoned or counted. `Site.DroneFix(now, altitude)` places the middle of the drone's box on the plane at the flight's barometric altitude, which part (f) gets from the drone side, for each camera that saw exactly one drone track in the last second, and returns their mean as decimal degrees to six places about the owner's origin (flat earth over the lot). It is a position only, the input the DRONE-sim GPS/camera check weighs (`camera_fix`, #3609); a service that fakes drone boxes can only push the fix off, which can only make that check return the drone. `ZoneOf` names an object's zone for the owner's per-flight tap. No output type has a waypoint, route, heading, speed or command, and no planner op reaches either (`TestDroneOutputsAreDisplayAndFixesOnly`). | NET-2 Drones; brief point 6 | If the GPS check is dropped (#3516 question 3), `DroneFix` goes with it. |
| VIS-A25 | Zone events: an object's entry into an owner zone, and its dwell after 1 minute (once per stay), go to the bus as a `ZoneEvent` (zone, kind, class, the client's object number, the class's count in the zone, coalesced count, unix seconds): no box, frame or face. Each zone's events are rate-limited as sound labels are (VIS-A20): a class's first event in a minute always passes, so a run of deer never starves a person; repeats past 6 events a minute are dropped and counted onto that class's next event. So a zone gives at most 17 events a minute (6 plus one for each of the 11 classes it can carry, `warm` included). A planner's only ask is `count`, answered by `Site.Answer` with a `Count` (ids, a class, a number), at most 12 asks a minute. A count must name one of the owner's zones (a camera alone is refused), so every object it counts entered the zone through an event the owner sees. Every count and coalesced count leaving the site is capped at 10 (`MaxCount`, meaning 10 or more). This is the #3730 Security review's release finding: uncapped, camera-only counts gave a hostile service about 6 to 7 bits an ask that no zone event showed. A compromised service shaping fake tracks now gets under 4 bits per ask or event, and only by fakes the owner sees as phantom visitors. | DEV-8; brief points 2 and 5 | Part (f) puts `ZoneEvent` on the bus as private, untrusted content; owner rules over verified cameras decide urgency (DEV-8), never the service. |
| VIS-A26 | Pacing: each camera has a declared rate (5 frames a second by default) and a floor (1 a second by default, never slower than one every 5 s). `Pace.Take` passes frames (none stamped before the camera's last taken one) at the rate while `Headroom` says admission has headroom, and at the floor once it does not, so detection goes on at the floor (brief question 3: detection, not identification, is what the floor guarantees). Part (f) wires `Headroom` to agentosd's admission controller (`admission.Controller.Busy`). | RES-2; brief question 3 | Part (e) measures the floor and may lower the default rates. |
| VIS-A27 | The runtime (part e1): ONNX Runtime 1.29.0 (MIT), reached through `github.com/yalue/onnxruntime_go` v1.36.0 (MIT; cgo, vendored), which loads the shared library with `dlopen` at run time. Only `agentos-vision` built with `-tags vision_onnx` and `CGO_ENABLED=1` links it; the default build is unchanged (VIS-A1), and agentosd links neither (`TestAgentosdLinksNoInference`). The library and every model are read from `-models` (`/usr/lib/agentos/vision` by default) and checked against `models.pins`, which the binary embeds: a missing file is `missing`, a file off its pin `corrupt`, and nothing off its pin is ever loaded. The detector is loaded from the very bytes that were hashed; the library is opened by path after its check, so the check rests on the directory being the signed image's and read-only to the service. `hello` reports the SHA-256 of `models.pins` itself, the same on every box running one build, so part (f) can check it against the image and the service chooses none of its bits (comment 6461801310 point 1). A reload stops the runs still going on the old session (`RunOptions.Terminate`) and frees it once they return. Each frame's scaled copy, the detector's input, is zeroed when its run ends. Each call uses 2 runtime threads (`-threads`). Part (f) builds the image's `agentos-vision` with the tag and installs the pinned files beside it (the library and, from part e2, three models), as `image/camdecode.sh` does for libavcodec. | RES-3, DEV-7; brief point 1 | A runtime upgrade is a new pin and a new `onnxruntime_go` that matches its C API. |
| VIS-A28 | The detector is YOLOX-Tiny at a 416-pixel input (Apache-2.0, Megvii release 0.1.1rc0, trained on COCO), chosen for the floor (VIS-A30): by YOLOX's published COCO figures, Nano finds clearly less (25.8 mAP against 32.8), and S at 640 (40.5) costs about four times the compute. Its COCO classes map onto the closed list: person; car, motorcycle, bus and truck to vehicle; bird, cat, dog and bear to themselves; horse, sheep, cow, elephant, zebra and giraffe to unknown-animal; every other class is dropped. COCO has no deer, fox, coyote or drone, so this detector never names them: a deer reads as one of the hoofed classes (unknown-animal), and a fox or a coyote most often as a dog. Boxes scoring under 30% (objectness times class) are dropped, as the tracker uses only confirmed tracks below 50% (VIS-A22). Boxes of one closed class that overlap by more than 45% are one, so a car the model also calls a truck is one vehicle. At most 64 are kept, best first, in whole pixels inside the frame. A gray8 frame (an infrared night picture) fills all three colour planes. Licences for brief question 1, read from each project's licence file: ONNX Runtime MIT, onnxruntime_go MIT, YOLOX Apache-2.0. For the later parts: YuNet (MIT) and SFace (Apache-2.0) from OpenCV's model zoo allow redistribution, so the image can ship face models (e2); for plates and sound (e3), fast-plate-ocr is MIT, the plate detector from open-image-models is published under MIT but was trained with GPL-3.0 YOLOv9 code, which e3 must weigh, and YAMNet is Apache-2.0, with only third-party ONNX conversions to choose from. | brief question 1; DEV-7 | A detector trained for deer, foxes, coyotes or drones is a new pin and a new map. Until a drone detector exists, `Site.DroneFix` (VIS-A24) gets no input. |
| VIS-A29 | Thermal (brief question 2): a y16 frame is searched for warm bodies by heat, with no model, as no thermal-trained detector with weights that allow use was found. A sample is read as a radiometric value in hundredths of a kelvin (the Lepton's and Boson's linear output), so CAM-node must run its cores in that mode. A body is an 8-connected region of at least 8 samples, and at most a quarter of the frame, whose samples are at least 3 K and 6 times the background's spread over the frame's median, and at most 45 °C, so an engine, an exhaust, a vent or a fire is never a body. Its class is `warm` (kind unknown): the visible stream and the drone say what it is. The service keeps no state between frames, so it gates nothing on motion: a still warm thing that meets the rule (a resting animal, a sunlit wall 3 K over the ground) is a `warm` detection on every frame and becomes a still track. A scene more than half as warm as a body hides bodies, since the median then moves. `TestThermalDetect` renders 60 scenes from -5 °C to 25 °C on Lepton and Boson sized frames and finds every body with no false detection. | DEV-7; brief question 2 | Motion gating belongs to the client, which has a camera's history (part f, or the tracker). A thermal-trained detector, if one with usable weights appears, replaces this for classes. |
| VIS-A30 | The figures (part e1), on a 4-core Xeon at 2.8 GHz with 2 runtime threads, recorded by `TestRealRuntimeDetect` and `TestFloorFigures` under `vision_real` and rerun by `.github/workflows/vision-real.yml`. Recall and precision are measured on COCO val2017 mini-500 (a fixed 500-image subset of the public COCO benchmark; annotations CC-BY 4.0), fetched at a pinned revision and image-set hash and never committed. Objects under 32 by 32 pixels and crowds are not scored. Measured in colour: people 0.83 recall (precision 0.82), vehicles 0.77 (0.77), animals 0.83 (0.78). As gray frames, standing in for infrared night pictures: people 0.77, vehicles 0.73, animals 0.77. The test's floors are 0.80, 0.73 and 0.80 in colour, 0.74, 0.70 and 0.73 in gray, and 0.75 precision. A 1920x1080 colour frame takes 80 ms (p95 102 ms), a 640x512 thermal frame 7 ms, a cold start 0.4 s, and the runtime adds 65 MiB resident; on CI's runner the same test measured 50 ms (p95 58 ms), 0.10 s and 63 MiB. The limits are 400 ms, 50 ms, 5 s and 400 MiB, inside the floor's 2 GB inference budget, which agentos-infer shares (RES-2). [Inference] The N95 runs this about two times slower per core, so at the 1-frame-a-second floor the service's 2 threads keep up with about 6 cameras. The scene set shows real strangers: since part e2 the face detector finds their faces, but no template is lent in these tests, so no face of theirs is ever encoded (VIS-A31), and the face tests stay synthetic (VIS-A32). Small, far objects are what eave cameras see most, and these figures do not measure them. | brief question 3, RES-2, HW-4 | A new detector or runtime records its own figures here and moves the test's floors. |
| VIS-A31 | Faces (part e2): YuNet (`face_detection_yunet_2023mar`, MIT) finds faces and their five landmarks, and SFace (`face_recognition_sface_2021dec`, Apache-2.0) encodes each as 128 floats; both are OpenCV Zoo's, fetched from its Hugging Face repositories at fixed revisions and pinned in `models.pins` like the detector (VIS-A27). Their pre- and post-processing are plain Go (`facemodels.go`), checked against OpenCV 5.0's own `FaceDetectorYN` and `FaceRecognizerSF` while building: the same boxes and landmarks to under a pixel and encodings at cosine 0.995 or more. YuNet runs on the whole frame letterboxed to its fixed 640-pixel input, only on a frame where the detector saw a person, and only for visible and infrared frames; a thermal frame has no faces and cannot enrol. Each face goes to the smallest person box holding its centre that has no face yet, and a face on no person is dropped. A face is encoded only while the service holds at least one template (`Runtime.Detect`'s `encode`): with none, it is `unknown` with no encoding ever made. Otherwise faces are encoded best first, at most 8 a frame (`maxFaces`); one under 24 pixels a side, or past the bound, is `unknown` unencoded. The aligned 112-pixel crop, the face detector's input and outputs, the encoder's output and every encoding are zeroed when their call ends (VIS-A7, VIS-A14). An enrolment frame needs exactly one face scoring 0.9 or more, at least 80 pixels on its shorter side; else `no_face`. | DEV-7, D-207; brief question 1 | Faces far from the camera are under 24 pixels at 1080p after the 640-pixel letterbox divides them by three; finding them needs YuNet on each person's crop, a later change with its own floor figures. A new face model is a new pin and new figures here. |
| VIS-A32 | The match threshold and the face figures (part e2). The default `-match` is 0.7 cosine, against the 0.363 OpenCV gives SFace for a balanced error on LFW, because the two mistakes do not cost the same: an enrolled person left `unknown` on one frame is named on a later frame of the same visit, while a stranger named as household could quiet an owner rule, and a visit gives a stranger many frames to be misnamed on. On the face set, all 499,500 pairs of 1000 portraits score 0.7 or more in 25 pairs, every one a pair the generator made of one face twice. The face set is synthetic people only (SFHQ part 1, CC0, generated faces of no one; `faces.pins`), never real people's faces, as no public set of real faces with names may be used here (CLAUDE.md: synthetic data only). Each person has one portrait, so their other sightings are made from it: mirrored, turned up to 15 degrees, scaled to a quarter to nine tenths of a 1280x720 scene, relit and noised. With the first 32 people enrolled (`MaxEnrolled`) and four sightings of each: a face found on all 128 colour and 127 of 128 gray sightings, every one named rightly; of the other 965 people, a face found on 957 and none named, and the three copies of enrolled people (`faces.pins` twins) named as them. The test's floors are 0.95 faces found, 0.95 named rightly in colour and 0.90 in gray, and no sighting named as someone it is not.
