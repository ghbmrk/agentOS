# Reversible conversions (REV-3): assumptions

Built for package P2-rev3 against SPEC v0.12, on the grants gate (P2-grants), the owner channel (P1-5) and the journal (P1-1). Each row is a reading of the spec, or a gap left for a later package, that a reviewer may want to change.

| # | Assumption | Spec basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RV1 | **What is built.** Two of REV-3's four conversions are built. The first is a *delay*: an approved effect is held by the box for an undo window, and UNDO cancels it. The second is a *stage*: an approved effect is first made as an inert copy in the owner's account (a draft or a staging copy), the effect itself runs when the window ends and the recheck passes, and UNDO cancels the effect and runs the stage's inverse. "Drafts instead of sends" is a stage whose operation is a draft, so the message is visible in the owner's mail app while it waits. "Delayed send with an undo window" is held by the box, never by the provider (RV7). *Shadow runs* are not built for effects, because no adapter offers a sandbox to shadow against. For learned changes, the replay evaluator (P3-1a) is already the shadow run. | REV-3 | An adapter with a sandbox adds a third form. |
| RV2 | **Only owner-approved effects are converted.** A pre-allowed effect still runs at once, because ADP-9 says pre-allowed effects run "without approval and without notification", and an undo window nobody hears about undoes nothing. ADP-11 replies keep their own queued window. Secrets (CRED-6) are never held: `Check` refuses a form on a secret operation, and the gate applies a form only while the granted verb is in the irreversible class. | REV-3, ADP-9, ADP-11, CRED-6 | Converting pre-allowed effects needs a digest-only UNDO, which comes with CH-15's digest. |
| RV3 | **Forms are declared by the adapter, next to its verbs.** `grants.Config.Forms` maps executor → operation → `reversible.Form`. `New` keeps only the forms `reversible.Check` accepts against `Config.Declared`, and logs and drops the rest, so an operation with a bad form is asked with no undo window, as before. Check's rules: the operation is declared, in the irreversible class and not a secret; a stage and an inverse come together, differ, and are declared on the same executor at a reversible verb (so running them without asking is what ADP-2 already allows); the window is 1 min to 24 h, with 10 min (the ADP-11 window) as the default. A form never adds an operation an agent can request. | ADP-1, ADP-2, REV-3 | `reversible.Check`. |
| RV4 | **The undo window is the broker's to promise.** The gate sets the approval item's `UndoWindow` from the form, or to zero, and ignores any window a `Verifier` reports, so the CH-12 line "undo within 10 min" holds only where the gate actually holds the effect. The window is part of the line the recheck compares (GR8). | CH-12, OP-3 | — |
| RV5 | **The owner channel holds, the gate waits.** When the owner approves an item with an undo window, the channel queues it under its own UNDO ID, records it for a restart (`QueuedRef.Held`), and returns the approval with `Decision.Hold` and `Until`. The confirmation reply carries the run time and the ID (CH-16): `Approved A. It runs at 12:10 unless you reply UNDO K7.`. A batch reply gives one ID per held item, each with its own time when the windows differ (the earliest time when that would not fit), and stays within three segments at 20 items. The gate keeps the intent pending ("approved; held for the owner's undo window until 12:10 UTC") until `DueAutoReplies` releases it; it then records the approval and dispatches. An item that cannot be held (no free ID, or the restart record cannot be saved) does not run, and the reply ends "Could not hold 2 for undo, so it did not run. Ask your agent again."; its intent stays pending for the agent's retry (RV11). A hold decision that does not match its intent's request closes the intent rather than leave it pending. Holds count toward `MaxOpen` while they wait. | CH-12, CH-16, OP-3 | `owner.holdLocked`, `grants.hold`. |
| RV6 | **UNDO, STOP and restart.** `UNDO <id>` denies a held effect or queued reply that has not been released, whatever the clock (UX-76-1): one that STOP kept past its window has still not run. The reply is `Cancelled K7. It did not run.`; after the release it is `K7 is past its undo window; it was released.`. RESUME (by text, after a challenge, or on the local page) gives each held effect or reply due within 2 minutes a fresh 2-minute window and names it: `Resumed. K7 runs at 14:32 unless you reply UNDO K7.` (the local page holds the channel's lock across the resume and the new windows, so nothing is released in between). A restart cancels every held effect, so a box that is down past the window never sends; the boot text lists them as `Approved actions cancelled, not sent: K7. They will be asked again.` (not "ask your agent again", which could lead to a duplicate that sends twice), its staged copy is removed (RV7), and the intent is asked again as new with the rest of what the restart left open (RV11). Held approvals are not carried over a restart, because the approval itself is in memory (GR8). The recheck's freshness (`Fresh`, GR8) counts from the release, not from the owner's YES. | CH-11, CH-16, OP-3, OP-4 | — |
| RV7 | **Stage semantics (arbitrator ruling on #76, Security C3, C2).** A stage must be inert: a provider-side scheduled send is not a valid stage, since it would go out while the box is down and skip the release recheck. Nothing in code can tell the two apart, so this is a contract on adapters, checked when each adapter is reviewed. The stage runs as soon as the owner approves, as `~reversible/stage/<n>/<id>` (the n-th hold of the intent), with the effect's params plus `reversible_parent`. If the stage did not succeed, the effect is not sent at release, because the owner approved it as staged; the owner gets `K7 was not sent: its draft or staged copy could not be made. Ask your agent again if still needed.`. At release the recheck runs first. The adapter then completes the staged copy, found by the stage intent's ID, only if it is still present and its content hash unchanged. A deleted copy returns not-applied with `reversible.EvidenceGone`. That is a cancel (the native cancel), nothing is unstaged, and no owner line attributes the deletion to anyone (security R2). A copy that changed after the approval returns `EvidenceEdited`. It is not sent and not unstaged, so it stays where the owner can see and send it themselves. The owner gets a fixed notice that offers no YES: `K7 not sent: its draft changed after you approved it. Send it from your mail app if you still want it.`. A YES could not be bound to the edited version: anyone holding a draft-edit verb could rewrite its body or recipients and have the result approved under the original text (arbitrator re-ruling on #76). Any other outcome that did not happen (UNDO, a restart, a recheck denial, a failed send) runs the inverse as `~reversible/unstage/<n>/<id>`, with the stage's evidence in `reversible_staged`, after waiting for that hold's stage attempt in flight. The inverse targets the hold the cancel belongs to, which a restart resolves before re-issuing (security R1). An unknown outcome is left alone. Following #69's organize undo, the adapter applies the inverse only if the copy is unchanged, and it checks this with the provider's precondition on the stage-time version or etag, not by reading and then deleting (L3 on #76). Otherwise it returns not-applied. The copy is then left alone, and the owner gets `K7 did not run, but its draft or staged copy could not be removed, so it was left as is.`. | REV-3, ADP-2, OP-3 | Adapters (P2-6) implement stage, inverse and the checked completion. |
| RV8 | **Derived intents come only from the gate.** A stage or inverse is journaled like any effect (REV-2). It carries origin `broker:reversible` and an ID under `~reversible/`. Guest IDs start with their lineage (`[a-z0-9]`), and `Gate.Submit` (the guest plane's door) refuses that prefix from anyone, so a guest cannot take a derived ID first and block a stage or an inverse (arbitrator C1); policy denies a non-derived intent with a derived-shaped ID that reaches the journal another way. The gate allows such an intent only if the gate itself submitted it (an in-memory set, kept while the intent runs or waits), its operation is the one the form names, and the parent is in the right state: held under this stage's hold for a stage; cancelled, not sent, or no longer under this stage's hold for an inverse. Any other intent with that origin is denied, whatever its params name. A stage or inverse needs no grant of its own, because the owner approved the effect they belong to. Both need the account's adapter grant to be live: after a PAUSE or REVOKE the inverse is refused, and the owner gets the RV7 line. | REV-2, ADP-1, OP-1 | — |
| RV9 | **Reuse.** `reversible` is pure data and validation. It imports only `journal` and `verb` and is on the ARC-2 control path's allowed list. The hold reuses the owner channel's ADP-11 queue (IDs, restart record, STOP rule, UNDO parsing) rather than adding a second one. #69's organize undo is a different shape: it restores, after the fact, effects that already ran, using their journaled prior state. Nothing here depends on it, and organize operations are reversible, so `Check` gives them no form. Its "only if unchanged" rule is the one RV7 asks of inverses. | ARC-2, ADP-2 | — |
| RV10 | **Not covered here.** UNDO for effects that already ran is not covered; it comes with the digest (CH-16 via ADP-2's organize line, #69). Neither is the local page's view of held effects (P2-2), or wiring `Config.Forms` from real adapters' declarations (P2-6/P2-7). No adapter on main declares a form yet, so nothing on the box is held until one does. | CH-16, ADP-2 | Later packages. |
| RV11 | **A released effect that STOP holds** still has its staged copy settled when it ends, whoever dispatches it after RESUME (`endHeld`, run on the gate's Dispatch and on every Tick). **A hold that ends without the owner is not a refusal (PV1, PV2 on #76).** "not held" and a restart's cancel never deny the intent, so CAP-6's earned runs, ADP-11's edit rate and Loop 1's corrections never see them as the owner's NO. A "not held" intent stays pending, and the reason tells the agent to retry the same request_id, which asks again. A restart's cancelled hold is asked again as new, in the GR10 batch. UNDO is the owner's answer and denies. Follow-ups, not built here: a planned restart (an update) waits until no held effect remains, bounded by the longest window (UX-76-2); and a stage in flight at a crash, which the journal later reconciles as succeeded, is not unstaged by a cancel that came first. | CAP-6, ADP-11, LOOP-1, OP-4 | BOARD carry-forward on P2-rev3. |
