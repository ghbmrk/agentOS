# Grants and approval policy: assumptions

Built for the P2 grants and approval-policy package against SPEC v0.12
(PR #15), on the journal (P1-1), the owner channel (P1-5), and the guest
plane (P1-7, PR #24). Each row is a reading of the spec, or a gap left for
a later package, that a reviewer may want to change.

| # | Assumption | Spec basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| GR1 | The verb list and each verb's class live in `broker/verb`, shared by this package and the egress proxy. read and draft are reversible; send, post, buy, share, delete-remote, and change-account are irreversible; reveal-or-create-secret is its own class (CRED-6). `ClassOf` reports the strictest class for an unknown verb, and every caller denies on `!ok`. | ADP-2, REV-2 | Changing the list is an owner-approved code change (OP-5, CHG-2). |
| GR2 | A grant is one of three shapes, each a `meta.grant` intent on the broker account: an **adapter grant** (account, executor, and the operations the owner accepts with the verb each maps to), a **pre-allowance** (account and rule), or a **resume** of a paused grant. Each executor declares its operations and verbs (`Config.Declared`, from the adapter's own declaration); the daemon refuses an executor without one. A grant can only choose among declared operations, at the declared verb or a stricter one, and policy applies the stricter of the two, so a grant can never weaken a verb. An account has at most one adapter grant, live or paused; replacing it means revoking it first. Operations not chosen do not exist for agents. Grants are rebuilt from the journal's succeeded records on every start, so there is no second store; a grant whose replay no longer validates (say its executor is gone) is dropped and logged. IDs are `G<n>`, numbered by the intent's place among all grant intents ever submitted, so replay gives every grant the ID it had live. | OP-5, ADP-1, ADP-2 | Medium: `grant.go`. |
| GR3 | Every grant shape is high risk (owner `Kind` GrantChange) and needs both the code-generator code and a local confirmation, in either order (`ConfirmLocal`, called by the P2-2 local page after it shows `Describe`). The recovery-key alternative in CH-3 is not built. Where no local page exists (`Config.LocalUI` false, which is the box today), grant intents are refused at once with the reason, rather than asking the owner for a code that cannot complete them. | CH-3, CH-10, ADP-9 | P2-2 sets `LocalUI` and calls `ConfirmLocal`. |
| GR4 | Only structural faults deny an effect: no grant, an operation not granted, an executor the grant does not connect, a malformed grant, a broker action from a guest, or narrowing from anyone but the owner. Denial reasons are fixed wording that never echo an account, action, recipient, or grant reference, because a guest reads them back through `effect_status`. Reads and drafts under a grant run at once. Every other verb is gated: it runs only under an exactly matching pre-allowance, or after the owner approves a CH-12 request through `owner.Channel.Request`. reveal-or-create-secret always asks, whatever the rules. | REV-2, REV-5, CRED-6, ADP-9 | Hard constraint: irreversible effects are always gated. |
| GR5 | The approval line comes from the account's `Verifier` (the adapter reading the source system) with the verb and kind set from the grant, never from the agent. With no verifier, or when verifying fails, the line shows the operation and the intent's own recipients (what will run) with no verified facts, so it is high risk, and it starts with a fixed `UNVERIFIED, details on the Wi-Fi page:` prefix outside every field cap (`owner.Item.Unverified`). No pre-allowance matches an unverified intent. No verifiers exist yet; they come with the adapters (P2-6, P2-7). | CH-10, CH-12 | — |
| GR6 | A pre-allowance matches only when all of these hold: params are exactly the rule's fixed params plus `record` (and `body` for a reply rule), so there is no free text; `record` is the record the verifier read; recipients equal, as a set, the ones that record names, and fall within the rule's list if it has one; any verified amount is at or under the cap (a zero cap means no money moves); the record is older than the hold (an unknown edit time fails the hold); and the scope bounds have room. Anything else becomes a normal approval request. | ADP-9 | `matches`. |
| GR7 | Scope bounds (per record per day, per day) count every intent on that account and operation authorized in the last 24 hours and not refused by the recheck, whether a rule or the owner allowed it. This is conservative: an owner-approved send uses up room under the rule. The count is from the journal (`Engine.AuthorizedSince`), so it survives restarts. Concurrent intents fail closed: each one's authorize check counts the others already authorized, and the dispatch recheck, serialized by the engine, counts every authorized intent but itself, so two concurrent intents under a bound of 1 can both be refused at dispatch rather than one asked. The authorization record does not say which rule allowed it. | ADP-9, OP-3 | Journal the basis if audit needs it (Security, non-blocking on #27). |
| GR8 | Owner approvals are held in memory for the recheck before dispatch (OP-3). The recheck refuses an approval that is older than `Fresh` (default 1 h, for example after a long STOP), one whose grant was paused or revoked, or one whose rendered line (object, recipient, amount, undo window, verb, kind) has changed since the owner approved it. A restart loses held approvals, so an approved intent still held by STOP across a restart is refused at its recheck. | OP-3, journal A4 | Persist approvals if this costs real work. |
| GR9 | **Batching and pacing.** An item asked of the owner joins the open batch. The batch is sent when it has been quiet for `CoalesceIdle` (default 30 s) or open for `Coalesce` (default 3 min), outside the owner's quiet hours (`Quiet`), and within `RequestsPerHour` request texts (default 3, CH-15's default). It goes at once, in quiet hours too, when it holds an owner-defined urgent item (`Urgent`), and at once outside quiet hours when the owner texted within 5 minutes (`owner.Channel.Active`). Quiet hours therefore yield one batch when they end. Each batch is split by tier (`owner.Channel.Tier`) into requests of at most 20 items. If the owner cannot be texted (no modem before P2-3, or `ErrFull`), the intent stays pending with the reason, and a retry of the same request_id asks again. The guest is told it is waiting, never the request ID. "Task idle" is approximated by the quiet gap, since the broker has no task boundary yet. The CH-15 budget shared with other unsolicited texts and the digest land with the CH-15 pacing package. | CH-10, CH-13, CH-15 | Tune the defaults; owner-set values come with the local UI. |
| GR10 | **Restart (P1-5's carry-forward from G12).** No approval request survives a restart (owner O7), so `Attach` denies every intent left pending, with a reason telling the agent to ask again under a new request_id. That includes an intent a crash caught between submit and authorize; G12's "finished on retry" now means "denied on retry". `Boot`'s restart decisions then find these intents already settled. | OP-4, CH-13 | Re-asking automatically would re-text requests that Boot just told the owner were cancelled. |
| GR11 | **ADP-11 replies.** A reply rule covers `send` with no money. A reply matches only when the submitting machine (not its lineage, so a fork of a composer is not one) is a reply-composer machine (`Config.Isolated`), the verifier confirms the thread starter and no attachments, and recipients are exactly the thread's participants. It then goes to `owner.Channel.QueueAutoReply`. If the commitment filter matches, it becomes a normal request. Otherwise it is queued, the owner is texted its first line and `UNDO <id>`, and `Tick` releases it after the undo window (never while STOPped). UNDO and a restart now send the owner channel's decision for the reply (`Why` "undo" or "restart"), which closes the intent. No composer machine exists yet, so `Isolated` is nil on the box and reply rules never match. The earned count (20 unedited replies) is CAP-6's offer condition and is not checked here. | ADP-11, CH-16 | The composer pipeline sets `Isolated`. |
| GR12 | **PAUSE and REVOKE.** These are owner texts (`PAUSE G3`, `REVOKE G3`; CH-11's pause and revoke commands) that need only the owner's number, like STOP. They become narrowing intents with origin `owner`, which are exempt from STOP holds and restart fences (journal A9). A guest cannot submit one. Pausing a pre-allowance sends its operations back to approval. Pausing an adapter grant stops routing to its account. Revoking an adapter grant also removes that account's pre-allowances, so a later grant cannot silently revive them. Unpausing is a `resume` grant (GR2), because it widens authority. HELP's word list (control package) does not mention PAUSE or REVOKE yet. | ADP-9, CH-11 | — |
| GR13 | **Provenance and labels.** Each intent records the submitting machine and its REV-5 label (`journal.Intent` Machine and Label; an unknown label is recorded as private). These are provenance, not parameters: they are excluded from the OP-1 fingerprint. The guest plane partitions a lineage's requests by label. Public machines use `<lineage>/<request_id>`, private ones `<lineage>/private/<request_id>`. A public machine therefore never sees, collides with, or learns timing from a private machine's request (the #27 review's REV-5 probe). A private machine repeating a request its lineage made while public gets that intent read-only: it sees the state but never drives it, since driving would let a private machine change what a public one observes. Approvals still settle it, through the owner. Because a private machine never drives a public-namespace intent, its recorded label is always that of the only kind of machine that can dispatch it, and no "highest label seen" update is needed. **Invariants (Security, #27 review):** (i) params carry content inline or by hash, and executors never read the submitting machine's state at dispatch, so a repeat from any fork runs exactly what was judged (the first executor package adds a test for this); (ii) once REV-5 policy reads labels, an undispatched intent is judged at the higher of its recorded label and the current submitter's, and its approval line says "from a private machine". | REV-5, OP-1 | — |
| GR14 | Guests never reach broker-state actions. The guest plane refuses `broker` and any `meta.*` action before routing, unjournaled, with a fixed reason. `Gate.Route` never routes `broker`. As defense in depth, the policy denies broker actions from any `guest:` origin, refuses narrowing from any origin but `owner`, and requires every grant to carry a code plus the local page whatever its origin. This overlaps #24's item 3 on purpose. | OP-5, ARC-7, REV-5 | — |
| GR15 | The box's redactor is still `redactAll` until the vault's redactor is wired with the vault unlock (P2-4; `daemon.Config.Redactor`). It replaces every journaled param string, so grant specs and template params cannot be read back on the box until then. Together with GR3 (no local page) and no adapters, nothing is grantable on the box yet. Every path is proven in tests with an identity redactor and the real owner channel on the modem simulator. | CRED-7, OP-5 | P2-4. |
| GR16 | **Recipients are never cut, hidden, or collapsed (CH-10, CH-12; Security on #27).** The owner channel renders recipients in full and folded. An item is not approvable by text (`owner.SMSApprovable`; `Request` returns `ErrLocalOnly`) when its recipients exceed 100 characters, contain a character outside the fixed alphabet or a line break, or look secret-shaped. Such an item is never texted for a code. The owner gets "An action for N recipients needs your approval on the box's Wi-Fi page", and the intent waits for the local page (P2-2). Until that page exists it stays pending until a restart closes it. | CH-10, CH-12, CH-19 | P2-2 adds local approval. |
