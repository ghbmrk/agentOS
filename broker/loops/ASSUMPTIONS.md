# Loop scheduler and Loop 1: assumptions

Built for PLAN P3 ("Loops 1–3") against SPEC v0.12 §11A: LOOP-0 to LOOP-3 (the scheduler shared by all three loops) and LOOP-4 to LOOP-6 (Loop 1). It closes the change pipeline's open items for the Loop 1 scheduler (`broker/change` ASSUMPTIONS C10, C14 (a), (b), (d)) and replay's model-access conditions K1–K3 (`broker/replay` ASSUMPTIONS R2). Covers `broker/loops`, plus (then) `meter.Overall`/`SetOverallCap`, the two `meta.loops` narrowing actions in `journal`, and `change.ClassTask`. Each row is a reading of the spec, or a gap left for a later package, that a reviewer may want to change.

| L3 | **Spare budget (UX-43-1, K2; on the kernel meter since MIG-4-spend-m, #2588).** One key of its own, the spare envelope (`Config.Spare`, a `*Spare`), never the guests', that every loop model call is charged to: loop plans cite `Spare.Key()`, and the model clerk (`broker/clerk/model`) charges each call to it and, through the chain, to the envelope (LOG-7). Its day spend cap is the owner's setting in minor units: `SpareCap(calls, OutPerM)`, each call `TokensPerCall` (10 000) output tokens at the spare route's price, rounded up; no fixed budget is `PaidRouteCalls` (1500 calls, the OP-8 default; a loop constant, since `cmd/agentosd` is a main package and builds no scheduler today). A zero budget is a zero cap: no call that costs anything fits. Every key loops cite is a `key.Derive` narrowing the loop holds and swaps, never the envelope itself: of the envelope for a cap at or under the current one, else of a fresh envelope from `Spare.Widen`, the owner's passkey (KEY-6, MIG-4-untrusted-loops-b-2), which like any grant is live only after `GrantHold`, so calls are refused until then. Each narrowing carries a random use cap no loop reaches, so a cap set again never re-derives a revoked key. **A lowering revokes every key handed out before it** (`Spare.Revoke`, the owner's revocation, `Kernel.Revoke`; a narrowing) before the new key is swapped in: a plan citing an older `Key()` or `EvalKey()`, one already running included, is refused (`revoked`), so the kernel holds every call under a spare key to the current cap (L3 on #2837). With no `Revoke`, or when a revocation fails, the lowering is refused and the cap stays; keys already revoked stay revoked, which only refuses calls. The kernel refuses a call past the current key's cap (`*key.Refusal`, `spend-day`); the scheduler also offers no `UsesModel` work while `Spare.Room` is false, reading the day's spend across every envelope it held (`Spare.Used`). **Earlier spend (#2838):** each key's day cap is `key.LowerSpendDay`, which counts the clerk's whole account, so spend made earlier the same day under any envelope or earlier key counts toward the new cap in the kernel, and a key set again at the same cap, or left live at a lower cap than a raise, adds nothing to the day. `EvalKey`'s cap stays `LimitSpendDay`, a share of the spare key's own spend. **Restart:** `Spare` knows only the keys it handed out since it started. Spare plans are signed by `Holder`, which only the broker holds, so the exposure is plans submitted before a restart: the wiring cancels the loop's open spare plans at start, or a lowering after a restart leaves them at their old cap (#2838). The digest says `Spare-time AI use, last 24 hours: N of M calls (self-tests and learning).` once anything was spent, with N the day's spend in calls rounded up (UX-49-4). **Conditions (lens gate on #49):** the wiring grants the envelope before `New` applies the setting, and asserts replay cites it (security C4); A11 measures spend per evaluation, with the owner shown an estimated daily cost (potency PS3).  **Evaluation share (LOOP-5).** `Spare.EvalKey()` (`Scheduler.EvalKey`) is a child of the spare key whose own day cap is `EvalReserve` (30%) of the budget, for replay machines to cite; `Scheduler.Evaluating()` stays the loop's own predicate, read before a call goes to the clerk. This replaces the old meter share, which kept 30% from other users while evaluation ran and lent it otherwise: a child key bounds evaluation to its share but does not reserve it from other spare users. No wiring passes either today. | LOOP-2, LOOP-5, OP-8, LOG-7 | Tune the fraction from A11; a kernel reserve would restore the lend-and-keep rule. |
| L4 | **Spare compute.** One unit of loop work at a time. None starts while `Busy` (foreground or accepted work, from admission) or `Stopped` (STOP). While work runs, `Busy` and `Stopped` are polled every 250 ms and the work is cancelled at once; `Preempt` does the same on demand and waits at most `YieldTarget` (default 2 s until RES-1's target is frozen), returning `ErrSlowYield` past it. The machines it runs are experiments and are frozen by admission independently (RES-1). Each cancellation carries a cause for the change pipeline (PE5, change C21): `change.ErrOwnerStop` for STOP, LOOPS OFF or pausing the running loop, `change.ErrOwnerWork` for the owner's accepted work without pressure, `change.ErrPressurePreempt` under pressure (which wins), read in one `BusyCause` call; with only `Busy` configured, only STOP while not busy is the owner's. A preempted or switched-off unit is not measured and is offered again later; this re-admits preempted loop work (P1-4 follow-up), but by re-running it in a fresh machine rather than `Manager.Resume`, since replay machines are thrown away (replay R1). | LOOP-1, RES-1 | Resume preempted machines once long jobs exist. |
| L5 | **Measured return.** A job reports its value; the scheduler measures its cost: the spare envelope's spend while it ran, as output tokens at the spare route's price (`Spare.Tokens`), plus 50 tokens per second of compute. Each loop keeps moving averages (weight 0.3) of value and cost per run, and recent spend decayed with a 24 h half-life. Its share is its return (value/cost) relative to the best loop's, at least 5%; an unmeasured loop gets a full share. Loops are tried in order of share over (1 + recent spend). A loop with 3 runs in a row of no value is parked for 24 h, unless its source reports `Urgent()` (Loop 3 with a security fix or a retry due, or back online; arbitrator ruling on #53), and `Wake` unparks every loop. When no loop offers work, the scheduler sleeps (1 h, or until `Wake`). Measurements are in memory: after a restart every loop earns its share again. Values are in each loop's own units (gain, findings, updates); a common scale or normalizing each loop against its own history, and persisting the averages, are open (potency PR1, PR2). | LOOP-3 | Tune the constants from A11 runs. |
| L15 | **Replay model access (K1, K3): superseded by W3a (#62).** `loops.EvalModel` is removed. Replay machines' model calls go from `agentosd` through `modelroute.Evaluation` to the vault process, carrying only the tree's routing rule, which egress applies within the `-eval-from` machine's grants as private data (egress K10, replay R2, R10). `agentosd` links no router. Loop 1 reads routing rules only through `routerule` (W3). | LOOP-5, ADP-4, CAP-9 | — |
| L20 | **Evaluator interruptions (PE3).** A unit whose result error wraps `change.ErrInterrupted` while its context is still live (admission refused or preempted an evaluation machine, replay R6) is treated as preempted: it is not measured, the scheduler waits `Retry` before the next unit instead of looking again at once, since admission can refuse for lack of room while `Busy` (memory pressure) stays false. A unit that ends in `change.ErrParked` (a candidate cut for the owner `MaxExempt` times, PE5) ran nothing: the scheduler does not wait or log it, orders its loop last until a unit of that loop runs, and goes on to the next source in the same tick; when only parked work was offered, it offers the sources again with the context marked idle (`change.WithIdle`), and only then does a parked candidate run. | RES-1, LOOP-1, LOOP-3 | — |
| L23 | **Update channel and cadence (UPD-4, UPD-5; UPD-c).** `Settings.Updates` holds the owner's update settings, read by Loop 3 at each check (maintain M6, M7). Texts (UPDATE or UPDATES, UX-130-1): `UPDATES STABLE`, `UPDATES FAST`, `UPDATES PINNED`; `UPDATE SOAK n [DAYS]` (the stable soak, 7 to 60 days; 60 stays inside maintain's 90-day memory of first sightings, so a late check never restarts a soak); `SECURITY UPDATES AUTO` (the default: a security fix with a passing listed attestation stages on its own) or `ASK` (every security fix goes to the owner); `HELP UPDATES` (`HelpUpdates`, one segment; `HELP LOOPS` points to it, UX-130 Q1). A soak set while on fast or pinned says it applies once on stable (UX-130-3). Out-of-range soaks are answered with the bound and change nothing. Saved settings read fail-safe: a soak outside 7 to 60 days is clamped and an unknown channel reads as stable, so a damaged state file cannot stop checks or end the soak early (L3 on #130). Pinning's undo names the channel the box was on. Each applies through `Set` like L2's. In a locked session only changes that make updates slower or more cautious are taken, as `UPDATE CHECKS OFF` is: a later channel (fast, then stable, then pinned), a longer soak, `ASK`. Pinning and `ASK` hold back security fixes, which is the same worst case as `UPDATE CHECKS OFF`, and their replies say how to undo them. Faster changes get the unlock prompt and, once applied, "Reply ... if this wasn't you." Only the slower or more cautious ones apply during STOP (L2). `Set` judges narrowing again under the lock that applies the setting, so concurrent texts cannot turn a narrowing into a widening (security R1 on #130). | UPD-4, UPD-5 | The local page's update settings; quiet-window timing and jitter are the apply side (UPD-a); a standing grant for behavior-changing release classes. |
| L25 | **A run owns its cancel (LOOPS-off-race, #1236).** `run` installs a `runCtl` (loop, cancel, done, preempted) as `s.cur` under `mu` and, in the same critical section, rechecks the saved settings: a unit whose loop went off after `tick` read the settings is cancelled with `change.ErrOwnerStop` before it starts. `Set` cancels `s.cur` under `mu` after the setting is in place, so whichever of the two takes `mu` second stops the unit. Any other save error is a failed save (MIG-4-untrusted-loops-b-2). Tests: `TestAnUnsyncedSaveCountsAsApplied`, `TestAFailedSaveRollsBackOnlyAWidening`. | HLD-4, RES-5 | If a store ever returns `ErrUnsynced` before the rename, this reports applied a setting that a restart does not load. |
| MIG-4-untrusted-loops-b-1 | **"Owner-approved" is an unlocked session (CH-14).** HLD-4 says turning a loop back on is an owner-approved change. This package reads that as: `LOOPS ON`, `LOOP n ON`, `START SHARING` and faster updates are taken only in a session unlocked with an approval code (CH-14), and a budget raise also needs the passkey (MIG-4-untrusted-loops-b-2). No approval card is sent. | HLD-4, CH-14 | If the owner wants a passkey or a card for turning loops on, `Set` asks for it as it does for a raise. |
| MIG-4-untrusted-loops-b-2 | **Every raise needs the passkey; narrowings never wait on it (KEY-6, RES-5).** `Spare.Prepare(n)` widens whenever `n` is above the current cap, not only above the envelope's, so a re-raise after a lowering asks too; with no `Widen`, or one that fails, the reply says the passkey is needed and neither the setting nor the cap changes. `Prepare` calls `Widen` with no lock held and checks the fresh envelope holds cap `n` and sits under no envelope already held; it returns a `Prepared` (the cap asked, the envelope and cap it saw, the widened envelope). `Commit` runs under `Spare.mu`, refuses with `ErrMoved` if the envelope or cap changed, then derives and revokes as before. `SetCap` is the two in a row. In the scheduler, `budgetMu` orders budget changes: a raise prepares first with no lock, then under `budgetMu` rechecks narrowing and STOP, commits, sets `SpareCalls` and saves; a lower takes `budgetMu` and commits at once. A raise whose cap moved is refused and the owner sends it again; so is a lower that a racing lower turned into a raise, through `prepare(n, false)`, which never widens. **Failed saves:** a widening (raise, `LOOPS ON`, faster updates) that fails to save rolls back, a raise by committing the old cap, which is a lowering (if that rollback itself fails, the raised cap stays in force and the setting stays matched to it, reported as in effect but not saved, so the next raise is judged against the cap the passkey approved); a narrowing that fails to save stays in effect and the reply says it lapses on restart, since a narrowing takes effect before any further step (KEY-6) and rolling back a lower would be a raise. A passkey answer for a raise later refused (moved, STOP, locked) leaves an unused envelope grant. | KEY-6, RES-5, HLD-4 | **Open:** RES-1 and RES-5's bound on spare work (a pool drawn only down to the owner's reserve and the foreground forecast) has no kernel admission class yet; it matters when the scheduler is wired again (A11, G4). |

## Loop 2 (P3-4): passive checks and findings

Built for P3-4 against SPEC v0.12 LOOP-8 to LOOP-10, in `secure.go` (`Guard`, Loop 2's `Source`). Narrow scope (2026-10-05): after a safety check paused the session, Mark restarted the work on the recommended narrow option: LOOP-7, active testing from inside the sandbox, is not built here.

| S1 | **Scope.** Passive checks (LOOP-8), finding handling (LOOP-9), and the suite-only-grows rule (LOOP-10). LOOP-7 is out of scope and still open. A11 no longer requires it (D-070); "loop 2 finds a seeded vulnerability" is a later criterion owned by P3-4c. | LOOP-7 to LOOP-10, A11 | A later package builds LOOP-7 as another `Guard` input. |
| S2 | **Passive checks.** One pass runs four checks over a `Box` the wiring supplies: every listed artifact's measured digest against the signed release (`update.Verified.Images` plus dependency digests); installed packages against the last advisory snapshot, offline included; config files in force against the change pipeline's active tree; vault items' expiry (names and dates only, never values). A package version that cannot be compared with an advisory's fixed version is neither assumed fixed nor assumed vulnerable: it is one Low finding per package and version in the digest ("Could not check X version V against known vulnerabilities. Check it on the box page.") with no pause and no fixture, texted once if it stays so for 7 days (L3 review blocker 2; arbitrator ruling 1 on #54). A snapshot older than 7 days, or never fetched, gives a digest line that the known-vulnerability checks are not current. | LOOP-8, DEP-4, LOOP-11 | — |
| S3 | **Cadence and value.** A pass is due every 6 h, or at once after `Trigger` (a new release or snapshot; pair with `Scheduler.Wake`). It makes no model calls, so it runs even with no spare budget. Its value is the number of new findings; three clean passes in a row park Loop 2 for 24 h under L5, so in steady state the checks run at least daily. **Follow-up for the scheduler (potency lens on #54):** exempt the passive pass from L5 parking, since it makes no model calls and costs little compute, so the 6 h cadence holds; that is done in W5a without a scheduler change: `Guard` is `Urgent` while a pass is due, so the scheduler offers it while L5 parks Loop 2. | LOOP-3 | — |
| S4 | **Finding handling.** Per new finding, in order: contain (pause the `Contain` target the wiring named for that artifact or package; drift and expiry name none); preserve evidence (the finding and its SHA-256, saved before anything slower runs, kept forever); add a regression fixture; propose a fix if a `Fixer` exists; tell the owner. A finding is handled once while it persists, again if it reappears after clearing. Evidence keeps one record per finding digest with an appearance count and last time; a finding that clears and returns is contained and recorded again, but texted again only if it stayed clear for 24 h; sooner, the digest marks it "Again:". A pause stays listed in the digest after its finding clears until the wiring calls `Resumed` (security lens R1, R2). Evidence is unbounded for now: passive findings are few and deduplicated; bound it when LOOP-7 adds high-volume findings (R3). High findings (hash, drift, expired credential, advisory rated high or critical) are texted in fixed wording, one text per pass within three segments, with the rest on MORE (`More`); Low ones (other advisories, expiry within 14 days) appear only in the digest. Owner text never says grant or executor: the wiring gives each target a `Label` ("pre-allowance P4", "the network gateway"). A pause says "It stays paused until you resume it on my Wi-Fi page.", since Loop 2 cannot resume anything and the page is the only place a paused grant resumes (W5a-resume, Security R2 on #169: the page asks, the owner approves with a fresh code-generator code bound to that grant and pause); when a paused finding clears, the owner is texted "Cleared: X. <label> stays paused until you resume it on my Wi-Fi page." if the finding was texted, and the digest keeps that line until the wiring calls `Resumed`, or until `Reconcile` at start finds the gate no longer holds the pause (a resume the guard missed in a crash, L3 S1 on #169). A revoked connection reports each pre-allowance it takes with it through the same hook (grants GR25). Each line ends with the next step where there is one ("Replace it on the box page."). The digest shows at most 3 findings (High first, advisories grouped per package with the highest fixed version) and "And N more". Text is marked urgent unless it is only about credential expiry (UX lens on #54). A failed containment is recorded, returned as an error, and said in the text ("Could not pause it. STOP pauses everything."). New findings are handled most serious first (High before Low; hash, then drift, advisory, expiry), so the pause cap and the text go to tampering before anything a noisy feed produces, and new findings lead the text ahead of cleared and uncomparable lines (L3 round 2, F1 and F3). Version strings in owner text keep `~` and `+`.
