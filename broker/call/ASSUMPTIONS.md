# Call loop: assumptions

Built for VOICE-ro-a (briefs/VOICE-ro.md, #4934) against SPEC CH-5, CH-17 and CH-21 and the flip-phone note §2.2 to §2.4. Covers `broker/call`.

| # | Assumption | Basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| VOICE-ro-a-C1 | **Read-only means no window.** The loop opens no kernel window, so no key means yes; 0 stops speech and every other key is ignored. Approvals by voice wait on S2 and the token (Decisions 8, items 5 and 6). | flip-phone note §2.2 rules 2 and 4 | A later slice adds windows here, with the earcon. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C2 | **The rung is the call's.** `Serve` answers only the owner's number, reads the speed-dial token (keys up to `#`, `MaxToken` keys or `TokenWindow`, default 6 s), and has `check` ask `Config.Token` once. `Serve` ends the token read and waits for it before it returns, in a deferred func, so no `Config.Token` try follows the call even when the loop panics; that wait rests on `Config.Token`, the kernel's, returning (#5259). `check` tries nothing once the call's ctx has ended, so keys read as the call ends are not a try (#5274). If it checks, `check` makes a P2 `grant` that lives until `Serve` has returned; otherwise the call has none and is P0. `Serve` serves one call at a time: an owner call that rings while another is served waits for it up to `RingWait`, and one still ringing then, or whose caller hangs up first, gets `ErrBusy` and is left ringing (#5232, #5252). A `Rung` is a label and proves nothing. A `grant` is unexported, so no other package can make one, and the answerer reads it only through `Ask.Rung()`. An `Ask` made outside the package is P0, and a kept `Ask` or `grant` reads P0 once its `Serve` has returned, so in every other call. So nothing the wiring (VOICE-ro-d) passes to `Serve` raises the rung a call's `Ask` reads; what an answerer discloses at that rung is VOICE-ro-c's, which `broker/call/answer` takes only from `Ask.Rung()` (#5225 re-plan, #5232). `TestOnlyServeChoosesTheRung` holds four rules over the type-checked non-test files, with a probe for each form. (1) `loop` is named once, as a direct call in `Serve`'s body, `check` only as direct calls in `token`, and `token`, whose ctx is the grant's life, once, as a direct call in `Serve`, all outside func literals; the lock `serving` is named only in `Serve`, which defers the unlock at once, then makes `tctx` and defers its cancel, answers, and only then starts the token read on it and defers ending and awaiting that read, in that order, so keys pressed before an `Answer` that fails reach no try; `token` defers closing its channel first; and nothing writes `Serve`'s `tctx` or `cancel` once they are set (#5252, #5274). (2) A `grant` literal appears only in `check`; nothing converts to `grant`, and no literal or conversion has a type-parameter type. (3) Nothing writes a `grant`'s fields or `Config.Token` (an assignment, `++`/`--`, `&`, a range target, a method on the field, or a literal key), no `Config` literal leaves out its keys, and nothing converts to `Config`. (4) No file imports `unsafe`, `reflect` or `C`. So every `grant` is `check`'s or a zero value, which reads P0 (its `live` is nil), and a whole-value write or copy only moves one of those (Security on #5229). Left to review and the Serve and token tests: what `check`, `token` and `Serve` do with their inputs, such as which keys and which `live` reach `check`. `//go:linkname` and assembly fail `tools/srclint.py` in CI. A `grant` held past its call (a package var, a field, a shared answerer) needs no rule: its call's ctx ends before the next call is served, so it reads P0 in any other (`TestOneCallIsServedAtATime`). That is not one call at a time end to end: a ctx-ignoring answerer's or synthesizer's call from one call can still run during the next (C8). Its `Ask` reads P0 there, and anything it fetched at P2 and keeps in shared state is VOICE-ro-c's to scope per call. It listens from the start, so what the owner says while the token is read is heard. That turn starts at once and is asked of the answerer once the loop has the rung, at that rung; until then the keys are the token's. Words heard in a turn stopped before it was asked (barge-in, or a new final transcript) are asked again with the owner's next words; 0 and a stop word drop them. The token's keys reach nothing else and none is STOP. Holding the token, comparing in constant time and limiting tries across calls are the kernel's (VOICE-ro-t); what P0 may disclose is the answerer's (VOICE-ro-c, #4948). Whether a contact's pause keys the token on VoLTE and the modem decodes it is S2's C18. | Mark 2026-11-03 ("make sure speed dial works"); flip-phone note §2.1 | If C18 fails, Mark picks the fallback; `check` stays the one place a grant is made. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C3 | **The check.** Each sentence passes `owner.AgentVoice` (CH-21, with its folds) and `KeyPrompt`, alone and joined with everything the answer has already spoken, so a prompt cut across any number of sentences is held (Security on #4944). A hold ends that answer and plays the box's held clip. `KeyPrompt` holds some ordinary sentences ("dial in at nine"); those are false holds, the safe direction. | CH-21; flip-phone note §2.2 rule 1 | Tune `keyVerb` on the false holds the errand diary shows. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C4 | **Sentences.** A sentence ends at `.`, `!` or `?` followed by space, at a line break, or at 300 runes cut at a space. An abbreviation ("Dr. Lee") splits a sentence in two, which the joined check covers. | flip-phone note §2.2 rule 1 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C5 | **Barge-in and stop words.** Any partial transcript with text stops the box speaking; a final transcript that is only "stop" (or "stop it", "be quiet" and the like) stops it and asks nothing. Stopping speech narrows nothing else: STOP as a pause of the agent stays CH-3's word by text and its key. | rearchitecture note §2.3 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C6 | **End of turn is the recognizer's proposal.** The loop takes `Heard.End` as the end of the owner's speech and does not cap it with its own silence limit yet; VOICE-ro-b measures silence detection as the baseline (flip-phone note §2.4). A zero `End` counts from when the transcript arrived. | D-153, flip-phone note §2.4 | VOICE-ro-b adds the fixed cap. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C7 | **Timing and the one-moment clip.** Per turn, from the end of the owner's speech (`Heard.End`) to the first audio (clip or sentence) and to the first sentence, as nearest-rank median, 95th and 99th percentiles. The clip's wait also counts from `Heard.End`, and it plays once, while nothing has played. Each turn has one deadline, set when the turn starts and spent at its first audio, and every wait before that audio (the token still being read, the answerer, the synthesizer) is a case of the same select, so no wait falls outside it (UX on #4944); a sentence ready when it falls due (synthesized and waiting to play) goes first, and one still being synthesized plays after the clip (#5130). Targets 300 ms and 1.5 s are checked on hardware, not here. | flip-phone note §2.4; L3 on #4944 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C8 | **Pipelined, one sound at a time.** A producer checks and synthesizes the next sentence while the one before plays, one sentence ahead; the line plays one sound at a time. A failed answerer or synthesizer, or a stopped recognizer, plays the box's fixed failed clip, so a failure is never silence. A turn ends only once its producer has, so no producer outlives `Serve`; the producer stops at its ctx's end even while the answerer or synthesizer it called ignores ctx (that call is left on its own goroutine, which ends when the call returns), so a part that ignores ctx holds neither the turn nor the call (#5252). Such a call can still be running in the next owner call; C2 says what it may then read. A turn ends its producer before it drains it, so a sentence that fails to play stops the rest of the answer being synthesized (#5274). | L3 on #4944 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C9 | **One call, on one line or modem, at a time.** `Serve` holds a process-wide lock, `serving`, for the whole call, so it serves one call on one line at a time, and its bounds are per `Serve`. An owner call that rings while another is served waits for that call to end, up to `RingWait` (default 10 s, inside the time a carrier rings before voicemail), so a redial right after a dropped call is answered once the dropped call has unwound; one still ringing then, a caller who gives up, or an ended ctx returns at once and is left ringing (`TestARedialAfterADropIsAnswered`, `TestOneCallIsServedAtATime`). Each call's ctx ends at `MaxCall` (default 30 min): a call end the line never reports (a lost `NO CARRIER`), or an `Answer` that never completes, is hung up there and returns `ErrTooLong`, and the next owner call is answered (`TestACallEndsAtMaxCall`). The hang-up runs on its own ctx, `Serve`'s bounded by `HangupWait` (5 s), so a line that wedges on it, respecting ctx, holds the next call that long at most (#5274). A minute (`EndingNotice`) before the call's ctx ends, the loop lets the sentence then playing end, up to `EndingWait` (5 s; a sentence still playing then is cut), stops the rest of that answer and plays `Clips.Ending` (#5304); a 0 or a new question while it waits stops the sentence and plays the notice at once (before the new answer); the notice never starts while the owner speaks (#5690): a barge-in stops the sentence and the notice waits for the owner's final words or a 0, up to `EndingWait` from the barge-in, and a notice due while the owner speaks with no turn running waits the same way from falling due, so an owner who keeps talking hears it at most twice `EndingWait` after it fell due. A turn hushed by the notice starts no further sound once it has seen the hush; one closing between the turn's check and its `Say` starts that one sound, which the `EndingWait` cut bounds. That cut (`stop`) waits on the `Say` playing, so the bound holds only for a line whose `Say` respects ctx; a `Say` that ignores it is the modem driver's to bound, as for `Answer`. The clip is the box's fixed clip saying the call ends in a minute and the owner can call again, played on that ctx, so the notice cannot outlast `MaxCall`; a call up with less than a minute left, or with no `Ending` clip set, hears none and keeps its answer. A question the answerer had not yet seen, or one whose answer had started playing nothing (no sentence, held clip or failed clip; "One moment" alone is not an answer), is carried past the notice and asked again with the owner's next words (#5684). "Started" is one record, set as the sound passes the hush check and before `Say`, as `FirstContent` is: a clip cut once started counts as heard, and an empty clip plays nothing, so it does not. The loop waits on the clip, as on the failed clip, so a barge-in or a 0 during it acts once it has played (`TestTheOwnerHearsTheEndBeforeMaxCall`, `TestTheEndingNoticeKeepsTheCallsState`, `TestTheEndingNoticeWaitsForTheSentence`, `TestTheEndingNoticeCarriesAnUnspokenAnswer`, `TestTheEndingNoticeCarriesAnAnswerItKeptFromItsClip`, `TestTheEndingNoticeKeepsNoQuestionWhoseClipStarted`, `TestTheEndingNoticeNeverStartsOverTheOwner`, #5262, #5304). An `Answer` that ignores its ctx can still hold the lock; that is the modem driver's to bound. `Serve` takes the lock before anything else can return and defers the unlock at once, so a panic after the take (a nil ctx) frees the line (`TestAPanicInServeFreesTheLine`); its other deferred func, which runs first, cancels the call's ctx, ending its grant, and awaits the token read (`TestOnlyServeChoosesTheRung` pins the order). A caller that spoofs the owner's number can hold the line for `MaxCall` per call and redial; that is bounded, and trusting caller ID stays S2's (#5274). | #5232, #5252; Lens and reviewer on #5251 | Two lines served at once need a grant per line that still dies before that line's next call; the lock becomes per line. |
