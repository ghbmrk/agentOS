# Call loop: assumptions

Built for VOICE-ro-a (briefs/VOICE-ro.md, #4934) against SPEC CH-5, CH-17 and CH-21 and the flip-phone note §2.2 to §2.4. Covers `broker/call`.

| # | Assumption | Basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| VOICE-ro-a-C1 | **Read-only means no window.** The loop opens no kernel window, so no key means yes; 0 stops speech and every other key is ignored. Approvals by voice wait on S2 and the token (Decisions 8, items 5 and 6). | flip-phone note §2.2 rules 2 and 4 | A later slice adds windows here, with the earcon. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C2 | **The rung is the call's.** `Serve` answers only the owner's number, reads the speed-dial token (keys up to `#`, `MaxToken` keys or `TokenWindow`, default 6 s), and has `check` ask `Config.Token` once. If it checks, `check` makes a P2 `grant` that lives until `Serve` has returned; otherwise the call has none and is P0. `Serve` serves one call at a time: an owner call that rings while another is served waits for it up to `RingWait`, and one still ringing then gets `ErrBusy` and is left ringing (#5232, #5252). A `Rung` is a label and proves nothing. A `grant` is unexported, so no other package can make one, and the answerer reads it only through `Ask.Rung()`. An `Ask` made outside the package is P0, and a kept `Ask` or `grant` reads P0 once its `Serve` has returned, so in every other call. So nothing the wiring (VOICE-ro-d) passes to `Serve` raises the rung a call's `Ask` reads; what an answerer discloses at that rung is VOICE-ro-c's, which `broker/call/answer` takes only from `Ask.Rung()` (#5225 re-plan, #5232). `TestOnlyServeChoosesTheRung` holds four rules over the type-checked non-test files, with a probe for each form. (1) `loop` is named once, as a direct call in `Serve`'s body, `check` only as direct calls in `token`, and `token`, whose ctx is the grant's life, once, as a direct call in `Serve`, all outside func literals; the lock `serving` is named only in `Serve`, and nothing writes `Serve`'s `tctx` or `cancel` once they are set (#5252). (2) A `grant` literal appears only in `check`; nothing converts to `grant`, and no literal or conversion has a type-parameter type. (3) Nothing writes a `grant`'s fields or `Config.Token` (an assignment, `++`/`--`, `&`, a range target, a method on the field, or a literal key), no `Config` literal leaves out its keys, and nothing converts to `Config`. (4) No file imports `unsafe`, `reflect` or `C`. So every `grant` is `check`'s or a zero value, which reads P0 (its `live` is nil), and a whole-value write or copy only moves one of those (Security on #5229). Left to review and the Serve and token tests: what `check`, `token` and `Serve` do with their inputs, such as which keys and which `live` reach `check`. `//go:linkname` and assembly fail `tools/srclint.py` in CI. A `grant` held past its call (a package var, a field, a shared answerer) needs no rule: one call is served at a time, so it reads P0 in any other (`TestOneCallIsServedAtATime`). It listens from the start, so what the owner says while the token is read is heard. That turn starts at once and is asked of the answerer once the loop has the rung, at that rung; until then the keys are the token's. Words heard in a turn stopped before it was asked (barge-in, or a new final transcript) are asked again with the owner's next words; 0 and a stop word drop them. The token's keys reach nothing else and none is STOP. Holding the token, comparing in constant time and limiting tries across calls are the kernel's (VOICE-ro-t); what P0 may disclose is the answerer's (VOICE-ro-c, #4948). Whether a contact's pause keys the token on VoLTE and the modem decodes it is S2's C18. | Mark 2026-11-03 ("make sure speed dial works"); flip-phone note §2.1 | If C18 fails, Mark picks the fallback; `check` stays the one place a grant is made. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C3 | **The check.** Each sentence passes `owner.AgentVoice` (CH-21, with its folds) and `KeyPrompt`, alone and joined with everything the answer has already spoken, so a prompt cut across any number of sentences is held (Security on #4944). A hold ends that answer and plays the box's held clip. `KeyPrompt` holds some ordinary sentences ("dial in at nine"); those are false holds, the safe direction. | CH-21; flip-phone note §2.2 rule 1 | Tune `keyVerb` on the false holds the errand diary shows. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C4 | **Sentences.** A sentence ends at `.`, `!` or `?` followed by space, at a line break, or at 300 runes cut at a space. An abbreviation ("Dr. Lee") splits a sentence in two, which the joined check covers. | flip-phone note §2.2 rule 1 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C5 | **Barge-in and stop words.** Any partial transcript with text stops the box speaking; a final transcript that is only "stop" (or "stop it", "be quiet" and the like) stops it and asks nothing. Stopping speech narrows nothing else: STOP as a pause of the agent stays CH-3's word by text and its key. | rearchitecture note §2.3 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C6 | **End of turn is the recognizer's proposal.** The loop takes `Heard.End` as the end of the owner's speech and does not cap it with its own silence limit yet; VOICE-ro-b measures silence detection as the baseline (flip-phone note §2.4). A zero `End` counts from when the transcript arrived. | D-153, flip-phone note §2.4 | VOICE-ro-b adds the fixed cap. |
| VOICE-ro-a-C7 | **Timing and the one-moment clip.** Per turn, from the end of the owner's speech (`Heard.End`) to the first audio (clip or sentence) and to the first sentence, as nearest-rank median, 95th and 99th percentiles. The clip's wait also counts from `Heard.End`, and it plays once, while nothing has played. Each turn has one deadline, set when the turn starts and spent at its first audio, and every wait before that audio (the token still being read, the answerer, the synthesizer) is a case of the same select, so no wait falls outside it (UX on #4944); a sentence ready when it falls due (synthesized and waiting to play) goes first, and one still being synthesized plays after the clip (#5130). Targets 300 ms and 1.5 s are checked on hardware, not here. | flip-phone note §2.4; L3 on #4944 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C8 | **Pipelined, one sound at a time.** A producer checks and synthesizes the next sentence while the one before plays, one sentence ahead; the line plays one sound at a time. A failed answerer or synthesizer, or a stopped recognizer, plays the box's fixed failed clip, so a failure is never silence. A turn ends only once its producer has, so no producer outlives `Serve`; the producer stops at its ctx's end even while the answerer or synthesizer it called ignores ctx (that call is left on its own goroutine, which ends when the call returns), so a part that ignores ctx holds neither the turn nor the call (#5252). | L3 on #4944 | — |
| VOICE-ro-a-C9 | **One call, on one line or modem, at a time.** `Serve` holds a process-wide lock, `serving`, for the whole call, so it serves one call on one line at a time, and its bounds are per `Serve`. An owner call that rings while another is served waits for that call to end, up to `RingWait` (default 10 s, inside the time a carrier rings before voicemail), so a redial right after a dropped call is answered once the dropped call has unwound; one still ringing then, a caller who gives up, or an ended ctx returns at once and is left ringing (`TestARedialAfterADropIsAnswered`, `TestOneCallIsServedAtATime`). Each call's ctx ends at `MaxCall` (default 30 min): a call end the line never reports (a lost `NO CARRIER`) is hung up there and returns `ErrTooLong`, and the next owner call is answered (`TestACallEndsAtMaxCall`). An `Answer` that ignores its ctx can still hold the lock; that is the modem driver's to bound. `Serve` cancels the call's ctx, which ends its grant, before it unlocks, in one deferred func, and takes the lock before anything else can return (`TestOnlyServeChoosesTheRung` pins the order). | #5232, #5252; Lens and reviewer on #5251 | Two lines served at once need a grant per line that still dies before that line's next call; the lock becomes per line. |
