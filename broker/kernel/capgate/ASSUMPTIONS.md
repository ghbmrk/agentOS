# Kernel byte-cap check: assumptions

Each row is a reading of the spec, or a property the code relies on, that a
reviewer may want to change (issue #4173, KRN-cap-first; D-255, D-260, D-262).

| # | Assumption | Spec basis | If it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| KRN-cap-1 | **A count is fresh by the kernel's own clock, from when its summary was asked for; the clock only ages it.** The age is `now - ask` on the monotonic clock, never the enforcer's timestamp, so neither clock's drift nor the answer's travel time makes an old count look new; a reading earlier than the ask has no fresh count; fresh means no older than `Fresh`, twice `wire.SummaryInterval`, and exactly `Fresh` is still fresh. The caller passes `time.Now`, whose monotonic reading never steps back; a wall clock stepped back by less than a count's age would make it look younger (#4374). No clock reading orders anything (KRN-cap-2, -5, -6). | NET-2 Byte caps ("no older than twice the summary interval") | Measuring from the answer instead would let a slow answer extend a count's life. |
| KRN-cap-2 | **"What the kernel sent since the count" is every send the count may not hold.** The gate numbers each ask under its lock (1, 2, 3...) and `End` records the last number issued, so a send is held by a count only if it ended before that count's ask was issued (one ending after it, at any clock reading, is not); every other reservation (reserved after the ask, or still in flight at it) is added in full, to its group, or to every group when the latest summary puts its destination in none (the enforcer may count it in any). `Ask` comes before the request is written, and `End` only once no byte of the send can still reach the enforcer, so after the transfer is acknowledged end to end, not when the core has written it (#4374); `End` of a send the gate does not hold (another gate's, or one already counted) does nothing. This counts some bytes twice and never one zero times, so it errs toward asking. | NET-2 Byte caps ("adds what it has sent to that group since the time that count covers ... plus its bytes in flight") | A per-byte sent figure from the transport would let the kernel subtract what the enforcer has already seen. |
| KRN-cap-3 | **The retry margin is one full re-send.** Each reservation is its size times `1 + Retries` (`Retries = 1`), so a send the transport retries once still lands inside the cap; a second retry needs a fresh `Reserve`. Sizes are IP bytes, headers and acknowledgements included, as the enforcer counts them; the caller estimates them. | NET-2 Byte caps ("a retry margin"); A18 ("with bytes in flight, a retry") | A smaller margin uses more of the cap and needs the transport to bound its retransmissions in bytes. |
| KRN-cap-4 | **At the cap is allowed; past it is refused.** The gate and the enforcer share one edge, `wire.PastCap` (count > cap), so they cannot disagree by a byte. A refusal is `*PastCap`, carrying the raise the owner is asked for (`Raise`). | NET-2 Byte caps ("traffic past a cap is dropped"); D-255 item 3 | If the enforcer's edge moves, both sides move with `wire.PastCap`. |
| KRN-cap-5 | **The enforcer's day and groups come only from its summary.** A summary replaces every group held, so groups that merged mid-day count as one; a summary on an earlier day, answering an ask issued at or before the one held (so each ticket answers once), or for a ticket another gate issued, is refused (`ErrSummary`); a destination in no group of the latest summary has no count. | NET-2 Byte caps ("on the enforcer's day", groups) | A day held by the kernel would let a clock difference reset a cap early (D-255 item 3). |
| KRN-cap-6 | **A paused summary is no count.** After a core trip the enforcer pauses summary requests until the "core reflashed" step (D-260, D-262). On a Paused answer `Paused` drops the count and records the last ask number issued, so an answer to any ask issued before the call is refused, whatever the clock reads, and only a summary asked for after it counts. Reservations are kept across the pause. A request the enforcer drops unanswered (as #4347's A18 words it) needs no call: the count ages out at `Fresh`. | KRN-12 Response, NET-2 The channel (as #4347 words it) | If summaries resumed on the passkey instead of the reflash step, nothing here changes: the gate only waits for a summary asked after the pause. |
| KRN-cap-7 | **This package is the check only.** Reading summaries off the channel, not asking while paused, refusing the step and texting the raise, ending an edge fetch before its acknowledgements pass a cap, and checking each channel message against the shared format and rate before it is sent are the follow-up rows filed from #4173. Until they land nothing links the gate, so it adds no trusted-core lines. | #4173 | Wiring it into the kernel grows the trusted core and needs its D-159 row. |
| KRN-cap-8 | **A group can split at the enforcer's midnight, so each send also stays within its destination's own cap.** Groups are formed per day ("share an address resolved that day") and a count can be up to `Fresh` old, so the gate may hold a summary from before a midnight at which its group split. A send is checked twice: the group's count, the sends it may not hold and this one within the group's cap (the summary's day), and this destination's own sends since the count, plus any to a destination in no group, within its own cap (the next day, when counts restart). As every send passed the second check, any group the next day forms, its cap the sum of its members', holds its members' sends. A summary gives each destination's own cap, and the group's cap is their sum; a sum that overflows is refused (`ErrGroup`). | NET-2 Byte caps (groups, "per day on its own clock") | If the enforcer kept a day's groups into the next day, the own-cap check would only ask early. |
