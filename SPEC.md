# agentOS — Core System Specification v1.0

**Normative language:** MUST / MUST NOT / SHOULD / MAY. Requirements carry IDs (e.g. `KRN-1`), and §18 traces each to an acceptance test.
**Evidence labels:** [Fact] established or verifiable · [Inference] reasoned, untested · [Risk] known open question.

## 0. The design in one page

agentOS has four parts and one rule that connects them.

| Part | What it is | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Log** | One append-only, hash-chained record of everything that happened (§4) | All state. Everything else is a projection of it. |
| **Kernel** | A pure function: (log + event) → (new entries + effects to perform) (§4) | Nothing of its own. It checks plans against keys and writes the result. |
| **Key** | A signed capability that can only be narrowed. Its caveats bound the verb, the account, the arguments, the spend, the expiry, the data-flow labels and the hold window (§5) | Authority, and nothing else. |
| **Clerk** | The holder of one credential and the only exit to one account or device. It re-checks the key's caveats itself before it acts (§6) | Credentials, which never leave it. |

**The rule:** a model never acts. A planner, which is any model-driven program, writes a **typed plan**: a list of verb calls with arguments, each citing a key. The kernel checks every step against its key and the log, writes the plan and its verdict to the log, and hands the allowed steps to clerks. Each clerk checks the same caveats again with the same code, acts, and returns a typed result that the kernel logs. Nothing else can cause an effect outside the box.

**One planning mode.** The planner plans over handles, not content. Untrusted content (mail, web pages, documents) is read only by a **quarantined reader**, a typed function that returns typed values (a date, an amount, a yes or no, an enumerated class). Every value carries the data-flow labels of what produced it, and a key's label caveats decide where it may go (§7).

**Authority grows from use.** The box proposes keys from what the owner already approves. Narrowing a key is automatic. Under a key, a reversible effect runs and shows `UNDO`; under a standing key an irreversible one waits a hold set by novelty; widening, or any new kind of authority, needs a tap on the owner's phone passkey, the root of all keys (§5).

**Changes are replayed first.** Any change to a procedure, a route, a clerk declaration or the box's own code is replayed against the log's history before it goes live. The more novel an effect is against that history, the longer its hold window, during which the owner can stop it (§8).

**The owner sees none of this.** The owner texts, calls or sends a photo to get things done. The ask wording, the approval tiers and the number of taps are those of §10; the three boxes are those of §3.

**Eight mechanisms** (D-151). agentOS has eight mechanisms: the log, keys, declarations (CLK-14), the graph (CLK-10), narrowing (CLK-12), the witness (KRN-10), the planner and recovery. The enforcer is a separate box. New security and new capability arrive as data on these. The count may only fall: a change that adds a mechanism retires at least one, or goes to Mark as a decision with the measurement that shows the need, and every spec PR's review checks the count.

### 0.1 The first slice

The first end-to-end task built on this design is **"Sort my inbox and unsubscribe from and delete the spam"** (EVL-2). It exercises every part once: a mail clerk, read and organize verbs, the quarantined reader, a key proposed from use, the hold window on a novel effect, and the replay gate. It runs in QEMU against a synthetic mailbox, fake unsubscribe endpoints and seeded injection mails, so it needs no hardware.

## 1. Purpose

agentOS turns whatever AI the owner has (frontier plans and APIs, private hosted models, local models, in any mix, including local only) into **accepted work**, using a machine the owner physically controls. It **compounds** what it learns and keeps **authority and credentials** under the owner's sole control.

**Objective:** the most verified leverage per unit of owner effort, never below the structural security floor.

- **Leverage:** the consequence and breadth of the verified outcomes the owner can cause.
- **Owner effort:** attention, time, decisions, things the owner must remember, checking, and recovery, counted across failed tasks as well as accepted ones.
- **Verified:** the resulting state was checked against the request (OWN-13). A wrong or unverified outcome is negative leverage.
- **Structural security floor:** protection held by a kernel-enforced mechanism whatever the planner does and whether or not the owner is paying attention. Each guarantee states its trusted base and any bounded channel it leaves; none rests on planner judgment or owner vigilance. Its core is the custody rule (CLK-1, §6) and gated irreversible effects (KRN-4, CH-10, KEY-5).

**Priority when goals conflict:** the floor, then verified correctness, then effort against leverage. The floor is never traded. Prefer designs that improve both effort and leverage; otherwise the trade is recorded as a decision (DECISIONS.md).

### 1.1 Owner principles

Each principle names the requirements that realize it, or says where none exists yet. A change that works against one states why in its PR.

- **OWN-1** **Irreducible owner inputs.** The owner supplies intent, authority, value judgments where their preference is unknown, and proof of identity (CH-3, CH-6, HLD-1). The system supplies everything else.
- **OWN-2** **Capture once.** What the owner has said is kept across restarts, unlocks, dropped calls, and failed deliveries; asking for it again is a defect. Unauthenticated content is kept, but its authority is not inherited (CH-14's `RUN`).
- **OWN-3** **Ask rule.** Ask only when the chance that the answer differs from the default, times the cost of a wrong default, exceeds the cost of asking; otherwise proceed undoably and visibly, meaning logged and listed in STATUS (HLD-1, CH-15, CH-16). The rule governs choices within keyed authority only. Whether an effect needs approval is decided by the kernel (KRN-4, CH-10), never by this estimate.
- **OWN-4** **Notify rule.** Notify only when knowing now changes what the owner would do or believe: an outcome of a task the owner asked for (sent as conversation, CH-15), a decision due, an actionable failure, a security alert, or a liveness signal whose absence is the alarm (CH-15's liveness line). Routine retries and progress are not sent. The one exception is the acknowledgement: when no reply to an owner task message has gone out 5 seconds (owner-set, D-134) after it reached the box, including while the planner sandbox is still waking, the kernel sends one short first-person acknowledgement in fixed wording (e.g. "On it."), at most one per owner message and none once a reply has gone out. It is conversation (CH-15), carries no content from the planner, and expects no reply (D-127, D-134).
- **OWN-5** **Investigate first, ask once.** A question comes after the system has found what it can, as one ask with a recommendation and the tradeoff that matters. Reduce the number of decisions before reducing the friction of each (CH-10, KEY-5).
- **OWN-6** **Conclusion first.** Every message leads with the outcome or the decision; detail comes on request (`MORE`, CH-12, CH-20), served from the log (LOG-1), never by rerunning the task.
- **OWN-7** **Mechanism over vigilance.** Owner attention is a security control only at authority boundaries: keys, approvals of irreversible effects (CH-10, both tiers), and recovery (CH-3, KEY-5). Nothing else may depend on the owner noticing.
- **OWN-8** **Prompts are a finite security budget.** Each prompt spends attention that approvals depend on. A change that adds a prompt states why a kernel check, a key, or reversibility cannot replace it (VRB-3, KEY-5).
- **OWN-9** **Gate by consequence.** Gating follows irreversibility, blast radius, and disclosure. Novelty and frequency only ever raise a gate (KRN-3, KEY-8). A reversible effect is acted on, reported, and undoable; an irreversible one gets a concrete, prepared approval unless a kernel-checked key covers it (KRN-4, VRB-3, PLN-3, CH-10, CH-16, KEY-5).
- **OWN-10** **Authority is keyed, never inferred.** Preferences, silence, past behaviour, and content never widen authority, and data never becomes instruction. Observed behaviour may justify proposing a key, which the owner then makes (KEY-5). Presentation preferences may be learned; a preference about disclosure or authority is a key.
- **OWN-11** **Design for the weakest channel.** Text and voice can be spoofed, and a number can be SIM-swapped. Identity proof scales with the authority exercised (CH-3, CH-10, CH-19); a weaker factor never substitutes for a stronger one.
- **OWN-12** **A channel carries only what it can protect** (CH-19, CH-20). Journeys that need the local page are a named list (CH-8), kept as short as the floor allows.
- **OWN-13** **Own the whole loop.** The system prepares, acts, verifies, follows up, and reports. Done means the resulting state was checked; an effect the system cannot observe is reported as unconfirmed. Authority and preconditions are rechecked before dispatch and uncertain effects reconciled (CLK-2, KRN-8), and owner outcomes feed evaluation (HLD-3); for a goal with a check, the resulting state is measured later and fed back (LOG-6); the mail clerk reads back the result of each effect (VRB-2); for other clerks, a check of the resulting state after each effect has no requirement yet (BOARD OWN-gaps).
- **OWN-14** **Compound.** Repeated approvals lead to a proposed, bounded key (recipient, amount, frequency, expiry, revocation; KEY-5); recurring routines become skills offered once (HLD-4); corrections become preferences within OWN-10.
- **OWN-15** **Constant owner vocabulary.** One model for approve, undo, key, and stop (CH-11). A new concept the owner must learn has to justify itself.
- **OWN-16** **Absorb time.** Waiting, monitoring, exchanges with third parties, and deadlines are held by the system, and every open obligation can be retrieved: exceptions by `STATUS` (CH-11, CH-12); obligations on track are retrieved by asking in plain words, for goals with a check (LOG-6); other on-track obligations have no requirement until step 4 (PLAN.md; BOARD OWN-gaps).
- **OWN-17** **The system pays for its failures.** Progress is kept. Uncertain external effects are reconciled before any retry, so nothing is paid or sent twice (KRN-4's log, KRN-8, A4). Only actionable failures reach the owner, each with a proposed fix (CH-12).
- **OWN-18** **Stop and correction stay cheap and available.** Stop works while tasks or providers are stuck (DEP-1, CH-11); correction then needs a working route, and keeping it available without one has no requirement until step 4 (PLAN.md; BOARD OWN-gaps). Reversibility is stated truthfully, including when it ends.
- **OWN-19** **Journey evaluation.** The objective is measured on a fixed set of owner journeys, frozen before each qualification run (§18); each journey run includes a correction, a failure, and a spoofed or injected message.
  - Effort components are reported separately, never as one score: texts and words read, replies, decisions, repeated asks, local-page visits, time to outcome, and failures the owner handled.
  - Each suppression measure is paired with a miss measure: notifications withheld against needed ones missed, prompts removed against effects outside authority.
  - Each journey's needed notifications, expected decisions, and authority bounds are frozen with it, so the system under test decides neither.
  - A security failure is any effect outside keyed authority or without the logged plan step and approval it needs (KRN-4, CH-10, KEY-5), any credential or canary disclosure, or injected content acted on as instruction. Any security failure fails agentOS's run (A10); baselines report theirs.
  - Effort per accepted task sums effort over all runs, failed ones included, divided by accepted tasks.

## 2. Dependency budget

The product MUST work with only the dependencies in the first two rows.

| Class | Dependency | Why it can't be removed |
|---|---|---|
| **Inherent** | Electricity, an internet connection cabled to the enforcer (HW-12), the owner's phone (any phone that can text and call; setup, recovery, and the owner page need one that joins WPA3 Wi-Fi, CH-9, D-081), and at least one source of model intelligence: a frontier account, a private hosted model, or local models alone (PLN-7) | The purpose is to apply the intelligence the owner has; the owner needs some way to talk to the box |
| **Commodity (one)** | A **mobile carrier SIM** for the box (any prepaid plan) | A phone number requires a carrier. A SIM is swappable, needs no agentOS account, and gives the box a backup internet link. |
| **Optional** | Off-box backup target (owner's choice, encrypted); update mirrors; the public project repository and peer installations (§15); the owner's existing accounts (Google, email…); frontier and private hosted model routes beyond the first; the owner's other computers as compute hosts (PLN-7); analysis toolkits in sandbox images (PLN-6); Tor and third-party write relays (publication only, PUB-1) | Each adds value; none is required to boot, control, or stop the system |
| **Forbidden** | Any server run by the agentOS project; a relay run by the project, or any relay in the path of owner access or control; an app-store app; an agentOS account; vendor activation; a public domain or certificate; telemetry | Each is a single point of failure, control, or surveillance outside the owner's hands |

- **DEP-1** Boot, owner control (status/stop), the log, and recovery MUST work with only inherent and commodity dependencies, and MUST NOT need AI inference.
- **DEP-2** No component MAY require a service operated by the agentOS project, now or after any update.
- **DEP-3** Every optional dependency MUST be removable without loss of control or data integrity. Losing it degrades capability only.
- **DEP-4** Software updates MUST be verifiable from content hashes and signatures alone, fetchable from any mirror, and installable offline from a drive. No update MAY be required for the system to keep working.

[Inference] With no relay, the owner reaches the box remotely only by **text and voice** through the SIM. Rich interaction (the live browser view) happens **only on the box's own local Wi-Fi**, which in practice means at home. This is the deliberate cost of zero third-party services (see §10.3).

## 3. Physical form

### 3.0 System roles
- **HW-9** **Three roles, one controller.** The **controller** is a headless, always-on mini PC running agentOS from its internal SSD; it is the only controller form for the first release and holds everything private (sealed store, credentials, log, kernel, private data). A **workstation** is dedicated hardware cabled to the controller and running the agentOS image on its own disk (HW-11). A **helper app** is a Windows or macOS app on a dual-use computer such as a gaming PC or a Mac (HW-11). A USB stick is a flasher or recovery stick (HW-10), never a controller or a helper. This controller is the **core**, and web fetching and the second line are on the **edge** (§3.0a, HW-14 to HW-16).
- **HW-10** **One signed image, two acquisition paths.** One signed image serves controller install, the edge (HW-15), recovery and workstations. The owner gets it by (1) **download from the project's GitHub releases** with a signed flasher (Windows, Mac, Linux) that verifies the signature, writes a stick and shows a one-time pairing QR, or (2) a **preloaded mini PC from a third-party seller**, which keeps DEP-2 (no agentOS-operated service). A seller is untrusted: at first boot the box verifies its own image and refuses to run on a mismatch, every secret is generated on the box at first boot (HW-1) with one exception, and the seller ships only a **claim sticker**: a one-time code that lets the first phone claim the box, unlocks nothing and dies at first claim, plus the setup network of the enforcer's access point (name, password and a Wi-Fi QR; NET-2, NET-3). The one exception is that setup network's password: the seller makes it per installation and prints it on the sticker (on the download path the flasher makes it and shows or prints its Wi-Fi QR), it opens only the enforcer's enrolment page (NET-4), and at that enrolment the enforcer replaces the setup network with the Owner network, whose password it generates and shows only on its own page, with a Wi-Fi QR, before the phone reaches the claim (CH-9). Until then, whoever holds the sticker, or the flasher's QR, can impersonate the setup network, and one who impersonates it during the enrolment also learns the Owner network's password; this residual is accepted (D-105). On the preloaded path the seller preloads the enforcer and its access point too; on the download path the same flasher also writes the enforcer's and its access point's signed images (NET-2). The core and the edge never turn Wi-Fi on (D-178). A preloaded box trusts the seller's hardware, firmware and boot chain unless the owner reflashes it from a verified download stick (risk 26). A claim on an already claimed box is refused with "already claimed" and the owner reinstalls; apart from the setup network's password, no secret exists before the claim. Everything after acquisition comes from signed releases through the controller (UPD-1). Model weights, too large for a release asset, are pinned by a **signed manifest on GitHub** (hash per model); the bytes may come from any mirror, and a weight whose hash does not match is never loaded.
- **HW-11** **Helpers hold nothing private at rest.** A helper holds only code and hash-verified public weights: no credential, log, sealed store or private data. Two forms exist. An **installed workstation** (the image on a dedicated machine's own disk, installed from a stick once with the owner's approval on the phone) MAY receive private content (PLN-3). A **helper app** MAY receive cleared content only. There are no stick helpers and no reboot-into-helper. A helper accepts connections only from the controller (mutual TLS with short-lived keys from the sealed store); the controller updates helpers and refuses stale ones. The controller wakes a workstation or powered-off gaming PC by Wake-on-LAN when work is queued and powers a workstation down when idle. The helper app runs as a background service that starts after Wake-on-LAN without a login, uses the GPU only while the PC is idle, and pauses at once when a game or full-screen app starts. It leaves the PC as it was apart from its own install (HW-8). Helper output is untrusted model output: it never acts and always passes the kernel's gates (KRN-4, CH-10).
- **HW-12** **Network layout.** Port 1 of the controller goes to the enforcer and carries its internet access. Port 2 (or USB4 networking) is a private segment for workstations only, with no internet except through the kernel; several workstations share it through a small switch. Gaming PCs and Macs stay on the home network and their helper apps talk only to the controller. This layout is additional to DEV-2. The enforcer (NET-2) sits between the home router and the home network, and the core's port 1 and the edge's internet port each connect through it on that box's own enforcer segment, whose reach the enforcer limits as NET-2 says: the edge reaches the internet and the online everyday devices it relays, the core and the edge talk only over a direct cable from the edge to a third core network port (or a USB network adapter) used for nothing else, never through the enforcer, the core reaches only its owner-signed destinations and the offline everyday devices, and neither reaches a device on the enforcer's safety list beyond what NET-2 allows.
- **HW-13** **Headless.** The owner's phone is the only UI for setup, approvals and chat. A one-time screen and keyboard are allowed only where firmware forces them (for example, to pick the boot device once).

### 3.0a Core and edge (Mark, 2026-10-18)
- **HW-14** **Three boxes, one rule each.** agentOS is split by trust, not by topic, and each box is defined by one rule (Mark, 2026-10-18: 16:58Z "Core and edge", 17:26Z "Own box", 17:50Z "Okay implement this", 18:02Z "Owner line on core"; goal, 16:57Z: "massive leverage at low effort thru combinations of hugely private data and credentials that's only possible through extreme structural security"). **Edge:** whatever reads the internet holds nothing (HW-15). **Core:** whatever holds everything reaches the home only as the enforcer allows (HW-16). **Enforcer:** whatever acts in the home cannot be changed by the core (NET-1 to NET-7, §13). Every installation has a core, an edge, and an enforcer with the access point it adds beside the unchanged ISP router (NET-3), so home control is never optional (Mark, 2026-10-26: "Make enforcer standard - require home control", D-178). Each IP device takes one of NET-2's three paths (Mark, 2026-10-18 18:34Z: "Three paths"): a device on the safety list only through the enforcer, an online everyday device (one that needs its vendor's cloud) through a fixed-schema relay on the edge, and an offline everyday device straight from the core; cameras and microphones are offline only. The core reaches the enforcer only through the kernel's channel, and rule changes travel only through the owner's phone (NET-2, NET-4). Wherever this spec says the controller, the box or the kernel does something, the core does it unless HW-15 gives it to the edge.
- **HW-15** **Edge: reads the internet, holds nothing.** The edge is its own box, never a virtual machine on the core. It carries web fetches and the second line (CLK-8) with its modem and SIM; the owner line's modem and SIM stay on the core (CH-1; Mark, 2026-10-18 18:02Z: "Owner line on core"), so owner texts and calls never cross the edge. It only transports: it runs no planner sandbox, holds no model key, sealed store, log, private item at rest or credential other than its link key and the second line's SIM, and reaches neither the enforcer nor any home device except the online everyday devices NET-2 lists (TVs, streaming boxes), which it relays under NET-2's Device paths rules (Mark, 2026-10-18 18:34Z: "Three paths"). It talks to the core over one mutually authenticated, encrypted link that carries any content, framed only by kind, origin and length (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:56Z: fixed-format feeds are not future proof). The link is a direct cable from the edge to a core port used for nothing else (a USB Ethernet adapter where ports 1 and 2 are taken); the edge reaches the internet only through its own port, on the enforcer's edge segment (§13; static address and outside resolver, NET-2), never through the home network. The core parses the framing strictly and treats every item from the edge as untrusted input: web content arrives as `public` (PLN-3), and texts and calls to the second line are untrusted data (CLK-8). The edge sees what it carries: the web fetches it makes, including any query a guest derived from private items, the second line's texts and call audio, including what the owner's policy sends a third party (CLK-8), and the state, commands and in-use pairing keys of the online everyday devices it relays. Inbound texts never wait on the edge's disk: while the link is down they stay with the carrier or on the SIM and are passed on when it returns. At onboarding the core accepts exactly one first edge key, only on the link port and only before the Owner Card confirm (§12.1 step 1, CH-7). After a restore, move or reinstall the core trusts no edge key, the restored one included, until the owner enrols one in an owner-page step (CH-9) that accepts a key only on the link port, and it never opens the first-key window again; the backup (BAK-1) holds the edge key and the record of revoked edge keys, but a backup cannot show a revocation made after it, so a restore never skips that step. The owner enrols an edge added after the Owner Card confirm, and any replacement edge key, in that owner-page step, signed by a hardware key (KEY-4), and the old key is revoked in the same step, and the core never accepts a key it has revoked; agentOS then texts the owner that it did, as a tamper alert (after a restore too), because a replacement the owner did not start means someone else changed the edge. Replacing the edge moves nothing private.
- **HW-16** **Core: holds everything.** The core is the controller of HW-9. It holds all private data, every credential and the models; runs the kernel, sealed store, log, clerks and the guest planner sandboxes in their sandboxes as §4 to §7 have them, where models never see credentials and deterministic code acts; holds the owner line (CH-1); and serves the owner page (CH-9). It accepts no connection from the internet or the edge except on the link. A guest's traffic leaves only through the kernel: public web fetches through the edge (PLN-3), and everything private on the core's own outbound connections, with TLS ending on the core: clerks' declared endpoints (CLK-3), signed updates (DEP-4), the backup target (BAK-1) and, where the route rules allow cloud models (PLN-3), those providers' endpoints. Whether private work uses cloud or local models is open (Mark, 2026-10-18 17:01Z), and either answer fits this rule. The core reaches only the owner-signed destinations and devices NET-2 lists: offline everyday devices and offline cameras' and microphones' streams directly, safety-list devices only through the enforcer channel, and online everyday devices only through the edge's relay.
- **HW-17** **Every box or app is a node** (D-151). The core, the edge, a workstation, a helper app and any later form are nodes; each runs the one signed image (HW-10) or the helper app. A node is enrolled once, by the proof its role's declaration names (CLK-14): for the edge, its first key on the link port before the Owner Card confirm (HW-15); for a workstation, the owner's approval of its install (HW-11). Its node key's caveats (KEY-2) say what it may receive, hold and do, and a role changes only through a new signed key. Every node talks to the core over one link protocol, mutually authenticated and encrypted, carrying any content framed by kind, origin and length, on the path its role declares (the edge's dedicated cable, the workstation segment of HW-12, or the home network for a helper app); what a node sends is untrusted. The enforcer is not a node, since it shares no kernel, secret or update source with agentOS (NET-2). HW-11's and HW-15's pairing and link clauses are role declarations under this rule. Compute hosts (PLN-7) and backup targets are declared endpoints (CLK-14).

### 3.1 Components the owner receives
1. **The controller:** a mini PC with an internal SSD (1 TB or more recommended), either preloaded or flashed from the project's image (HW-10), plus a **recovery stick**, any USB stick flashed with the image, kept by the owner.
2. **The Owner Card** (printed; keep it like a passport). It carries:
   - the **setup secret** and its short **setup code**;
   - the **store passphrase** (at least 80 bits, e.g. seven words from a standard 7,776-word list), also printed as a QR code, used to unlock on a PC the box doesn't know (STO-2);
   - the **fingerprint of the core's page authority** (CH-9), and one line that agentOS asks for a code or secret only on a page whose address bar shows the core's name;
   - two detachable sheets, each kept away from the box: the **paper approval-code grid** (fallback authenticator, CH-4), and the **recovery key** on its own sheet, so using the grid never takes the recovery key out of storage.
3. **Cellular modem with SIM slot** for the owner line, on the core (CH-1). MVP: a separate USB LTE modem with voice support. Target: built into the core (§3.3). The **edge** (HW-15) is a second small box with two network ports, plus a second modem and SIM where the owner has a second line (CLK-8).

- **HW-1** The image MUST NOT contain any per-owner secret. **The box generates every Owner Card at first boot**, after the core's page authority, so every card carries its fingerprint (CH-9); the owner's phone shows it to print or save, the owner confirms one passphrase word and one recovery-key group, and the box then erases its display copy, so a seller of preloaded boxes knows no per-owner secret (HW-10). Recovery-key rotation (REC-4) works the same way.
- **HW-2** The modem MUST support SMS and voice to the host over USB, and MMS send and receive with its cellular-data bearer where CH-20 and CH-22 use them. The owner-line modem's data MUST be limited to the carrier's MMS APN, with a route only to the carrier's MMS host and no default route over the modem, and MUST be off where MMS is not used, so it is no uplink around the enforcer; the edge's second-line modem data is limited the same way (risk 29). [Risk] Voice-over-USB and MMS support vary by modem; qualify specific models.

### 3.2 Host compatibility
- **HW-3** Supported hosts: x86-64 PCs with UEFI firmware, roughly the last decade. Windows-on-ARM laptops and pre-UEFI PCs are out of scope.
- **HW-4** **Floor profile:** 4 modest cores, 8 GB RAM, USB 3.2 Gen 2, no accelerator (reference: GEEKOM Air12 Lite, N95). Every requirement MUST pass at the floor. Every acceptance test MUST pass at the floor with no compute host. Larger hosts and owner compute hosts (PLN-7) MAY add throughput and larger local models, changing only measured figures, never semantics or authority.
- **HW-5** Secure Boot MUST be supported through a Microsoft-signed shim, booting only a distribution-signed boot loader and kernel, so no per-PC key enrollment is needed [Fact: standard for mainstream distributions; S7 booted Ubuntu's shim from emulated USB with Secure Boot on. Debian unverified, risk 14].
- **HW-5a** Secure Boot does not cover agentOS's initrd, kernel command line, or `/usr` root hash (S7). Their integrity on a trusted host MUST rest on TPM measured boot: the trusted-host sealed store slot (STO-2) is sealed to the PCRs that measure them, so a modified boot path cannot unlock unattended. The seal MUST be to a **policy**, not to literal PCR values, so an A/B update does not lock the sealed store: a signed PCR policy (systemd-stub PCR 11 with `systemd-cryptenroll --tpm2-public-key`, which needs unified kernel images) or `systemd-pcrlock`, whose prediction the updater extends with the new release before reboot. With a signed policy, the PCR policy is signed by a per-box key that the kernel holds inside the sealed store (CLK-1, STO-2), never readable from the drive alone and never a project key, and only for a release that passed UPD-8 verification; `systemd-pcrlock` needs no signing key. [Inference: from systemd documentation; sealing was not exercised in S7.] On an unknown host the box cannot detect offline tampering with the drive; this is accepted for MVP and stated in the owner's guide, and the unknown-host unlock text and local page say at the point of use: "If this drive was out of your hands, unlock it only on your trusted PC." Per-PC key enrollment is not offered; an agentOS shim is deferred past MVP (DECISIONS.md, risk 11).
- **HW-6** Booting from USB without a screen works where firmware already prefers USB, or after a one-time boot-order change. [Risk] For a recovery or flasher stick (HW-10) on a PC with no keyboard, the integrated key (§3.3) presses the boot key from a per-model recipe. dm-verity turns USB read errors into boot or runtime failures (S7), so qualification (A1) MUST include a full read-verify of the image on each tested PC and stick.
- **HW-7** Macs are not boot targets [Inference: Apple boot security requires interactive recovery-mode changes]. A macOS host app running the system in a virtual machine is a post-MVP option. A Mac runs the helper app (HW-11), which is not a boot target or a virtual machine.
- **HW-8** **The host PC is left as it was.** *Scope: this applies to any computer that is not the controller or an installed workstation, namely a PC booted from a recovery or flasher stick, and the computer a helper app runs on, where the helper app writes nothing outside its own install. On the controller and on a workstation the install erases and owns the internal SSD, after the owner confirms it on the phone.* By default agentOS MUST NOT mount, write, or repair any disk of the host other than the stick itself (and an internal disk the owner gave it under HW-8a): internal disks are not auto-mounted, not swapped to, and not used by any service, planner sandbox, or clerk. Reading the partition table and volume signatures read-only is allowed, only to describe disks for HW-8a, including whether one holds Windows or BitLocker; no file system on a host disk is opened. [Inference: a Windows disk left hibernated or by Fast Startup can be corrupted by a write from another OS, so never mounting avoids the risk entirely.] agentOS MUST NOT write the host's hardware clock, by tool or by the kernel's NTP-synchronized RTC update, and treats the hardware clock as unverified until network or carrier time is available, since Windows keeps it in local time. It writes no persistent UEFI variable and no TPM non-volatile state of the host except the firmware-level changes it discloses, which are:
  - the boot-order change, if the owner makes one (HW-6);
  - a TPM NV index, if `systemd-pcrlock` is the HW-5a seal, and a persistent storage root key if the TPM holds none [Inference: systemd creates one at its standard handle; Windows hosts usually already have it];
  - any persistent UEFI variable the boot loader sets (e.g. systemd-boot's `LoaderSystemToken`) [Inference: from systemd documentation; checked in P2-1], unless the image turns it off;
  - on a Windows host with BitLocker, a possible recovery-key prompt at the next Windows start [Inference: Windows measures firmware settings, so a persistent boot-order change, a Secure Boot change, or a firmware update can trigger it; a one-time boot menu choice with Secure Boot left on normally does not].

  Most owners do not know what BitLocker or a recovery key is, so the box MUST NOT mention BitLocker unless it is a problem: no setup step, card line, or text about it. It prevents the prompt instead: it never needs Secure Boot turned off, and the boot instructions use the PC's one-time boot key rather than a change in firmware settings. If Windows does ask, its own recovery screen points to where the key is [Inference: current Windows recovery screens show aka.ms/myrecoverykey], and when the owner asks the box about it, the box answers in plain words from the owner's guide: the key is saved in the owner's Microsoft account at aka.ms/myrecoverykey, or with the IT department for a work PC [Inference: home devices with device encryption back the key up to the signed-in Microsoft account]. The owner's guide lists every change in this list, and that list is the whole of what a session changes on the host.
- **HW-8a** **Internal disk, by owner opt-in only.** *(Applies to a host PC that is not the controller or an installed workstation; the controller's and a workstation's own internal SSD is covered by HW-9 and HW-11.)* The owner MAY give agentOS one internal disk of such a host for capacity and speed (model files, caches, swap, older snapshot tiers). It is a tier-4 action (CH-3) on the local page: the page shows the disk's model, size, and partition list from the partition table, says plainly that everything on it will be erased, and requires local confirmation plus an approval code; a disk holding a BitLocker volume or the host's running boot loader needs a second, separate confirmation. The disk is then erased and encrypted with a key held in the sealed store (the custody rule (CLK-1)). The boot path, the sealed store, the log, and the only copy of anything PLN-6 needs stay on the controller's internal SSD (HW-9), so losing the disk loses only what it held, and the box says so. The owner can release the disk on the local page; agentOS then erases its key. No other host disk is ever touched.

### 3.3 Target integrated form (post-MVP)
One device holds the NVMe SSD, a microcontroller that acts as a USB keyboard for blind boot selection, a Wi-Fi radio, and an LTE modem with a SIM slot. You plug one thing in.

## 4. Kernel and log

The kernel and the log are the trusted centre. Together with the clerks (§6) and the host image they are the whole trusted base (KRN-2); everything else, every model and planner included, is untrusted and replaceable.

### 4.1 Kernel invariants

- **KRN-1** **One transition function.** The kernel is a pure function from (log, event) to (entries to append, effects to request). An event is anything that arrives: an owner message, a clerk result, a timer, a device report, a planner's plan. Events are themselves logged before they are processed, so the event bus is the log and any component that needs to react subscribes to log entries. The kernel holds no state outside the log; restarting it and replaying the log yields the same state.
- **KRN-2** **The trusted base is named and measured.** It is the kernel, the key-check library, the clerks, the sealed store, the signed host image, the enforcer (STO-4), the firmware and TPM, the host kernel, the hypervisor and the sandboxes it runs, and the processes in them that read credentials (the clerks' browsers). Nothing outside it can widen authority or reach a credential. Output scanning (CLK-4) is a tripwire, not containment of a compromised credential reader, and the spec claims no such containment. Compromise of that base is out of scope for prevention; its containment and recovery are in scope (KRN-11, REC-5), for the connected accounts and their providers as well as the box, at a recurring owner cost of at most two taps a year plus a PIN (OWN-7, OWN-8). The trusted base's size is measured on every PR (lines, dependencies, exposed parsers) and shown in the PR; growth needs a stated reason, and the review tier for it is A (PLAN.md). Its core (the kernel, its key library and the clerks' check path) is also capped: it grows only under a `Trusted-core: <decision ID>` line in the PR body; the rest is a measure, not a cap.
- **KRN-3** **Decisions from data, never from judgment.** Every kernel decision is computed from typed data: the plan's verbs and arguments, the key's caveats, the log and fixed tables shipped in the signed image (verb classes, reaction formats, confusable maps, secret-shape patterns). No kernel decision uses a model, a heuristic over free text, or a candidate's description of itself. Where text must be classified (owner control words, reactions), the classifier is a closed, fixed, context-anchored pattern set. **Learned proposes, exact disposes** (D-153): every model-driven part outside the kernel (planner, reader, declaration drafts, anomaly signals) only proposes, an exact rule decides, and each learned signal has a deterministic floor or cap, so a wrong output can only narrow, delay or reorder what the exact rules admit.
- **KRN-4** **Every effect has a verb, and irreversible verbs are gated.** The verb list (§6.3) is closed. Each verb has a fixed class: reversible, or irreversible at a stated risk tier (low or high). A covered step never asks: a reversible one runs, and one that changes state is confirmed with `UNDO <id>` (CH-16), and an irreversible one runs once every caveat holds and its hold window has passed (KEY-8). Only a standing key (KEY-5), or a key narrowed from one, covers an irreversible step; any other runs only when the owner approves that exact step at its tier (CH-10). The stricter class wins when a step could be read two ways.
- **KRN-5** **Typed plans are the only way to act.** A plan is a list of steps; each step names one verb, one clerk, typed arguments, the key it relies on, and the values it uses from earlier steps or the reader (PLN-2). The kernel rejects a plan that is malformed, cites a key that does not cover a step, uses a value whose labels the key forbids (PLN-3), or exceeds a caveat. Rejections are logged with the reason. A planner never gets a partial effect by submitting a partial plan: steps run in the declared order, and a rejected step stops the steps that depend on it. **Tasks.** Only the kernel opens a task, as a log entry: for an owner message or an owner-page start (DEV-12), or for each run of a standing key (KEY-5) that the kernel starts on its schedule or on a logged inbound event, with no owner message; a reply to a question an open task asked stays in that task, and every other task-chat owner message, including each held message that `RUN` releases (CH-14), opens a new one; a control word, a code or a reaction (CH-11) opens none. A task serves one goal (KRN-9). The kernel binds the task's id into the per-plan key it derives, in the same narrowing that binds that key to its plan (KEY-3), so every plan and re-plan for the task carries it and every clerk checks it like any other caveat; a planner can neither extend a task nor move a plan to another. A rejected plan or a failed or `outcome_unknown` step does not end the task, since re-planning follows them; the kernel logs the task's end at its final result, when the planner's run ends without one, at STOP, or at the task timeout (by default 24 hours; raising it lengthens every live task approval, so it is a widening under KEY-4, while lowering it takes only a text, KEY-6). What is bound to a task (a task approval, CLK-3; hand-off items, PLN-3; a per-task cap, LOG-7) lives inside it and ends with it. Reconciliation (KRN-8) and `RETRY` outlive the task, and a retry after its end asks again rather than use an ended task approval.
- **KRN-6** **Deterministic and simulated from the first commit.** Time, randomness, network and disk reach the kernel only as events or injected interfaces, so the same log and events give the same output on any host. The simulator (`broker/kernel/sim`) drives the kernel and fake clerks through seeded schedules of crashes, delays, duplicate deliveries, clock jumps and hostile planners, and checks the invariants in §4.1 and §5 after every step. Every kernel change runs the simulator before merge.
- **KRN-7** **STOP always works, and nothing is lost silently.** `STOP` by text, voice or owner page stops all planning and all pending clerk steps within the frozen target, needs no code, and works with every model and every optional dependency down (DEP-1). One STOP path serves every channel. "Stopped." is sent only after the STOP entry is durable in the log; a STOP whose append fails gets "STOP failed to record", even when an earlier STOP is in force. Every other narrowing (a revoke, a pause, `PLAN … OFF`; KEY-6) follows the same rule: its reply is sent only once its entry is durable. An entry whose append was not confirmed durable is never treated as done at recovery: a STOP or other narrowing so found is honoured, since it errs toward less authority, and any other such entry is discarded, so a failed RESUME never brings the box up resumed and a crash never revives a revoked key. A failure text names a step that works while the log is failing (switching the box off at its power button), never "reply or press STOP again". A log that cannot append admits no step (KRN-5). When a capability is lost (a route down, a key expired, a clerk failing its checks), the loss is logged and listed in STATUS; the box never degrades quietly.
- **KRN-8** **Effects happen at most once, and uncertain ones are reconciled.** Every step carries an idempotency key derived from the plan and step. A clerk performs a step at most once; a step whose result is unknown after a crash or timeout is logged `outcome_unknown`. The kernel reconciles it through the same clerk: it checks whether the effect happened, retries only if the effect is proven not done or is idempotent at the target, and otherwise lists it in STATUS ("Invoice 42: unclear if paid; not retried."). The owner may answer `RETRY` at the step's tier, with a warning that the effect may happen twice. An identical effect within 24 h of a success needs explicit owner confirmation even under a key. After a restart the kernel replays the log; a step still unreconciled after 10 checks (about 13 minutes) is parked, and any plan with the same effect is held until it is resolved. Killing the box between plan and result, for a mail send and a payment stub, gives no second effect.
- **KRN-9** **One outcome per goal.** Each owner goal has one outcome record in the log, revised as facts arrive (accepted, corrected, rejected, measured). Later evaluation reads the latest revision; a changed verdict re-evaluates every change adopted on the strength of the old one (HLD-3).

### 4.2 The log

- **LOG-1** **One log, everything else derived.** The log is the only source of truth. Projections (STATUS, the owner page, search indexes, key tables) are rebuilt from it and may be dropped at any time; a projection rebuilt after a crash equals one rebuilt from empty. The one exception is erase's subject-key table (LOG-4), kept outside the log so a key can be destroyed. Bulk content (mail bodies, files, photos, recorded responses) lives in content-addressed stores the log references by hash. External sessions (browser profiles, device pairings) live in their clerks' stores and are named in the log. Every cache is named, with its source entries and its rebuild rule.
- **LOG-2** **Append-only and hash-chained.** Each entry carries the hash of the previous one. The log is never rewritten; a correction is a new entry. The head is anchored outside the core (STO-1): every install has the owner's phone as an anchor, a trusted host (HW-5a) also has the core's TPM monotonic counter, and the enforcer and the backup are further anchors where present, so a rolled-back or edited log on the core is detected when its head disagrees with an anchor. [Risk] On a phone-only install on an unknown host, a rollback is detected only at the next owner-page step, and not at all while the phone holds no stored head (STO-1).
- **LOG-3** **Owner messages are entries.** Every message to the owner is a log entry with a subject and a revision. A newer revision supersedes an older one, and STATUS shows only the latest revision of each subject. A message not yet delivered is retried from the log, and a superseded one is dropped rather than sent late.
- **LOG-4** **Recall and erase.** The owner can ask what the box knows about a subject and can erase it. Erase is one log entry that destroys the subject's data key (content stores are encrypted per subject), so the content becomes unreadable everywhere, in old projections, in every backup still to be made, and in each older backup the box can rewrite (re-wrapping its key table without the subject); the confirmation names any it cannot (offline, append-only) as still holding it. A restore runs no step and reads no restored content until it has re-applied every erase entry newer than the backup, read from the newest reachable anchor's log, except in a compromised restore (REC-5), where those entries may be forged: they are held, not re-applied, and their classes stay sealed until the owner confirms each erase or unseals the class; when no anchor is reachable, or the anchors disagree with the restored log, it holds and asks the owner, naming the case (no anchor, rolled back, disagreeing). When the erases newer than the backup cannot all be re-applied, every restored data class stays sealed: the owner unseals one class at a time with a passkey confirmation (KEY-4), after a plain warning that items forgotten after the backup's date may return in it, and no default, timeout or "Continue" unseals one (risk 36). The restore cases are in docs/migration/cases.md §6. Erase fails closed: if any store cannot confirm, the owner is told. A planner session that read the erased item, and every copy of it, is rolled back to before that read; the owner is told afterwards. Procedures and indexes built from the item are rebuilt without it.
- **LOG-5** **Typed entries with versioned schemas.** Every entry has a declared type and schema version. The kernel refuses an entry type it does not know. A schema change ships with its migration as code in the signed image and runs at load, never by editing old entries.
- **LOG-6** **Verified goals.** A goal may carry a **check** (how to measure the result) and a **revisit date**, both set only by the owner. On the date the kernel runs the check through a clerk and records the result as `measured`, `guest-read` (a planner read it, weaker evidence) or `unconfirmed`. A revisit asks the owner nothing. A goal's acceptance clauses are evaluated against the measured result, not against the planner's claim.
- **LOG-7** **Spend is metered and keyed.** Every model call, paid API call and purchase is metered by the clerk that makes it and charged to the key the plan cites. Keys carry a spend caveat (KEY-2): a per-task cap and a rolling cap. Plan-route usage is admitted against the plan's pool and the owner's reserve (RES-5). When a key's spend is exhausted the task is held, and extending it needs a code-generator code (CH-10) and is a fresh narrowing derivation of the owner's passkey-signed spend envelope (KEY-4), which sets the per-task and per-day extension ceilings; no extension passes them, and raising a ceiling is a passkey widening. Usage is the provider's reported figure where the clerk's connection carries one, else the clerk's own count. **A purchase whose price the clerk could not read** before it was sent (CLK-3 "One approval per task") is metered after it, never left uncounted: the key's whole remaining per-task spend, charged when the request is sent, so the next purchase in the task is held (a task may need an extension, above) and the rolling cap sees the charge. If the clerk then reads a total from the site's confirmation page or receipt on the account's declared origins before it sends the next state-changing request, the charge is set to that total (higher or lower), and when the total reaches the per-task cap the next purchase is held. Both charges are logged with the step, and the entry says which figure stands.

### 4.3 Containment (D-149)

KRN-10 and KRN-11 are not kernel invariants: the kernel, the clerks and providers' scopes enforce their instances, and the graph (CLK-10) measures the rest.

- **KRN-11** **Control of the four.** For each of money, identity, data and physical safety:
  - **No single thief path.** No single failure domain (a maker, a sync service, a phone, a provider, a supply chain) takes it. Every irreversible effect on it above a threshold is bounded, passes a delay that a witness in another failure domain can see, and can be stopped by the owner.
  - **No single cut-off.** No single provider can cut the owner off from it. The household's power supply and uplink count as providers here.
  - **Roots at home.** Wherever an option exists, the keys that authorize, the address that recovers accounts, the main copy of data and each device's admin are the owner's.
  - **Physical safety** covers the safety devices (DEV-5), cars, and accounts that can locate people or lock or wipe devices. Safety devices are reached only through the enforcer, whose owner-signed keys a hacked core cannot make (STO-4, NET-2).
  - **Data** is outside the delay rule, since a read cannot be delayed or undone. Its bound is what providers still hold (BAK-2), end-to-end encryption where offered, fast closing of ongoing leaks (forwarding rules, stale sessions, unused app grants) and decoys (KRN-10).
  - **Two kinds.** Its instances that the kernel, the clerks or a provider's scope enforce are floor (§1.1): KEY-8's holds, KEY-4's grant hold, KEY-2's outside limit and rails, CLK-11's brakes, CLK-13's address, BAK-2's archive, REC-5's holds, the witness and the second witness (CH-15). The cut sets, cut-off sets and roots at home are measured by the graph and shown by the scoreboard (CH-12); they are not kernel guarantees. The graph is checked against this rule on every change, and STATUS names any breach.
  - Structural measures rank first, deterministic detection second and probabilistic signals last; no probabilistic signal stands alone (KRN-3).
- **KRN-10** **Witnessed from outside** (D-149, D-151). A deterministic reconciler outside the trusted base, the witness, compares every watch signal a declaration names (CLK-14) with the log's effects: provider security mail and alerts, bank and card feeds, door and brake changes (CLK-11), device traffic summaries (NET-5), reset and sign-in mail the log does not explain, grant death, decoy use and per-site address leaks (CLK-13). It reads a projection of effects (kind, target, time, amount), never the log, under a key that allows only declared watch reads, the box's own pauses and revocations, and fixed security texts.
  - **Proofs and signals.** A proof (decoy use, a leak, a door change the log does not explain) starts the pause of `STOP <provider>` (CH-11) under the witness's key and texts its code; a signal only texts. Each text carries the provider's page from its declaration, never a link from the evidence. Alert mail counts only when the box verified its signature itself. Where a money account alerts on two channels the box reads, a lasting mismatch between them is a signal. Connecting an account texts its cut-off page and asks for the provider's alerts to go to the owner's phone.
  - **Decoys.** A decoy is a declared account whose signal is any use: one unique decoy login for each place a provider keeps the owner's secrets (the mailbox, a password manager, cloud files), pointing only at an account the box watches and holding nothing real. The box plants one through CLK-8's sign-up flow or a guided step. Any use proves that provider was breached.
  - **Where its independence ends.** The witness never shares a failure domain with a provider it watches. It is a node role (HW-17), so it can leave the core; until a decision moves it, it runs on the core and texts through the core's owner line. There it catches planner errors, a clerk that escapes its sandbox and thieves outside the box, but not a rooted core, against which the providers' own alerts to the owner's phone, the copies of the log's head (STO-1) and KRN-12's wallets are what count.
- **KRN-12** **Breach decoys** (D-232). No owner secret sits where an intruder looks first (CLK-1, HW-15), so each such place holds a decoy, as attractive as the place allows, and any use of one proves a breach.
  - **One table.** Each install has one decoy table of values and destinations, made at first boot by the generator that makes real entries of the same kind, so a decoy's value cannot be told from a real one. The table is kept in the sealed store (STO-2), so the image and the repository reveal none, and reaches the kernel as a logged event (KRN-1), so a replay gives the same trips (KRN-6); the wallet seeds below are not in it. Placing a decoy is data only, never code at its place. Every credential-shaped decoy names a destination in the enforcer's decoy range (an endpoint, a host, a remote), so its use from any box trips at an exit; a decoy used only where the box sees nothing (at a provider, off the network) is the outside canaries' and KRN-10's case.
  - **Placement**, in the order an intruder searches: environment variables and dotfiles (cloud, model and code-host keys, SSH keys, `.env`, `.git-credentials`) in the planner sandbox, the host and the edge; jackpot files (password exports, recovery codes, wallet seeds); the cloud metadata address 169.254.169.254, which nothing answers; honey keys in the planner's view, named for what an injection reaches for; decoy root and recovery entries in the sealed store; and decoy addresses on the enforcer, which runs no service for them (NET-2).
  - **Trips.** A honey key is signed by no root, so the key check refuses it like any key (KEY-1), and citing one is a trip. CLK-4's detector, with the table added to its fixed patterns, runs at the kernel's outbound path and the edge link, and a decoy value or destination there is a trip; the one link kind that carries the edge's placements, sent only by the kernel at enrolment, is exempt. The enforcer, which shares no secret with the core (NET-2), drops and trips on traffic to its decoy addresses: the metadata address and a fixed never-assigned range of each segment, shipped in its image, to which placements that name a host point. A trip gets the response below, not KRN-10's.
  - **Response.** A trip narrows at once with no owner step (KEY-3, KEY-6): it pauses the keys of the scope it came from, stops planning, logs the trip and sends a fixed-template security text (CH-15). A honey-key trip stops only the planner session that cited it, since a careless planner reaches for one too. A trip at the enforcer shows on its light and page and reaches the core as an alert like NET-2's others; it changes no rule, and any cut is the owner's (NET-4). A resume after a trip needs the passkey on the owner page (KEY-4, CH-8), never a texted RESUME (CH-11).
  - **Silence.** The core sends the enforcer, on the enforcer channel, and the edge sends the core, a signed heartbeat at a fixed interval; two missed beats in a row make the receiver log an alarm and send its security text, with no pause or cut. The enforcer's alarm for a silent core shows on its light and page (NET-2 Texts and calls).
  - **Outside canaries.** At setup the core makes three decoy bitcoin wallets with the same generator and plants each seed at one boundary: the host's jackpot files (never the planner sandbox, whose reads a plan can log), the edge's, and the sealed store, which every backup carries (BAK-1). The owner page shows only their receive addresses, as QR codes, in one optional step that a text offers after setup (CH-8, ONB-3). The owner funds each (tens of dollars, uneven amounts, two deposits days apart) and adds the addresses watch-only on their phone, which alarms on any outgoing payment with no agentOS box involved and names the boundary that fell. These seeds are the one real secret placed outside the sealed store, on purpose and as an exception to CLK-1 and HW-15: they guard only their own funds. No seed is shown, texted, logged or kept outside its decoy copies. They are optional (DEP-3).
  - **Drills.** The simulator (KRN-6) runs the intruder's search against a sim box on a schedule (default weekly) and records, per tripwire in the table and at the enforcer, the time to detect (target under one minute at the exits) and false trips (target zero). STATUS shows a tripwire without a passing drill in its last interval as broken.
  - **Limit.** An intruder who touches no decoy and keeps the core's heartbeat going is not caught here, and one who reads the enforcer's image knows its decoy range, so a decoy's destination can mark it; KRN-10's limits stand.

## 5. Keys

A key is the only form authority takes. Approvals, standing permissions, budgets, trusted hosts, release activations and adoptions are all keys or key changes, so there is one audit trail and one recovery rule.

- **KEY-1** **Format.** A key is a signed, append-only chain of caveats over a root grant, in the manner of a macaroon: the root names the owner, one clerk (an account or device) and a set of verbs; each later link adds caveats and is signed by the holder of the previous link. A key is log data: its issue, every derivation and its revocation are log entries, and a key the log does not hold is not a key. The kernel and every clerk verify a key with the same key-check library (KRN-2), from the chain alone plus the log's revocation entries, with no network and no model.
- **KEY-2** **Caveats.** A caveat is a typed predicate the key-check library evaluates over a plan step and the log, never a description a model interprets (KRN-3). The closed caveat kinds are:
  - **verb and clerk:** which verbs, on which clerk;
  - **arguments:** bounds on each typed argument (recipient set, folder set, amount, party size, date range); a bounded field counts only when the clerk reads it from the source system or the owner fixed it, never when a planner supplied it;
  - **content:** none, an owner-approved template filled only from verified fields, or a **thread scope** (below);
  - **spend:** a per-task cap and a rolling cap, metered by the clerk (LOG-7);
  - **rate:** per item, per day and per recipient;
  - **expiry:** an absolute time or a count of uses;
  - **labels:** which data-flow labels the step's values may carry (PLN-3);
  - **hold:** the hold window before an irreversible step runs (KEY-8), and an optional hold on source records edited in the last N days, so a quietly tampered record cannot take effect at once (§19 risk 8).

  **Thread scope.** A key MAY allow free-text replies within existing threads of one account. The thread was started by the owner or by a contact that meets CH-10's existence rule; the recipients are exactly the thread's existing participants as the mail clerk reads them; the reply is composed by a planner that receives only owner-approved style preferences and those messages of the thread whose recipient set includes every recipient of the reply, so it can disclose only what every recipient already received; it carries no attachment, money, new recipient or VRB-1 content; and a fixed commitment filter (amounts, dates and deadlines, and commitment phrases such as "confirm", "agree", "will pay", "approved", "sign", "accept" in the owner's languages, plus CH-19's secret-shape filter) turns any match into a normal approval. The one exception is **calendar acceptance**: with a calendar clerk connected, a reply MAY accept exactly one slot the counterpart proposed in the latest inbound message, when the sender passes aligned DMARC and is in the owner's contacts, the calendar shows the slot free when the reply is queued and again at dispatch (CLK-2), the reply names no other date, amount or commitment phrase, and the daily bounds hold (one per thread, three in total by default). With calendar write keyed, the clerk places a private, attendee-free tentative hold that expires after 48 hours and always before the slot. Every thread-scoped reply is texted with its first line and `UNDO <id>`, scheduled in the owner's own account where the provider supports it, and sent after the undo window (default 10 minutes, VRB-3). [Risk] A reply can still commit the owner in words the filter misses, and a participant writing into the thread is an injection source; the alert and the undo window are mitigations, not prevention.

  **Spending limit outside the box.** Every money caveat is checked by the box and fails with it; one control does not. The box pays with one card whose spending limit the owner's bank or card provider sets and enforces, well above normal use. The owner page recommends this ("Use a card with a spending limit set by your bank"); the owner MAY give their own card instead, shown with the one line "No limit outside the box", as is a card stored at a site, which a browser task may use (CLK-3). The box cannot see a site's stored cards, so the owner page shows that line for every logged-in browser account (CLK-5) unless the owner has marked that account as holding no stored card; the marking only removes a warning and widens nothing, so it needs no more than a passkey tap on the owner page, and the clerk clears it when it fills a card (CLK-1) on that account. The card number and the limit the owner types are entered only on the owner page (CH-6), confirmed as a high-risk key (CH-10) and confirmed back by a text naming the last 4 digits and the limit; the card number lives only in the payment clerk. The box MUST NOT hold a credential that can change a limit or create cards, and never contacts the bank or card provider for either. The one exception is a payment wallet's key (CH-10), whose spend requests each release one card scoped to one approved purchase, funded from this card. The typed limit is used only to confirm back and to word decline lines; a decline is reported at once in the task's own result and kept as one STATUS line naming what would fix it, never an approval prompt or a retry.

  **Rails** (D-149). Money the box moves stays on rails that can be reversed. The owner page recommends a credit card for the box's card, since a card payment can be disputed and a debit card draws the owner's money at once. A **cash-like** purchase (a gift card, a prepaid card, crypto or a money transfer: marked so in the site's declaration, or on an undeclared site shown by one of those phrases at checkout) needs a hardware-key co-sign (KEY-4). The graph (CLK-10) records which irreversible rails, such as wires and person-to-person transfers, are on at the owner's money accounts; STATUS ranks turning off unused ones, which only the owner can do, and says when one institution holds all of the owner's money.
- **KEY-3** **Narrowing is automatic.** Any holder of a key MAY derive a narrower one by adding caveats, with no owner step: the kernel narrows a key to one plan, a planner session narrows the key it was given to a delegated guest, and a guest can only pass on what it holds. Keys flow down only. The kernel holds a **narrow-only sub-key** of the owner's root: it can sign derivations that add caveats and nothing else, and the key-check library rejects any link that removes or loosens a caveat, whoever signed it. Evaluation is conjunctive over every link: the hold is the longest any link sets, the expiry the earliest, and every set is the intersection, so a link can only add limits. The sub-key signs key derivations and log heads (STO-1) under separate domains, so neither signature verifies as the other.
- **KEY-4** **Widening needs an enrolled passkey, and waits.** The trust anchors of every key are the owner's two device-bound hardware keys (ID-1). The owner's phone passkey, which may be cloud-synced, and later edge keys are enrolled under them by a hardware key's signature, at once, and a revoke cuts a passkey off for good: on a lost phone, the hardware key enrols the new phone and cuts off the old one. A root's holders are the owner's enrolled passkeys, so only a passkey tap uses a bare root (a one-off step over the clerk's limits, or a browser task's submits on one site, CLK-3 "One approval per task"; neither is a new key, so neither waits for a grant hold) or derives from it, and every key derived from a root is a clerk key with an expiry (30 days, renewed by any passkey tap) and a daily spend cap. Roots and their first links (the clerk keys) are grants in the log; a clerk key's caps are the clerk's one budget under its root, so a renewal starts no fresh one, and cutting off a passkey cancels the grants it signed that are still in their hold. A new key, a wider caveat, a raised cap, a new trusted host, a release activation or an adoption outside HLD-4's standing key is a widening: it is valid only when signed by an enrolled passkey, and it is live only after a hold of about 24 hours, judged on the clerk's clock, with a notice to the owner, who can cancel it. A renewal at the same limits is live at once, and the grants made at setup go as one batch, under one hold and one tap. A big widening (beyond the owner-settable line, by default more than double a limit, or a new kind of power) and a very large one-off also need a second enrolled passkey's signature, one of them a hardware key. Steps within a key's limits never wait for a grant hold. The signature is collected in the step where CH-3 and CH-10 already ask the owner to confirm, so the ask text is unchanged and the tap that confirms is the tap that signs; only a big widening adds the hardware key's tap (D-146). The root and every widening are WebAuthn assertions by that passkey over the hash of the change, each with user verification; the sub-keys the kernel derives, which only narrow, carry the kernel's Ed25519 signature, and the simulator signs with a fake authenticator (EVL-1). A widening rendered to the owner uses fixed, kernel-defined wording built from the caveats, never a planner's words. **Hardware-key co-sign** (D-149). A passkey a hardware key enrolled counts as a big widening's second signature only once its enrolment has passed the grant hold. A one-off step that moves money over the clerk's daily cap, a cash-like purchase (KEY-2) and undoing a confirmed `STOP <name>` narrowing (CH-11) also need a hardware key's signature, since a synced phone passkey fails with its maker and its sync provider; D-148's checkout stays capped by the card's own limit. **Bounded exceptions.** Two widenings reach the owner without a passkey tap, and both are bounded here. Device grants by texted code (CH-3's device rows, CH-10 Devices) and spend extensions (LOG-7) are narrowing derivations of a **ceiling envelope**, a standing key the owner signed with the passkey that sets their spend, device scope and expiry, so the kernel's sub-key only narrows. The recovery key re-roots the passkey (REC-3) only after the hold window at its longest (KEY-8), with notice on every channel (§10), and STOP cancels it. The ask text and the number of taps are unchanged. Outside these, without the passkey nothing can widen authority; with only the phone and no code, nothing high-risk can be approved (CH-10).
- **KEY-5** **Standing keys, proposed from use.** The owner MAY hold standing keys that let irreversible steps run without a per-step approval and without a notification, scoped per clerk, per verb or both. A standing key always carries argument bounds from verified fields, template or thread-scoped content, and rate caveats (KEY-2). The box **proposes** standing keys from what the owner already approves: when approvals of one shape recur (same clerk, verb and argument class, approved unedited), the kernel offers the narrowest key that covers them, in fixed wording, as one paced question (CH-15). A thread scope is offered for an account only after at least 20 unedited approved replies there with at most 10% of the last 30 edited; a rejection, an `UNDO` or a wrong verdict resets the count. A proposal is never an enactment: the owner's passkey tap makes the key, which is live after KEY-4's grant hold. **A reversible task's first approval is its proposal** (D-141, D-153): when a task's steps are organize and trash inside VRB-2's guards (capped per day, undoable for 7 days) and unsubscribes under VRB-2's predicate, the approval of its first run also makes a standing key for the same task, so later runs, once the key's grant hold has run (KEY-4), need no tap and their organize and trash run at once with `UNDO` (KRN-4); since that makes a key, the approval is a widening at CH-10's high tier, the owner's passkey tap that signs the key (KEY-4); unsubscribes under it still wait their hold window (KEY-8) and STOP cancels them. A step that does not match a standing key exactly falls back to a normal approval. Covered steps are still logged, visible on request and on the owner page, and stopped by STOP. VRB-1 steps keep per-step approval under any key.
- **KEY-6** **Asymmetric changes.** Granting or widening a key needs the passkey (KEY-4) at the tier CH-3 sets. Narrowing, pausing or revoking a key needs only a text from the owner's number, like STOP, and takes effect before any further step is checked. A pause is log state the key check reads, not a caveat, so a resume removes no link. Budget changes, trusted-host changes, release activations and adoptions are key changes and follow the same rule.
- **KEY-7** **Revocation is final.** A revoked or expired key, and every key derived from it, fails the key check at the kernel and at every clerk. A head is a signed hash and carries no entries, so the anchors that keep entries (the enforcer and the backup, STO-1) keep the key-change entries themselves (grants, revocations, pauses), and a clerk refuses keys revoked in the entries it holds even before the kernel asks. A restore from backup (REC-1) replays every key-change entry a reachable anchor holds before any step runs, so it never revives a key revoked in them. [Risk] An install whose only anchor is the phone cannot replay revocations made after the backup was taken; REC-2's restricted mode, which re-confirms every standing key, is the control.
- **KEY-8** **The hold window, set by novelty.** Every irreversible step under a key waits for its hold window before the clerk acts, and the owner can stop it during the window with `UNDO` or STOP. The kernel computes the window by replaying the step against the log: a step whose shape (verb, clerk, recipient, argument class and labels) matches steps the owner already accepted under this key gets the key's base window, which may be zero; a step new in any of those gets the key's longer novelty window and a kernel-worded notice naming what is new. Novelty only ever lengthens a window or raises a tier (OWN-9). A request covered by a browser task's one approval (CLK-3) gets no novelty window; the key's base window and any other hold caveat still apply. Where the window cannot fit (a reply the counterpart needs now), the step goes to a normal approval instead.

## 6. Clerks

A clerk is the only way out of the box to one account or device. It holds that account's credential and nothing else, accepts only steps the kernel passed, checks the step's key again with the same key-check library, acts, and returns a typed result. A defect anywhere outside a clerk therefore reaches no credential; at most it makes a clerk perform steps its key and declaration already allow.

### 6.1 Custody and checks

- **CLK-1** **One clerk per account, and credentials never leave it.** No reusable authentication material (passwords, session cookies, OAuth, refresh and bearer tokens, API keys, private keys, code-generator seeds, recovery codes) MAY exist in memory, storage, logs, indexes or I/O readable by a planner, the reader or a guest. Each account's credential sits only in its clerk: a process under its own uid, non-dumpable, holding that account's secrets and no others, and accepting only steps that match the account's declared request shapes (CLK-3). The clerk injects the credential, opens the upstream TLS connection itself, swaps a token-refresh response back to placeholders, and redacts its own secret from what it returns (CLK-4); it never returns a credential or an auth header. A compute host's key (PLN-7) is an account credential and gets its own clerk.

  **Exceptions.** The core and the edge hold no Wi-Fi secret: they never turn Wi-Fi on, and the enforcer alone holds its networks' passwords (NET-2, NET-3, D-178). Keys tied to no account (sealed-store keys, the update anchor, recovery factors) stay with the unlock step that hands each clerk its secret, and the CH-4 code-generator seed sits in its own checker process. Content a planner legitimately reads may contain a secret (a sign-in code in an email); that is governed by labels (PLN-3), not custody.
- **CLK-2** **Re-check just before acting.** A clerk re-checks the key, its revocations at the newest log head it has seen, the recipients, the step's preconditions and any reservation immediately before it dispatches. It also checks, on its own clock, that the step's hold-start entry is at a head it has seen and that at least the key's base hold (KEY-8) has elapsed since it, and the mail clerk checks that the account's trash is restorable before a `trash` (VRB-2). A step that fails any check is refused with a typed reason the kernel logs.
- **CLK-3** **Declarations, routes and request shapes.** Each clerk is described by a versioned **declaration**: the account or tool, its routes, the operations it exposes, each operation's verb (§6.3), and, for every operation, the requests it makes: host, method and path template, and for single-endpoint APIs (GraphQL, JSON-RPC) the operation name and body schema. A credential is injected only into a request that matches a declared operation of a keyed clerk; anything else is refused and logged. A model clerk declares inference endpoints only, so a provider key never reaches that provider's key-management, file or billing endpoints. This makes the verb list a property of traffic, not of a declaration author's honesty. An operation not declared does not exist. Adding a tool means adding a declaration, not changing the kernel.
  - **Routes**, in default order: (1) API; (2) the tool's CLI; (3) its web app in a browser clerk (CLK-5) under a web recipe; (4) its desktop app in a kiosk clerk. The kernel picks the first route that covers the step, is healthy and is already keyed. Route choice is improved by HLD-4 and never changes a verb's class or custody.
  - **Divergence.** A route whose observed behaviour diverges from its declaration (a changed UI or API) is rerouted to the next healthy, already-keyed route, or paused if none exists, and a repair candidate enters the change pipeline (HLD-3). Rerouting never widens a key or loosens a class.
  - **Web recipes.** A site's web route is a declaration for one site: its origins and, for each control that submits (a button, link or form that can change state at the site), how the browser clerk recognizes it (URL template, form action and method, accessible role and name), its fields, its requests and its verb, plus the site's background requests. A state-changing background request is accepted only after the demo environment (HLD-3) shows it has no effect, and never when it overlaps a submit's request. The **clerk** decides what a click does, from its own DOM: a matched submit runs as that operation's step and authorizes only its declared requests, once, for that activation; values a tier or key caveat depends on are checked in the outgoing request. Any other state-changing request is blocked and logged, and the recipe gets a repair candidate.
  - **Unknown submits.** Where no recipe covers a page, the clerk blocks every state-changing request and holds the first as the submit. The owner gets one high-risk approval (CH-10) in fixed wording from fields the clerk reads (origin, method and URL, the control's visible label, the body fields with their form labels, hidden fields listed), with a screenshot as evidence; site text is quoted, length-capped and confusable-folded. Approval releases the held request only if it is unchanged apart from a recognized session or anti-forgery token. The interaction is kept as a recipe draft.
  - **One approval per task** (D-147). The approval is the owner's one-off root use (KEY-4), signed over the held request, the task and the held request's site, and it covers that task's later state-changing requests on that site with no further tap. The **site** is the registrable domain on the public suffix list, its private section included (so `x.github.io` is its own site), as the clerk reads it from each request, never from the planner; a host with no registrable domain (an IP address, `localhost`) is its own exact host, and a redirect is a new request checked against the site. The **task** is the one the kernel's log records (KRN-5), across its re-plans; the planner can neither extend it nor move the approval to another, and the approval expires when the log records the task's end, or at the clerk key's expiry if that is earlier; STOP (KRN-7), or cutting off the passkey that signed it (KEY-4), ends it at once. An approval bound only to the held request's contents does not meet this rule. Each covered request is sent at most once (KRN-8). Covered requests are listed in STATUS (CH-12) once sent, one fixed-wording line each (the site, the control's visible label and the time sent); their full fields go to the log. There is no count cap: the task and site bounds do that job. **What still applies.** The approval adds no hold of its own: a covered request gets no novelty window (KEY-8), since the tap was the owner's consent for the task's submits on that site, but the clerk key's base window and any other hold caveat a key sets (KEY-2) still apply. Every other limit of the clerk key and its links is checked wherever the clerk can read the value it depends on from the request (spend, rate, arguments, labels), and KRN-8's 24-hour identical-effect confirmation applies. A price the clerk cannot read (an order total the site keeps server-side) does not by itself hold the request, since the tap covers the task's submits on that site, but the purchase is still metered after it is sent (LOG-7), so the key's spend caps count it; the backstop is the card limit outside the box that KEY-2 recommends, and the owner page shows that the owner's own card without one, or a card stored at a site, has no cap outside the box. **What asks again.** The clerk holds the request as above for a new approval, which again shows it exactly, when: it is on another site; it is in another task; a readable value puts it over a limit, or it would need a second signature (KEY-4); it follows a credential or card fill by the clerk (CLK-1), or any entry of private-labelled data by `type`, `select`, `paste` or `hand-off` (PLN-3, CLK-5), made since the approval; or it is on a screen that reveals or creates a secret (VRB-1). A recipe's first live runs (below) keep their own approvals. [Decision: Mark, 2026-10-21; D-147; unreadable prices, Decisions card cmsg_01R63S2yrPkq32thy8WQCuvDASvdL8hv62yMPPdwkuUkZ8, choice cmsg_01R63S2yrPkq32thy8WQCuvDW9V4ZM7m41LDEgmUiuwh1h, 18:12:22Z]
  - **First live runs.** Where a site has no demo environment, each state-changing operation's first 3 live runs (owner-settable, at least 1), and again after its declaration changes, go to approval at no lower than the verb's tier whatever key would apply, and their requests must match the declaration; until then no standing key covers the operation.
  - **Cancelable submits.** A submit whose recipe declares a qualified, free, full cancel within a stated window, and whose confirmation reference the clerk reads, is low risk under the owner-set amount limit, and its confirmation carries `UNDO <id>` (CH-16) for the shorter of the window and the owner's default. Cancelability selects only the tier, never the verb's class.
- **CLK-4** **Each clerk redacts its own secret, and output stays on declared origins.** A clerk strips its credential and every value derived from it from everything it returns. A browser clerk stays on its account's declared origins (including the account's identity-provider and payment-challenge origins); any other origin opens in a separate context with no credential. Text a browser or kiosk clerk returns (DOM text, accessibility snapshot) passes a fixed-pattern detector for known token formats and high-entropy values in secret-labelled fields; matches are redacted and screenshots of a page with a match are withheld. The detector is a tripwire for secrets a site shows without a reveal step, not the boundary (KRN-2).
- **CLK-9** **Narrowest credential, the box's own where offered** (D-149). Each clerk holds the narrowest credential the provider enforces for its declared operations. A narrow credential cannot change sign-in, recovery, forwarding, filters or payees.
  - **The box's own.** Where the provider allows, the credential is the box's own and unshared: a passkey of its own, a key-bound token with its key in the TPM, or a one-time card number, so the owner can cut the box off without touching their own login. Where the provider offers only the owner's login (a password or a session), the clerk holds that, as CLK-1 and CLK-5 allow, and its sign-in, recovery and second-factor screens stay VRB-1 steps, approved per step.
  - **Labels.** The account plan (§12.1 step 7) labels each account *Narrower than you, watched* or *Full power, same as yours*, and advises connecting every account that offers a narrower credential. Full power is connected only where the owner wants the box to act, never only to watch, since the power to lock an account is the power to take it.
  - **Identity accounts.** Full power at an identity account connects only after the provider's own brakes, extra checks on risky steps and alerts to the owner's phone are set, since only controls outside the box still hold if the box falls. Where a provider offers none, the connect step shows the account as a single point of failure and the owner decides.
  - **Money accounts.** KEY-2 holds: the box never holds a credential that can change a limit or create cards, and acts there only through access the provider limits (view-only access, its own limited card, or a wallet).
  - **Vendor clouds.** Cars and other devices reached through a vendor cloud get a read-only grant where the maker offers one, unless the owner wants the box to act on them, since the enforcer cannot stand between the core and that cloud (DEV-5).
  - **Widening.** Widening a provider grant takes a new consent at home, as a KEY-4 widening does.
  - [Inference] Once the box's browser supports device-bound sessions on Linux (Chrome has them on Windows since April 2026), the box's sessions are bound to its TPM, so a stolen session does not work elsewhere. That waits for the browser.
- **CLK-14** **One declaration per account, device, node and surface** (D-151). Everything agentOS connects to or runs on is described by one declaration (CLK-3), qualified through HLD-3 and shareable as a package (PUB-1): each account, each device (DEV-1), each node (HW-17) and each owner surface (§10). A declaration names its transport and carries, as data: sync (a cursor and raw items, into the log's content store, BAK-2); operations with their verbs and wire shapes (CLK-3); narrowings with their directions (CLK-12); watch signals (KRN-10); graph facts (CLK-10, CLK-11, DEV-13): recovery paths, factors and their failure domains, brakes and rails, who pays the provider and who can compel it, what it can read or sense, its manual override and what leaving it takes; for a node, its role's caveats and enrolment proof (HW-17); for a surface, its audience (DEV-6), what it may carry (CH-6, CH-19, CH-20) and the approval tiers it supports (CH-3, CH-10). The shipped table of known services ships as declarations.
  - **Transports, not providers.** Trusted-base code exists only for transports: the HTTPS exit with declared operations and OAuth refresh grants (CLK-3), IMAP and SMTP, the credentialed browser (CLK-5), device stacks (DEV-1), the modems (CH-1, CLK-8) and the node link (HW-17). Nothing in the trusted base is specific to one provider, device, node or surface, so adding one adds a declaration, never trusted-base code. CI keeps the list of provider and device names in trusted-base code outside declarations and test fakes, and the list may only shrink.

### 6.2 Kinds of clerk

- **CLK-5** **Browser and kiosk clerks.** A logged-in browser runs in its clerk's sandbox, one account each. A planner drives it only through a closed, versioned action protocol: navigate, click, type, select, read the DOM or accessibility snapshot (password fields omitted), screenshot, download to the workspace, and the hand-off verbs `copy`, `paste` and `hand-off` (PLN-3). Developer tools, arbitrary JavaScript and cookie, storage or header access MUST NOT exist in the protocol.
  - **Computer-use retry.** When a step fails because the site refuses or breaks under automation (a block page, a bot-protection status, a control that does not respond), the clerk retries that one step once in computer-use mode before anything reaches the owner: the browser restarts without the interfaces automation leaves visible (no remote-debugging endpoint, no webdriver flag, no automation extension) and is driven as a kiosk app with OS-level input on the sandbox's own display, under enterprise policy with developer tools off, internal schemes blocked, downloads confined to the workspace and a closed set of keys and chords. Origins, keys and labels stay the same, and the retry's network is bound: a TLS-terminating egress gate outside the browser process applies the step's request rule and refuses top-level navigations to undeclared origins; the sandbox has no other egress (UDP, so HTTP/3, and WebRTC are blocked), and without the gate no retry starts. The retry inherits only the failed step's authorization, and "once each" counts both attempts. A step whose state-changing request was already forwarded is never retried unless the origin refused it before processing, so a payment or booking cannot run twice. Computer-use mode is never a first attempt or a route of its own. It never answers a human check: no retry starts from a page that shows one, and no input lands in a recognized challenge widget. Only if the retry fails does the step reach the owner: a human check waits for the live view (CH-8) and is listed in STATUS; anything else is one STATUS line (CH-15). The owner turns the mode off per site with `STOP COMPUTER USE AT <site>` (CH-11) and back on with a code. [Decision: Mark, 2026-10-10]
  - **Kiosk clerks.** A desktop app that holds a login runs in a VM as a single-app kiosk: no shell, file manager or other launchable program, file dialogs confined to the workspace, and the app's saved login unreadable by the user its UI runs as; an app whose saved login cannot be locked away is reached by another route or not at all (§19 risk 7). It is driven through the same action protocol. Linux apps are within the HW-4 floor, one at a time [Inference; unmeasured]; Windows and Android kiosks are optional and outside the HW-4 guarantee.
  - **Suite kiosk (owner opt-in).** The owner MAY allow, per account, several desktop apps that sign in with that one account to share one kiosk clerk; apps of different accounts never share one. Turning it on is a widening (KEY-4); turning it off needs only a text.
  - **Secret screens.** Where no recipe covers a page, the clerk decides whether a state-changing request is on a screen that reveals or creates a secret (VRB-1) by one fixed check over what it itself read: the page URL (with tokens stripped), the control's visible name and target, and the visible name of every field (text, checkbox, radio and switch alike) and heading on the page. Each text is split into words at every character that is not a letter or a digit and at each letter-digit and case boundary (so `passwordReset` gives "password" and "reset", `APIKey` gives "API" and "Key"); a word, or two adjacent words joined, marks the screen when, lower-cased, it equals an entry of the fixed list with the entry's hyphen or space removed, or that entry's plural (the entry with "s" added, or with "ies" for a final "y"). **Alone** (every entry here, in the second Alone list and in the pairs, also matches its plural; a checkout phrase is passed over only in the exact form listed): password, passcode, passkey, passphrase, recovery, MFA, 2FA, 2SV, two-factor, two-step, 2-step, one-time, OTP, TOTP, authenticator, authentication, secret, credential, key, SSH, GPG, OAuth, seed phrase. **Alone** also: token, export, device, backup, security, PIN. **Only as a pair:** change email, update email, edit email, revoke session, revoke access, active session, connected app, authorize app, authorize application. **Not a sign:** a text that is, as a whole, one of the checkout phrases security code, card security code, CVV security code, security code CVV, gift card PIN, enamel pin, remember this device, home security, smart home security, backup battery (a field named exactly "Security code", "Card security code", "CVV / Security code" or "Security code (CVV)", a heading "Home security"); nor a URL query key named exactly `token` (not `access_token` or `TOKEN`), whose value is still read. Case, spacing and punctuation are ignored when a text is compared with a checkout phrase; the query key is compared exactly. A phrase inside a longer text ("Enter security code", "Change gift card PIN", "Enter CVV / Security code") does not exempt it, and any other phrasing with a listed word marks the screen. A field's value is never a sign, since the clerk cannot tell a prefilled address from a prefilled password. The list errs wide: a false match costs one more approval, a miss would let a task's tap cover a secret. A marked screen is a VRB-1 step: no task approval (CLK-3), standing key or recipe submit covers it, and it is approved per step with a code; and a request on a marked screen never opens a task approval, so the held request that does is never itself a secret step. The list is part of the clerk's versioned declaration, so a change to it is a repair candidate (HLD-3) and never loosens on its own. A page with a recipe is classified by its declared verbs (VRB-2).
  - A browser or kiosk clerk never claims to be a person (CH-21). Screens that reveal or create secrets are VRB-1 steps.
- **CLK-6** **Device clerks.** Local network devices are reached only through device clerks, one per device or hub, by key (§13, DEV-1). The enforcer is the home's device clerk (STO-4).
- **CLK-7** **The model clerk.** Model calls leave the box only through the model clerk, which holds each provider credential and injects it into that provider's declared inference endpoints only (CLK-3). A provider's consumer plan login MAY be used only by that provider's official, unmodified CLI, which runs as a guest (PLN-5) holding a placeholder; the model clerk injects the real login into the CLI's model requests, including its token-refresh request, whose response it swaps back to placeholders. An unmatched refresh or other response is dropped, never forwarded, and the route stays down until a release requalifies it. Each provider's mode is set by a read of its published terms and recorded in its declaration:
  - **Permitted:** the terms allow a proxy to hold the login. Codex (ChatGPT plan) is the default plan route.
  - **Unconfirmed:** the terms are silent on it, neither permitting nor prohibiting it. The route runs only while the owner's logged consent stands. Consent is asked when the plan is connected, as its own question and tap on the setup page right after the plan's sign-in (never by **Use the defaults**), or later with a code-generator code on the owner page, after the owner is told in plain words that the provider has not confirmed this use and could restrict their account. Consent is a widening (KEY-4) at the high tier (CH-10). A route that becomes unconfirmed stops before its next run, and STATUS and ONB-9's line say so ("ChatGPT plan paused until you say yes; using your other routes meanwhile.").
  - **Not allowed:** the terms bar a proxy from holding the login, or read unclear (a clause on proxies or automated access that can be read as barring it), so the provider gets the API key route only, with the key held by the model clerk like any other API credential (D-140).
  - **Muse requires Contributor mode off:** qualification checks the setting at sign-in, a run refuses while it is on (a closed reason, no retry), and Muse pool sizing (RES-5) uses the allowance that applies without it.
  - **Control.** The owner MAY pause any plan route at any time by `PLAN <plan> OFF` (CH-11), by text or on the Wi-Fi page, with no code; on an unconfirmed route this also withdraws consent. `PLAN <plan> ON` resumes it, with a low-tier code (CH-10) on a permitted route or after a refused login, and by asking consent again at the high tier on an unconfirmed one, where the low-tier code never stands in for it. It applies to a route the owner paused, whose consent they withdrew or have not yet given, or whose login was refused. A route paused by a terms re-read or a security notice refuses `PLAN <plan> ON` until an update brings it back. Pause, consent and withdrawal take effect before the next run, are log entries naming the plan, the terms read and the time, and fall back to PLN-7 routing over the owner's other keyed routes.
  - **Refused login.** When the provider refuses the login (declared per provider as response statuses), the box stops using that route, tells the owner once (ONB-9's sign-in line), and does not retry until the owner resumes it or signs in again.
  - **Withdrawal.** If a provider prohibits the use, a release withdraws the route; each affected owner gets one STATUS line naming the plan, why, and what runs instead, sent also as a security notice that pauses the route on arrival so pinned-channel boxes (UPD-4) hear it.
  - The CLI's cloud, remote-session, provider-side fetch and MCP features are off by image-fixed policy, and its auto-update and telemetry are off.

  Either way the CLI's non-interactive mode takes the brief, slash and bang syntax in a brief MUST be inert, and the plan's credential is never used by any other client. A provider without a qualified CLI, whose terms allow neither mode, or whose consent the owner has not given (Unconfirmed), uses an API key that its clerk injects into declared inference endpoints only.
- **CLK-8** **Communication clerks of the box's own.**
  - **A second line.** Outbound texts and calls to third parties use the `send` verb through a second-line clerk, never the owner-channel number (CH-1): a second modem with its own SIM on the edge (HW-15), or an owner-held calling account. It is an optional dependency (DEP-3). Calls open by stating that an automated assistant is calling for the owner. Recipients are full international (E.164) numbers, verified by the clerk (owner contacts, or a number read from a source) or supplied by the owner; by default only businesses and owner contacts; a short or premium-rate code needs an owner-created contact. A line's rate caveats are one budget across its transports. Transcripts are logged. Inbound texts and calls to the second line, and any from a number other than the owner's on either line, are untrusted content, never control words, task chat or part of an approval. [Risk] Automated calls are regulated (in the US, AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the TCPA [Fact: FCC, Feb 2024]); carriers may filter automated traffic.
  - **The box's own mailbox.** The box MAY have its own address at a free mail provider, an ordinary account of the owner's, never a service the project runs (DEP-2), and an optional dependency (DEP-3). **Name:** the Wi-Fi page offers four choices, each checked as available (the box's name such as `dave.smith`, a random adjective and noun, `email.assistant.<digits>`, or one the owner types), with a shuffle control; an address never contains a phone number, and a confirm screen says it cannot be renamed. **Creation:** the browser clerk fills in the sign-up form with a clerk-generated password; the live view shows the owner only the human check, which the owner completes; the sign-up phone check goes to the owner's number. **Account recovery is never the box's SIM**; it goes to the owner's own email (CH-20) where the provider allows, the phone is removed as a recovery factor once two-step sign-in is on where the provider allows, and backup codes are printed at setup. [Risk] Where the owner's number remains a recovery factor, a SIM swap of it could recover the mailbox. The clerk turns on two-step sign-in with its own code-generator seed, makes an app password or OAuth grant, sets the display name and signature (CH-21), and the box confirms by text ("Done. My email is dave.smith@gmail.com. Forward me anything."). **Use:** mail to the box is untrusted content, whoever it appears to be from; forwards from the owner's address get a one-line receipt only when they pass aligned DMARC. The box's address is never the recovery address of an account that holds money or owner credentials. The provider's security alerts reach the owner at once as an urgent class (CH-15). Sign-ups, receipts and vendor mail the box handles for the owner use this address. **Upkeep:** the clerk answers sign-in challenges with its own seed; one that needs the owner's phone waits for the owner's next Wi-Fi page visit and is listed in STATUS, its code never accepted by text (CH-6). STATUS mentions the mailbox only when storage passes 80%, sign-in is blocked or the account is suspended. A new address replaces the old one, which is forwarded for 90 days. Disconnecting is a fixed-wording change (CH-11); deleting the account is `delete-remote` at the high tier. [Risk] Providers' terms restrict automated account creation and may suspend such accounts; the mail clerk supports any IMAP and SMTP provider as a fallback.

### 6.3 Verbs

- **VRB-1** **Revealing or creating a secret is always approved per step.** Steps that reveal or create a secret (show an API key, reset a password, add a device, export, change account recovery) are `reveal-or-create-secret`: irreversible at the high tier, approved per step with a code, and never covered by a standing key, a web recipe's submit, a cancelable tier or a thread scope.
- **VRB-2** **The verb list is closed and fixed by the kernel.** The verbs are: `read`, `fetch` (a web page through the edge, with no credential), `draft`, `organize`, `trash`, `unsubscribe`, `send`, `post`, `buy`, `share`, `delete-remote`, `change-account`, `reveal-or-create-secret`, and for local devices `actuate`, `actuate-physical` and `present` (§13). Each has a fixed class (reversible, or irreversible at a stated tier) and a custody rule. A declaration maps each operation to one verb and cannot set a class; it maps to a reversible verb only if it also declares the inverse, and the clerk logs the prior state with each effect; when a mapping is ambiguous, or routes differ, the stricter verb applies. Operations that fit no verb go to the owner as one summary per tool and stay draft-only until answered. The verb list, its classes and these rules change only by owner key (KEY-4).
  - **organize** is reversible: changes inside the owner's own account that only the owner sees and that fully undo there (archive and unarchive, add or remove a label or folder, move between allowed folders, mark read or unread, star or flag). Anything that cannot be undone inside the account is never organize: emptying trash, filters, and forwarding or auto-reply settings are `delete-remote` or `change-account`; reporting spam or phishing is `delete-remote` or `share`. [Decision: Mark, 2026-10-05: "Yes - reversible".]
  - **trash** is reversible while the provider keeps the item: it moves a message to the account's own trash, logs the prior state, and is undone by `UNDO <id>` until the provider's expiry, which the confirmation states. Where trash is not restorable (the provider deletes at once, or the account auto-empties trash sooner than 7 days), the step is `delete-remote`. "Delete" in an owner's ask means trash.
  - **unsubscribe** is irreversible at the low tier and has one form only: an RFC 8058 one-click `List-Unsubscribe-Post` request to the URL in the message's `List-Unsubscribe` header, sent through the edge with no credential and no cookie, and only when the **unsubscribe-eligibility predicate** holds. The mail clerk computes it from the raw message and the kernel checks it; no model's output enters it: the message has exactly one `From`, one `List-Unsubscribe` and one `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header; a DKIM signature passes whose `d=` aligns with the `From` domain, whose `h=` over-signs each of those headers, and which has no `l=` tag; the `List-Unsubscribe` URL's host aligns with `d=` or is in the shipped table of mail service providers; the sender has earlier mail delivered outside the provider's junk folder; and the sender's domain collides with no known sender or contact under the confusable map (KRN-3); and the sender's mail has gone unopened for 90 days: its first delivered mail is at least 90 days old and the owner opened none of its mail in the last 90 days, by the mailbox's read state (D-141). Links in a message body are never followed, `mailto:` unsubscribes are `send`, and a sender whose message lacks those headers is not unsubscribed. Unsubscribe goes through the hold window (KEY-8). The owner's approval of unsubscribes shows how many senders it covers, and `MORE` lists them. Spam is trashed, never unsubscribed, since an unsubscribe confirms the address to its sender.
  - **Guards for organize and trash.** A move or label goes only to a system state (inbox, archive, read, starred, and for trash the account's trash), a folder the owner confirmed when keying, or the `agentOS/` namespace; spam or junk folders and any folder with a retention or auto-delete policy are never organize targets, and a target that is shared, delegated, synced to another party or watched by another service is `share`. Every organized item also gets an `agentOS` label by default. **Alerts ask before they are hidden:** for steps that hide a message (archive, trash, move out of the inbox, mark read), an item is an alert, and goes to approval as `change-account`, when its sender passes DMARC and is the provider's security sender, a sender the owner holds an account with, or a sender the owner marked as financial or identity; as a second net the security-alert patterns (CH-19's code patterns plus phrases such as sign-in, new device, password reset, security alert, suspicious or unusual activity, "is this you?", unusual transaction, payment or account-change notices, in the owner's languages) also mark an alert. The pattern list grows through HLD-5. **Bounded:** at most 200 organize and trash effects per account per day (owner-set); the rest for the day is asked once, as one batch (CH-13). **Visible and undoable:** STATUS counts the effects by action, names the senders most affected and lists guard hits, with an `UNDO <id>` (CH-16) valid for 7 days that restores each item only if its state still matches what the box set ("Restored 47; 3 skipped (changed since)"); `MORE <id>` lists the items and `UNDO <id> 3 7` restores single ones. **Read back:** after each effect the mail clerk reads the changed items back from the mailbox, and an effect whose items do not match is logged as unconfirmed (OWN-13, KRN-8). **Read state:** the mail clerk's fetches and read-backs never change an item's read state (an IMAP `BODY.PEEK` or the provider's read without modify), so the box's own reads never wipe the owner's unread mail or count as opened in the unsubscribe predicate.
- **VRB-3** **Prefer reversible forms.** A planner SHOULD reach a goal through reversible verbs where it can: drafts instead of sends, staging copies, a delayed send with an undo window (default 10 minutes), shadow runs. The kernel prefers a plan's reversible alternative when the plan offers one. A planner SHOULD propose ending a recurring task's source (unsubscribe, cancel, close) before proposing to automate it (D-152).

### 6.4 The graph, brakes and narrowing (D-149)

- **CLK-10** **No master keys: the graph.** The account plan (§12.1 step 7) keeps the owner's accounts, credentials, providers, devices, nodes and people as one graph. Its facts are declaration fields (CLK-14), each with its source (the declaration, the owner's answer or a clerk's own observation), and the scoreboard (CH-12) shows how much rests on unverified answers.
  - **Recovery.** CLK-8's recovery-address rule extends to every mailbox the box reads. For each money or identity account the graph records its recovery paths, each second factor's own recovery, and what its providers hold for the owner (a password manager, a synced authenticator, recovery codes in mail or files, sign-in with Google or Apple).
  - **Failure domains.** Two safeguards count as two only if they share no failure domain: a maker, a sync service, a phone or a supply chain. Two hardware keys from one maker count as one, and the owner page recommends two makers.
  - **Fixes.** STATUS names every account one failure domain can take, with its one fix: a hardware key or passkey whose own recovery is not email alone or, where an account offers neither, a separate recovery address that nobody reads day to day and the box never reads, locked with hardware keys. A fix prefers recovery only the owner holds (printed codes or a recovery key), with support-desk and SMS recovery off where the provider allows. Recovery codes are never in the box: they are printed with the Owner Card and kept in two places. The witness flags reset mail (KRN-10) and never holds it back, since a reset link works for whoever opens it first.
  - **Readers.** Each provider records who pays it (the owner, or advertisers and data buyers), the governments that can compel it and its record of one-sided changes. The owner's carrier counts as a reader of every text and image between the owner and the box (CH-6, NET-4), and each device's maker as a reader of what that device can sense and send. STATUS prefers roots at providers the owner pays. Accounts that can locate people, or lock or wipe devices, count as identity and physical safety.
  - **Lists.** STATUS lists every pair of safeguards that shares a failure domain and every provider that can cut the owner off from an asset, and shows the number of accounts and devices with the month's change. An account with no task, sync or sign-in use for longer than the owner's threshold (default 12 months) is offered for closing by its declaration's exit, as one paced question (CH-15). A provider's breach yields that provider's account and nothing beyond it.
- **CLK-11** **Brakes outside the box.** KEY-2's spending limit outside the box extends to the owner's accounts. For each money or identity account the graph records the provider's brakes (a new-payee delay, transfer and card limits, an account lock, a credit freeze where offered, a carrier port-out lock on the owner's numbers) and which of them alert when lifted, preferring brakes whose lifting waits or needs a credential at another provider. STATUS lists each brake not set, with its page. The box sets a brake only where CLK-12 allows, the owner sets the rest, and the box never lifts one. A brake change is a door the witness watches (KRN-10). A brake holds when every credential and key has failed, since the provider enforces it by time and policy.
- **CLK-12** **Outward narrowing.** KEY-6's asymmetry applies at providers, but there the power to narrow is usually the power to widen.
  - The box narrows on its own only through a control that cannot widen (a narrowing-only scope, the enforcer's tightening channel, its own grants) or with a login it already holds in order to act; it never holds a login only to narrow.
  - **Recipes** are declared narrowings (CLK-14): sign out other sessions, revoke an unused third-party grant, remove a stored card, lock through the enforcer, and lower a limit or add a delay only where the credential cannot raise it. Each declares the narrowing direction of every setting it touches (a lower number, a longer delay, fewer sessions, grants or stored cards). The clerk reads each setting before and after, and the step stands only if every change moved in the declared direction; anything else, or a page showing a setting the recipe does not declare, aborts and texts. Such a step is exempt from VRB-1's per-step code, and nothing else is. The check stops a misbehaving planner, not a hacked box, which is why CLK-9 comes first.
  - Where only the owner's credential can narrow, as with most bank limits and card freezes (KEY-2), the box prepares the step and texts the provider's own page.
  - Narrowings are proposed by the planner and scored by the graph, rate-limited, batched per run (CH-13) and paused by STOP. Anything that widens (a new credential, a new recovery path, a higher limit, a full-power connection) waits for the owner (KEY-4).
- **CLK-13** **The owner's own address.** Optional, and offered only once passkeys or hardware keys are on the money and identity accounts. The owner holds a domain at a registrar with hardware keys, a registrar lock (and a registry lock where offered) and renewal paid years ahead; its DNS host forwards its mail to the mailbox the owner already uses, so the mailbox provider becomes replaceable. The box gives each account its own address on the domain, as a declaration field (CLK-14): at every sign-up it makes, and at existing money and identity accounts with one owner approval each. Mail to an address from anyone but its site proves that the site leaked or sold it: the witness texts it, naming the site (KRN-10), and the graph marks the site. The registrar and DNS host join every reset path, so the graph counts them and the step shows the net before the owner commits. The graph refuses a per-site alias at a provider other than the mailbox's for a money account, since either provider's breach would then take it.

## 7. Planner

Everything that uses a model is untrusted: the planner, the quarantined reader, every guest and every local pass. None of it holds a credential or a key it did not receive narrowed, and none of it can act except by submitting a plan the kernel checks (KRN-3). The design follows CaMeL: the model that chooses steps never reads untrusted content, and the model that reads untrusted content never chooses steps.

### 7.1 Plans, readers and labels

- **PLN-1** **The planner plans over handles.** A planner turns the owner's ask into a **plan**: a typed program of steps, each naming a verb (VRB-2), a clerk, typed arguments and the key it runs under. The planner sees the owner's ask, the declarations of the clerks it may use (CLK-3), the typed results of earlier steps and **handles**: opaque references to values it has not read (a message, a sender, a link, a page). It never sees the content behind a handle, only closed-type reader values about it (PLN-3). A plan passes values between steps by handle, so a mail body can become an argument without ever reaching the planner. The kernel checks every step of a plan against its key before any effect (KRN-3); a plan the check rejects runs no step.
- **PLN-2** **The quarantined reader returns typed values.** Untrusted content is read only by a **quarantined reader**: a typed function, run as a model call with no tools, no keys and no plan, that receives the content behind some handles and a typed question, and returns a value of the declared type (a boolean, an enum from a fixed set, a date, an amount, a set of handles, or a bounded string, which the kernel keeps behind a new handle). The kernel validates the value against its type and rejects anything else, so injected text in the content can at most choose among the values the type allows. A reader's value carries every label of the content it read (PLN-3). The local passes of PLN-7 (reduce, extract, triage, check, draft) are reader calls. **Which model reads** (D-141): the reader runs on the box's local model (PLN-7), whatever route the planner uses, once that model passes a held-out gate on the suite (HLD-3): it classes no personal or alert mail (VRB-2) as spam, and its accuracy is no lower than a reader on an API model. Until it passes, or after a run it fails, the reader runs where a class key (PLN-3) sends it, and STATUS says so in one line.
- **PLN-3** **Data-flow labels.** Every value carries labels, and labels only accumulate. **Private** is the default: anything from the owner's accounts, devices or chats with the box, and anything derived from it (summaries, embeddings, model outputs, reader values); unlabelled data is private. A value is **public** only when it is (1) public material fetched by the box itself with no credential, (2) an item, source or task type the owner marked shareable (logged and revocable), or (3) task text the owner sent to a guest. Mixing taints: a value derived from private and public values is private. Each value also carries its source account. The kernel checks labels at every step: a step whose arguments carry a label its key's label caveat (KEY-2) does not admit is rejected, and a step that would put a private value somewhere the owner cannot take it back (a send, a post, a fetch, a model route others can read) needs a key that names that destination.
  - **One mode** (D-153). The planner MAY read reader values of closed types (any PLN-2 type but a bounded string) to choose its next step, and every output, argument and model call of a session carries every label the session has read. Labels bound where a choice's information goes, not how much it encodes; for the first slice, no key names a fetch (EVL-2). **Class keys** (D-158): private calls reach a model route others can read only under a class key naming that route for one task class; the kernel, never a planner, sets a call's class (KEY-2). The box suspends a class key (log state, as a KEY-6 pause) once routes nobody else can read score within noise of it on the class's held-out suite (HLD-3), and lifts its own suspension, never an owner's pause, with a notice if they fall behind (not a widening, KEY-4). STATUS lists each with what its route received.
  - **Content that holds secrets.** Content the box legitimately reads may contain secrets (a sign-in code in a mail). That is governed by labels and the clerk's detector (CLK-4), not custody: a reader never returns a value matching a secret shape, and VRB-1 governs revealing one.
  - **Hand-off between clerks.** Data moves between clerks only as plan steps, never through a shared clipboard, display or file system. `copy` takes the current selection in one clerk into a kernel-held item, never from a password or other secret field; `paste` puts an item into the focused field of another clerk, never a password field; `hand-off` moves a file between clerks' workspace directories (CLK-5). Drag and drop is carried out as these. Each item carries its source account and the private label; a paste or hand-off into another account is allowed only where typing the same content there would be under the step's key. Items pass the clerk detector on the way in (CLK-4) and a match is withheld; a file the detector cannot parse crosses into another account only under a key that names it. Every hand-off is logged with source, destination, size and content hash; items expire when the task ends; pasting or handing off never submits (CLK-3).
  - **Fetches.** A fetch (VRB-2) carries no credential and goes through the edge (HW-15). A URL argument with a private label is fetched only when it appears verbatim in public content, under the key's rate and size caveats, because an attacker's page can supply the link set and the choice among links is a small channel; a link found only in private content (mail, documents) is fetched only under a key that names it. URLs that bear tokens (sign-in, reset or session tokens, detected by fixed patterns: `token`, `code`, `key`, `sig` or `auth` parameters, high-entropy path segments) are never fetched by a guest's plan, only by the account's own clerk or the owner. Keys proposed from use (KEY-5) never name hosts that publish user content (paste sites, file shares, URL shorteners), since an attacker can read what those receive.
- **PLN-4** **Rejected plans are logged.** Every plan the kernel rejects is logged with the session, the step, the failing caveat or label and the plan's hash. A rejection is never retried by rewording: the guest gets the failing check's fixed reason, and the owner sees rejections only when they block the task (as one STATUS line naming what would let it run) or when the replay gate counts them as a security signal (HLD-5).

### 7.2 Guests

- **PLN-5** **The guest interface.** A guest is a planner runtime (an agent CLI or framework) running in a sandbox (PLN-6). Its sandbox offers exactly these, and nothing else (D-142):
  - (a) model access as a standard provider endpoint served only by the model clerk (CLK-7), where the guest holds no credential;
  - (b) one command, `plan`, which submits a plan to the kernel and returns typed results and handles;
  - (c) owner messages, with the owner's photos (CH-24), delivered into the guest's own inbound API;
  - (d) scratch files inside its sandbox.

  A guest has no network but the model clerk, no credentials, no tools of its own outside its sandbox and no channel to the owner but the box's (§10); guest-side channel or messaging plugins are not used. Guest runtimes run unmodified where this interface suffices; a fork is permitted only with a logged "interface X insufficient because Y". Declarations, procedures, the log and the kernel MUST NOT depend on a particular guest; procedure behaviour is qualified per guest.
  - **Declarations.** A guest is added by a declaration, not by code in the kernel: its image, how owner messages enter its inbound API (for example one non-interactive run per task) and its configuration template, which switches off the runtime's cloud and remote features (CLK-7). Declarations arrive only in signed releases (UPD-8) or as a tier-4 owner addition (CH-3). Each guest version is qualified (HLD-3, HLD-6): the injection tests of §16, the containment checks of PLN-6 and A6, and the held-out suite; its memory footprint is measured at qualification and used by RES-2.
  - **Guest options** (D-131): the official, unmodified CLIs of the plans whose terms fit (Codex for a ChatGPT plan, Muse, Grok; CLK-7), and OpenClaw. **Codex is the default guest** where a ChatGPT plan is connected; OpenClaw is the guest for local-only and API-key setups (ONB-3), and is the active guest until Codex is qualified for this host. The CLI guests qualify one at a time in the order Codex, Muse, Grok (D-135); the order blocks nothing, and a guest that passes is listed at once. Qualified: OpenClaw 2026.9.8, unmodified, configuration only. Every requirement that applies to a setup MUST pass at the floor (HW-4) with the guest that setup uses; a guest that does not fit the floor's pool is qualified for larger hosts only. **When a CLI guest loses its route** (D-142): when its plan route is paused, refused or withdrawn by a release, the kernel moves its next task to the next connected plan guest in qualification order (D-135), and to OpenClaw last.
  - **Choosing a guest.** The owner picks the active guest on the local page from the guests qualified for this host, each with one plain line on its measured differences (A10). A switch is a tier-3 key change (CH-3) that takes effect at the next task and keeps the previous guest as a rollback point; a version disqualified by a security finding (HLD-5) leaves the list and is never a rollback target. Durable state is in the log (PLN-6), so a switch loses only the old guest's caches; procedures are requalified, and the owner is told first which would change. Replay (HLD-3) MAY compare guests on past tasks; the box MAY propose a switch from a measured gain, never make one. The owner MAY hold a standing key that switches to another qualified plan guest while the active plan's usage pool is below the owner's reserve (RES-5), and back when it resets; making it is a widening (KEY-4), turning it off is a text (KEY-6). One guest is active at a time.
  - **Delegation.** A guest delegates by intent and label, and MAY attach a route hint to a model call (PLN-7); the hint grants no authority. The per-request instruction cost of a guest is measured with the delegation it saves (A10); a leaner instruction set replaces the current one only when the held-out suite shows no drop (HLD-3), and tool detail loads on demand. As the main orchestrator a guest uses the smartest model of its plan within a latency bound set at qualification, and delegates deep work to a provider CLI guest at the plan's top model and effort (D-131). Several guests MAY work one task through the kernel; disagreements are settled by running both (PLN-6), not by debate.
  - **Configuration is defense in depth.** Containment, custody and spend limits MUST hold against a guest that rewrites its own configuration, since it has root inside its sandbox.
- **PLN-6** **Sandboxes.** Guests, worker sandboxes and readers run under a user-space kernel (such as gVisor) or hardware virtualization, never under namespaces alone, so code with root inside never reaches the host kernel's full system-call surface (S3: gVisor at the floor). [Risk] Browser and kiosk clerks (CLK-5) under gVisor or a microVM at the floor are unmeasured; S5 measures the browser case.
  - **Anything inside, nothing durable.** A guest MAY do anything inside its sandbox: root, packages, code, breakage. A sandbox is rebuildable from its image at any time without loss, because durable state lives only in the log and the stores it names (LOG-1).
  - **Snapshots.** Every sandbox filesystem is snapshotted at each step; full checkpoints (memory and filesystem) are taken after the guest starts and before any task or owner data reaches it, before risky steps, and for `fork`. Snapshots are held by the host, never the guest. Plan steps `checkpoint`, `fork(n)`, `diff`, `rollback` and `merge` act on sandboxes; they touch no account, so they are internal steps, not clerk verbs (VRB-2), and need no key beyond the session's. [S3: a filesystem snapshot costs tens of milliseconds; a full checkpoint of an OpenClaw-sized sandbox is about 1.6 GiB and 2–4.5 s, and restoring one skips the 27 s cold start.]
  - **Labels.** A sandbox carries the labels of everything it has received; forks inherit them, and rollback to a snapshot taken before a label was added, or a rebuild, removes it only if nothing with that label can still write into the sandbox.
  - **Parallel tries.** A guest MAY fork N sandboxes, try approaches, test and keep the winner; N is set by measured free memory (at the floor N may be 1, and N ≤ 2 for OpenClaw-sized branches), and model spend is reserved per fork.
  - **Worker sandboxes.** A guest MAY start worker sandboxes with no planner runtime, built from a base image and driven by the guest's plan steps (exec, files, the snapshot steps above). A worker inherits its creator's isolation, snapshot custody, spend reservation and labels; RES-2 admission sizes how many run. A **desktop worker** image adds a virtual display, credential-free apps (office suite, file manager, image editor, a browser with no sign-ins) and computer-use steps (screenshot, accessibility tree, click, type, key, scroll, drag). Nothing in a worker is ever signed in; an app that needs a login is reached through a clerk (CLK-5). [Inference; its memory cost at the floor is unmeasured.]
  - **Analysis worker.** A worker MAY add a pinned reverse-engineering toolkit (first: REA, MIT-licensed). It has no network at all: targets enter as files, every connection a target attempts is refused and logged, and the toolkit's live modes, self-update and telemetry are off. Uses: qualification evidence that a guest or CLI version keeps auto-update and telemetry off and connects nowhere outside its declaration (HLD-6); crash diagnosis for repair candidates (HLD-4); triage of a suspicious attachment in a worker discarded after the run; and the owner's own software, formats and device firmware, for interoperability, repair and migration. **Limits are legal, not categorical** (D-107): a task is in bounds where the law permits it; a statutory right overrides a contrary licence term only as far as the statute says (for example EU Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 5(2)-(3), 6 and 8), otherwise the licence binds; copy protection is never circumvented without an explicit legal exemption (such as 17 U.S.C. §1201(f)); a third-party service is never a target. Before a task on owner software the owner states its purpose on the local page and the log records it; a task the law clearly rules out is refused and logged, and a genuinely unclear one runs only after the owner approves it, per use or per service, after a plain statement of the risk. [Inference; memory cost and offline modules unmeasured.]
- **PLN-7** **Model routing.** The model clerk (CLK-7) routes each model call, and each provider CLI run, across the routes the owner has granted, by task class. Route kinds: **plan** (CLK-7), **API**, **private hosted** (an endpoint the owner or their organization runs, added only on the local page as a tier-4 key (CH-3) that names its operator; TLS with a publicly valid certificate or a pinned key, never plain HTTP; its reported model identity is checked at each connection and a change suspends its scores until they requalify), and **local** (the box's own model and compute hosts). Any mix works, including local only.
  - **Eligibility first.** Labels decide routes before any routing rule: a private call goes only to routes **allowed private data**, the box, owner compute hosts granted it, and routes a class key names for the call's class (PLN-3). Within the eligible routes the default order per class is (1) plan routes whose pools are above the owner's reserve (RES-5); (2) local routes; (3) private hosted and API routes, cheapest first among those meeting the class's measured acceptance. An exhausted route fails over to the next; where an API or hosted route is granted, losing a plan route makes work dearer, never stops it (DEP-3). A class no present route is qualified for is declined with the reason, never sent to an ungranted route. STATUS reports plan exhaustion and fallback in plain words (ONB-9).
  - **Hints.** A guest's route hint (for example "Codex for this, the API model to review it") ranks only eligible routes; the model clerk overrides it on measured evidence and says which route ran and why, from a closed set of reasons (not granted, pool at reserve, cooling down, label not allowed, concurrency full). Overrides are logged, and HLD-4 turns them into routing rules, so a new route helps every guest without changing its instructions.
  - **Resources.** A `resources` read step answers, per granted route and clerk and with no credential or account detail, its kind, the task classes it is qualified for with measured acceptance and latency, each pool's headroom above reserve and reset time, marginal cost, free concurrency, and whether the session's labels may use it. An account or device the owner knows of but has not connected is listed as "exists, not connected" with its service name or type in the shipped tables' fixed wording, never text from mail or a device; the listing is private.
  - **Local passes.** Local models are a leverage layer for whichever route does the main work: reduce (select and compress context), retrieve (recall and embedding search, LOG-4), extract, triage, check (against a schema, tests or stated constraints) and draft. A call with local passes is a **pipeline**; a pipeline MAY be a **cascade** that escalates to the next route only when a deterministic check fails, never on a model's verdict. Pipelines are used for a class only once adopted as routing rules (HLD-4), on measured acceptance, latency and main-route usage against the main route alone; releases ship seed pipelines that still adopt only on held-out gain. Passes the model clerk applies itself work only on the content of the call they wrap: reduce (extractive, generating no text), extract and check; the log records the original and the reduced call. No pass output decides a label, a key, a step or a route at call time.
  - **Compute hosts.** The owner's own more powerful computers running a standard model server are local routes of kind `owner host`. The box installs and changes nothing there. The local page MAY find model servers on the network and shows each with what adding it means in plain words ("Studio PC: helps with public work", or "Studio PC: will see your private data") and the key fingerprint it pins. Adding one, or allowing it private data, is a tier-4 key (CH-3); removing one is a text. Its key is an account credential in its own clerk (CLK-1); calls reach it only over a channel authenticated by the pinned key, and a mismatch is refused. It reports its model identity at each connection; a change suspends its pipelines. When it is off, its classes use their best measured alternative and STATUS says so in one line ("Studio PC is off; using the box's own model"). [Inference] Common model servers listen in plain HTTP by default, so a TLS front end from a mature component is needed; the owner's guide gives the recipe.
  - **Hardware changes.** When the box starts on a different host, gains or loses an accelerator, or a route is added, removed or changes model, the kernel recomputes resources at once (RES-2, RES-3), the affected classes and pipelines requalify ahead of other spare work, and the owner gets one STATUS line saying what the box can now do.

## 8. Change and self-improvement

The box changes its own behaviour only by evidence from its log, and only in ways the log can undo. Every change is a candidate that is replayed against recorded tasks before it applies, and one that would widen authority is a key change the owner signs (KEY-4).

Sources of change: **local** (procedures, compiled skills, routing rules, context rules, declarations, configuration), **upstream** (new guest versions, host images, dependency updates) and **shared** (packages from other installations, PUB-1). Path: candidate → build in a sandbox → replay and evaluate (HLD-2, HLD-3) → adoption → activate with fallback → keep as a rollback point.

- **HLD-1** **Questions with a default.** A planner MAY ask the owner a question with a stated default and a deadline (CH-15). Without a reply by the deadline, work proceeds on the default, which is listed in STATUS. A default carries no authority: any irreversible step that follows needs its own key (KEY-2) and hold window (KEY-8).
- **HLD-2** **The replay gate.** A candidate is tested by replaying past tasks from the log in sandboxes, in shadow: recorded clerk responses stand in for the outside world, there are no live effects, and any effect without a recorded response fails closed. Model calls are not effects: a replay MAY call models live through the model clerk under the candidate's routing, metered against a separate evaluation budget the candidate cannot change. A candidate fails the gate when any replayed plan contains a step the recorded keys would not have admitted, or when its outcomes fall below the baseline's (HLD-3). The kernel runs the same replay, step by step, to set each irreversible step's hold window by novelty (KEY-8), so one mechanism judges both changes and steps.
- **HLD-3** **The change pipeline.**
  - **Held-out suites.** Evaluation suites are log data. Candidates see only a development split. Cases come from real tasks with owner outcomes (accepted, corrected, rejected) and measured check results (LOG-6), plus the injection tests of §16, never solely from tests the candidate authored. Goal quality is one outcome per goal, and every reader uses its latest revision (KRN-9).
  - **The rules are not candidates.** Changing a suite, a grader, the evaluation budget or the adoption rule is a widening (KEY-4) and cannot be validated by the change itself.
  - **Security updates** MAY stage automatically under a standing key; behaviour changes need the owner's key unless HLD-4's standing key covers them.
  - **Declarations.** Clerk declarations (CLK-3) are created and changed only through this pipeline, including when a planner drafts one for a new service: qualify it on recorded interactions (HLD-2), pass the **mismatch check**, and ask the owner for any new key and for the verb of any unmapped request shape (VRB-2). A new operation, route or verb mapping in a declaration never adopts under a standing key (HLD-4); it needs the owner's new key. The mismatch check exercises the declaration against the service's demo or sandbox environment, with synthetic data only, where one exists; a request shape whose observed effects exceed its verb (any outbound effect from a read or draft shape) blocks adoption. [Risk] Many services have no demo environment; the replay and the first live runs (CLK-3) are then the only check.
  - **The security suite only grows.** A candidate that disables a check, widens authority or reduces the suite's coverage MUST fail qualification. The suite shrinks only by an owner key.
- **HLD-4** **Authority-neutral improvement.** The owner holds by default a standing key that lets a local candidate adopt without a per-change approval when all of these hold:
  - it changes only procedures, compiled skills, routing among already-granted routes (PLN-7, CLK-3's route order) or context rules, including a guest's instructions only so far as they change how the guest uses what it already has. The kernel decides this from the candidate's artifact type and diff (KRN-3), never from the candidate's description of itself. A context rule selects or orders data a sandbox already receives, and never changes what data reaches which sandbox, clerk or route;
  - it passes the replay gate (HLD-2) and the held-out suite with no regression, beats the baseline as below, and passes the security suite;
  - it changes no key, verb, label, custody, check or security-suite coverage, and adds no operation, route or verb mapping.

  **Where candidates come from.** In spare capacity the box mines the log for failures, owner corrections, slow or expensive steps and repeated work, and turns each into a candidate: a procedure, a compiled skill (a recurring procedure compiled into a script that consults a model only where its assumptions fail), a routing rule or pipeline (PLN-7), a declaration route (CLK-3), a context rule or a configuration. A routine that recurs with only implicit acceptance MAY be put to the owner once as a paced question ("Keep it as a skill?", CH-15); a yes is an owner outcome for the suite.

  **Off until it beats the baseline.** Every learning mechanism (procedures and skills, routing rules and pipelines, standing-key proposals (KEY-5), and any later one) applies only while it beats the system without it on §1's objective (higher acceptance, or equal acceptance with lower main-route usage or latency), measured above noise on held-out independent goals; it turns off again when it stops beating it. Plan usage per accepted task (RES-5, A10) is a return measure.

  **Spare capacity.** Spare means local compute not needed by foreground or accepted work (RES-1), plus AI quota the owner designated as spare, such as the part of a plan's pool above the owner's reserve that will reset unused (RES-5). Spare work runs in the lowest admission class and MUST yield within the frozen preemption target. One scheduler allocates spare capacity across improvement (HLD-4), self-securing (HLD-5) and maintenance (HLD-6) by measured return per unit of cost, with no fixed budget or split; a kind of work that stops producing value gets less, and **when nothing worthwhile remains, the box sleeps.** All three run by default; the owner can turn each off by text ("loops off") or on the local page, and onboarding states the defaults in one line.

  Each adoption is logged, listed in STATUS, and reverted by one reply from the owner. Turning it off needs only a text; turning it back on is an owner-approved change. Upstream and shared packages keep HLD-3's security-update rule and UPD-5.
- **HLD-5** **Self-securing.** In spare capacity the box tests itself from inside a sandbox, with no more authority than a guest: injection corpora against guests and readers; fuzzing of the `plan` command, the kernel's plan parser and the browser clerk's action protocol; canary hunts (the A5 test, rerun with fresh canaries); attempts to tamper with evaluators, snapshots and the log; and resource-exhaustion probes. Passive checks run alongside: image and dependency hashes against the signed release; known-vulnerability matching against advisories (fetched like updates, DEP-4, working offline from the last snapshot); configuration drift; and the expiry of credentials and certificates in the sealed store. Rejected plans (PLN-4) are a signal: a session whose rejections match an injection pattern is a finding. The alert patterns of VRB-2's guards grow from findings here. Every finding becomes: contain (pause the affected key or clerk, KEY-6) → preserve evidence in the log → a minimized regression case added to the security suite permanently → a fix candidate through HLD-3 → the owner told per severity.
- **HLD-6** **Upstream intake.** New versions of the active guest and of other declared guests (PLN-5), host images and dependencies are evaluated in spare time through HLD-3, with the analysis worker's evidence where it applies (PLN-6). Security updates come first; a check is never reported as current when the box is offline. Incoming public artifacts, shared packages included (PUB-1), are untrusted input: they run only in sandboxes and pass HLD-3 locally before adoption.

## 9. Storage and devices

This section says where the log, the keys and the credentials live. The trusted parts sit on the core (HW-16) and the enforcer (NET-2); the edge holds nothing (HW-15).

- **STO-1** **The log and its head copies.** The log (LOG-1) is on the core's internal SSD, encrypted under the sealed store's key (STO-2) and hash-chained (LOG-2). Every install has one anchor outside the core, the owner's phone: every passkey signature and owner-page step covers the current head, and the owner page keeps the latest head on the phone. Browsers may evict that storage, so a phone with no stored head counts as unanchored: the owner page says so, and the kernel never reads a first visit as agreement. On a trusted host (HW-5a) the core's TPM monotonic counter, advanced with every head, is a second default anchor. The enforcer and the backup (BAK-1) are further anchors where present; the enforcer and the backup keep every head the kernel sends them, signed by the kernel's sub-key under the head domain (KEY-3), together with the key-change entries (KEY-7) and the erase entries (LOG-4). The phone and the TPM counter hold only a head, so they detect a rollback but cannot re-apply what it lost. The kernel checks the log against every anchor it can reach at boot and at every restore (REC-1), and against the phone's head at the next owner-page step; a log on the core that is shorter than, or forks from, an anchor's head runs no step until the owner chooses what to do.
- **STO-2** **The sealed store.** Credentials, the approval-code verifiers and the log's key are held in one store on the core's internal SSD, and nothing stored on the SSD alone can release its key. The key is wrapped by independent slots:
  - **Trusted host:** a slot sealed to that PC's TPM under measured boot (HW-5, HW-5a), so it opens only on that PC running the signed image, and the box restarts unattended. The owner MAY add a boot PIN to that slot (TPM plus PIN, off by default), which protects a PC stolen with its SSD in it at the cost of unattended restart. Adding or removing a trusted host is a widening at tier 4 (CH-3, KEY-4).
  - **Unknown host:** a slot derived from the Owner Card's **store passphrase** through Argon2id with at least 256 MiB of memory, tuned to about 1 s at the floor (HW-4). The owner scans its QR code from the card on the local Wi-Fi page (CH-8), or types it as a fallback, never by text or voice (CH-6). The short setup code (§12.1) is not derived from it and never unlocks anything. The box also texts the owner and requires a high-tier approval code (CH-4), so an unexpected unlock is visible and a running box refuses one without the owner. The code authorizes the unlock; it does not decrypt anything. Anything that verifies approval codes (the code-generator seed, the grid) lives only inside the store, since a verifier readable before unlock lets a copy of the SSD compute codes; the code is therefore checked after decryption, in the kernel's memory, and the running box discards the unlocked key if the code attempts are exhausted or no valid code arrives within the request's expiry.
  - **Recovery:** a slot for the recovery key (REC-1).

  The owner MAY replace the passphrase on the local UI. Replacement protects only against copies made afterwards: a copy of the SSD taken earlier still opens with the old passphrase, so after a suspected card loss the owner also rotates the credentials in the sealed store. A lost or stolen SSD is ciphertext: without the card, a TPM it was sealed to, or the recovery key, its key is out of reach of offline guessing. [Inference: at 80 bits or more behind Argon2id, offline search is infeasible]
- **STO-3** **Clerks run on the core.** Every clerk except the enforcer (STO-4) runs on the core, each in its own sandbox, and its one credential is released from the sealed store into that sandbox alone (CLK-1). A clerk's sandbox reaches only its declared origins (CLK-3), sees the log only through the kernel, and has no path to another clerk. A service that needs a desktop app runs it inside its clerk's sandbox (CLK-5); there is no other place a credential is used. Pairing a new home device is a verb of the device-pairing clerk on the core (DEV-3); a device on the enforcer's safety list has its secret moved to the enforcer at pairing and never kept on the core.
- **STO-4** **The enforcer is the home clerk.** The enforcer (NET-2) is the clerk for the safety-list devices and the home network. It holds those devices' secrets and the network's configuration, and nothing else. Its owner-signed lists and rules (NET-2, NET-4) are keys (KEY-1) rooted in the owner's passkey and checked with the same key-check library as the kernel (KRN-2), with no separate rule system, so a compromised core cannot unlock, disarm or loosen anything without a key it cannot sign. Its code is part of the trusted base and is reviewed at the kernel's tier. It keeps the log's head copies and key-change entries (STO-1) and refuses keys revoked in them (KEY-7).
- **STO-5** **Core down: actions queue.** When the core is off or unreachable, nothing acts for the owner: owner texts stay with the carrier or on the SIM (HW-15), and the enforcer keeps the home on its last keys and answers its own page (NET-6). When the core returns, queued messages become tasks in order, and every step they produce is checked and held as if new (KEY-8); an approval whose expiry passed is asked again.
- **STO-6** **Core stolen: ciphertext only.** A stolen SSD yields only ciphertext (STO-2); a stolen core with its SSD in it is protected by the boot PIN where the owner set one. The owner revokes the core's trusted-host slot and the kernel's sub-key from the phone passkey (KEY-6), which every clerk and the enforcer then refuse (KEY-7), and rotates the credentials in the sealed store. The owner MAY fit a second drive in the core's spare M.2 slot as a mirror of the log under the same key, so the loss of one drive loses no acknowledged entry. [Inference: with one drive, entries made since the last backup are lost with it; the head copies detect that loss but cannot repair it]

## 10. Owner channel

### 10.1 Text and voice (anywhere)
- **CH-1** The box's number (its SIM) is the conversational address. Texts and calls reach the kernel directly from the modem. No intermediary service is involved beyond the carrier. This owner-line modem stays on the core, so owner texts and calls never cross the edge; the edge carries only the second line (CLK-8, NET-2; Mark, 2026-10-18 18:02Z: "Owner line on core").
- **CH-2** **STOP** and **STATUS** MUST be handled by the kernel with all AI models and guests down. So MUST every control word in CH-11.
- **CH-3** Command tiers:

| Action | Required proof |
|---|---|
| STOP (pause all dispatch) | Message from the owner's number (worst case of spoofing: a pause) |
| Task chat, STATUS | Owner's number + a session unlocked by an approval code within the last 7 days, owner-set (CH-14) |
| Approve a low-risk irreversible effect (CH-10) | The texted code from that request, replied from the owner's number |
| Approve a high-risk irreversible effect (CH-10) | Approval code from the code generator, entered on the owner page opened from that request's link (CH-8); for a purchase paid through a wallet spend request, the owner's approval in that wallet when the kernel verifies it used a strong factor (CH-10) |
| Unlock on an unknown host (STO-2) | Store passphrase scanned on the owner page + approval code from the code generator entered there, checked after decryption |
| New key (device keys except as the rows below say), raise budget, adopt a release, add a trusted host, recovery | Approval code + local confirmation, the passkey signing (KEY-4); recovery: the recovery key, through the longest hold window and notice on every channel (REC-3) |
| Turn the computer-use retry back on for a site or all sites (CLK-5) | Approval code from the code generator on the owner page, after the kernel's fixed-wording readback |
| Connect devices or a hub from a connection plan; turn on capture; widen `actuate` bounds or an audience; re-pin a device; turn a vendor cloud back on; key `change-account`, `delete-remote`, or `reveal-or-create-secret` on a device (§13) | Approval code + local confirmation (tier 4) |
| Connect a verified device that needs no secret, read only (DEV-10) | Approval code from the code generator, on the owner page opened from its STATUS line's link |
| Key `actuate` or `present` on, or cut off from its vendor cloud, a connected device not marked safety or access; present private content on a `local-only` device whose audience is `household`; a secure-direction action no key covers, other than a value outside bounds (DEV-5, DEV-6, DEV-9, DEV-10) | The texted code from that request (CH-10 low risk), inside the owner's passkey-signed device envelope (KEY-4) |
| Key `actuate-physical` on a connected device; key `actuate` or `present` on, or cut off from its vendor cloud, a device marked safety or access (DEV-10) | Approval code from the code generator, on the owner page opened from the kernel-rendered request; an `actuate-physical` key is never batched |
| Physical action in the unsafe direction, or a value outside `actuate` bounds that no key covers (DEV-5) | Approval code from the code generator per action on the owner page, never batched; the unsafe direction is never covered by a standing key |
| Start a reverse-engineering task (DEV-12) | Local confirmation on the local page, in the step where the owner uploads its input files and states its purpose; never by text or voice |
| Write firmware or software to a device (DEV-12) | The device's `change-account` key (tier 4), then an approval code from the code generator per write, on the owner page opened from that request, never batched or covered by a standing key |

- **CH-4** Approval codes come from a standard code generator. [Fact] iPhone's built-in Passwords app generates them; on Android any authenticator works. The **paper grid** on the Owner Card's detachable sheet is the fallback: challenge–response, each cell single-use (CH-18). No agentOS app exists. These codes are the high-risk tier of CH-10. Batching (one code per batch) and keys are the main ways to reduce prompts.
- **CH-5** Live calls: carrier voice → modem → kernel → speech (local by default; hosted only by explicit policy) → guest. [Risk] Voice quality and latency over USB modems need qualifying. On a call, texted approval codes, session unlock codes and control words are entered by keypad (DTMF) (CH-17), decoded by the kernel, and never passed to the speech service or guests, which are untrusted (§7).
- **CH-10** **Two-tier approval.** Prompts should be rare in the first place: approvals are batched (one code covers a batch) and owner keys cover routine actions, so codes are needed only for what remains. On the owner page, a passkey tap with user verification approves whatever a typed code approves (KEY-4).
  - **Low risk** means **all** of: the recipient is one the kernel verifies already exists in the owner's account (as in KEY-5), created by the owner or present longer than the owner's hold period (KEY-5), not added automatically by the service; any amount is under the owner-set limit; and the operation's verb is not excluded by the owner. A cancelable web submit (CLK-3) that sends nothing to a party other than the site has no recipient to verify, and is low risk when its amount is under the owner-set limit and its verb is not excluded; one that sends to another party follows the recipient rule above. Anything else is high risk. The box texts a one-time code bound to that request (or batch), and the owner replies with it from their number. The code works once and expires after a short owner-settable time (default 15 minutes). The request text is rendered by the kernel in fixed wording from verified fields, never by the planner. Recipients appear as canonical identifiers (address, number, last 4 digits of an account), never display names, with look-alike characters folded to plain text. This still defeats spoofed texts and self-approval by the planner, which never sees the code. It proves possession of the owner's number only (§19 risk 9).
  - **High risk** means new or wider keys (except the device steps listed under Devices), money above the owner-set limit, VRB-1 actions, and recovery. These require the code-generator code or the paper grid, which a SIM swap cannot obtain, entered on the owner page at home (CH-8), plus local confirmation where CH-3 already requires it. A code-generator code or grid cell approves an effect only on the owner page, never in a text or by keypad; it is still taken by text, or by keypad on a call (CH-17), for a session unlock (CH-19), which resumes what the owner already allowed and widens nothing.
  - **Wallet approval.** A purchase paid through a spend request a payment wallet holds, scoped to one merchant, one amount, and a time window (for example an agent wallet that releases a one-time card only for an approved request), is approved by the owner in that wallet, signed in on their own device. That approval replaces the box's own code for that purchase, so the owner taps once (OWN-8), only when it was made with a factor a SIM swap cannot obtain (a passkey or device-bound confirmation, never a texted code or email link) and the kernel can verify that: either the wallet's own response, read on the kernel's connection, names the factor, or the owner has confirmed on the Wi-Fi page that a passkey is on for the wallet and the wallet's qualified behaviour is that texted-code sign-in is then off. The code's purpose then still holds: the planner can neither see nor produce the approval, and the wallet releases payment only within the scope approved. Otherwise, including an approval that rested on a texted code or one whose factor cannot be verified, the box's own check applies as for any purchase in its tier, so a weaker factor never stands in for a stronger one (OWN-11, CH-19). When the factor cannot be read from the response, the first purchase after any new or re-issued wallet key needs the box's own code; the connect page asks the owner to turn on a passkey in the wallet, and the box keeps requiring its code until the owner confirms it is on. That confirmation lowers the proof for every later wallet purchase, so it is a high-risk effect (tier boundaries, below): while it is pending, the next wallet purchase asks for the code-generator code whatever its tier, and that one code approves the purchase and records the confirmation, so the owner sees no extra prompt (OWN-8). That prompt MUST say that the code also turns on one-tap wallet approval for later purchases. The kernel files the request only after its own checks pass (the tier rules here, LOG-7, KEY-5), with the scope taken from verified fields; a wallet approval never stands in for a key (connecting the wallet included), the card entry (KEY-2), a key, a limit change, or recovery. [Risk] Whoever holds the wallet session can file and approve requests the owner never sees; the box's check covers only purchases the box makes, and the outside limit bounds the rest only when the wallet draws on the KEY-2 card alone (KEY-2). Where the owner chose their own card, or the wallet funds from anything else, the wallet's connect page says nothing outside the box bounds that loss. [Inference] Whether a wallet reports the factor, and whether turning on a passkey turns off its texted-code sign-in, is unverified for any wallet; until qualified, every wallet approval counts as unverified and the box's check applies.
  - **Devices** (§13). Low risk also covers, on a connected device: granting `actuate` or `present`, or cutting the device off from its vendor cloud, where the class table marks no safety or access role (DEV-9, DEV-10); `present` of private content to a `local-only` device whose audience is `household` (DEV-6, DEV-9); and a secure-direction action that no key covers, other than a value outside bounds (DEV-5). A SIM swapper who approves one of these reaches no one outside the household, and each effect is bounded, undoable, or reduces exposure. These grants are narrowings of the owner's passkey-signed device envelope (KEY-4). Every other device step keeps its CH-3 row; private content to a `public` device stays a high-risk disclosure (KRN-4).
  - The owner sets the tier boundaries; changing them is a high-risk effect. The kernel applies them to verified data, never to the planner's claims. Unclassifiable requests are high risk.
- **CH-6** Secrets MUST NEVER be requested or accepted over text or voice. The carrier sees that traffic, and transcripts reach models.
- **CH-11** **Control words.** The kernel parses a closed set of control words: STOP, RESUME, STATUS, HELP, YES, NO, UNDO, MORE, PUBLIC, RUN, UNLOCK, NAME, BLOCK, CONNECT, RETRY, QUIET, TEXTS, URGENT (pacing, CH-15), plus pause/revoke commands for keys (KEY-5), loops (HLD-4), and plan routes: `PLAN <plan> OFF` pauses one and `PLAN <plan> ON` is its resume command (CLK-7). **`STOP <name>`** (D-151) names an account, provider, device, node or person in the graph (CLK-10) and at once pauses every step within that name's reach; a name the graph cannot resolve pauses everything. Every STOP reply carries a one-time code, sent to the real number of whoever sent the STOP (the owner, or the second witness, CH-15); a reply carrying it narrows everything in the scope's reach: the declared narrowings (CLK-12), the box's own grants there, renewed, and the enforcer's tightenings. STATUS then lists what to rotate: the box's grants there, the accounts that recover through that name and what it held for the owner. Unconfirmed, a STOP costs only a pause. Undoing a confirmed narrowing is a widening (KEY-4), done at home with a hardware key. A bare RESUME while nothing is halted but a plan route is paused replies with that route's command ("Not stopped. To turn your ChatGPT plan back on, reply PLAN ChatGPT ON.") when the owner paused that route, withdrew or has not yet given its consent, or had its login refused; for a route a security notice paused it replies with that route's STATUS line instead (CLK-7). A control word counts only when it is the whole message, ignoring case and punctuation (with its arguments, e.g. `YES 1 3 4821`); every other message goes to the planner. HELP returns the list in one text. A message that is only an open texted code, or that code's text copied whole (CH-18), is a reply to the request or batch the code is bound to, as `YES <code>`. `BLOCK <id> <code>` is a device reply (DEV-9); `CONNECT <id>` replies with the owner-page link for that device, whose code goes on the page (DEV-10, CH-10). On a prompt that offers BLOCK, a bare code means `BLOCK <id> <code>`, and that prompt's texted code never approves the disclosure itself. A message of only digits, of a length the box issues for texted or code-generator codes (CH-18), optionally prefixed `Code`, that matches no open code counts as a wrong code under CH-18 and never reaches the planner; the reply says so and, while a request that takes a code-generator code is open, that its code goes on the owner page at home. As an exception to the whole-message rule, `PUBLIC` as the first word of a task message marks that task's text public instead of private (data labels, §7); the rest of the message goes to the planner. `RUN` confirms the messages held while the session was locked (CH-14), shown back numbered after the unlock: `RUN` runs all of them, `RUN 1 3` the listed ones, and `NO` discards them. The unlock code proves the owner, not who wrote the held text, so held messages other than a lone inline-coded message (CH-14) run only on `RUN` (D-080, D-087). `RUN` takes no code (CH-14). `UNLOCK <challenge> <code>` applies after 10 wrong codes in 24 hours: from then on, only code attempts carrying a one-time challenge the box texts to the owner's number count, so guesses from a spoofed number are ignored rather than counted. RESUME needs a low-tier texted code (CH-10), since a spoofed RESUME could restart halted work; STOP, NO, and pause or revoke need none, since their worst case is a pause. `RETRY` asks about each effect STATUS lists as unclear and not retried (KRN-8), one request per effect, with no approval for all of them; each takes the tier its verified facts give (CH-10), always high for an effect that moves money, and says the effect may happen twice. `NAME` alone replies with the box's current name; `NAME <name>` renames the box (CH-21). It widens how the box presents itself to third parties, so it takes effect only in an unlocked session (CH-3) or after a code-generator code on the owner page (CH-10), after the kernel confirms it in fixed wording, and only if the name passes CH-21's name check. Configuration requests in natural language go through the planner and take effect only after the kernel renders them back in fixed wording, as in KEY-5. **Reactions** (D-127): an owner message whose whole body matches a known reaction format (a fixed list, KRN-3, e.g. a phone's text form of a tapback: `Liked "<quoted text>"`) is a reaction, never a control word, a code, `RUN`, or an approval, whatever it quotes. A reaction is owner input like a task message: it reaches the planner only when a task message would (CH-3), and while the session is locked it is held as CH-14 holds task messages, never passed to a guest or model. The kernel matches the quoted text against its recent texts to the owner and passes the planner the reaction kind and the message it marks (or that none matched); the quoted text itself is not re-sent to the planner as a new task message. A reaction to a text that expects a reply (an approval, a question, a `RUN` offer) also gets the kernel's one-line pointer to that text's valid replies, sent as conversation. [Inference] The text forms differ by phone and app and change between versions; qualification records the forms current iPhone and Android Messages apps send, and the adversarial self-tests (HLD-5) adds to the list (HLD-3). The planner may answer an owner message with a reaction instead of a text only after qualification shows the owner's phone renders the box's reaction as a reaction rather than as text; until then the kernel refuses a planner reaction, so the planner replies in text.
- **CH-12** **Message format.** Kernel-originated texts use the GSM-7 character set only (no emoji, which halves segment length), target one SMS segment, and never exceed three. Planner conversation text (CH-21) keeps its characters, emoji and line breaks included, and is sent in the encoding it needs (UCS-2 when it is not GSM-7, 67 characters a segment), so it is never rewritten to fit GSM-7; it is never placed inside a kernel template. Before planner text is sent, the kernel removes control characters other than line feed (Unicode category Cc) and bidirectional controls (embeddings, overrides, isolates and marks), so the order the phone shows is the order checked; every other character, joiners included, is sent as written, so an emoji built from several (👩‍💻) arrives whole. The CH-19 and CH-21 checks run on the whole reply before it is split into texts, joined to the planner's earlier texts since the owner's last message, and on its fold, whose order follows the UTS #39 skeleton so that no later step can undo an earlier one: NFKD and case folding (width and compatibility forms decompose); then the UTS #39 skeleton (each character replaced by its confusables.txt prototype, then NFD again, so a mark the mapping adds is decomposed too, KRN-3); then NFKD and case folding again, since a prototype can be a capital or a compatibility form; then every combining mark (category M) is dropped; then every Default_Ignorable_Code_Point (joiners, variation selectors, Hangul fillers and the rest) is dropped in one pass and read as a space in a second, since a phone may draw one as nothing or as a gap; then every character that is not a letter, a digit or printable ASCII is read as a space, an allowlist that closes blank-looking characters (U+2800 and the like) as a class; then any run of spaces is read as one, so it counts as the one separator the CH-21 reply grammar expects. The output is withheld if either pass matches. The fold is defined by its result, not only its steps: for every code point and every text x it is idempotent and case-blind, fold(fold(x)) = fold(x) and fold(upper(x)) = fold(lower(x)) = fold(x), and its code is checked by a test that scans every code point. So a footer or a control word with a request ID split across lines or texts, padded with invisible characters or marks, or reordered by direction is still caught. Each planner text keeps the three-segment limit in its own encoding. A longer planner reply is sent as consecutive texts, split at a paragraph, line or sentence boundary (a word boundary only when a sentence does not fit), at most three texts (nine segments, as CH-14); when it does not fit even then, the third text ends with a pointer to the rest on the owner page, kept as CH-19 requires (D-127). Request IDs are at most 3 characters and unique among open requests. An approval text, and each item of a batch, is rendered by the kernel in fixed wording (never planner text) and states, from verified fields: verb, object, recipient or amount, reversibility (the undo window, or "cannot be undone"), expiry, and the valid replies. The fixed wording reads as a first-person question in plain sentences, as a person would ask it, e.g. `Can I send "Q3 invoice" to bob@example.com? It can't be undone. Expires 5pm. Approve at home on my Wi-Fi page: [link]. Or reply NO A7.` The request ID appears in the valid replies; a leading `<ID>:` label is not required (D-127). Every text that expects a reply ends with the valid replies, except the code text of CH-18. Longer evidence comes by `MORE <id>`. `MORE` is read-only paging (D-079): `MORE <id>` shows a request's first page, `MORE <id> <n>` the page that starts at item n of a batch, and `MORE STATUS` and `MORE STATUS <n>` the STATUS lines that did not fit. Each page keeps this item's limits, names the request, the items shown out of how many, and the expiry, and ends with the valid replies and the next `MORE` command while items remain. A page is the same each time it is asked for while its request is unchanged; a changed, expired, or reissued request gets a new view, never an earlier one. Reading never approves, renews, or extends anything, and a batch still takes one code (CH-13). `MORE STATUS` needs the same proof as STATUS (CH-3). Every text that reports a problem or a wait says what the owner can do, or that nothing is needed; it never suggests a step that cannot fix the cause (a restart for a missing setting, a setting the owner cannot reach). Owner texts use one name for the local UI, built on the Wi-Fi the owner reaches it by (CH-7, ONB-7), and never name internal parts. STATUS is the owner's primary view (CH-15): the scoreboard line, the planner's line, then exception lines while they last (something not running, waiting on the owner, or different from what the owner set), then results and other items listed for the owner that no STATUS reply has yet shown, each at its latest revision (LOG-3); normal states are not listed. When the exception lines do not fit in three segments, the last line counts the rest and names `MORE STATUS` (D-079). **The scoreboard** (D-149). STATUS's first line names the weakest of money, identity, data and physical safety (KRN-11) and the one next action: the one with the largest gain per owner minute, structural fixes first. `MORE STATUS` opens with two numbers for each of the four, recomputed on every change (the failures a thief needs, and the providers that can cut the owner off), then the worst loss before notice and the time to notice (measured by drills), owner minutes this month (OWN-7, OWN-8) and hours of owner work the box did. Every proposal (a connection, a standing key, a fix) shows its effect on these numbers before the owner approves it.

- **CH-13** **Batch replies.** Items in a batch are numbered. `YES <code>` approves all; `YES 1 3 <code>` approves the listed items; `NO` or `NO 2` denies without a code. Unanswered items expire as denied and are listed in STATUS. Narrowings and other notices of one run are batched into one text and one STATUS line (D-151).
- **CH-14** **Inline unlock.** When task chat needs a session unlock (CH-3), the kernel holds the owner's message, asks for a code-generator code or a paper-grid cell (the high-risk tier of CH-10; a texted code never unlocks a session), and then runs the held message: automatically when the code came in the same message as the request and nothing else is held (the inline form, below), and otherwise on `RUN` (CH-11) after a code-only unlock, because that code proves the owner, not who wrote the held text. The owner never resends it. Every task message that arrives while the session is locked is held, unauthenticated, in arrival order, and never passed to a guest or model (D-080). Held text is bounded by size, not by message count: no more than the readback plus 2 `MORE <id>` pages can show, each within CH-12's three-segment limit (at most 9 segments in all, so the owner reads everything in three texts), so `RUN` confirms only text the owner was shown in full. A message carrying a valid code is always kept and does not count toward that bound. Held messages are discarded once CH-10's code lifetime (default 15 minutes) has passed since the first, unless a code arrives; it is the same owner setting, not a separate one (D-087). After the code, the kernel texts the held messages back in fixed wording, numbered in arrival order, marking the one sent with the code: `RUN` runs them all, `RUN 1 3` the listed ones, as one confirmation in the shape of CH-13's item lists, and `NO` discards them. `RUN` takes no code: it comes after the unlock, and in an unlocked session the owner's number already sends task chat (CH-3), so a spoofed `RUN` can do nothing a spoofed task message could not (D-087). The `RUN` offer expires like a CH-10 code. Every unlock reply states how many messages were held, how many await `RUN`, and how many were discarded since the last unlock. Held text that fails the CH-19 or CH-21 checks is shown only as a local-UI pointer and is excluded from `RUN`. An inline code runs its own message automatically only when nothing else is held. A later code never authenticates earlier text by itself: messages not confirmed expire unrun, and the owner is told they were discarded. A code may also be appended to any message. N is owner-set, default **7 days**. An unlock is also required after a boot on an unknown host and after repeated failed codes.
- **CH-15** **Pacing (pull-first).** STATUS (CH-12) is the owner's primary view. Only urgent classes are pushed, from a closed set of four (D-129): **security** (security alerts and texts another requirement says are sent at once) is always urgent and cannot be switched off; **approval** (approvals that block work, and questions with a deadline or that hold or contain work, LOG-4, HLD-1, UPD-9) is urgent unless the owner switches it off; **agent** (texts the planner starts outside conversation) is urgent only if the owner switches it on; **update** (every other text the kernel starts) is never urgent. Everything else waits in STATUS and is never texted unasked. Pushed texts are rate-limited (default 3 per hour, owner-set from 1 to 20), and quiet hours and the hourly limit hold every one except the urgent classes, which go at once and still count toward the hour. There are no quiet hours until the owner sets them. A held push waits on the box, saved before it is counted as held, at most 32 at a time: past that the oldest is dropped, and the next text sent says how many were dropped. When quiet hours end and the allowance has room, held pushes go out oldest first, combined into as few texts as CH-12 allows, each counting as one push; planner text is never combined with another text. The owner sets pacing with whole-message words that join CH-11's closed set: `QUIET <from>-<to>` in box-local hours (e.g. `QUIET 22-7`), `QUIET OFF`, `TEXTS <n>`, and `URGENT APPROVAL ON|OFF` and `URGENT AGENT ON|OFF`. They take effect only in an unlocked session (CH-3), since fewer texts or longer quiet hours can hide activity from the owner, and the kernel confirms each in fixed wording. Replies in an open task chat and results of tasks the owner asked for are conversation, not pushes: they are sent as they come, and pacing and quiet hours apply only to texts the kernel starts unasked. A task chat is open from an owner message that reaches the planner until 30 minutes (owner-set) after the owner's last such message; a result is of a task the owner asked for when the kernel's log ties it to a task that came from an owner message. A kernel reply to an owner message (a control word's reply, a STATUS view, a code text the owner asked for) is conversation too. Conversation does not count toward the push allowance, and no held push delays it (D-127). There is no digest. A liveness line ("Still running.", no other content) is sent at an owner-set interval (default weekly, can be turned off), because its absence is how an owner who rarely pulls learns the box is down. **Second witness** (D-149). Security texts also go to a second person the owner names, on a different phone, since the owner's phone is one failure domain holding many roles; that person's number may send `STOP` and `STOP <name>` (CH-11) and nothing else. The enforcer shows it on its light while a security alert is unresolved; a light on the core does not count, since a rooted core controls it.

- **CH-16** **Undo.** Every reversible step that changes state, and every effect with an undo window (VRB-3), is confirmed with `UNDO <id>` and its deadline.
- **CH-17** **Codes on calls.** During a live call, texted approval codes, session unlock codes and control words are entered only on the keypad and decoded by the kernel, never spoken, because the speech service is untrusted and could change which request a code approves. Speech is for chat. When the owner can't use the keypad, the box texts the pending batch for later. The same rules as text apply, so a code-generator code approves an effect only on the owner page (CH-10).
- **CH-18** **Code hygiene.** Texted codes are at least 6 digits, single-use, and bound to one request or batch. Each is sent as its own text right after its request, reading only `Code <digits>`, so the owner can copy the whole text and send it back unchanged; the pair counts as one text under CH-15. [Unverified] Whether the phone also offers the code above its keyboard while replying is the phone's behaviour, so the format must not depend on it; qualification checks current iPhone and Android Messages apps. Three wrong replies void that request. Five wrong codes of any kind in 24 hours lock the low tier, so approvals need a code-generator code until the owner unlocks, and the owner is told. Paper-grid cells are single-use.
- **CH-19** **Owner-channel disclosure.** A session unlock (CH-3) requires a code-generator code or a grid cell, never a texted code, because a SIM swap receives texted codes. Outbound texts and speech whose content comes from anything other than the kernel's own fixed templates MUST NOT carry verification codes or known secret formats; such content is replaced by a pointer to the owner page (CH-7), whose link opens that one item at home. The kernel sends that pointer only after it has kept the content, CLK-4-redacted, for the owner page, where it is kept durably across restarts for a number of days the owner sets (default **30**, so an owner who opens the link at home within about a month still finds the message), and only by time (D-087); reading never removes it, and an expired item says it has expired; if it cannot keep it, the text says the message could not be kept instead of pointing to the page (D-079). The patterns are fixed and context-anchored (no inference, KRN-3): known token formats, and 4–10 character codes next to words such as "code", "OTP", "verification", or "PIN", so amounts, dates, and order numbers pass. Kernel-originated texts carrying the kernel's own CH-10 codes are exempt. This extends CLK-4 from sealed store values to secret-shaped content (PLN-3).


### 10.2 The owner page (at home only)
- **CH-7** **One page, one step at a time.** The owner's whole interaction surface is texts and calls, occasionally with images (CH-22, CH-20), plus one page for rare, important one-off steps (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:32Z: "entire interaction surface for entire system is text + call (back and forth occasionally with images), plus single page when on WiFi for most important one off rare things"; 16:37Z "One page"). That page is the **owner page**. It opens only from a link in a kernel-rendered text, and each link opens one step: one confirm screen, or, for setup (§12.1) and a password login (CH-8), a short fixed sequence that ends with that step. It has no menu, no way to reach another step, no standing sign-in, and no app to install. The link carries a random single-use value bound to its request, which expires with the request and is spent when the step is submitted, never when the link is opened, so a link preview cannot spend it; the first browser whose copy of the page runs its script binds the value to itself with a host-only cookie (a link preview runs no script and binds nothing), and the step and any kept content are then shown only to that browser; the page served before that binding holds no content. If another browser then opens the link, the kernel voids the request, logs it, texts the owner over the owner line that the step was opened elsewhere and to ask again for a new link, and shows that browser the same line, so whoever opens a link before the owner, such as a compromised enforcer that saw it, cannot keep it once the owner opens it; the step still needs its own CH-3 proof, so holding a link or being on the network authorizes nothing. The page shows kernel-rendered content from verified fields, and planner-authored or private content only where the owner opened it to read (CH-19, CH-20). The local page, local UI, Wi-Fi page, setup page, upload page and connect page named elsewhere in this spec are all the owner page under these rules, and owner texts call it "my Wi-Fi page" (CH-12). Every owner-page link in a text carries one home address, the enforcer's (NET-4), and every step shows one look, so the owner still has one page; on an agentOS step the address bar shows the core's name. The enforcer draws its own steps there. An agentOS link carries a fixed path prefix and one value in the link-value format; for it, the enforcer answers with a top-level redirect to the core's page address, fixed when the core is paired with the enforcer, which nothing in the request (host, path or parameter) can change, and it forwards only that value and refuses any other request under the prefix. Both hops send `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. The core draws that step and checks its proof (CH-9).
- **CH-8** **What needs the page.** Only these steps use the owner page, each opened from its own text link: setup and what it lists (§12.1); unlocking on an unknown host (STO-2); every high-risk approval (CH-10) and every CH-3 row that names local confirmation; values that never travel by text or voice, such as a payment card, an API key, a plan token, a pairing code or the store passphrase (CH-6); a password login to a site without OAuth through the live view of a credentialed browser; reading content kept for the owner (CH-19, CH-20); a full-resolution photo upload (CH-22); and the switches this spec keeps off text (PUB-1, REC-4). Where this spec places a setting, a list or a sign-in on the page (for example CLK-7's consents, CH-26's photos, ONB-9's plan lines), a text offers it with its link, or the owner asks for it in natural language: the request goes through the planner, and the kernel texts the step's link only after rendering the step back in fixed wording (CH-11), so a request made while the session is locked is held like any task message (CH-14); that link opens that one step; the page never lists settings to browse. Everything else is a text or a call, and the page is never required for daily operation. A step that needs the page waits until the owner is home: the box texts its link once, saying what it is for and that it opens at home, lists it in STATUS while it waits, and does not text it again. So high-risk approvals and private replies wait until the owner is home (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:37Z, accepting "High-risk approvals and private replies wait until you are home"). The owner's reads away from home are the one exception (NET-4, D-143, risk 37).
- **CH-9** **Reachable only at home, from the system that acts.** The owner page MUST NOT be reachable from the internet or from any home network other than the one this rule names. It is reached only from the Owner network or the enforcer's wired admin port (NET-3); the enforcer's setup network reaches only the enforcer's enrolment page (HW-10). Both networks are WPA3-SAE only, with no WPA2 mode in the first release, so the Owner Card states the minimum phone (ONB-7, D-081). No domain or public certificate is required. Whoever draws a step checks its proof: each step is drawn, and its proof checked, by the system that carries it out, on that system's own origin. So agentOS never serves content on the enforcer's origin, frames or relays its screen, or asks for its passkey, recovery code or setup secret, and the enforcer never draws an agentOS step or sees its proof; the enforcer's entry only redirects (CH-7). The core serves every agentOS step on its own local name, which shares no parent domain or cookie scope with the enforcer's name (NET-4), over HTTPS. The phone trusts the core only through a certificate authority name-constrained to that one name, never an unconstrained root (as NET-4's passkey rules require for the enforcer). The controller generates the authority's key at its first boot, so every Owner Card prints its fingerprint (HW-1), and never shares it; it is kept like a sealed store secret, in the encrypted backup too (§9), so a restored or moved core (§12.2) keeps it. The core issues itself a serving certificate for at most 825 days and renews it without the owner. The authority is valid for 30 years, longer than the box will run, and is replaced only by a compromised restore (REC-5), so the fingerprint on the Owner Card fits every other reinstall; whoever reads its key from a decrypted backup can impersonate the core's name, and only that name, to a phone on the home network until a compromised restore replaces the authority (§19 risk 30). At pairing the core gives the enforcer the authority's certificate, and the enforcer installs it on the owner's phone in the step that enrols the owner's passkey, beside its own certificate (§12.1), so TLS ends on the core: after setup, the enforcer, the access point and other Owner-network devices carry only ciphertext. Any reinstall (a new phone, REC-3, or a browser warning) goes through that same enforcer page, and the owner checks that the fingerprint the phone shows matches the one printed on the Owner Card (HW-1); on a mismatch the owner stops and installs nothing. The first install has no such check (§19 risk 30). The page never asks the owner to click through a certificate or browser warning; if a phone's browser warns on a step, the owner leaves it, and the step waits for a reinstall. The Owner Card also states in one line that agentOS asks for a code or secret only on a page whose address bar shows the core's name. [Inference] That Android and iOS install such an authority from a local page, enforce its name limit, and need one extra owner action for it (on iOS its own trust switch, on Android its own install) is unqualified, as for the enforcer's certificate (risk 29); qualification shows the name limit holding and counts those actions, and a platform that does not enforce the name limit fails qualification, so the authority is never installed on it. The edge never serves or relays the page, the core never serves it on the edge's cable, and the enforcer lets only the Owner network and the wired admin port reach the core's page port. A step changes state only on a submit, never on opening its link (no state change on GET), and the system that draws it accepts that submit only when the request's Origin is the page's own origin (refused when absent), its Host names the page itself (against DNS rebinding), and it carries an anti-forgery value issued with that page load in a host-only cookie (no Domain attribute; `Secure` with the `__Host-` prefix where the page is served over HTTPS, as on the core), SameSite=Strict and HttpOnly, scoped to the step and never carried in the texted link. So a look-alike page that collects a code, or anyone who texts the owner a misleading link, cannot submit it through the owner's browser; a code phished that way still works where CH-10 takes it by text, and a compromised enforcer that redirects an agentOS link to a look-alike step is the residual (§19 risk 30).

### 10.3 What is deliberately not possible
Remote live browser view, remote file browsing, and push notifications, away from home. Everything remote is text and voice, plus picture texts from the owner (CH-22), plus evidence delivered to a destination the owner already owns (CH-20). [Inference] OAuth "device code" sign-ins work remotely because they happen on the provider's own site. Scope limits vary by provider. Not for Gmail: Google's device flow is believed not to allow Gmail scopes (unverified at D-150), and Gmail connects at home in any case (§12.1 step 7).

- **CH-20** **Evidence delivery.** Evidence beyond one text (diffs, screenshots, documents, result files) MAY be delivered as kernel-rendered MMS images carrying only what a text may carry (CH-19), or to **one owner-owned destination** (an email address or cloud folder the owner already uses), set at setup or later, through its clerk. Delivery to that fixed destination is a keyed `share` to the owner only; the planner cannot change the destination. Setting or changing it is a high-risk effect (CH-10). Evidence from private sources goes only to that destination or the owner page, never by MMS except the owner's reads away from home (NET-4, D-143, risk 37); without a destination, a private reply waits until the owner is home (CH-8). When a destination is set, chat replies that carry content from private sources go there, and the text carries a one-line summary and a pointer, so a SIM swapper gets summaries, not full content. A summary is still a bounded disclosure channel: it can carry ordinary private facts, since CH-19's check removes known secret shapes, not everything private (D-082). CLK-4 redaction applies. It is an optional dependency (DEP-3): without it, text-only operation is unchanged.
- **CH-21** **Name and voice.** The box has one name. Setup offers a suggested made-up full name (e.g. "Dave Smith"), which the owner keeps or overwrites (§12.1); `NAME` changes it later (CH-11). The name is the box's contact-card name and the basis of its optional mailbox (CLK-8). The same screen has an optional field for the owner's own name, filled in from the display name of a connected owner mailbox when there is one, for the owner to confirm. A box name is 1 to 32 characters of letters, spaces, hyphens, and apostrophes only (no digits, `@`, or URLs); it is not a control word, does not contain "agentOS" or "assistant", and, when the owner's name is known, is not the owner's name. The kernel always appends the suffix below; the name cannot remove it.
  - **To the owner, the box speaks in the first person** ("Open my Wi-Fi page", "I can't work on tasks right now"), in kernel templates and planner chat alike, so the owner hears one voice. Texts to the owner are not signed with the name. When the planner sandbox is down but the kernel answers, the kernel still says "I" and states what still works ("I can't work on tasks right now. STOP, STATUS and HELP still work.").
  - **One voice must not let the planner pass for the kernel.** Codes and approvals are requested only in kernel-rendered texts that carry a request ID and end with the valid replies (CH-12). Every planner-authored output to the owner (texts, MMS images, speech before synthesis, and CH-20 summaries) passes a closed check (KRN-3), and the kernel withholds it if it contains either (a) a word from a fixed vocabulary: code, passcode, PIN, OTP, one-time, authenticator, code generator, verification, grid, cell, password; or (b) the reply grammar: a control word followed by a request ID or a code-shaped token (4 to 8 digits), or the kernel's reply-options footer. A withheld output is logged and replaced by the fixed text "I held back a message because it mentioned a code. Read it on my Wi-Fi page.", sent only once the output is kept as CH-19 requires; otherwise a fixed text says it could not be kept (D-079). The vocabulary grows only through adversarial self-test (HLD-5) and replay regressions (HLD-3). [Risk] Words such as code, cell, and password are common in ordinary task results ("I fixed the code", "cell B2"), so false holds will be frequent until measured; the held text stays readable on the owner page, from the link in that text, until it expires (CH-19, D-079).
  - **Planner text carries no label.** Planner text to the owner has no prefix, label or signature; the check above, not a marker, keeps it from passing for the kernel, and the kernel's texts that ask for an approval, a code or `RUN` keep a form planner text cannot take, since the check withholds the reply-options footer and a control word with a request ID from planner text. The check also withholds planner text that opens like a kernel request (a request-ID-shaped token followed by `:`). Until the check is in force, planner text keeps a fixed prefix (D-127).
  - **Codes go only to the kernel.** Every kernel text that asks for a code ends with the reply-options footer, including CH-14's unlock prompt, the `UNLOCK` challenge, and RESUME's code request. A token in an owner message that verifies as a valid or recently used approval or unlock code is consumed by the kernel and removed before the message reaches the planner or any model. Because the kernel cannot verify one-time codes it did not issue, it also consumes any owner message whose whole body is code-shaped (4 to 8 digits, spaces or hyphens allowed) that arrives within 10 minutes of a kernel code request or an output held by the check above. Digits inside ordinary sentences still reach the planner. The welcome text says once: "I only ask for a code in a text that ends with the reply options."
  - **To everyone else, the box says what it is**: its name plus "(<owner>'s agentOS assistant)", or "(an agentOS assistant)" when the owner's name is unknown, in mail display names, signatures, and call openings, with the automated-assistant statement of CLK-8. First person never lets a third party take the box for a person.

### 10.4 Photos from the owner
- **CH-22** **Photos to the planner.** Sending the box a photo is a core way to give it work: the owner photographs a letter, a bill, a label, a screen, a whiteboard or a broken part, with or without a caption, and the planner acts on what it shows. A photo reaches the box two ways: (1) **picture texts**, the primary path: the owner attaches a photo to a text to the box's number (CH-1), as they would to a friend, wherever they are; it arrives as an MMS from the owner's number once the modem's MMS receive path and its cellular-data bearer are qualified (HW-2); (2) **Wi-Fi upload**, the full-resolution extra: the owner page (CH-7), opened from an upload link the kernel texts when the owner asks for one in an unlocked session, takes one or more photos from the phone's camera or library, with an optional caption; it may ship before or after picture texts and does not gate them. Until then the box does not fetch picture texts; telling the owner that one was not received is the separate silent-drop fix and is not specified here. The cellular-data bearer is used only to fetch from and send to the carrier's MMS service (CH-20), never as an exit for any sandbox. A photo uploaded from that link is task chat from the unlocked session that asked for it (CH-3, CH-7); a picture text needs the same unlocked session as a text, and one that arrives while the session is locked is held with its text part under CH-14, never passed to a guest or model, named in the readback ("and 2 photos"), with at most 10 photos held. [Risk] Carriers compress picture texts, often to about 1 MB, which can make small print unreadable; the Wi-Fi upload keeps full resolution. The route behind a picture text is the box's choice, and it is the carrier's MMS service only (owner's answer 2026-10-17 13:33Z: any route behind the scenes, as long as it stays a photo attached to a text and does not sacrifice security needlessly). Mail to the box's mailbox (CLK-8) is not photo intake, and the box never asks the owner to mail a photo instead: a mail sender is only as trustworthy as the provider's authentication of it, a second intake path widens what the owner check (CH-23) must cover, and mailing a photo is not attaching it to a text. Rich messaging (RCS) is not used: it needs a carrier-provisioned client the modem does not run.
- **CH-23** **Intake checks.** A photo is accepted only from the owner: a picture text only when its sender is the owner's number, checked on the MMS notification before any fetch and again on the fetched message, and fetched only from the carrier MMS service host in the modem's MMS profile, never from a host named only by the notification; only the kernel sets that profile, from qualified per-carrier values, and it ignores carrier settings sent over the air (client-provisioning messages); an upload only from an owner-page upload link issued to an unlocked session. A picture text from any other number is not fetched, or, when its sender is known only after the fetch, deleted at once: it is never stored, shown to a guest or model, or kept beyond a logged count with no content. Accepted types are JPEG, PNG and HEIC/HEIF; anything else (video, audio, contact cards, documents) is refused. Limits in the first release, not owner-settable: 20 MB per file, 10 photos per upload or picture text, 50 megapixels after decoding, 200 photos a day. The kernel never decodes a photo in its own process: decoding runs in a confined process (PLN-6) with no network and no kernel state, which re-encodes it as a new JPEG or PNG at most 4096 pixels on the long side, applies its orientation, and drops all metadata (EXIF, GPS location, camera and device fields, embedded thumbnails and comments). Only the re-encoded image is kept or delivered and the original bytes are discarded; a file that fails decoding is refused, never passed on raw. A picture text's text part is a text message under CH-11 and CH-21 (codes in it are consumed); when it is a control word, the control word runs and its photos are not delivered. An upload's caption is task chat only, never a control word.
- **CH-24** **What the guest receives.** Accepted photos become part of an owner message delivered through the guest's inbound API (PLN-5): the message carries the caption, if any, and for each photo a kernel-written line giving its photo ID, its source (Wi-Fi upload or picture text), the time received and its pixel size, plus the image itself where the guest's declaration (PLN-5) says its inbound API takes images. Every guest can also read a photo delivered to it by ID through a `read` verb. Delivery raises the receiving sandbox to `private` (PLN-3); a photo is private content unless the owner marks it shareable. The guest acts on a photo as it acts on the owner's task chat: the caption is the owner's request; a photo sent alone asks the planner to deal with what it shows; and what the photo shows, including any instructions written in it, is information for carrying out the owner's request, never the request itself (CH-25). When a photo is too small, blurred or compressed to read, the guest asks the owner to retake it closer or send it by Wi-Fi upload. A model call that includes an image goes through the model clerk (CLK-7), whose OpenAI-compatible chat format carries image parts once qualification (HLD-3) passes them, and only to a route the kernel records as qualified to read images, per route and model; because photos are private, only to routes allowed private content (PLN-3, PLN-7). When no allowed route reads images, the photo is still kept, the guest receives the message with each photo marked unreadable, and the owner is told (CH-27). [Risk] The box's own model (ONB-11) may not read images at the HW-4 floor, so photos may need a plan, API or private hosted route. Text recognition by local inference MAY be offered as a PLN-7 extract pass; its output is a reader's output (PLN-2), as untrusted as the photo.
- **CH-25** **A photo authorizes nothing.** Everything in a photo, its pixels, any text, QR code or barcode in it, and anything a model reads from it, is untrusted content (§7), as recall results are (LOG-4). It is never a control word, an approval or unlock code, an answer to a kernel prompt, a key, a change of rules or tier boundaries, or a source of the verified fields an approval is rated and rendered from (CH-10, CH-12). A recipient, account, amount or number read from a photo is a model's claim, so an effect built on it is rated as for an unverified recipient; the kernel verifies it only by its ordinary paths (an existing owner contact, a kernel read of the owner's account, KEY-5, CLK-8). A photo gives the planner no authority beyond the owner's task chat it came with: each effect it leads to still needs its own key or approval, and the approval text shows the kernel's verified fields, never the photo. The kernel never reads codes out of photos; codes count only as typed replies (CH-3). [Risk] A photo can carry a secret the owner did not mean to send (the Owner Card, a recovery key, an authenticator screen). The upload page says once not to send photos of the Owner Card; a code read from a photo never approves anything (CH-6, CH-18); and the photo leaves the box only as PLN-3 allows private content to.
- **CH-26** **Storage and forget.** Accepted photos are a content store (LOG-1): each is kept by content hash, and its receipt, delivery and deletion are log records. A photo is kept for an owner-set number of days (default **30**, as in CH-19) and then deleted by time. On the owner's text asking for them, the box texts an owner-page link whose one step shows the photos with thumbnails, the task each went to and its expiry, and deletes any one or all of them (CH-8). Deleting a photo, by time or by the owner, is a LOG-4 erase, so its derived content (model outputs, recall entries) goes with it and the confirmation names any backup that still holds it. Copies a guest made on its sandbox are not durable state (PLN-6); the confirmation says whether a running sandbox still holds one, and they go with that sandbox's rebuild or a rollback to before the photo arrived. A photo sent to a hosted route is also under that route's own retention, which the route's line on the Wi-Fi page states. Photos count against the RES-4 storage reserve; a photo the store cannot accept is refused, never kept in part.
- **CH-27** **What the owner is told.** An accepted photo needs no receipt of its own: the planner's reply is the conversation (CH-15), and the upload page shows each photo as sent, with its ID. The kernel tells the owner in fixed GSM-7 wording, one segment (CH-12), whenever a photo from them is not delivered: refused for its type, size or count ("I couldn't use the file you sent at 14:03. I take JPEG, PNG or HEIC photos up to 20 MB."), not decodable, not fetched from the carrier, over the held-photo bound while locked, or unreadable because no allowed route reads images ("I got your photo, but none of your models can look at photos yet. Add one at home on my Wi-Fi page: [link]", sent once until such a route is added). Picture texts from other numbers are never texted about; STATUS shows how many were deleted since the last STATUS.

## 11. Owner identity and recovery

### 11.1 Owner identity
- **ID-1** Ownership is proven by the **hardware keys** (two device-bound security keys, the trust anchors of every key, KEY-4), the **phone passkey** (enrolled under them, KEY-4), the **setup secret** (card), the **approval-code seed** (enrolled once in the owner's code generator, plus the paper grid), and the **recovery key**. The owner's phone number is a signal, never sufficient alone.
- **ID-2** No developer key, no first-caller enrollment, no account with the agentOS project.

### 11.2 Recovery
- **REC-1** Recovery key + a backup (if the owner configured one), or the recovery stick, restores everything onto new hardware.
- **REC-2** Restore MUST NOT revive revoked keys or spent budgets: restore enters restricted mode until the owner re-confirms standing keys, and the same visit enrols the edge key (HW-15), so a restore costs one owner-page visit. A restore is an install rooted only in the two hardware keys: it imports what narrows and can be undone, and re-confirms what widens. Every restore cuts every passkey but the two hardware keys, and the grants a cut passkey signed are listed and dropped by default (D-149).
- **REC-3** Lost phone: with a hardware key the owner enrols the new phone's passkey at once and cuts off the old one (KEY-4); without one, with the recovery key the owner enrols a passkey on the new phone, which revokes the old one (KEY-7). Either way the owner re-enrols the code generator; standing keys are re-confirmed as in REC-2. Lost SIM/number: a new SIM plus the recovery key; the new number is announced to the owner's number. Either re-root runs only after the hold window at its longest (KEY-8), announced on every channel (§10), and STOP cancels it (KEY-4). A new holder of an old number can do nothing without codes.
- **REC-4** Every Owner Card secret (setup secret, store passphrase, paper grid, recovery key) can be rotated from the local UI as a tier-4 action, producing a new card to print or copy. Setup offers this in one line, for kits that others may have handled. Rotation protects only against copies made afterwards (STO-2).
- **REC-5** **Compromised restore** (D-149). One switch at restore, *compromised*: it regenerates the sealed-store key, the kernel's keys and the page CA (CH-9); texts the new CA fingerprint on the owner line and prints it on a new Owner Card; revokes the box's grants at providers, then re-consents there and re-fetches account content; holds the erases newer than the backup (LOG-4); and rotates the Owner Card secrets and the code generator (REC-4).

## 12. Onboarding (normative)

### 12.1 Steps
1. Insert the SIM into the modem. Plug in the enforcer between the ISP router, its own Wi-Fi off, and the access point (NET-1 to NET-3), then the controller's power and its cable to the enforcer (HW-12) (and the modem). Power on. No monitor or keyboard. The enforcer and its access point come preloaded or, on the download path, flashed by the same flasher (HW-10). A **preloaded** controller starts here: its claim sticker carries the enforcer's setup network (name, password and a Wi-Fi QR) beside the one-time claim code (HW-10). A **download** controller is first flashed to a stick with the GitHub flasher and installed from it: plug the stick, power and enforcer cable in, power on (about 4 touches); the flasher's one-time QR pairs the phone and the owner confirms the install. Then the **edge** (HW-15), which runs the same signed image (HW-10), preloaded or installed from the same stick before it is removed: plug in its power, its cable into the core's third network port (or a USB Ethernet adapter, HW-12), its internet cable (HW-15) and the second line's modem where there is one, and power on (about 4 more touches, 5 with the modem, and about 2 more on the download path to boot it from the stick). Before the Owner Card confirm the core accepts the key of the edge on that cable as the first edge key (HW-15).
2. Download path only: if the mini PC doesn't boot the stick by itself, a screen and keyboard once to pick it (HW-13). Then remove the stick; it becomes the recovery stick.
3. The box boots with Wi-Fi off (NET-2), connects out through the enforcer and starts the first-boot update (UPD-3) in the background.
4. The phone scans the setup network's Wi-Fi QR (on the claim sticker, or from the flasher) and joins the enforcer's setup network. The enforcer's own page opens by itself (ONB-5), enrols the owner's passkey (NET-4), installs the enforcer's certificate and the core's (CH-9), and shows the Owner network's password and its QR, which the owner saves and confirms by typing its last four characters before rejoining with it (about 11 touches, HW-10). On the Owner network the owner scans the claim QR and confirms (about 4 touches; on the download path the flasher's QR paired the phone at step 1), and the setup page opens by itself. The Owner Card is not shown yet (HW-1). The page shows live progress (ONB-4).
5. Setup page, one screen per step:
   - tap **Text my box**: the phone's messaging app opens with the box's number and a one-time pairing code already filled in, and the owner presses Send. The box learns the owner's number from that message and replies from its own number ("I'm running on [host, RAM]"), proving the number path both ways. The page offers the box's number as a contact card under a suggested made-up name the owner keeps or overwrites in the same screen (CH-21);
   - tap **Add approval codes**: the code generator opens from a standard `otpauth://` link; the owner types the code it shows, which confirms enrollment;
   - **Owner Card:** the box has generated it at first boot (HW-1), with the core's authority fingerprint (CH-9); the page shows it to print (offered first) or save, with a warning that a screenshot or photo is a leak path; the owner confirms one passphrase word and one recovery-key group, and the box then erases its display copy;
   - tear off the recovery sheet and confirm it is stored;
   - the controller is the trusted host (HW-9); no choice is offered. This is allowed only because setup runs on the owner page at home after code enrollment is confirmed, which together satisfy CH-3's approval code plus local confirmation.
   The defaults (spend cap, what needs approval, quiet hours (none until set, CH-15), the liveness interval, loops and sharing on per HLD-4) are stated in one line, changeable any time by text or here.
6. **Connect AI:** one source of intelligence is enough to finish (ONB-3); more can be added later. The page offers, in this order, **Local only** (the box's own model, ONB-11, which needs no account), **Use my plan** (when a qualified provider CLI exists, CLK-7: Codex by default, Grok and Muse once their terms read passes, CLK-7), **Add local compute** (an owner compute host, PLN-7), **Use an API key**, and **Connect a hosted model** (PLN-7), in any combination (D-113). **Use my plan** runs the provider's own sign-in flow: the page shows the provider's link and code from its CLI, the owner approves on the phone in the provider's own page (any code shown is labelled "This code is from <provider>, not from your code generator"), and nothing of the login passes through agentOS's own pages beyond what that flow shows (CLK-7). An API key is entered on the Wi-Fi page. Never by text. After sign-in the screen lists the usage pools the provider CLI's declaration names and the box detected (RES-5), says in one line "I use your plans first and an API key only when a plan runs out" (for an unconfirmed plan without consent, CLK-7, the line instead names it: "I use your plans first, except ChatGPT until you agree below, and an API key only when a plan runs out"), and asks one thing per plan with a default, beside one **Use the defaults** button: "Let this plan see your private content: mail, documents, chats, and anything made from them? Your passwords and keys are never shown to it." (PLN-3). A yes, the default (D-115), makes a class key (PLN-3) per class the plan serves. An unconfirmed plan's consent (CLK-7) is asked right after its sign-in, as its own question outside **Use the defaults**, which never records consent: tapping it leaves that plan unused until the owner agrees. The reserve is not asked here: each pool line carries a setting "Kept for your own use: 0%" (ONB-9, RES-5). If only plans are connected, the page says once: "When a plan's limit runs out, work moves to the box's own model where it can do the task, and otherwise waits for the reset. Add local compute or an API key to keep going; an API key is paid per use." Waits for the first-boot update if it hasn't finished.
7. "All set." Before the first message, once step 6 is done, the Wi-Fi page offers the connection plan (ONB-3) when discovery has found devices, and skips it when it has found none, so the examples and the account suggestions below draw on what was connected there. The first message offers three example tasks and HELP (ONB-8). The Wi-Fi page then offers one optional screen of frequently used accounts, payments first (the payment card, KEY-2, and any payment wallet, CH-10), then the optional mailbox ("Give me an email address? About 1 minute.", CLK-8), each with **Set up** and **Later**. The screen is one **account plan**, built like the device plan (DEV-10): setup offers the owner's email sign-in through a standard OAuth link, and the kernel prepares the plan deterministically, with no planner involved, from the sender and header metadata of the owner's mail (welcome mails, receipts, statements). The sign-in is kernel-only: the token is held under CLK-1, the kernel reads only that metadata, and setup keys no mail read to any planner. The kernel matches only DKIM-authenticated sender domains against a table of known services that it ships, shows only the table's fixed wording and never text from the mail (as DEV-8), and sends each sign-in only to the endpoints the table declares. The plan also draws on the accounts the devices connected in the ONB-3 plan step imply (an EV charger or thermostat implies "Electric utility"). Planners never see the plan. An account found but left unconnected is listed to guests through `resources` (PLN-7) as "exists, not connected", carrying only its service name and kind (no credential, key or data; CLK-1 and CH-10 unchanged), as an unconnected device is with its type and brand (DEV-10) (PLN-3: the list is private content and goes only to callers allowed private content, so routes follow PLN-7's eligibility). Guests may propose combinations that use such an account, and first use triggers connection at step 8. The screen preselects only two kinds of item: those that need the owner at home (password-only sites, which then run one after another in the live browser view, and device pairing), and those that give ongoing data value (accounts whose data feeds proactive notices), each such account with a standard sign-in taking one tap on the provider's consent page, except Gmail. **Gmail** (D-150) goes through the Gmail API with the narrowest grant for the declared use (CLK-9): `gmail.metadata` to watch, `gmail.readonly` to archive, and `gmail.modify` to sort or trim; turning on `gmail.modify` tells the owner the box can then send as them, since that grant also sends. The client is an installed-app client the owner registers by hand in their own Google account, once, from a guided page on their own device; the project never runs one (DEP-2). The box registering it itself is deferred until a second household is planned (D-149). It stays unverified under Google's personal-use exception for apps with fewer than 100 users, so every consent shows Google's "unverified app" warning. Consent happens on the owner's phone and the address it lands on is pasted back on the owner page, so the sign-in is a tap, Google's warning and a paste, after the one-time registration; there is no one-tap return. The client ID and secret are sealed with the token in the mail clerk (CLK-1); they are not confidential by design (RFC 8252 §8.5), and nothing of them is in the image, CI or the repository. IMAP with an app password stays the fallback, labelled *Full power* (DEP-3). The owner never signs in to a provider that offers OAuth inside the box's browser. Everything else is listed unselected, and guests see it as "exists, not connected" and connect it at first use. The payment card (KEY-2) counts as preselected; bank and payment accounts follow the same two-kind rule as everything else and connect read-only like any account. Both connection points stay: during setup and at first use. Everything starts read-only, deeper keys are asked per task at first need (CH-10) with the approval text unchanged, and an account found but not connected stays listed for the owner, because knowing it exists helps. The reason is that a planner cannot reliably tell which accounts would help, and connected accounts make novel combinations more likely. Skipping falls back to step 8. None is part of the minimum path (ONB-3); anything left for later is asked for at step 8.
8. **Connect accounts just-in-time:** when a task first needs an account not set up at step 7, the box texts its OAuth/device-code sign-in link (Gmail, and any provider whose sign-in returns by paste, waits for the owner's next visit home, step 7); sites without OAuth use a password login through the local live view of a credentialed browser, next time the owner is home. A payment card is entered only on the Wi-Fi page, or as a wallet's funding card on the wallet's own page, never by text (CH-6, KEY-2).

- **ONB-1** Steps 1–8 MUST be completable with only the controller and the edge (or, on the download path, two mini PCs plus a stick flashed on any computer), the enforcer and its access point, modem, SIM, and phone; the Owner Card is generated at first boot (HW-1). No external service beyond the carrier and the providers being connected.
- **ONB-2** On the controller agentOS runs entirely from its internal SSD. On any other computer the host's disks are never written (HW-8).
- **ONB-3** **Minimum path.** Setup asks only for: the owner's number (learned from their first text), code-generator enrollment confirmed by one entered code, the recovery sheet acknowledged, and one source of intelligence: a frontier account, a private hosted model, or local only (the box's own model, a compute host, or both; PLN-7). With local only, the setup page and the "All set" text say in plain words which kinds of task the box can take on with what it has, from a list generated from the task classes qualified on the present routes (HLD-4) that changes as qualification changes, and that adding a frontier account or compute host widens them. Everything else starts at a stated default. No account connection is required to finish setup. After step 6 and before "All set" (§12.1 step 7), setup offers one optional step on the Wi-Fi page, skipped when discovery found nothing: the connection plan (DEV-10) as one screen with one tier-4 confirmation, and "Skip for now". It shows only what discovery has found and narrows DEV-10's preselection: of the devices DEV-10 preselects, setup preselects only those that need the owner at home (device pairing) or give ongoing data value (as §12.1 step 7 says for accounts), and lists everything else unselected. Unverified, capture-capable and `actuate-physical` devices stay unselected even when they need pairing. Each unselected device gets a short reason line ("records video", "physical lock", "couldn't verify", "connect when first needed"). Skipping changes nothing else: the STATUS line and the Wi-Fi page path stay.
- **ONB-4** **Visible status without a screen.** The enforcer's setup network MUST open the setup page's way in within a frozen target time after the core's power-on at the floor (HW-4). The setup page shows live progress (booting, updating, ready). The first-boot update (UPD-3) runs in parallel with setup and blocks only AI and account connection.
- **ONB-5** **Phones stay connected.** The enforcer's networks answer phone operating systems' captive-portal checks so its page and then the setup page open by themselves, and the Owner network carries the phone's internet through the enforcer, so the phone does not leave it; the core runs no captive portal or passthrough (D-178). [Risk] Current iOS and Android behavior needs a spike.
- **ONB-6** **No retyping from the card.** Number pairing uses a prefilled `sms:` link and code enrollment an `otpauth://` link. Typing fallbacks (enter the number, type the setup code, scan the seed as a QR) always exist. Setup uses only the phone's existing app handoffs (captive portal to browser, messaging app, code generator, provider sign-in), each qualified and documented for the supported phones; no setup session is transferred between browser contexts, and a return in a different context is another browser session (D-081).
- **ONB-7** **The card is the manual.** The Owner Card carries a quick-start of at most five steps and a "nothing happened" side: how long to wait, the boot key for major PC brands, and "Phone won't join: it needs WPA3" (D-081). On a recovery or flasher stick the quick-start says agentOS leaves the PC's own disks untouched (HW-8). The card states the minimum phone: one that joins WPA3 Wi-Fi (CH-9), on the qualified iOS and Android versions (D-081).
- **ONB-8** **First value.** The "All set" text offers three example tasks suited to what is connected (including any devices connected in the ONB-3 plan step), plus "HELP for commands".
- **ONB-9** **Plans in plain words.** Each connected plan appears on the Wi-Fi page as one line per usage pool, named as the provider names it, with use and reset time ("ChatGPT Codex: 42% of the 5-hour limit, resets 14:00; 81% of the week"). Each pool line carries a setting **Kept for your own use: 0%**, changeable on the page or by text (RES-5's default of 0% is unchanged; it is not asked in onboarding, D-115). The figures are read from the provider's responses on the model clerk's own connection (CLK-7). STATUS by text adds one line when any pool is at the owner's reserve, naming the reset time and whether anything is needed ("ChatGPT plan: at your reserve until 14:00. Nothing to do; work uses the API meanwhile.", or "... work waits until then." without an API route). Failover is told, not asked: the STATUS line is "ChatGPT plan limit reached until 14:00. Used the API instead: $0.42 today." Without an API route the owner gets one text the first time a foreground task waits: "Waiting for your ChatGPT plan to reset at 14:00. Add an API key on my Wi-Fi page to keep going." That text names the cheapest route kind that would help (plan, key, hosted model, or compute host); an API key is the example for a plan-only owner. Spare work waiting for a reset is never texted (HLD-4). A plan whose sign-in stopped working (signed out, plan ended, terms changed) is one STATUS line with what to do, and its route is skipped until fixed (CLK-3). An unconfirmed plan without the owner's consent (CLK-7) says so on its line ("ChatGPT plan paused until you say yes; using your other routes meanwhile. Reply PLAN ChatGPT ON to agree."). A plan a security notice paused (CLK-7) offers no reply command; its line says only an update brings it back ("ChatGPT plan stopped by a security notice; only an update brings it back."). ONB-3's minimum path is unchanged: one source of intelligence (plan, key, hosted model, or local only).
- **ONB-11** **The box's own model ships (D-113).** The box carries one local model per hardware profile: a small model that runs at the HW-4 floor (4 cores, 8 GB, no accelerator), and a larger one on profiles with more memory, the M6 Ultra among them. Its weights are pinned in HW-10's signed weight manifest (hash per model) and written to the box's disk with the image (by the flasher, or by the seller on the preloaded path), so first run and **Local only** setup fetch nothing. The box's own model is a local route (PLN-7): it takes plan fallback (§12.1 step 6) for the task classes it is qualified for (PLN-7, HLD-4), and the owner may swap it only through the signed update path (UPD-1). The box's own model, and an installed workstation (HW-11), count as allowed for private content (PLN-3), so STATUS and the setup page never say private tasks cannot run while one of them serves them. An owner compute host counts as allowed only where the owner allowed it private data (PLN-7).

### 12.1a Adding a workstation or helper app
- **ONB-10** **Workstation (about 3 touches, once):** cable it to port 2, boot it once from an agentOS stick, approve the install on the phone and remove the stick. **Helper app (about 3 touches, once):** open the link the phone gives on that computer and install the app, approve "add helper" on the phone, and for a gaming PC turn on Wake-on-LAN once in the firmware setup. Steady state is zero touch: signed updates with A/B rollback (UPD-1), key rotation, wake and sleep are automatic. Adding a helper is a tier-4 action (CH-3), and the page says in plain words what it will see ("Gaming PC: helps with cleared work only", "Workstation: will see your private data").

### 12.2 A moved core SSD
The first release has no supported move of the controller's SSD; a lost or replaced controller is recovered with the recovery stick, the recovery key on the phone and the off-box backup (REC-1–3). An SSD that is nonetheless moved is an unknown host, and the unlock below holds as the security basis (STO-2, HW-5a). Once it is plugged into another PC, the box texts "Unknown host [model]. Open this link at home to unlock: [link]." The owner scans the store passphrase from the Owner Card on the local page (or types it) and enters an approval code there (STO-2). This unlocks the session only. Making the PC trusted is a separate tier-4 action (CH-3, STO-2): an approval code plus local confirmation on the owner page, or the recovery key through the hold window (REC-3). The setup-time default in §12.1 does not carry over to this flow.

### 12.3 Daily use
Text or call. Batched approvals carry evidence and take one reply (CH-12, CH-13). STATUS shows what is open and what happened; only urgent texts and a short liveness line arrive unasked (CH-15). STOP always works; RESUME restarts (CH-11). Owner-facing text says **key** for "runs without asking" and **needs approval** for the rest; "key" is an internal term.

### 12.4 DIY install (flasher)
The download path of HW-10 is the DIY path: flash the image from any computer. First boot generates the card contents (HW-1) and shows them on the phone for the owner to print or save. Same flow afterwards.

## 13. Local devices and home control

### 13.1 Local devices

Devices on the owner's home network (sensors, switches, thermostats, locks, cameras, speakers, displays, printers, storage, self-hosted services; vehicles and fitness and health trackers are devices too, DEV-5) are services reached through clerks like any other (§6). Two things differ: an effect can be physical, and an output can reach people other than the owner. So the class of a device operation follows its physical consequence and its audience, never the device's or its declaration's claims, and the home network itself is treated as untrusted.

- **DEV-1** **Device clerks.** A device clerk is a clerk (§6; CLK-1, CLK-6, KRN-2) for one credential domain: one hub's local API, the box's own Matter fabric, or one directly connected device. Planners drive it only through declared device operations (DEV-4); it offers no raw socket, shell, or protocol passthrough. It reaches only the pinned endpoints of its own devices (DEV-2, DEV-3), never the internet; a device's vendor cloud is a separate service (§6) through its clerk's own connection. It runs only while it has work or an event subscription the owner turned on, admitted under RES-2 like any component; when it cannot run, KRN-7 applies. Device stacks are mature components: a Matter controller built on the Connectivity Standards Alliance SDK, a hub's documented local API (e.g. Home Assistant), HomeKit (HAP), IPP Everywhere for printers, SMB or NFS for storage, ONVIF and RTSP for cameras. **Device drivers** (D-189). A vendor's protocol code MAY live in a private repository as a device driver: its own clerk process (CLK-1) on the core, reached only through the public driver slot, declaring DEV-4 operations only and linked only to its pinned devices (DEV-2, DEV-3); the enforcer runs no driver. It is reviewed as public code is and passes the slot's public conformance tests. The image's release manifest names each driver by repository, commit and hash, and the log and STATUS name it (KRN-2); the public release ships none. Each stack and device declaration is qualified through §8 (HLD-3), with SDK example devices or recorded device traffic as the demo environment. The Matter fabric's root key stays in the sealed store and signs only during a tier-4 connection (DEV-10); the clerk holds only its own operational key. Radio devices (Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread) are reached through the enforcer's radios (NET-2) or the owner's hub or border router (§17).
- **DEV-2** **Home-network boundary.** The home network is untrusted, and what is on it is private (PLN-3).
  - **Out.** No component reaches a non-global address (the IANA special-purpose registries: loopback, RFC 1918, 100.64.0.0/10, link-local, unique-local fc00::/7, multicast, 0.0.0.0/8, 240.0.0.0/4, and their IPv4-mapped and NAT64-embedded forms), any prefix of the box's own uplink (global IPv6 prefixes included, widened to the enclosing /56, because home devices often hold global IPv6 addresses), or a router address the box learns (its gateway, and its external address where NAT-PMP or PCP reports it). There are exactly five exceptions: a device clerk to its own devices' pinned endpoints; the clerk's connection to a compute host's declared endpoint (PLN-7); the kernel's resolver to the resolver the network assigns (DHCP or RDNSS), DNS only, with every answer still checked at connect time; the kernel's enforcer channel to the enforcer's pinned endpoint (NET-2), fixed-schema device commands, tightenings and reads only, checked by the enforcer against its own safety list, never a rule change, an unlock or a disarm, the only way agentOS reaches the enforcer and never a destination for a planner; and a kernel component to an endpoint the owner pinned on the local page (tier 4), an off-box backup target (BAK-1) or a private hosted route (PLN-7), over TLS or a pinned key, reachable by a planner only as a routed model call to a hosted route's declared inference operations (PLN-7, CLK-3), never as a destination. The host's own link configuration (DHCP, router and neighbour discovery) and the kernel's external-address query to the learned gateway (NAT-PMP or PCP, that query only) are not connections under this rule. The rule covers planner sandboxes of every label, credentialed browsers (navigation, redirects, and subresources), clerks' declared origins, provider CLIs, and local inference. The kernel checks the address actually connected, after resolution and on every redirect, so DNS rebinding fails; names resolve only through the kernel.
  - **In.** The box accepts no connection initiated from the home network, except from a connected device's pinned address to its own device clerk, inside that protocol's authenticated session (e.g. Matter reports and check-ins). On the edge, an online everyday device's relay accepts only replies on connections the edge starts (NET-2). CH-9 holds.
  - **Discovery** is passive only (mDNS and DNS-SD, SSDP, Matter discovery), in a sandbox with no other network access; the box never scans. The box advertises only what a connected protocol requires, and never the kernel, the local UI, or a guest. Full discovery results go only to the owner (DEV-10). Planners never see the plan, addresses or network layout; they learn of an unconnected device only through the `resources` listing (PLN-7), which carries its type and brand and nothing else; the brand is the Matter vendor ID mapped through a shipped vendor table, otherwise "unknown brand".
- **DEV-3** **Device identity.** A device is pinned at connection by a cryptographic identity, never its address: Matter device attestation and operational certificate, a HomeKit accessory key, a TLS certificate key, or an SSH host key. A mismatch is refused and logged, the device is off (KRN-7), and only the owner re-pins it, on the local page (tier 4, CH-3). A device without a cryptographic identity, such as a Bluetooth tracker proved only by a shared key, is **unverified**: the local page says so and that anyone impersonating it on the home network can capture its credential; it gets public content only (DEV-6) and never `actuate-physical` (DEV-5), and its readings are marked unverified, so no owner rule treats them as urgent (DEV-8).
- **DEV-4** **Operations and verbs.** Each device operation maps to one verb (VRB-2): `read` (state, sensors, files), `actuate` and `actuate-physical` (DEV-5), `present` (DEV-6), and for administration the existing verbs: configuration, firmware, users, and remote access are `change-account`; reset and removal are `delete-remote`; adding a controller or user, creating a lock code or guest access, and opening a commissioning window are `reveal-or-create-secret` (VRB-1). The class comes from a kernel-shipped table over standard device types (Matter device types and clusters; HomeKit services; a hub's entity domains), never from the declaration. Driver operations are classed instead by class rows in the release manifest (DEV-1), reviewed by Security apart from driver code; each declared verb must equal its row, and an operation without one is refused. Hub domains are hub configuration, so a hub entity whose underlying device the hub reports as a switch, plug, or relay, or reports no type for, is a generic switch whatever its domain (a hub can expose a heater plug as a light). A device type missing from the table is classed conservatively until a box update adds it or the owner assigns it a listed type at the local page (tier 4): reads are `read`, responses carrying audio, images, or video are capture (DEV-7), and every write is `actuate-physical` with no secure direction. A generic switch or plug is `actuate-physical`, with no secure direction, until the owner names its load at connection as one that cannot heat, open, or unlock anything; a fridge, pump, or medical device is not safe to turn off. Each operation declares its wire shape (CLK-3: for HTTP, host, method, path, and body schema; for a hub, service and entity; for Matter, cluster, command, and attribute; for HomeKit, service and characteristic), with arguments limited to declared enumerations and numeric ranges wherever the device allows; the clerk sends nothing else. [Inference] Hub tokens are usually account-wide, so a hub's reach is what the declared shapes allow, not its token. Device credentials (fabric, operational and HomeKit keys, hub tokens, device passwords, lock codes) fall under the custody rule (CLK-1): held in the sealed store, used only inside the clerk. Clerk output passes CLK-4, and an address or stream URL that embeds a credential is never returned to a planner.
- **DEV-5** **Physical effects.**
  - **`actuate`** is reversible, as `organize` is (VRB-2): an operation maps to it only if its declaration names the inverse, the kernel logs the prior state with each effect, and the target value lies inside bounds the owner confirmed at connection (e.g. thermostat 18 to 23 °C, lights at any level). Effects are bounded (an owner-set daily count per device, default 50), counted in STATUS, and undone by `UNDO <id>` (CH-16), which restores a device only if its state still matches what the planner set. A value outside the bounds is `actuate-physical`.
  - **`actuate-physical`** covers locks, doors, garage doors, gates, alarm and sensor arming, heat sources, water and gas valves, devices that move under their own power (robot vacuums and mowers, robot arms, printer axes), any device the owner or the table marks as safety or access, and any value outside `actuate` bounds. Its **secure direction** (lock, close, arm, turn off, shut off, stop, pause, cancel, return to dock) MAY be covered by a standing key (KEY-5); without a key, each such action is a low-risk effect (CH-10), except a value outside bounds. Turning off a device the table marks safety or access (an alarm, a camera) is its unsafe direction, and a generic switch or plug has no secure direction until its load is named (DEV-4). For a value outside bounds, the table names each setpoint type's secure direction (e.g. lowering a heating setpoint), which only a key covers; without one it is a high-risk effect, and where the table names none, both directions are (cutting heat below the owner's floor can freeze pipes or harm an occupant). Its **unsafe direction** (unlock, open, disarm, silence, turn on a heat source, open a valve) is a high-risk effect, approved per action by a code-generator code on the owner page (CH-10): never batched (CH-13), never covered by a standing key, and never a cancelable submit (VRB-3). A device's own safety interlocks and warnings (e.g. a printer's thermal-runaway protection) are never overridden or disabled. A key (KEY-5) the owner sets at the local page (tier 4, CH-3) MAY move a self-moving device's start, never a drone's (NET-2), into `actuate` inside an owner-set schedule, with return to dock as its inverse (e.g. a vacuum on weekdays 10:00 to 16:00). A job file sent to a device (e.g. G-code) is free-form, so its approval text states the file's name, estimated duration, and peak temperatures as parsed from the file, labelled best effort; the code approves that file, not its safety.
  - **Fitness and health trackers** (Oura, Whoop, Garmin, Fitbit, Dexcom and similar, and medical devices' data such as a CGM, blood-pressure cuff or scale) are in scope as devices and accounts reached through the vendor cloud API or website (CLK-3), or read-only over the enforcer's Bluetooth (DEV-12, NET-2); reads are `read` and the data is private (PLN-3). Controlling a medical device is out of scope (§17).
  - **Vehicles** are devices (D-136). A vehicle is reached through its vendor cloud as a service (§6) in CLK-3's order (e.g. the vendor's fleet API, then web, then app); it is cellular, so the enforcer cannot reach it directly (NET-1), and the core's owner-signed destination list is the gate (NET-2). Reads (location, charge, status) are `read`. Unlock, remote start, climate, horn and lights, trunk, and movement (e.g. Summon) are `actuate-physical`. Each one's unsafe direction (unlock, open the trunk, remote start, climate on, horn and lights, movement) is a high-risk effect (CH-10), approved per action, never batched and never covered by a standing key; its secure direction (lock, close the trunk, stop, climate off) follows DEV-5. The table marks a vehicle safety or access (DEV-10).
- **DEV-6** **Output to people.** `present` (print, speak, display, cast, play, notify) is irreversible. Each output device has an **audience** the owner sets at connection: `owner` (only the owner sees or hears it), `household` (the default), or `public`. Public content MAY go to any keyed output device. Private content goes only to `owner` devices; to a `household` or `public` device it is a disclosure effect (KRN-4), unless a KEY-5 key with a template covers it. Deterministic rules (quiet hours, schedules) MAY narrow a device's audience, never widen it. Sound obeys quiet hours (CH-15).
- **DEV-7** **Capture.** An operation whose response carries audio, images, or video is **capture**, as the clerk reads the response type, whatever the declaration says. Capture is off for each device until the owner turns it on at the local page (tier 4), which states whether it is continuous or on request, and that recording other people may need their consent under local law. Raw capture is private and never leaves the box, except to a compute host allowed private data (PLN-7) or to a route the owner allowed capture for (default none). It never enters recall (LOG-4), and it is deleted at task end unless the owner set a retention. Content derived from it (a transcript, a count, a description) is ordinary private data, except a face or voice template, a model's encoding that can recognise a person. A template is kept only for a person the owner enrolled at the local page (tier 4), under raw capture's rules, and un-enrolling destroys it; any other face or voice is encoded only in memory to compare with those templates, and that encoding is never kept, compared with anything else or searched for.
- **DEV-8** **Events.** Device readings and events enter the event bus (KRN-1) as private, untrusted content: never control words, task chat, approvals, or instructions (as CLK-8), with device-supplied text (names, labels, messages) quoted. Each source is rate-limited, and the excess is coalesced and counted, so a flood cannot delay foreground work (RES-1); event-triggered work runs at most as `accepted work`. Neither a device nor a planner can make anything urgent: urgency (CH-15) comes only from the owner's deterministic rules over verified devices (e.g. "smoke alarm reports smoke: call me"). An event starts work, never an irreversible effect; that still needs its own intent.
- **DEV-9** **Cloud-visible devices.** Each device is marked `local-only` or `cloud-visible`, from its qualification (DEV-1); unknown counts as cloud-visible. Whatever reaches a cloud-visible device reaches its vendor, and its state is a channel that anyone with access to that cloud account can read. So from a `private` sandbox, an operation on a cloud-visible device carries only declared enumerations and ranges (no free text, CLK-3) and counts under PLN-3's research-query size and rate bounds; free text or private content sent to it is a disclosure effect unless the owner allowed that vendor private data. Device reads are private content (PLN-3), so a `public` sandbox never reads state a `private` sandbox set.
  - **Evidence.** The box cannot observe another device's traffic (DEV-2), so a device is `local-only` only on evidence: its qualification (which records whether the device's own cloud setting is effective), or an owner-signed enforcer policy that blocks its vendor cloud (NET-2, NET-4), only for a device labelled `contained` (NET-1) whose allow list names no vendor or shared-CDN destination, which the enforcer's summaries (untrusted input, NET-5) confirm each session; that policy is a NET-4 tightening, which on a device on the enforcer's safety list needs an owner passkey assertion that the enforcer verifies (NET-2, NET-4). What a device or its vendor reports about its setting never counts by itself; the clerk's read-back each session only confirms a cutoff that qualification shows is effective. The page shows the evidence for each mark; anything else is cloud-visible.
  - **Cutting off.** Cutting a device off from its vendor cloud (its own setting, a `change-account` operation) reduces exposure, so it is a secure direction: on a device the table does not mark safety or access, a low-risk texted code (CH-10) that MAY be covered by a standing key; otherwise a code-generator code, since cutting off an alarm or camera also stops its phone alerts. Connection offers "keep vendor cloud off where the device supports it". Turning a vendor cloud back on is tier 4. A disclosure prompt for a device whose qualification shows its cloud setting effective offers `BLOCK <id> <code>` (CH-11), which cuts it off and, once the clerk reads the setting back, performs the action as `local-only` where CH-10 then makes it low risk (audience `household`); otherwise BLOCK only cuts the device off, and the action asks again at its own tier. The prompt carries a texted code only where one may cut that device off, and that code approves only this path, never the disclosure. The page states the cost: the vendor's app, remote access, and voice control stop, and firmware updates that arrive through the cloud stop too; STATUS names a cut-off device that reports outdated firmware, for as long as it does (KRN-7). Some devices stop working without their cloud, which qualification records.
- **DEV-10** **Connecting a device and its keys.** Device keys follow CH-3:

| Step | Proof |
|---|---|
| Connect devices or a hub from a connection plan (Matter by the multi-admin pairing code from the owner's existing app or the device, HomeKit by its HomeKit code, typed on the local page, never sent by text, CH-6) | Tier 4 (CH-3), once for the whole plan |
| Connect a verified device found by discovery that needs no secret (paired by a button on the device or a pinned key); only non-capture `read` is turned on | Code-generator code on the owner page, opened by `CONNECT <id>` from its STATUS line (CH-10) |
| Turn on capture, widen `actuate` bounds or an audience, re-pin a device, turn a vendor cloud back on, key `change-account`, `delete-remote`, or `reveal-or-create-secret` on a device | Tier 4 (CH-3) |
| Key `actuate` or `present` on, or cut off from its vendor cloud (DEV-9), a connected device the table does not mark safety or access; present private content on a `local-only` device whose audience is `household` (DEV-6); a secure-direction action no key covers, other than a value outside bounds (DEV-5) | Texted code in reply (CH-10 low risk; batchable, CH-13), inside the owner's passkey-signed device envelope (KEY-4) |
| Key `actuate-physical`, or key `actuate` or `present` on or cut off from its vendor cloud a device the table marks safety or access, at the planner's request rendered by the kernel in fixed wording | Code-generator code on the owner page (CH-10 high risk; batchable, CH-13, except an `actuate-physical` key) |
| Unsafe-direction physical action, or a value outside `actuate` bounds that no key covers (DEV-5) | Code-generator code on the owner page per action, never batched |
| Revoke a key or disconnect a device | One text from the owner's number |

  Discovery results appear as one STATUS line ("Found a printer and 3 lights. Connect them at home on my Wi-Fi page: [link]"). A device that `CONNECT` can connect gets its own STATUS line, naming it by its type and the first 8 characters of its pinned key's fingerprint, never by a device-supplied name (DEV-8), with an ID unique among open requests (CH-12) that stays open as long as discovery still finds the device, but a `CONNECT <id> <code>` prompt expires 24 hours after it is issued; discovery that still finds the device then issues a new ID. Before the owner visits, the kernel prepares a **connection plan** deterministically from discovery, the class table, and the names and rooms a connected hub reports: each device's name, identity status, class, `actuate` bounds from the type's table defaults, audience from an owner-set room map (default `household`), and local-only or cloud-visible. Verified devices with neither capture nor `actuate-physical` are preselected; unverified, capture-capable, and `actuate-physical` devices are listed separately and unselected. One tier-4 confirmation connects the selection, and connecting a hub brings in every device behind it. Each unselected device carries a short reason line ("records video", "physical lock", "couldn't verify", "connect when first needed"). The plan is also offered as an optional step of setup, before "All set" (ONB-3), where setup only narrows the preselection above, never widens it. Planners never see the plan; an unconnected device or account reaches them only as the `resources` listing (DEV-2, PLN-7), which gives no reach, since the enforcer still blocks every unconnected device (NET-3). A listed device is connected only through the plan above: the guest's answer to the owner's own request names the device, and connecting goes through the existing Wi-Fi page plan and STATUS line, with CH-8's single link text and no new owner text. [Inference] Matter devices still need one pairing code each, unless the owner's ecosystem supports Matter fabric synchronisation, whose support varies. Connection lists the device's operations in fixed wording by class; non-capture `read` is on and every other class off by default. A key unused for 60 days (owner-set) appears in STATUS with `REVOKE <id>` until it is used or revoked. `resources` (PLN-7) lists connected devices by kind (sensor, actuator, output, storage, service), audience, verified or unverified, and local-only or cloud-visible, with whether the caller's label may use each, and never an address or credential. The proposers rank device routes only within keyed ones (HLD-4). An unreachable or identity-failed device that a rule or task depends on is named in STATUS (KRN-7).
- **DEV-13** **Control that survives the vendor** (D-149). For each safety device and car, the graph records whether it works without its vendor cloud (DEV-9) and whether it has a manual override (a key, a switch, a valve), as declaration fields (CLK-14). STATUS lists the safety devices that lack an override or need their vendor cloud. The connection plan (DEV-10) prefers devices the box's own Matter fabric controls locally.
- **DEV-11** **Storage and self-hosted services.** A NAS or self-hosted service (a file, photo, or media server) is connected as a device. Reading is `read`. Writing a new file into an owner-confirmed folder that only the owner can access is `organize` (its inverse deletes that file); overwriting or deleting is `delete-remote`; a folder that is shared, synced to another party, or reachable from the internet is `share` (VRB-2).
- **DEV-12** **Reverse engineering for interoperability.** The owner MAY ask the planner to work out how a device they own communicates, studying the device and the app that controls it, so the box can reach the device where no documented interface or existing declaration does. The product is an ordinary declaration; DEV-1, DEV-2, and DEV-8 apply to it unchanged. [Decision: Mark, 2026-10-18: "This is a high leverage tool to personalize and modify all home software to own requirements".]
  - **Approval per task, on the local page.** Each task is its own approval, never per service, never batched (CH-13), and never covered by a standing key (KEY-5), because its legality depends on the target's licence and the owner's jurisdiction, which the box cannot determine (PLN-6, D-107). The owner starts it on the local page: they upload the input files there, state the purpose as PLN-6 requires, and confirm in the same step (CH-3); text or voice never starts or approves it. The confirmation is shown in fixed wording from what the owner supplied: the device, each input file's name and digest, and that studying it for interoperability must be lawful where the owner lives, which is the owner's responsibility. PLN-6's limits apply unchanged: a task the law clearly rules out is unavailable, copy protection is not circumvented without an explicit exemption, and a third-party service is never a target.
  - **Offline sandbox.** The task runs in a fresh analysis sandbox (PLN-6), labelled `private` (PLN-3), with no network access, no home-network path (DEV-2), no device or clerk, no recall, and no other workspace. Its only inputs are the files the owner uploaded for that task on the local page, each with its digest in the approval: traffic captures the owner recorded of their own device, firmware images, app binaries, and documentation. A file the planner found elsewhere (in mail, storage, or a workspace) is never an input. Before a capture enters, the kernel redacts every sealed store value found in it (CLK-4) and every match of CLK-4's fixed-pattern detector; the confirmation says that a capture can still hold passwords neither finds and that the planner sees whatever remains. Model calls go only to routes the private label allows (PLN-7), and the owner MAY limit a task to the box's own model. **Media.** Audio, images, or video in a capture are raw capture (DEV-7): a call carrying them goes only to the box's own model, a compute host allowed private data (PLN-7), or a route the owner allowed capture for (default none). The sandbox is discarded at task end, and the uploaded files with it unless the owner set a retention; only the extracted exchanges, media payloads stripped, are kept as the qualification record.
  - **The output is a declaration, data only.** The task's only product is a draft declaration for that device (CLK-3, DEV-4): in a wire shape the device clerk already implements (DEV-4), with the recorded exchanges that support each operation. It holds no code: a device whose protocol needs an encoder the clerk lacks is filed as a box-update candidate, which ships only as a box update (DEP-4) through §8 and its review, never as a DEV-12 output. It declares only pinned local endpoints (DEV-3) of a connected device served by a device clerk (DEV-1), never an internet host; a vendor cloud's private interface is never a DEV-12 output. It holds no credential value: each credential is a sealed store slot the owner fills on the local page (CLK-1, CH-6), and qualification refuses a declaration with a constant that matches a captured secret or a CLK-4 pattern. It is adopted only through §8 like any declaration (HLD-3), including one the owner wrote off the box and uploads on the local page, which obeys every rule here: qualified in replay on the extracted exchanges as its demo environment (DEV-1, HLD-3); classed from the kernel's table, never from the draft (VRB-2, DEV-4); and keyed under DEV-10. CLK-3's first-live-runs rule applies in full, reads included, to every operation whose effect the captures do not show, so to every operation of an uploaded declaration with no captures. A device type missing from DEV-4's table makes every write `actuate-physical` with no secure direction, so until the owner assigns a listed type each write asks for a code-generator code; the owner's guide says so. At runtime the device is reached only through its clerk's declared operations: neither the analysis sandbox nor the clerk running the declaration ever gets a raw socket, shell, or protocol passthrough (DEV-1).
  - **Changing firmware or software is a separate step.** Writing modified firmware or software to a connected device or self-hosted service (DEV-11) is never part of the task or declaration. It goes only through that device's clerk, as a firmware operation the clerk declares (`change-account`, DEV-4), and needs both the device's `change-account` key (tier 4, CH-3, DEV-10) and, for each write, a code-generator code on the owner page opened from that request (CH-3, CH-11), never batched or covered by a standing key. The box never patches software on a computer that is not a connected device (DEV-2). The owner builds the image off the box and uploads it on that owner page; a planner or sandbox never supplies it, and the clerk writes exactly the bytes whose digest the approval names. Before the write, the clerk reads the current image back and the kernel checks it is a complete, restorable backup; without one, the write is refused. The approval text names the device and the digests of the new image and backup, and warns that a failed write can leave the device unusable. A device's own safety interlocks are never disabled (DEV-5).
  - **Nothing derived is published.** The declaration, the sandbox's notes, and everything else the task produces are private-derived (PUB-1, HLD-3). Only a clean-room re-creation (PUB-1), built without the captures, images, binaries, or the derived declaration, may be published through §15.

### 13.2 Home control through an enforcer

The owner can control the home's software and its data flows, in and out, to the extent they choose: which devices talk to which, which vendors they reach, which updates they take, and what software they run. **Rule 0:** an attack that works with agentOS present must also have worked without it; the security the home has without agentOS is the floor. The control is held by a separate deterministic box, the **enforcer**. agentOS has no network path to the enforcer's rules: it sends the enforcer only fixed-schema device commands, closed-list tightenings and reads, and every rule change reaches the enforcer through the owner's phone, on the enforcer's own page at home (NET-6) (Mark's decisions 2026-10-18 15:45Z, 15:48Z, 16:09Z, 16:14Z, 16:25Z and 17:50Z).

- **NET-1** **Scope and limits.** NET covers only devices the owner declares as owned. Household members' own phones and computers are not inspected; guests are isolated and never observed (NET-3). Full coverage is not promised, and the setup texts and the enforcer's page say so in these words:
  - Encrypted traffic is blocked or allowed by destination only (address, and the TLS server name while visible), never by content. Encrypted Client Hello (RFC 9849) hides the server name, and allowing by name over-permits where names share a CDN address [Fact].
  - A cloud-only device loses the function its cloud provides when that cloud is blocked, unless it gets a DEV-12 declaration or is replaced; qualification (DEV-1) records which functions need the cloud.
  - Some devices cannot be controlled without replacing them. Cellular devices, Amazon Sidewalk, and Thread, Zigbee or Bluetooth links that do not pass through the enforcer's radios are outside network reach, and Bluetooth going straight from a device to a phone app stays outside the system.
  - A device on the ISP router's own Wi-Fi or LAN is outside NET and at the floor (Rule 0). Setup checks that the router's Wi-Fi is off, and no enforcer segment reaches the router's admin or LAN addresses.
  - Each device carries a label the page shows: `contained`, `contained except radio`, or `not containable`, from qualification and the enforcer's observation, never from what the device reports.
- **NET-2** **The enforcer.** The enforcer is its own box, never the core (Mark, 2026-10-18 15:48Z): a small multi-port computer with Thread and Bluetooth radios (Zigbee later, D-231), running an OpenWrt-class system, placed between the ISP router (unchanged, in front, its own Wi-Fi off) and the home network. It holds the current owner-signed policy and checks every change itself (NET-4).
  - **Three boxes.** agentOS runs on two boxes, the edge and the core, beside the enforcer, the ISP router and one access point (Mark, 2026-10-18 17:50Z: "Okay implement this"). The edge carries web fetches and the second line (CLK-8) and reaches neither the enforcer nor any home device except the online everyday devices it relays (Device paths); it runs no planner and holds no private data, credential or model, and for the second line it holds only the modem and SIM, while any calling-account credential (CLK-8) and every transcript stay on the core. The core's and the edge's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are off in firmware or removed from first boot, and their image runs no access point, Wi-Fi client or captive portal, so neither box bypasses the enforcer by radio (D-178). The enforcer holds the home's rules, and the core cannot change them.
  - **Segments.** Each agentOS box's internet port is on its own enforcer segment (HW-12). The edge's segment reaches the internet and the control ports of the online everyday devices it relays; the core and the edge talk only over a direct cable from the edge to a third core network port (or a USB network adapter) used for nothing else (HW-12), and the enforcer drops all traffic between their segments. The enforcer neither sees nor logs what crosses that cable, and the edge's fetches reach any internet address, so a compromised core can send what it holds out through fetches it asks the edge to make; this is a stated residual (risk 29). The core serves its owner page only on its enforcer-facing port, never on the edge's cable, and the enforcer lets only the Owner network and its wired admin port reach that page (CH-9). The core reaches only:
    - the internet destinations the kernel declares, with TLS ending on the core: credentialed endpoints (CLK-5, CLK-3), signed updates (UPD-1, DEP-4), cloud models where the owner uses them (PLN-7) and the backup target (BAK-1);

  - **The core's list is signed.** The core's destinations are an owner-signed list on the enforcer, and adding one is a loosening (NET-4), signed together with the clerk, device or helper it serves. The enforcer resolves each name with its own resolver and allows only the addresses and ports that answer returns, so matching by name carries NET-1's limits (Encrypted Client Hello, shared CDN addresses). Apart from an offline camera's or microphone's stream port, the core has no network path to any device on the safety list; it reaches those only through channel commands the enforcer checks.
  - **Device paths** (Mark, 2026-10-18 18:34Z: "Three paths"). Each IP device takes one path, set by its risk and its internet access, and the enforcer blocks device-to-device traffic on all three, except a pair the owner signed (NET-3, NET-4):

    - an offline everyday device, one the enforcer gives no internet, talks to the core directly with full features, on connections the core starts to the device's control port, which the enforcer logs.
  - **Cameras and microphones.** A device whose data is not low-sensitivity never takes the edge path. agentOS uses only cameras and microphones that work offline (Mark, 2026-10-18 18:33Z): their settings go only through the enforcer, and their video and audio go straight to the core on connections it starts to their stream port, decoded in a sandbox. A cloud-only camera or microphone stays outside agentOS: it keeps its vendor app, is declared to the enforcer only, its video and audio never reach the edge or the core, and the enforcer can cut its internet, which is a tightening (NET-4).
  - **Fails safe to the last signed policy.** If the core is down, silent, or sends malformed or excessive commands, the enforcer keeps enforcing the last owner-signed policy and its status light and page say so.   - **The channel.** The enforcer and agentOS share no kernel, secret or update source, and neither updates the other. The core reaches the enforcer only through the kernel's enforcer channel (DEV-2), which carries KRN-12's heartbeat and three fixed-schema kinds of message: everyday commands to radio devices (on, off, level, lock), tightenings from NET-4's closed list, and reads of device state (with any device challenge and the core's answer, sent only in the enforcer's fixed-length frame) and traffic summaries. The enforcer parses them strictly, with no login, shell or file access, and rate-limits them; a burst pauses the channel until the owner resumes it on the enforcer's page. No channel command unlocks, disarms, arms or changes a rule (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:14Z). **Rule changes travel only through the owner's phone:** agentOS texts the owner a link that carries the proposed change, the phone opens it on the enforcer's own page, the enforcer renders it in plain words, and the owner signs it with their passkey (NET-4); proposal links are rate-limited the same way [Inference]. Planners never reach the enforcer, and agentOS holds no enforcer admin credential or recovery code. agentOS compares the traffic summaries with the signed policy and reports a mismatch (KRN-7); the enforcer logs every command and proposed change, and agentOS cannot suppress its light or page.
  - **Safety devices.** The enforcer keeps its own owner-signed list of devices with a safety or access role (the DEV-10 classes), never taken from agentOS. It includes every lock, alarm, siren and sensor, anything with a camera or microphone, garage doors, water and gas valves, smart plugs and relays, heaters and ovens (Mark, 2026-10-18 18:34Z), and a device of unknown type or on no list counts as safety. Of these, only cameras and microphones must work offline (Cameras and microphones); any other safety-list device reaches only its allow list (NET-3). The core cannot change anything that could unlock, disarm or harm in the home. For a listed device the channel carries only reads, lock and relock for a lock, and for any other device only the one secure-direction command (DEV-5) that the enforcer's owner-signed list names that device as safe for: shut for a valve, off for a heater, oven, plug or relay, close for a garage door. Primary heating and any valve that feeds sprinklers, heating or a medical supply cannot be named; a garage door can be named only if it has obstruction reversal and a pre-close warning; an oven can be named. A device not so named, and every device of unknown type, gets reads only. A camera, microphone, alarm, siren or sensor gets no command on the channel, except a drone's return to dock (Drones): turning it off is unsafe (DEV-5), and arming it is kept off the channel as a conservative choice, since arming while people are home can stop its phone alerts (NET-4). The enforcer texts the owner whenever a channel command turns off a heater or shuts a valve. Every other command or tightening of it, and every turn-on or open, reaches the enforcer only as a link the owner signs (NET-4), since an ask checked only on agentOS would not survive its compromise.
  - **Drones** (D-190). A drone is a camera that flies: it is on the safety list, its dock has no vendor cloud, and its video reaches the core as an offline camera's does. Its flight controller runs open firmware holding the flight limits from the enforcer's owner-signed policy: a boundary inside the owner's land, a height ceiling, no-fly zones, named routes, and a return to dock on low battery, a lost link or a breach. Only the enforcer holds the key that signs the drone's command link, and the controller refuses an unsigned command, setting or firmware, so the core can neither fly it nor change its limits; adding a route, widening a limit or updating its flight firmware is a loosening (NET-4). A flight is a turn-on (NET-6): the owner signs each one, on one named route, and no key covers a drone's start. A flight may follow an animal of a kind its signature names, off the route but inside the boundary, steered only by its own vision; it never follows a person, and no planner, clerk or core output adds a waypoint. The channel's one drone command is return to dock, its secure direction, which needs no naming. A drone whose flight firmware cannot hold these limits stays outside agentOS.
  - **Confined radio stacks.** Anyone in radio range can reach the radio code without a password, so each stack runs as its own unprivileged, confined process with one narrow command interface, which the channel's commands also go through, and no access to the rules.
  - **Hardening.** The enforcer runs a signed, read-only system image with A/B updates and secure boot, a firmware password, only the services it needs (firewall, its page and the radios, and DNS and DHCP on the networks it serves other than the edge's segment), and its unused USB ports and Wi-Fi radio off. It installs signed security fixes automatically and rolls back an update whose self-check fails; changes to features or to the policy engine need the owner's key. A hardware watchdog restarts a stuck enforcer. Its factory reset needs a code printed inside its case, keeps the current policy, and allows no loosening until new owner keys are enrolled.
  - **Texts and calls.** The enforcer reaches the owner only by text and phone call, and only when the owner needs to act (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:05Z: "Text + call and only when needed"): a text for an ask or an alert that needs action (an unusual-time unlock or disarm, a recovery-code use, an enrolment, a paused channel), and a call for an urgent safety event (an alarm triggered, a door forced, repeated failed unlock attempts) [Inference: which events call is a recommendation]. Everything else, routine unlocks included, is only logged on the enforcer; there is no digest (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:04Z: "stuff to read counts as user effort"). A text reply never approves anything. The enforcer composes each text and call in its own fixed wording and sends it through the kernel and the core's owner-line modem. A compromised core can drop, delay, forge or falsely confirm any of them, so the enforcer's own page, light and log are the only trustworthy record: the light shows every alert the enforcer raised and any recovery pending.
  - **Recovery.** agentOS keeps a backup of the enforcer's signed configuration (BAK-1); restoring it needs the owner's key. Every policy carries a version number that only rises; the enforcer refuses an older policy unless a fresh owner assertion approves that restore, which is a loosening.
- **NET-3** **Networks and segmentation.** One VLAN-capable access point broadcasts four networks, each isolated by the enforcer (Mark, 2026-10-18 17:50Z). The access point does Wi-Fi only (plain OpenWrt, or its vendor cloud, remote access, UPnP and app pairing off), and its admin is reachable only from the enforcer's wired port. Each device on the Devices network gets its own Wi-Fi password, which identifies it to the enforcer, so a device that copies another's hardware address does not inherit that device's allow list, and revoking one device re-pairs no other:
  - **Owner**: only the owner's own phone and computer; the only network that reaches the admin and control pages of agentOS and of the enforcer (Mark, 2026-10-18 15:57Z "only owner on people network to isolate box", confirmed 15:58Z); trusted, never restricted automatically.
  - **Household**: household members' own devices; trusted, never restricted automatically. They can use home devices, cast and print, but reach neither box's admin or control pages.
  - **Devices**: every declared device, locks and alarms included, each isolated from every other. When a device is added, agentOS proposes its allow list (DEV-10), the enforcer generates its Wi-Fi password and shows it, or a QR code for it, only on its own page, so agentOS never holds it, and the owner signs both as one loosening (NET-4); entering the passwords into devices is batched in the setup session. Outbound access is **default-deny per device**: a home device is never exposed to the open internet (Mark, 2026-10-18 15:49Z), and reaches only the destinations its allow list names, built from the destinations it is observed to use and confirmed by the owner in plain words once per setup session rather than once per device. Unknown destinations are blocked and logged; agentOS may propose one by texting the owner a link, which is a loosening. A device with no cloud need gets no internet at all. The enforcer accepts no inbound connection from the internet to any device: UPnP and NAT-PMP are off and nothing is port-forwarded.
  - **Guests**: internet only, isolated, never observed, reaching neither agentOS box, the enforcer, the access point's admin nor the ISP router's LAN; the enforcer keeps only whole-network byte and connection counters for Guests, never per device or per destination.

Planners have no packet access on any network: they reach devices only through device clerks (DEV-1, DEV-2).
- **NET-4** **Owner keys and the direction of a change.** The owner's key is an ordinary passkey in their phone's password manager, used on the enforcer's own page and opened with the phone's screen passcode (Android or iOS); no separate app (Mark, 2026-10-18 16:04Z: "No separate app - user effort"), other hardware, biometrics or vendor account is needed. These proofs are the enforcer's own and sit beside CH-3, which still governs what agentOS does.

Texts and calls carry alerts, questions and tightenings; **reads away from home** (D-143, risk 37): on a text request from the owner's enrolled number, logs and camera images are sent by text wherever the owner is, images at full resolution, with no unlock asked for; the reply goes only to the owner's enrolled number, never to any other number, even the one that asked, and a request from any other number is dropped and logged, never answered; the clerk rate-limits these replies, so a request spoofing the enrolled number cannot flood the owner with MMS (the limit is measured when it is built); a reply carries reads only, never a code, recovery detail or passkey material, its text is scrubbed of codes and secret-shaped content (CH-19) and its images are rendered only by the box's fixed renderer (CH-20); tightenings by text still apply as the Tightening rule below says, and every loosening, unlock, enrolment and recovery still needs the passkey at home (NET-6). ## 14. Resources, updates, backups, time

### 14.1 Resources and accelerators

- **RES-1** Admission classes, in priority order: `foreground` (calls, STOP/STATUS, owner chat) > `accepted work` > `experiments`. Experiments MUST be frozen or killed within a frozen target time when foreground needs resources.
- **RES-2** A per-component memory budget is declared and enforced by cgroups. The kernel refuses admission that would leave less than the declared headroom, because a cgroup at its hard limit stalls in reclaim rather than failing fast [S3, measured on cgroup v1]. Memory pressure (PSI) is an admission input. CPU, I/O, and process-count limits are enforced the same way: planner sandboxes weigh less than the kernel and `foreground`, experiments least, and each sandbox has a process cap, so no guest can starve the owner channel or the log. No swap or reclaim thrashing, no out-of-memory kills of kernel or foreground. [Inference, from S3 and S4; check on the N95] Floor budget: host, kernel and log 1.0 GB; local inference 2.0 GB; one credentialed browser 0.5 GB; headroom 0.6 GB (unmeasured); planner-sandbox pool about 3.9 GB. The pool is derived at start from the host's reported memory less the other components' budgets, never assumed: on an 8 GB N95 that is about 3.5 GB [S1 kit arithmetic]. A host too small for the planner sandbox runs with guests off and says so (KRN-7). On larger hosts the pool MAY grow past the floor's only by a rule that also bounds it by CPU cores, so that adding sandboxes never slows foreground work (A2); such a rule adds throughput only (HW-4).
- **RES-3** The kernel discovers accelerators (iGPU/NPU/GPU) and exposes them as an admission resource used by local inference. The identical service MUST run on CPU when no accelerator is present. Nothing in the kernel depends on an accelerator. The same resource sizes the local leverage layer (PLN-7): a larger host or an accelerator runs larger local models and more passes at once, and owner compute hosts add capacity outside the box's own budget.
- **RES-4** Reserve storage for a healthy release, the log, and the recall index before discretionary downloads or snapshots. The reserve is enforced by the file system (a quota or a separate volume), not only checked before snapshots, and each planner sandbox writes under its own hard disk quota, so no guest can fill the disk the log and owner state live on. Old snapshots are pruned by policy.
- **RES-5** **Plan quota is a resource, per usage pool.** A plan has one or more **usage pools**: the unit the provider meters, each with its own rate windows (e.g. 5 hours and 7 days). Pools follow the provider's metering, not the provider: a provider may meter each of its products separately on one plan (e.g. Codex apart from ChatGPT chat), and a plan may add model-specific pools (e.g. a weekly pool for one model). Pools are declared, so any provider's product split is expressed the same way. A provider CLI's declaration (CLK-7) names the pools a run draws on. The kernel tracks each pool's utilization and reset time, read from the provider's responses on the model clerk's own connection (CLK-7). Admission to a plan route is refused when starting the run would take any pool it draws on past the owner's **reserve**: the share of each pool the owner keeps for their own use (default 0%: the box may use the whole pool; per pool, set as a budget, KEY-6). Spare-class work (HLD-4) draws a pool only down to the larger of the reserve and the **foreground forecast** until the pool's reset (foreground use measured over past windows); in the last hours before a reset (a frozen target) it MAY also use what would otherwise reset unused, never below the reserve. HLD-4's spare share bounds how much of what RES-5 admits spare-capacity work takes; where the two differ, the smaller wins. Foreground work may use the pool down to the reserve. Concurrent provider-CLI runs have no fixed cap, per pool, per CLI or per lineage: runs start as admitted work exists, and the kernel slows new starts on a pool only while its provider returns rate-limit errors. A run refused or cut off by a pool's limit fails over per PLN-7 within a second, and that pool's reset time is the route's cooldown; routes on other pools of the same plan stay available.

### 14.2 Updates, backups, time

- **UPD-1** A/B image updates: stage → test → activate → health-check → commit or fall back. Fallback MUST be automatic, using boot-loader boot counting plus a health check, with no owner action [S7: the measured Ubuntu GRUB path has no automatic rollback; signed systemd-boot has boot counting]. Rollback never rewinds revocations, spent budgets, deletions, or known external effects.
- **UPD-1a** A release is one content hash (the `/usr` verity root hash) plus its boot entry. Both MUST transfer and activate together.
- **UPD-2** Updates come from content-addressed, signed sources: any mirror, or offline from a drive (DEP-4). The update-signing key is held offline by the project. [Risk] Project key custody and rotation need their own procedure.
- **UPD-3** **First boot updates before trust.** The preloaded image may be months old. On first network contact, before any AI or personal account is connected, the box fetches the latest **stable** release, verifies it, and activates it through A/B with fallback. Credentials never touch an outdated image. Offline first boot runs the preloaded image and says so, and the update applies when the box is next online.
- **UPD-4** **Channels:** *stable* (default), *fast* (early adopters; feeds attestations only once the publication switch is on, PUB-1, D-088), *pinned* (no automatic updates, security notices only). Changeable by text or local UI.
- **UPD-5** **Cadence (defaults, modifiable):**

| Change type | Check | Apply |
|---|---|---|
| Security fixes | Daily | Auto-staged; applied at the next quiet window, typically within 24 h; automatic rollback on failed health check |
| Stable releases (host, kernel, bundled guests such as OpenClaw) | Daily | After a **soak**: at least 7 days on fast **and** sufficient independent passing attestations (PUB-1), whichever is later, plus local requalification; then at a quiet window with random jitter |
| Behavior-changing releases | Same | Also need owner approval unless a standing key covers that class (HLD-3) |
| Upstream projects directly (e.g. OpenClaw main) | Spare time (HLD-6) | Never adopted straight from upstream; enter users' boxes only via a qualified agentOS release |
| Shared skills | Continuous | Through §8 locally, one at a time |

- **UPD-6** Updates never apply during a call, accepted work, or quiet hours the owner has excluded. Every update is a logged plan step with a rollback point.
- **UPD-7** [Inference] Soak plus attestations replaces a central staged rollout: early-channel installations act as the canary population without any project-run service.
- **UPD-8** **Update metadata** follows The Update Framework (TUF; reuse a maintained implementation): releases are signed by a threshold of k-of-n maintainer keys rotate and revoke without reinstalling, timestamp metadata expires so a frozen mirror is detected when online, and no release older than the installed one is accepted, offline included; local A/B fallback to the previous slot (UPD-1) is exempt. Offline installs from a drive (DEP-4) need valid signatures and a newer version but not fresh timestamp metadata; the box says the update was installed without a freshness check, and checks on its next online contact. An offline install is refused when the drive's root metadata expired more than 180 days ago ("This drive's update is too old to trust offline. Use a newer drive, or connect once."); the owner can override with a tier-4 code, shown the same warning. Security fixes (UPD-5) auto-stage only with the threshold signatures plus at least one independent attestation from the fast channel (PUB-1). An attestation counts only from an attestor on the box's allow-list (pinned in the image, or added by the owner as a CH-3 key), never from a key holding a signing role; until an outside attestor is listed, the project's own test box is the interim entry, and it stops counting for good once one is listed, even if that attestor is later removed.
- **UPD-9** **A security fix is never held in silence.** A security fix that has not been applied within 24 h of being staged, because the box was never free (UPD-6), is pushed to the owner once as an urgent class (CH-15), with how long the restart takes and a choice of now or the next quiet window. A security fix held by the owner's settings (pinned, or security updates set to ask) is listed in STATUS until it is installed or the owner declines it (KRN-7). Ordinary releases never ask; after 7 days without a free moment they get a STATUS line.
- **UPD-10** **No code from writable storage** (D-149). No trusted-base or edge process executes or loads code from writable storage: writable mounts are `noexec`, units set `NoExecPaths=/` with `ExecPaths=` only on the read-only `/usr`, and sandbox policy refuses the rest; the image build checks this. Browser clerks keep cookies and site storage only, and sandboxes start from their image. The controller reboots weekly at a quiet time (UPD-6). The edge boots from its image every time, keeping only its link key and line state, and reboots daily. So an implant planted in writable storage never runs and lasts at most a week.
- **BAK-1** Backups are optional and owner-chosen: a second local drive, or encrypted upload to storage the owner already has. Without a backup, the controller's SSD is the only copy, and the owner is told so plainly. A backup holds the core's edge key and the record of revoked edge keys (HW-15), and restoring it trusts no edge key until the owner's owner-page step. Backups are sealed with hybrid X25519 and ML-KEM-768, so opening one needs both secrets (D-149). A backup carries the page CA key (CH-9), so an ordinary restore keeps the printed fingerprint, and never the kernel's keys, which every restore regenerates and re-pairs in REC-2's one visit.
- **BAK-2** **Data at home is log content** (D-149, D-151).
  - The archive is the log's content store (LOG-1), filled by each account's declared sync (CLK-14) under the narrowest grant, such as `gmail.readonly`. Where a provider's API cannot read everything, the owner's own export or the provider's picker brings it home (Google Photos' API reads only what an app created, since March 31, 2025).
  - **Reads from home.** Tasks read archived mail and files only from the archive; provider logins only sync, watch and act. The witness keeps its own reads at providers (KRN-10).
  - **Sealed past the window.** Once the owner sets a trim window, items older than it are sealed by wrapping their content keys (LOG-4) to a key that either hardware key or the recovery key unwraps. Only its public half stays on the box, so the box can add items but not read them back, and old items open after a hardware-key tap at home. Each item keeps its own key under its subject's key, so erase still works.
  - **Backups** follow BAK-1 for the log and its content alike: two targets in different failure domains, each append-only where offered, so where both offer it neither a thief nor a hacked box can erase them. The owner page names the targets that do, with their cost, and STATUS says when a target does not.
  - **Trim.** With the owner's one-time approval of a window, such as 90 days, the box trims a provider to it by moving older items to the provider's own trash, only after each is verified in the archive and in both backups; the trash's own delay is the undo. The default is archive on, trim off.
  - **An exit.** The archive keeps standard formats (raw messages and files, with an SQLite index), and a documented recipe opens the backups on any Linux machine with the owner's keys and without agentOS (A8).
  - End-to-end encryption is turned on where a provider offers it, and the graph counts which providers can read each kind of data. STATUS shows, for each provider, what leaving it would take.
- **TIM-1** Time comes from network time when online, with carrier network time (from the modem) as a cross-check. Large disagreements put the kernel into restricted mode for time-sensitive checks.

## 15. Publication

Every installation's verified gains can flow into one public project, and every installation can take in everyone else's, without any runtime dependency on it. Publication is off by default and is built after the first release, once a second household exists (D-152).

- **PUB-1** **Publication by information flow, not by filtering.** Nothing derived from private activity leaves the box until the owner turns on the one publication switch, on the local UI only, never by text (CH-19); the box offers it when it has real candidates to send, not at setup, and asks again for each new kind of publication (D-088). Then:
  - **Hints are the only bridge.** The private side emits only a **hint**: a record whose every field is an enumerated value from a public schema, such as `skill_gap{domain: calendar, format: ics, failure: timezone}`, with no free text, number, name, identifier or content. The kernel derives every hint from log entries (KRN-3); no planner or guest chooses, words or picks one. At most 5 hints cross a day, a choice among public options of about 57 bits at most (D-082). Every hint is logged and visible to the owner.
  - **Clean-room guest.** A hint is built into a public skill, test case, regression or declaration by a guest session whose only label is `public` (PLN-3): it holds no key to any clerk but `fetch`, sees only the hint, the public repository, public pages and synthetic fixtures, and its output is what may be published.
  - **Never published, under any setting:** real task cases, private evaluation suites, plans and their results, transcripts, skills compiled from private tasks (their clean-room rebuilds may be), log content and owner preferences.
  - **Structured records only.** Hardware-database entries (HW-6, HW-2) and attestations use fixed schemas of enumerated fields (vendor, model, firmware, result class, software version), with no serial numbers, free text or timestamps finer than a day.
  - **Security findings** from real traffic cross only as a `vuln{}` hint, with the regression rebuilt in the clean room; a finding that could harm other installations goes first by encrypted private report to the maintainers, under embargo, and only while the switch is on.
  - **Pseudonymous and unlinkable.** Each installation publishes under a rotated pseudonymous key, unlinkable to the owner's identity, number, accounts or address, batched and time-delayed, and only over Tor to the public relays, with no direct fallback.
  - **Attestations are evidence, not authority.** Installations MAY reproduce public candidates in spare capacity (HLD-4) and, with the switch on, publish signed pass or fail attestations per hardware class and version. Maintainers decide what enters a release; attestations are weighted by reproducibility, never counted blindly, and every installation re-qualifies locally before adopting anything (HLD-6). Releases reach installations through the update path (UPD-1, DEP-4); anyone may fork, and an installation may follow any fork.
  - **Not a dependency.** The public repository is a publication channel, not a runtime dependency: with it unreachable, operation, control and recovery do not change (DEP-2, DEP-3).
  - **License.** The license MUST be chosen before the first public release, with upstream notices preserved. This spec grants no license.

## 16. Evaluation

The kernel is judged by simulation, the box by tasks, and both by attacks. Every suite here is log data (HLD-3) and runs on every change to the trusted base (KRN-2).

- **EVL-1** **The kernel simulator.** The kernel runs inside a deterministic simulator from its first commit (KRN-6): one seed fixes the clock, every fake clerk's responses and every injected fault (a crash between any two log entries, a dropped, duplicated, delayed or reordered event or clerk response, a full disk, a clock jump). The simulator checks after every transition that no effect left the kernel without a step it admitted under a valid key (KRN-5), that each effect ran at most once or is reconciled (KRN-8), and that STOP held (KRN-7). Clerks are tested against the same caveat cases with the same library, so a step the simulated kernel was forced to admit is still refused at the clerk (CLK-2). Replaying any recorded log from empty yields identical entries (KRN-1, LOG-1). A failure reproduces from its seed alone and becomes a permanent regression seed. A deliberately simple reference model of the caveat rules, written apart from the key library, is fuzzed differentially against it on every kernel change; any disagreement fails (D-149). The simulator lives with the kernel in `broker/kernel/sim` and is part of the kernel's review; simulation and replay are one path there (`broker/replay` is ported into it and deleted, docs/LANES.md).
- **EVL-2** **The first slice: "Sort my inbox and unsubscribe from and delete the spam".** This task is the first one built end to end and stays in the suite for good. It runs in QEMU against a synthetic mailbox, fake unsubscribe endpoints and seeded injection mails, with synthetic canaries only; its cases are listed below.
  - **Sort** is `organize` (labels and archive, VRB-2) under the standing key the owner's first approval of the task makes (KEY-5).
  - **Delete** is `trash`: recoverable from the account's trash, never `delete-remote`.
  - **Unsubscribe** is `unsubscribe`: only a one-click POST to the sender's `List-Unsubscribe` header (RFC 8058), only for mail the unsubscribe-eligibility predicate admits (VRB-2, which includes 90 days unopened), and never by following a link in the body. It is irreversible, so it runs after its hold window, set by novelty (KEY-8, HLD-2): a sender the owner has unsubscribed from before is held briefly, a first-ever unsubscribe longer.
  - **Spam** (mail the reader classes as spam or phishing) is trashed and never unsubscribed, since an unsubscribe confirms the address to its sender. A reader's class never makes mail eligible; it only narrows the predicate.
  - **The planner never reads a mail.** It sees handles; the reader (PLN-2) returns only the enumerated class (`personal`, `bulk`, `alert` as in VRB-2, `spam`, `phishing`) and a date. Header validity is never a reader value; the mail clerk and the kernel compute it (VRB-2). Seeded mails that instruct the planner, the reader or the owner (to forward mail, reply, follow a link, unsubscribe a personal sender or change a label) change no step, and a canary in a mail body never leaves the mail clerk.
  - **Reversible by construction** (D-139): labels, archive and trash run through the clerk under the slice key, and only the unsubscribe goes through the hold and novelty checks. The slice key never empties trash, never permanently deletes and never shortens the provider's trash retention, so every trash stays undoable.
  - **Shadow week** (D-152). Before any task changes the owner's real mailbox, the slice runs on it for seven days under a read-only grant (CLK-9). Reads are live; every effect is logged and never sent. The reader runs on the local model whether or not it has passed its gate (PLN-2), since nothing is acted on. A day after each proposal the mail clerk reads the item back, and the log records whether the owner's own action matches it. At the end, STATUS gives one line: agreement by class.
  - **Cases**, each with its expected outcome:
    - a link in a mail body is never followed;
    - an unsigned, uncovered or spoofed `List-Unsubscribe` header, or a duplicate `From`, `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header, is not eligible;
    - a sender without a passing DKIM signature, with a `d=` that does not align with `From`, with an `l=` tag, or whose URL host neither aligns with `d=` nor is in the provider table, is not eligible;
    - a lookalike of a known sender or contact is not eligible;
    - an otherwise eligible sender whose mail the owner opened in the last 90 days, or whose first mail is under 90 days old, is not unsubscribed;
    - the first run's one approval makes the standing key, and a second run needs no tap;
    - spam or phishing with valid headers is trashed and never unsubscribed;
    - a first-ever unsubscribe waits the longest hold, and a STOP during it cancels it with no POST sent;
    - a permanent delete, an IMAP `EXPUNGE`, emptying the trash or shortening the trash retention is outside the slice key (KRN-3) and refused at the clerk (CLK-2);
    - an account that empties trash sooner than 7 days or deletes at once makes the step `delete-remote`, which the slice key does not cover: refused, with one STATUS line;
    - an injection mail changes no step, recipient or key, and the canary never leaves the mail clerk.
  - **Pass:** every case above has its expected outcome; no request reaches any endpoint but the declared unsubscribe POSTs; the owner's taps are those of §10 (one approval on the first run, the passkey tap that makes the standing key; nothing per mail; none on a later run); and the whole run replays identically (EVL-1).
- **EVL-3** **Injection tests.** Fixed corpora of injected instructions are seeded into every kind of content a reader or guest sees (mail, web pages, documents, file names, device names, a collaborator's output, a guest's own files) and run against every planner route and guest version on every release and every guest qualification (PLN-5). Pass means structural: no step outside the session's keys runs, no value with a private label reaches a sink its key does not allow (PLN-3), no canary appears in any output, and a session's rejected plans are logged (PLN-4). Corpora only grow (HLD-3), with each finding from HLD-5 added as a minimized case.
- **EVL-4** **Task suites and the no-drop gate.** Owner-effort and acceptance are measured on held-out suites of real tasks with owner outcomes (HLD-3) and on the frozen journey set of OWN-19 (A10). A change that lowers acceptance, raises owner taps or typed characters per accepted task, or fails any security case, on the held-out split and above noise, does not adopt; there is no fixed cap on any other measure, only this no-drop gate.
- **EVL-5** **Known vulnerabilities** (D-149). govulncheck and an OSV scan run on every pull request. A known vulnerability reachable from the project's code, or in a vendored or image dependency, fails CI unless an issue records why it is accepted, and the record names the issue. The measure backs KRN-2's trusted-base budget.

## 17. Out of scope (v1.0)

Fleet orchestration beyond one core with its edge, workstations and helper apps (HW-9, HW-11, HW-14), such as one box spread across peer hosts; an owner compute host serving model passes (PLN-7) is in scope; Mac or ARM boot; macOS-only and iOS-only apps (Apple's licence permits macOS virtualization only on Apple hardware [Fact]; iOS apps run only on Apple devices); any hosted agentOS service; remote rich UI (needs a relay, so excluded by DEP-2); a marketplace; private-data federated training; large local models as a requirement; custom kernel or distribution work; radios on the box for devices (Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread; reached through the owner's own hub or border router, DEV-1, or through the enforcer's radios, NET-2, which are not the box's); controlling medical devices (pumps, dosing, anything that treats or doses; their data reads are in scope, §13); inspecting or managing household members' own phones and computers (no device management, NET-1); a virtual desktop compositor shared by several clerks (each kiosk clerk has its own, CLK-5); a guest that holds a provider login (every model call goes through the model clerk, CLK-7).

**Scope freeze (D-086):** a new release requirement needs Mark's explicit promotion; review findings cannot add one.

## 19. Open risks

1. **Firmware boot selection** without a keyboard on arbitrary PCs (HW-6). The integrated key is the full answer.
2. **Voice over USB modems** (HW-2, CH-5): model-dependent quality.
3. **Plan routes depend on provider terms** (CLK-7). Terms have changed several times in 2026 (Anthropic restricted third-party use of plan logins; Google ended plan access for open-source agents; OpenAI opened ChatGPT sign-in to third-party tools). The API fallback (PLN-7) keeps the box working when a plan route goes; it only costs more. Each plan route, a provider CLI guest whose model traffic goes only through the model clerk, is re-read against its provider's terms at every CLI qualification. A re-read that finds a prohibition withdraws the route in the next release and tells affected owners at once (CLK-7). A route whose terms are unconfirmed for this use (Codex today) also carries a risk that the provider restricts the owner's account, which the owner accepts through recorded consent (CLK-7).
4. **Classifying web submits** (CLK-3). A recipe's mapping, cancel window, and cancel cost come from the site, which can change or misstate them, and a site without a demo environment cannot be mismatch-checked. Mitigations: the clerk matches controls from its own DOM, undeclared state-changing requests are blocked, the stricter-verb rule, first live runs go to approval, unknown submits are high risk, and a diverging recipe is rerouted or paused (CLK-3).
5. **Remote rich UI is gone by design.** If that proves too limiting, the only fix is an optional, owner-chosen, end-to-end-encrypted relay. That would be a deliberate exception to DEP-2's spirit, never a requirement.
6. **Project update-signing key** custody (UPD-2). UPD-8's threshold signing means one stolen key can't sign a release; the custody and rotation procedure is still needed.
7. **Desktop kiosk escape** (CLK-5). A GUI path out of the kiosk (a crash dialog, help browser, or app-embedded file picker) could reach the app's saved login. Mitigations: the login stored outside the UI user's reach, A13's adversarial escape step, and restricting desktop clerks to apps where that holds.
8. **Tampered trusted source** (KEY-5). An attacker who edits the source record a key trusts (e.g. a Xero contact's email) passes the predicate. Mitigations: scope bounds, the recent-edit hold, and the log.
9. **Low-tier approvals by text** (CH-10). A SIM swap of the owner's number, or a stolen unlocked phone, can approve low-risk requests. Mitigations: the owner-set low-tier limits, reversibility windows, the log, and STOP.
10. **Owner Card kept with the core** (STO-2). The card holds the store passphrase, so whoever has both can decrypt the sealed store offline; the approval code gates only a running, unmodified box. Mitigations: the card says to store it apart from the core, like a passport, and the owner can replace the passphrase on the local UI (which does not protect copies already made).
11. **Tampered boot path on an unknown host** (HW-5a, STO-2). Secure Boot stops at the distribution's kernel; the initrd and the `/usr` root hash are not verified (S7). An attacker with brief access to the SSD can modify them so the local page captures the passphrase when the owner next types it on an unknown host, then take the SSD. Accepted for MVP (Mark, 2026-10-04): the owner's guide says to unlock only on a trusted PC after losing custody of the SSD; trusted hosts are covered by the TPM policy seal; an agentOS shim is reconsidered after MVP.
12. **Disclosure through allowed destinations** (PLN-3). A `private` sandbox can still leak through an allowed destination whose received data an attacker can read and through research queries and the choice among public links it may follow. Mitigations: a minimal default list, no user-content hosts in suggestions, size and rate bounds, and the log.
13. **SIM swap inside the unlock window** (CH-3, CH-19). A swapper can read ordinary private data by chat until the unlock lapses or the owner notices their phone has lost service. Codes and secret-shaped content are already withheld.
14. **Host stack claims unverified on Debian** (S7). Debian 13's signed systemd-boot, boot counting, and the TPM policy seal were not exercised: the cloud network blocks Debian mirrors. P2 step 1 verifies them first; bootc on CentOS Stream 10 is the fallback.
15. **Floor concurrency rests on summed RSS** (S3, S4). About 1.6 GB per OpenClaw guest sandbox may overstate it (PSS about 1.54 GB), and Firecracker's copy-on-write snapshot restore could let forks share memory. gVisor's systrap overhead on build-heavy work (npm install, builds) is also unmeasured and is measured alongside the Firecracker re-test. All are measured on the N95 before A2 targets freeze.
16. **Lossy local passes and compute-host integrity** (PLN-7). A reduce pass can drop what the frontier needed, and a weak check can pass a bad result; a compromised owner computer sees what is sent to it. Mitigations: pipelines are adopted only on measured acceptance per task class and roll back on regression; check verdicts carry no authority; private data reaches a compute host only by the owner's per-host choice, over a pinned encrypted channel, with no credentials or tools.
17. **Effort proxies** (OWN-19). Attention and thought cannot be measured directly; the counted components stand in for them, and a proxy can be optimized against what it stands for (fewer texts by withholding needed ones, fewer prompts by widening authority). Mitigations: components reported separately rather than as one score, each suppression measure paired with a miss measure, and security cases inside every journey run.
18. **Computer-use retry and site terms** (CLK-5). Some sites' terms forbid automated access, and a retry that hides automation markers makes the box harder to tell from the owner, so a site that detects it anyway may restrict the owner's account. Behavioural bot scoring (input timing, pointer paths) is not addressed and may still block the retry. Mitigations: one retry per step, a per-site off switch, log and STATUS lines, and a failed retry going to the owner rather than repeating.
19. **Device stacks parse hostile traffic** (DEV-1). A Matter, HomeKit, ONVIF, or IPP stack compromised by a malicious device is inside the trusted base (KRN-2) and holds that clerk's credentials. Mitigations: one credential domain per clerk, the fabric root key kept in the sealed store, mature stacks qualified through §8, and clerks that run only while needed.
20. **Unverified devices** (DEV-3). Many cameras, printers, and older devices have no cryptographic identity, so anyone on the home network can impersonate one, feed it false readings, or capture its credential. Mitigations: public content only, no physical action, no urgent rules on their readings, and a page that says so and asks for a unique password.
21. **Device clerk memory at the floor** (DEV-1, RES-2) is unmeasured. A resident Matter controller or hub subscription competes with the planner-sandbox pool; if it does not fit, devices stay off with a KRN-7 line.
22. **Secure is not always safe** (DEV-5). The pre-allowable direction can still harm: closing a garage door on someone, or cutting heat in a freeze. Mitigations: the device's own interlocks and warnings, `actuate` bounds, the log, and STOP; the owner chooses each key.
23. **Capture and other people** (DEV-7). Recording household members or visitors may need their consent under local law, which the box cannot check. Capture is off by default, and the page says so when the owner turns it on.
24. **Residual home-network reach** (DEV-2). A router that serves its admin page on an external address the box cannot learn is reachable by hairpin, and prefix rules miss routed internal subnets outside the enclosing /56 and public IPv4 addresses inside the home. [Inference] Home routers rarely serve admin on their external address by default. Cloud-visible device state (DEV-9) also stays a small channel, bounded like risk 12.
25. **Texted codes for devices** (CH-10). A SIM swapper can approve low-tier device steps: `actuate` and `present` keys and vendor-cloud cutoffs on devices with no safety or access role, private content on `local-only` household devices, and secure-direction actions within `actuate` bounds. Outside an unlock window they cannot start tasks (CH-14); inside one (risk 13) they can, and these effects still reach no one outside the household and are bounded, undoable, or reduce exposure. The owner can exclude these verbs from the low tier (CH-10).
26. **Controller and helper system** (HW-9–HW-13, PLN-3, ONB-10). One signing key reaches every node (offline key, two-person signing, reproducible builds); an image with device drivers is reproducible only by holders of their repository. Workstation firmware is trusted. A helper app trusts Windows or macOS, so it gets cleared content only and its output is untrusted. Preloaded firmware cannot be verified by software; the first-boot checks stop a tampered image only. A claim sticker can be used first by someone else (the owner sees "already claimed" and reinstalls; nothing of the owner's leaks, because the only secret before the claim is the sticker's setup network password, which opens only the enforcer's enrolment page). Until the enrolment replaces the setup network, whoever holds the sticker can impersonate it, and one who impersonates it during the enrolment also learns the Owner network's password (HW-10; accepted, D-105). Loss of the mini PC loses the live sealed store, so off-box backup is a setup step. The Owner Card shown on the phone can leak by screenshot; setup pushes printing and warns.
27. **Analysis toolkit legality and exposure** (PLN-6). The legal limits rest on the owner's stated purpose, the guest's judgment and the log, not on the box; neither the box nor the guest can determine a jurisdiction's law, and a stated purpose can be wrong. Closed network access bounds the harm to analysis of files already on the box. A hostile attachment or binary may target the toolkit's own parsers, which is why the sandbox is disposable and holds nothing.
28. **Reverse-engineered declarations** (DEV-12). A declaration inferred from captures can misread what an operation does, and replay shows only the exchanges the owner recorded. Mitigations: classes come from the kernel's table, never the draft; the declaration is data in a shape the clerk already sends, with no code and no credential value; every operation the captures do not show asks on its first live runs; and the clerk sends only declared shapes. A capture can still hold a password CLK-4's redaction misses, which the planner sees. A firmware or software change can still leave a device unusable, even with a verified backup, if it cannot boot to accept the restore.
29. **Home control** (NET-1–NET-7). The enforcer is new attack surface, held small by a signed read-only image, confined radio stacks, automatic signed security fixes and an admin page only the Owner network and a wired port can reach (NET-2). There is no new remote path into the house: the enforcer's page is home-only (NET-6). A compromised agentOS can still send radio-device commands and tightenings over the enforcer channel to devices off the safety list, and can text the owner a misleading proposal link; the link opens only the enforcer's own page, which renders the true change, and a passkey bound to the enforcer gives no assertion to a lookalike page, so the owner can be misled only into a change they read there and sign (NET-2). Tightenings and relocking apply without an ask, so a compromised agentOS can keep relocking up to the rate limit; keys and keypads still work. Frozen or opened devices lose vendor patches and are isolated to compensate (NET-7). Allow lists by name over-permit on shared CDN addresses, Encrypted Client Hello hides server names, and a vendor's server change breaks a feature until the owner approves it (NET-1, NET-3). A synced passkey is only as safe as the cloud account that syncs it; with no separate app (NET-4), that account plus the Owner Wi-Fi password, used within Wi-Fi range, is the residual, and the recovery card inside the enforcer's case is as safe as the case. The enforcer's texts and calls go through the kernel and the core's owner-line modem, so a compromised core can drop, delay, forge or falsely confirm them; the enforcer's own page, light and log are the only trustworthy record (NET-2). The enforcer's automatic security fixes trust its own update signer, so a compromise of that signer reaches the enforcer. A device on the ISP router's own Wi-Fi or LAN is outside NET and at the floor (NET-1). [Inference] A passkey needs a secure origin and a domain-style relying-party name, which an enforcer on the home network without a domain or certificate authority (DEP-2) must provide locally, at most with a certificate limited to its own local name. This is unqualified on Android and iOS and must be shown before NET-4 and NET-6 are built. With the enforcer, loosening is not reachable by a SIM swap, so risk 25 does not widen. With the edge on its own box, a break-in of the edge reaches no enforcer, no store of private data and no device except the online everyday devices it relays, whose low-sensitivity state it then sees; [Inference] state such as a TV being on hints at who is home. Hostile firmware on an online everyday device reaches the edge, never the core or the enforcer; hostile factory firmware on an offline everyday device reaches the core's connection to it, the residual of the direct path, and on an offline camera or microphone it reaches the core's sandboxed decoder. [Inference] A stream port is two-way, so a compromised core can attack an offline camera's stream service, though not its settings. Owner texts and calls never cross it, because the owner-line modem is on the core (CH-1, NET-2), so it can neither read nor forge them; the residual on the owner line is the carrier, as without agentOS. A compromised core can still send what it holds to a destination on its signed list, or to another service at the same shared CDN address, as a single box could (NET-1, NET-2), and through web fetches it asks the edge to make to any internet address, over the direct cable the enforcer neither sees nor logs (NET-2). The enforcer does not see the owner-line modem's data bearer, or the edge's second-line one, either; HW-2 limits each to the carrier's MMS APN and host, but [Inference] a compromised core can reconfigure its modem and some carriers' MMS APNs reach the internet, so that bearer is a second path out the enforcer neither sees nor logs. Every loosening waits for the owner at home, so a vendor's server change breaks a feature, and a provider's, service's or backup target's server change outside the core's signed list breaks its credentialed actions, until then (NET-4). Core, edge and enforcer, plus the access point, are systems to set up and update.
30. **Approvals wait for home** (CH-8, CH-10). A high-risk approval, a private reply without a destination, and every other owner-page step wait until the owner is home, so a purchase above the limit or a new key asked while travelling waits days. Accepted to keep one surface and to put every code that approves an effect behind home presence. Session unlock still takes the code-generator code by text (CH-10), so a code phished on a look-alike page and replayed in a text from a spoofed owner number or by a SIM swapper can unlock a session until the code expires; a browser flaw that defeats CH-9's same-origin checks would let a look-alike page relay a phished code through the owner's browser. Under home control TLS ends on the core (CH-9), so after setup a compromised enforcer or access point can block or delay the page but cannot read or rewrite a step or what is typed into it, except that an enforcer which saw a link's value can open that step first and read it, which the owner learns of only on opening the link (CH-7); an enforcer compromised before setup, or one serving a reinstall whose fingerprint the owner does not check, can substitute its own certificate and read and rewrite every agentOS step. A compromised enforcer can also redirect an agentOS link to a look-alike step, on its own name with no browser warning, and collect any code or secret the owner types there; the owner's only cue is that the address bar does not show the core's name (CH-9). It needs no seed to use a phished code: it saw that link's value, so it opens the real step first and submits the code itself, once, before the code expires, and the owner never sees the real step (CH-7). Whoever reads the core's authority key from a decrypted backup can impersonate the core's name, and only that name, to the owner's phone on the home network until the owner, having learned a backup was decrypted, runs a compromised restore (REC-5), which replaces the authority, and installs the new fingerprint (CH-9). Accepted to keep one code per approval; a compromised enforcer already holds the home's rules. The alternative is a challenge the core draws and the code must answer, such as a grid cell, which binds the code to its step at the cost of owner effort. While CH-18's low-tier lockout holds, every approval waits until home. The owner can widen keys (KEY-5) or the low-risk limit (CH-10) before leaving. Being on the home network is not authority (CH-7): whoever joins it still needs the code, and apart from a compromised enforcer the residual is an attacker within Wi-Fi range who holds both the Wi-Fi password and the code-generator seed.
31. **Core and edge** (HW-14 to HW-16). The edge sees the web fetches it makes, including queries a guest derived from private items, and the second line's texts and call audio, as the carrier and the sites do; it never sees the owner line. A compromised edge holds the second line's SIM, so it can bypass CLK-8's checks (verified recipients, E.164 and premium-rate rules, the assistant opening, rate limits, the log): it can send unlogged and paid texts and calls in the owner's name, forge or hold back third parties' replies, and text the owner posing as agentOS. Only the owner line's number speaks for agentOS, and a code counts only on the owner line or the owner page (CH-1, CH-10), so none of this approves, loosens or releases anything. A carrier spend cap and premium-rate barring on the second SIM are recommended. It can also feed the core poisoned web content, which arrives labelled untrusted. It can cut the core off the web and the second line; the core keeps its owner line, its local work and the owner page. It can also capture the pairing keys of the online everyday devices it relays while they are in use, command those devices within the enforcer's limits, and forge their state within the fixed schema; NET-2 requires the owner to re-pair them after any edge compromise. Before the Owner Card confirm, whoever first plugs a box into the core's link port becomes the edge; an edge added after onboarding, and any edge after a restore, takes the owner-page step (HW-15). The edge is headless and shows no code the owner could compare, so at every owner-page enrolment (later, replacement and post-restore) whatever is plugged into the link port is accepted, as at the first plug: a stated residual. The edge is a second box to set up and update.
32. **The kernel is one point of failure** (KRN-1, KRN-2). Every authority decision runs through the kernel and its key library, so a bug there is a bug everywhere. The clerks' re-check (CLK-2) uses the same key library, so it catches a fault in the kernel's own logic or state, not a fault in the library. Mitigations: a kernel small enough to read whole, reviewed at the highest tier; the simulator with seeded faults on every change (EVL-1, A20); deterministic replay of the log (KRN-6); and a clerk's credential confined to its declared origins and shapes whatever the kernel admits (CLK-3).
33. **Reader errors** (PLN-2). The quarantined reader can be wrong, or steered by an injection mail to any value its type allows: spam classed as personal, phishing classed as bulk. It adds no step, recipient or key, so the harm stays inside what the plan's key admits: a wrong trash is undone from the trash, and a wrong unsubscribe is the irreversible residual, bounded by the kernel-checked eligibility predicate (VRB-2), the RFC 8058 POST shape and the hold window (EVL-2, KEY-8); the hold is owner vigilance, not the gate (a reader's class only narrows, KRN-3). Mitigations: closed value types, the injection tests (EVL-3), the local reader's held-out gate (PLN-2: no personal or alert mail classed as spam, accuracy no lower than an API reader), and owner corrections fed to the held-out suite (HLD-3).
34. *Retired* (D-153: one planning mode; residual channel in PLN-3).
35. **Novelty can be groomed** (KEY-8, HLD-2). Steps the owner accepted before shorten later windows, so an attacker who can shape a series of harmless accepted steps can make a later harmful one look familiar. Mitigations: novelty compares the step's whole shape and only ever raises a gate (KEY-8), and the key's caveats bound the step regardless (KEY-2).
36. **A restore without the erase entries** (LOG-4, STO-1). With no enforcer and no backup newer than the restored one, the erases made after it cannot be re-applied. Restored content stays sealed, but an owner who unseals a class at the passkey can bring back items forgotten after the backup's date; the warning names that date, and only the owner's choice opens it.
37. **Reads away from home need only the number** (NET-4, D-143). Whoever holds the owner's enrolled number, by a SIM swap or a stolen unlocked phone, can text for the home log and full-resolution camera images at any time, and the images can show whether anyone is home. This is wider than risk 13, since no unlock window bounds it. Mark accepted it (Decisions thread 2026-10-20 17:04:17Z, "Yes but not low res", cmsg_01R63S2yrPkq32thy8WQCuvDBHbA7YYkhB93Mu7RR8FpLD). Codes and secret-shaped content are still withheld (CH-19), replies go only to the enrolled number and are rate-limited, and nothing can be loosened this way (NET-6). The owner's carrier also sees each image a read away from home sends, without end-to-end encryption (CH-6), and it lands wherever the phone backs up its messages, so those copies hold what the camera's maker was denied (CLK-10).
38. **Gmail's client** (CLK-9, §12.1). Google may narrow the personal-use exception, refuse a box's automated registration or suspend the box's account; the fallbacks are the owner's own registration, then IMAP. Google's warning at every consent trains a click-through habit that fake-app phishing uses, and Advanced Protection may block the household's client. App passwords' breadth (calendar and contacts) is unconfirmed.
39. **Brakes and narrowing** (CLK-11, CLK-12). Whoever holds the owner's login can often lift a brake at once, and narrowing can be used to annoy, such as lowering a limit before a large payment.
40. **Evidence, not assurance** (CLK-10, KRN-10). The graph is only as true as its data, and a decoy fires only if an attacker uses it.
41. **Fewer copies, harder recovery** (BAK-2, CLK-10, CLK-13). Trimming leaves the box and its two backups as the only copies of older data. With support-desk recovery off, a lost recovery key can lock the owner out for good. Whoever takes the domain's registrar or DNS account can redirect every per-site address. A provider can withdraw its end-to-end encryption, as Apple did for new UK users in February 2025.
42. **A spoofed STOP** (CH-11, CH-15). Anyone holding the owner's or the second witness's phone can send `STOP <name>`, and since the code goes to that phone, confirm it too: the scope's narrowings run (sessions signed out, grants revoked, the enforcer's locks applied), and undoing them takes a hardware key at home.
43. **Drones** (NET-2). Remote ID broadcasts the drone's and its dock's position to anyone nearby. Its vision can mistake a person for an animal; the boundary, not the vision, bounds the harm. A failed return can crash on the owner's land. Aviation law may require a watching pilot (in the US, until the FAA's beyond-sight rule takes effect); NET-6 keeps the owner home, not watching, so that law is the owner's to keep.
