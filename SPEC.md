**Status:** Draft. v0.12 folds the P0 cloud spike results (S3, S4, S7) and Mark's boot-integrity decision (HW-5a) into v0.11 as amended by review 1. v0.11 superseded v0.10 (Google Doc titled "AgentOS — Specification v0.7"), which is left unedited.
**Normative language:** MUST / MUST NOT / SHOULD / MAY. Requirements carry IDs (e.g. `DEP-1`), and §15 traces each to an acceptance test.
**Evidence labels:** [Fact] established or verifiable · [Inference] reasoned, untested · [Risk] known open question.

## 0. What changed from v0.10

| v0.10 | v0.11 |
|---|---|

## 1. Purpose

AgentOS turns whatever AI the owner has (frontier plans and APIs, private hosted models, local models, in any mix, including local only) into **accepted work**, using a machine the owner physically controls. It **compounds** what it learns and keeps **authority and credentials** under the owner's sole control.

**Objective:** the most verified leverage per unit of owner effort, never below the structural security floor. It replaces v0.12's north-star metric (owner-minutes per accepted task), which counted only time.

- **Leverage:** the consequence and breadth of the verified outcomes the owner can cause.
- **Owner effort:** attention, time, decisions, things the owner must remember, checking, and recovery, counted across failed tasks as well as accepted ones.
- **Verified:** the resulting state was checked against the request (OWN-13). A wrong or unverified outcome is negative leverage.
- **Structural security floor:** protection held by a broker-enforced mechanism whatever the agent does and whether or not the owner is paying attention. Each guarantee states its trusted base and any bounded channel it leaves; none rests on agent judgment or owner vigilance. Its core is Invariant C (§7.2) and gated irreversible effects (REV-2, CH-10, ADP-9).

**Priority when goals conflict:** the floor, then verified correctness, then effort against leverage. The floor is never traded. Prefer designs that improve both effort and leverage; otherwise the trade is recorded as a decision (§16).

### 1.1 Owner principles

Each principle names the requirements that realize it, or says where none exists yet. A change that works against one states why in its PR.

- **OWN-1** **Irreducible owner inputs.** The owner supplies intent, authority, value judgments where their preference is unknown, and proof of identity (CH-3, CH-6, CAP-10). The system supplies everything else.
- **OWN-2** **Capture once.** What the owner has said is kept across restarts, unlocks, dropped calls, and failed deliveries; asking for it again is a defect. Unauthenticated content is kept, but its authority is not inherited (CH-14's `RUN`).
- **OWN-3** **Ask rule.** Ask only when the chance that the answer differs from the default, times the cost of a wrong default, exceeds the cost of asking; otherwise proceed undoably and visibly, meaning journaled and listed in the digest (CAP-10, CH-15, CH-16). The rule governs choices within granted authority only. Whether an effect needs approval is decided by the broker (REV-2, CH-10), never by this estimate.
- **OWN-4** **Notify rule.** Notify only when knowing now changes what the owner would do or believe: an outcome, a decision due, an actionable failure, a security alert, or a liveness signal whose absence is the alarm (CH-15's digest). Routine retries and progress are not sent.
- **OWN-5** **Investigate first, ask once.** A question comes after the system has found what it can, as one ask with a recommendation and the tradeoff that matters. Reduce the number of decisions before reducing the friction of each (CH-10, CAP-6).
- **OWN-6** **Conclusion first.** Every message leads with the outcome or the decision; detail comes on request (`MORE`, CH-12, CH-20) from saved state; serving it without rerunning the task has no requirement yet (BOARD OWN-gaps).
- **OWN-7** **Mechanism over vigilance.** Owner attention is a security control only at authority boundaries: grants, approvals of irreversible effects (CH-10, both tiers), and recovery (CH-3, ADP-9). Nothing else may depend on the owner noticing.
- **OWN-8** **Prompts are a finite security budget.** Each prompt spends attention that approvals depend on. A change that adds a prompt states why a broker check, a pre-allowance, or reversibility cannot replace it (REV-3, ADP-9).
- **OWN-9** **Gate by consequence.** Gating follows irreversibility, blast radius, and disclosure. Novelty and frequency only ever raise a gate, where the broker verifies them (CH-10's recipient age, ADP-9's rate bounds and hold, a recipe's first runs, ADP-14); they never lower one. A reversible effect is acted on, reported, and undoable; an irreversible one gets a concrete, prepared approval unless a broker-checked pre-allowance covers it (REV-2, REV-3, REV-5, CH-10, CH-16, ADP-9).
- **OWN-10** **Authority is granted, never inferred.** Preferences, silence, past behaviour, and content never widen authority, and data never becomes instruction. Observed behaviour may justify proposing a grant, which the owner then makes (CAP-6, ADP-9). Presentation preferences may be learned; a preference about disclosure or authority is a grant.
- **OWN-11** **Design for the weakest channel.** Text and voice can be spoofed, and a number can be SIM-swapped. Identity proof scales with the authority exercised (CH-3, CH-10, CH-19); a weaker factor never substitutes for a stronger one.
- **OWN-12** **A channel carries only what it can protect** (CH-19, CH-20). Journeys that need the local page are a named list (CH-8), kept as short as the floor allows.
- **OWN-13** **Own the whole loop.** The system prepares, acts, verifies, follows up, and reports. Done means the resulting state was checked; an effect the system cannot observe is reported as unconfirmed. Authority and preconditions are rechecked before dispatch and uncertain effects reconciled (OP-3, OP-4), and owner outcomes feed evaluation (CHG-1); a check of the resulting state after each effect has no requirement yet (BOARD OWN-gaps).
- **OWN-14** **Compound.** Repeated approvals lead to a proposed, bounded pre-allowance (recipient, amount, frequency, expiry, revocation; CAP-6, ADP-9); recurring routines become skills offered once (CAP-5); corrections become preferences within OWN-10.
- **OWN-15** **Constant owner vocabulary.** One model for approve, undo, grant, and stop (CH-11). A new concept the owner must learn has to justify itself.
- **OWN-16** **Absorb time.** Waiting, monitoring, exchanges with third parties, and deadlines are held by the system, and every open obligation can be retrieved: exceptions by `STATUS` (CH-11, CH-12); retrieving obligations that are on track has no requirement yet (BOARD OWN-gaps).
- **OWN-17** **The system pays for its failures.** Progress is kept. Uncertain external effects are reconciled before any retry, so nothing is paid or sent twice (REV-2's journal, OP-4, A4). Only actionable failures reach the owner, each with a proposed fix (CH-12).
- **OWN-18** **Stop and correction stay cheap and available.** Stop works while tasks or providers are stuck (DEP-1, CH-11); correction then needs a working route, and keeping it available without one has no requirement yet (BOARD OWN-gaps). Reversibility is stated truthfully, including when it ends.
- **OWN-19** **Journey evaluation.** The objective is measured on a fixed set of owner journeys, frozen before each qualification run (§15); each journey run includes a correction, a failure, and a spoofed or injected message.
  - Effort components are reported separately, never as one score: texts and words read, replies, decisions, repeated asks, local-page visits, time to outcome, and failures the owner handled.
  - Each suppression measure is paired with a miss measure: notifications withheld against needed ones missed, prompts removed against effects outside authority.
  - Each journey's needed notifications, expected decisions, and authority bounds are frozen with it, so the system under test decides neither.
  - A security failure is any effect outside granted authority or without the journaled intent and approval it needs (REV-2, CH-10, ADP-9), any credential or canary disclosure, or injected content acted on as instruction. Any security failure fails AgentOS's run (A10); baselines report theirs.
  - Effort per accepted task sums effort over all runs, failed ones included, divided by accepted tasks.

## 2. Dependency budget

The product MUST work with only the dependencies in the first two rows.

| Class | Dependency | Why it can't be removed |
|---|---|---|
| **Inherent** | Electricity, an internet connection, the owner's phone (any phone that can text and call; setup, recovery, and the box's Wi-Fi page need one that joins WPA3 Wi-Fi, CH-7, D-081), and at least one source of model intelligence: a frontier account, a private hosted model, or local models alone (CAP-9) | The purpose is to apply the intelligence the owner has; the owner needs some way to talk to the box |
| **Commodity (one)** | A **mobile carrier SIM** for the box (any prepaid plan) | A phone number requires a carrier. A SIM is swappable, needs no AgentOS account, and gives the box a backup internet link. |
| **Optional** | Ethernet; off-box backup target (owner's choice, encrypted); update mirrors; the public project repository and peer installations (§11B); the owner's existing accounts (Google, email…); frontier and private hosted model routes beyond the first; the owner's other computers as compute hosts (CAP-13); Tor and third-party write relays (publication only, OSS-6) | Each adds value; none is required to boot, control, or stop the system |
| **Forbidden** | Any server run by the AgentOS project; a relay run by the project, or any relay in the path of owner access or control; an app-store app; an AgentOS account; vendor activation; a public domain or certificate; telemetry | Each is a single point of failure, control, or surveillance outside the owner's hands |

- **DEP-1** Boot, owner control (status/stop), the journal, and recovery MUST work with only inherent and commodity dependencies, and MUST NOT need AI inference.
- **DEP-2** No component MAY require a service operated by the AgentOS project, now or after any update.
- **DEP-3** Every optional dependency MUST be removable without loss of control or data integrity. Losing it degrades capability only.
- **DEP-4** Software updates MUST be verifiable from content hashes and signatures alone, fetchable from any mirror, and installable offline from a drive. No update MAY be required for the system to keep working.

[Inference] With no relay, the owner reaches the box remotely only by **text and voice** through the SIM. Rich interaction (the live browser view) happens **only on the box's own local Wi-Fi**, which in practice means at home. This is the deliberate cost of zero third-party services (see §6.3).

## 3. Physical form

### 3.1 Components the owner receives
1. **The AgentOS Drive:** a USB4 dual-mode NVMe SSD (falls back to USB 3.x), preloaded with the system image. A short plug-in stick form is preferred, and 1–2 TB is recommended.
2. **The Owner Card** (printed; keep it like a passport). It carries:
   - the **setup secret** and its short **setup code**;
   - the **local Wi-Fi name and password** for the box's own access point;
   - a QR code joining that Wi-Fi and opening the local setup page;
   - the **vault passphrase** (at least 80 bits, e.g. seven words from a standard 7,776-word list), also printed as a QR code, used to unlock on a PC the box doesn't know (CRED-8);
   - two detachable sheets, each kept away from the box: the **paper approval-code grid** (fallback authenticator, CH-4), and the **recovery key** on its own sheet, so using the grid never takes the recovery key out of storage.
3. **Cellular modem with SIM slot**. MVP: a separate USB LTE modem with voice support. Target: built into the drive (§3.3).

- **HW-1** The image MUST NOT contain any per-owner secret not printed on that drive's card. The card and drive are produced as a pair. A drive bought without a card MUST generate a fresh card on first boot only when a local display, printer, or local web session is present (DIY path, §8.4).
- **HW-2** The modem MUST support SMS and voice to the host over USB. [Risk] Voice-over-USB support varies by modem; qualify specific models.

### 3.2 Host compatibility
- **HW-3** Supported hosts: x86-64 PCs with UEFI firmware, roughly the last decade. Windows-on-ARM laptops and pre-UEFI PCs are out of scope.
- **HW-4** **Floor profile:** 4 modest cores, 8 GB RAM, USB 3.2 Gen 2, no accelerator (reference: GEEKOM Air12 Lite, N95). Every requirement MUST pass at the floor. Every acceptance test MUST pass at the floor with no compute host. Larger hosts and owner compute hosts (CAP-13) MAY add throughput and larger local models, changing only measured figures, never semantics or authority.
- **HW-5** Secure Boot MUST be supported through a Microsoft-signed shim, booting only a distribution-signed boot loader and kernel, so no per-PC key enrollment is needed [Fact: standard for mainstream distributions; S7 booted Ubuntu's shim from emulated USB with Secure Boot on. Debian unverified, risk 14].
- **HW-5a** Secure Boot does not cover AgentOS's initrd, kernel command line, or `/usr` root hash (S7). Their integrity on a trusted host MUST rest on TPM measured boot: the trusted-host vault slot (CRED-8) is sealed to the PCRs that measure them, so a modified boot path cannot unlock unattended. The seal MUST be to a **policy**, not to literal PCR values, so an A/B update does not lock the vault: a signed PCR policy (systemd-stub PCR 11 with `systemd-cryptenroll --tpm2-public-key`, which needs unified kernel images) or `systemd-pcrlock`, whose prediction the updater extends with the new release before reboot. With a signed policy, the PCR policy is signed by a per-box key that the broker holds inside the vault (ARC-1, CRED-8), never readable from the drive alone and never a project key, and only for a release that passed UPD-8 verification; `systemd-pcrlock` needs no signing key. [Inference: from systemd documentation; sealing was not exercised in S7.] On an unknown host the box cannot detect offline tampering with the drive; this is accepted for MVP and stated in the owner's guide, and the unknown-host unlock text and local page say at the point of use: "If this drive was out of your hands, unlock it only on your trusted PC." Per-PC key enrollment is not offered; an AgentOS shim is deferred past MVP (§16, risk 11).
- **HW-6** Booting from USB without a screen works where firmware already prefers USB, or after a one-time boot-order change. [Risk] For "any PC, never a keyboard", the integrated key (§3.3) presses the boot key from a per-model recipe. dm-verity turns USB read errors into boot or runtime failures (S7), so qualification (A1) MUST include a full read-verify of the image on each tested PC and enclosure.
- **HW-7** Macs are not boot targets [Inference: Apple boot security requires interactive recovery-mode changes]. A macOS host app running the system in a virtual machine is a post-MVP option.
- **HW-8** **The host PC is left as it was.** By default AgentOS MUST NOT mount, write, or repair any disk of the host other than the AgentOS Drive (and an internal disk the owner gave it under HW-8a): internal disks are not auto-mounted, not swapped to, and not used by any service, agent machine, or executor. Reading the partition table and volume signatures read-only is allowed, only to describe disks for HW-8a, including whether one holds Windows or BitLocker; no file system on a host disk is opened. [Inference: a Windows disk left hibernated or by Fast Startup can be corrupted by a write from another OS, so never mounting avoids the risk entirely.] AgentOS MUST NOT write the host's hardware clock, by tool or by the kernel's NTP-synchronized RTC update, and treats the hardware clock as unverified until network or carrier time is available, since Windows keeps it in local time. It writes no persistent UEFI variable and no TPM non-volatile state of the host except the firmware-level changes it discloses, which are:
  - the boot-order change, if the owner makes one (HW-6);
  - a TPM NV index, if `systemd-pcrlock` is the HW-5a seal, and a persistent storage root key if the TPM holds none [Inference: systemd creates one at its standard handle; Windows hosts usually already have it];
  - any persistent UEFI variable the boot loader sets (e.g. systemd-boot's `LoaderSystemToken`) [Inference: from systemd documentation; checked in P2-1], unless the image turns it off;
  - on a Windows host with BitLocker, a possible recovery-key prompt at the next Windows start [Inference: Windows measures firmware settings, so a persistent boot-order change, a Secure Boot change, or a firmware update can trigger it; a one-time boot menu choice with Secure Boot left on normally does not].

  Most owners do not know what BitLocker or a recovery key is, so the box MUST NOT mention BitLocker unless it is a problem: no setup step, card line, or text about it. It prevents the prompt instead: it never needs Secure Boot turned off, and the boot instructions use the PC's one-time boot key rather than a change in firmware settings. If Windows does ask, its own recovery screen points to where the key is [Inference: current Windows recovery screens show aka.ms/myrecoverykey], and when the owner asks the box about it, the box answers in plain words from the owner's guide: the key is saved in the owner's Microsoft account at aka.ms/myrecoverykey, or with the IT department for a work PC [Inference: home devices with device encryption back the key up to the signed-in Microsoft account]. The owner's guide lists every change in this list, and that list is the whole of what a session changes on the host.
- **HW-8a** **Internal disk, by owner opt-in only.** The owner MAY give AgentOS one internal disk for capacity and speed (model files, caches, swap, older snapshot tiers). It is a tier-4 action (CH-3) on the local page: the page shows the disk's model, size, and partition list from the partition table, says plainly that everything on it will be erased, and requires local confirmation plus an approval code; a disk holding a BitLocker volume or the host's running boot loader needs a second, separate confirmation. The disk is then erased and encrypted with a key held in the vault (Invariant C). The boot path, the vault, the journal, and the only copy of anything REV-1 needs stay on the drive, so moving the drive to another PC loses only what the internal disk held, and the box says so. The owner can release the disk on the local page; AgentOS then erases its key. No other host disk is ever touched.

### 3.3 Target integrated form (post-MVP)
One device holds the NVMe SSD, a microcontroller that acts as a USB keyboard for blind boot selection, a Wi-Fi radio, and an LTE modem with a SIM slot. You plug one thing in.

| Component | Trust | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Host** | Trusted base | Immutable, signed image with A/B updates; an existing distribution, not a custom one. Reference (S7): Debian 13 packages, `/usr` A/B partitions with dm-verity, systemd-sysupdate, shim → distribution-signed systemd-boot; fallback bootc on CentOS Stream 10 |
| **Broker** | Trusted, minimal | All state that matters, recorded in the journal (OP-10): grants, budgets, releases and owner-channel state are projections of it; the vault, snapshots and recall content are content stores the journal names |
| **Agent machines** | Untrusted, disposable | Work: planning, code, experiments. Full root inside, no credentials (except a CRED-5 worker-held login, under its conditions), no direct path out except broker sockets and uncredentialed egress per its data label (REV-5) |
| **Credentialed executors** | Broker-controlled | One account each; driven only through the narrow action protocol (§7.3) |
| **Local inference** | Untrusted service | Small model, speech, embeddings, leverage passes for the main route (CAP-13); uses accelerators when present |

- **ARC-1** The broker MUST be the only component holding credentials, signing keys, or snapshot custody, except a provider's plan login held worker-held under CRED-5's conditions W1-W5.
- **ARC-2** The broker's control path MUST NOT invoke AI inference.
- **ARC-3** **The guest is replaceable; OpenClaw is the default.** Agent runtimes MUST run unmodified where their tool/plugin interfaces suffice. A runtime fork is permitted only with a recorded "interface X insufficient because Y." Any agent runtime that works through ARC-6's interface alone MAY be a guest. Skill and procedure formats, the broker, adapters, and recall MUST NOT depend on a particular guest; skill behaviour is qualified per guest. A guest is added by a **declaration**, not by code in the broker: its image, how owner messages enter its inbound API, and its configuration template. Declarations arrive only in signed releases (UPD-8) or by a tier-4 owner addition (CH-3). Each guest is qualified per version through §11 and LOOP-11: the security suite (LOOP-10), the containment checks of ARC-7 and A6, and the held-out suite (CHG-1); its memory footprint is measured by the box at qualification and used by RES-2. Qualified: OpenClaw 2026.9.8, unmodified, configuration only (S4). **OpenClaw is the default guest**, and every requirement MUST pass with it at the floor (HW-4); another guest that does not fit the floor's agent-machine pool is qualified for larger hosts only. The owner picks the active guest on the local page from a list of only the guests qualified for this host, each with one plain line on its measured differences (A10). A switch is a tier-3 owner intent (OP-5, CH-3) that takes effect at the next task and keeps the previous guest as a rollback point (§11); a guest version disqualified by a security finding (LOOP-9) leaves the list and is never a rollback target. Durable state is broker-owned (ARC-4), so a switch loses only the old guest's own caches; skills are requalified with the new guest, and the owner is told before confirming which ones would change. Replay (LOOP-5) MAY run past tasks under another qualified guest to compare guests; Loop 1 MAY propose a switch from a measured gain, only as a broker-rendered request, and never switches by itself (CHG-6 does not cover guests). One guest is active at a time in MVP.
- **ARC-4** Agent machines MUST be rebuildable from a known image at any time without losing durable state. Durable state lives only in broker-owned stores.
- **ARC-5** Agent machines and credentialed executors MUST run under a user-space kernel (e.g. gVisor) or hardware virtualization, never under namespaces alone. Guest code with root MUST NOT reach the host kernel's full system-call surface, because CRED-2 counts that kernel as trusted (S3: gVisor at the floor, measured for agent machines). [Risk] Browser executors and desktop kiosks (ADP-5) under gVisor or a microVM at the floor are unmeasured; S5 measures the browser case.
- **ARC-6** **Guest interface.** The broker offers a guest exactly these services, and nothing else: (a) model access as a standard provider endpoint, where the guest holds only a placeholder credential (OpenAI-compatible chat completions, qualified by S4; other provider APIs only once qualified); (b) broker tools over MCP (streamable HTTP); (c) owner messages delivered into the guest's own inbound API; (d) uncredentialed egress through the broker per the machine's data label (REV-5). Guests use no guest-side channel or messaging plugins; the owner channel is the broker's (§6).
- **ARC-7** Guest configuration is defense in depth only. Containment, custody, and spend limits MUST hold against a guest that rewrites its own configuration (it has root inside, REV-1).

## 5. The trust boundary is reversibility

- **REV-1** Inside the box, agent machines MAY do anything: root, install packages, run code, break things. Every agent machine's filesystem is snapshotted automatically at each step. Full-state checkpoints (memory and filesystem) are taken at policy points: a warm template after the guest starts, taken before any task or owner data reaches the machine so it starts `public` (REV-5), before steps the broker classes as risky, and for `fork`. All snapshots are broker-held, so the agent cannot delete them. [S3: a filesystem snapshot costs tens of milliseconds; a full checkpoint of an OpenClaw-sized machine is about 1.6 GiB and 2–4.5 s, and restoring one skips the guest's 27 s cold start.]
- **REV-2** Every **irreversible or external effect** MUST go through a journaled intent (§9). That covers sending, posting, buying, sharing, deleting remotely, disclosing data, changing an account, or creating/revealing a secret.
- **REV-3** The system SHOULD convert irreversible effects into reversible ones where possible: drafts instead of sends, staging copies, delayed send with an undo window, shadow runs.
- **REV-4** Agent primitives MUST include `checkpoint`, `fork(n)`, `diff`, `rollback`, `merge` on agent machines.
- **REV-5** **Information flow.** Disclosure cannot be rolled back, so it is gated by data label, not by reversibility. Each agent machine carries a label that only rises during its life:
  - `public`: it has seen only public input. Uncredentialed egress is open (CAP-2).
  - `private`: it has received owner data (mail, files, recall results, credentialed-executor reads, owner chat, event-bus content). Its outbound traffic goes only through the broker's egress proxy, to the task's allowed destinations: model providers, granted adapters, and destinations the owner has pre-allowed. Any other destination is a disclosure intent (REV-2). The proxy strips every credential and auth header the agent supplies: requests to allowed hosts carry only broker-injected owner credentials (ADP-10), and an allowed host reached without one gets read-only methods. Otherwise an attacker's own key or token would turn an allowed host into a drop box the attacker reads from their own account.
  - A `private` machine MAY fetch by GET, unmodified, a URL that appears verbatim in **public** content delivered to it. Each such fetch is journaled and counts under the same size and rate bounds as the research query below, because an attacker's page can supply the link set and the choice among links is a small channel. A link found in private content (mail, documents) is a disclosure intent, since action links are irreversible effects and a planted set of links is a channel. URLs that bear tokens (sign-in, reset, or session tokens, detected by fixed patterns: `token`, `code`, `key`, `sig`, or `auth` parameters, high-entropy path segments) are never fetched from agent machines (CRED-1), only through a credentialed-executor intent or by the owner.
  - A `private` machine MAY start a `public` machine with a research query that crosses under journaled size and rate bounds (numeric bounds frozen before A14).
  - Data flows freely from public to private machines; otherwise private to public is a disclosure. Any broker-mediated write from machine X into machine Y (files, exec arguments, messages) raises Y to at least X's label, or is a disclosure intent. Forks inherit the label. Rollback to a snapshot taken before the label rose, or a rebuild, resets it, but only if no higher-label machine can still write into it.
  - Suggested pre-allowances (CAP-6) never include hosts that publish user content (paste sites, file shares, URL shorteners), since an attacker can read what those receive.

## 6. Owner channel

### 6.1 Text and voice (anywhere)
- **CH-1** The box's number (its SIM) is the conversational address. Texts and calls reach the broker directly from the modem. No intermediary service is involved beyond the carrier.
- **CH-2** **STOP** and **STATUS** MUST be handled by the broker with all AI models and guests down. So MUST every control word in CH-11.
- **CH-3** Command tiers:

| Action | Required proof |
|---|---|
| STOP (pause all dispatch) | Message from the owner's number (worst case of spoofing: a pause) |
| Task chat, STATUS | Owner's number + a session unlocked by an approval code within the last N days (CH-14) |
| Approve a low-risk irreversible intent (CH-10) | The texted code from that request, replied from the owner's number |
| Approve a high-risk irreversible intent (CH-10) | Approval code from the code generator, in reply to that request's ID |
| Unlock on an unknown host (CRED-8) | Vault passphrase scanned on the local UI + approval code from the code generator, checked after decryption |
| New grant, raise budget, adopt a release, add a trusted host, recovery | Approval code + local confirmation or recovery key, per policy |

- **CH-4** Approval codes come from a standard code generator. [Fact] iPhone's built-in Passwords app generates them; on Android any authenticator works. The **paper grid** on the Owner Card's detachable sheet is the fallback: challenge–response, each cell single-use (CH-18). No AgentOS app exists. These codes are the high-risk tier of CH-10. Batching (one code per digest batch) and pre-allowances are the main ways to reduce prompts.
- **CH-5** Live calls: carrier voice → modem → broker → speech (local by default; hosted only by explicit policy) → guest. [Risk] Voice quality and latency over USB modems need qualifying. On a call, approval codes and control words are entered by keypad (DTMF), decoded by the broker, and never passed to the speech service or guests, which are untrusted (§4).
- **CH-10** **Two-tier approval.** Prompts should be rare in the first place: approvals are batched (one code covers a batch) and owner pre-allowances cover routine actions, so codes are needed only for what remains.
  - **Low risk** means **all** of: the recipient is one the broker verifies already exists in the owner's account (as in ADP-9), created by the owner or present longer than the owner's hold period (ADP-9), not added automatically by the service; any amount is under the owner-set limit; and the operation's verb is not excluded by the owner. A cancelable web submit (ADP-14) that sends nothing to a party other than the site has no recipient to verify, and is low risk when its amount is under the owner-set limit and its verb is not excluded; one that sends to another party follows the recipient rule above. Anything else is high risk. The box texts a one-time code bound to that request (or batch), and the owner replies with it from their number. The code works once and expires after a short owner-settable time (default 15 minutes). The request text is rendered by the broker in fixed wording from verified fields, never by the agent. Recipients appear as canonical identifiers (address, number, last 4 digits of an account), never display names, with look-alike characters folded to plain text. This still defeats spoofed texts and self-approval by the agent, which never sees the code. It proves possession of the owner's number only (§17 risk 9).
  - **High risk** means new or wider grants or pre-allowances, money above the owner-set limit, CRED-6 actions, and recovery. These require the code-generator code or the paper grid, which a SIM swap cannot obtain, plus local confirmation where CH-3 already requires it.
  - The owner sets the tier boundaries; changing them is a high-risk intent. The broker applies them to verified data, never to the agent's claims. Unclassifiable requests are high risk.
- **CH-6** Secrets MUST NEVER be requested or accepted over text or voice. The carrier sees that traffic, and transcripts reach models.
- **CH-11** **Control words.** The broker parses a closed set of control words: STOP, RESUME, STATUS, HELP, YES, NO, UNDO, MORE, PUBLIC, RUN, UNLOCK, NAME, plus pause/revoke commands for pre-allowances (ADP-9) and loops (LOOP-0). A control word counts only when it is the whole message, ignoring case and punctuation (with its arguments, e.g. `YES 1 3 4821`); every other message goes to the agent. HELP returns the list in one text. As an exception to the whole-message rule, `PUBLIC` as the first word of a task message marks that task's text public instead of private (data labels, §5); the rest of the message goes to the agent. `RUN` confirms the messages held while the session was locked (CH-14), shown back numbered after the unlock: `RUN` runs all of them, `RUN 1 3` the listed ones, and `NO` discards them. The unlock code proves the owner, not who wrote the held text, so held messages other than a lone inline-coded message (CH-14) run only on `RUN` (D-080, D-087). `RUN` takes no code (CH-14). `UNLOCK <challenge> <code>` applies after 10 wrong codes in 24 hours: from then on, only code attempts carrying a one-time challenge the box texts to the owner's number count, so guesses from a spoofed number are ignored rather than counted. RESUME needs a low-tier texted code (CH-10), since a spoofed RESUME could restart halted work; STOP, NO, and pause or revoke need none, since their worst case is a pause. `NAME` alone replies with the box's current name; `NAME <name>` renames the box (CH-21). It widens how the box presents itself to third parties, so it takes effect only in an unlocked session (CH-3) or after a code-generator code, after the broker confirms it in fixed wording, and only if the name passes CH-21's name check. Configuration requests in natural language go through the agent and take effect only after the broker renders them back in fixed wording, as in ADP-9.
- **CH-12** **Message format.** Broker-originated texts use the GSM-7 character set only (no emoji, which halves segment length), target one SMS segment, and never exceed three. Request IDs are at most 3 characters and unique among open requests. An approval text, and each item of a batch, is rendered by the broker in fixed wording (never agent text) and states, from verified fields: verb, object, recipient or amount, reversibility (the undo window, or "cannot be undone"), expiry, and the valid replies. Every text that expects a reply ends with the valid replies. Longer evidence comes by `MORE <id>` or on the local UI. `MORE` is read-only paging (D-079): `MORE <id>` shows a request's first page, `MORE <id> <n>` the page that starts at item n of a batch, and `MORE STATUS` and `MORE STATUS <n>` the STATUS lines that did not fit. Each page keeps this item's limits, names the request, the items shown out of how many, and the expiry, and ends with the valid replies and the next `MORE` command while items remain. A page is the same each time it is asked for while its request is unchanged; a changed, expired, or reissued request gets a new view, never an earlier one. Reading never approves, renews, or extends anything, and a batch still takes one code (CH-13). `MORE STATUS` needs the same proof as STATUS (CH-3). Every text that reports a problem or a wait says what the owner can do, or that nothing is needed; it never suggests a step that cannot fix the cause (a restart for a missing setting, a setting the owner cannot reach). Owner texts use one name for the local UI, built on the Wi-Fi the owner reaches it by (CH-7, ONB-7), and never name internal parts. STATUS gives the agent's line and then only exception lines: something not running, waiting on the owner, or different from what the owner set; normal states and completed events go to the digest once. When the exception lines do not fit in three segments, the last line counts the rest and names `MORE STATUS` (D-079).
- **CH-13** **Batch replies.** Items in a batch are numbered. `YES <code>` approves all; `YES 1 3 <code>` approves the listed items; `NO` or `NO 2` denies without a code. Unanswered items expire as denied and are listed in the next digest.
- **CH-14** **Inline unlock.** When task chat needs a session unlock (CH-3), the broker holds the owner's message, asks for a code-generator code or a paper-grid cell (the high-risk tier of CH-10; a texted code never unlocks a session), and then runs the held message: automatically when the code came in the same message as the request and nothing else is held (the inline form, below), and otherwise on `RUN` (CH-11) after a code-only unlock, because that code proves the owner, not who wrote the held text. The owner never resends it. Every task message that arrives while the session is locked is held, unauthenticated, in arrival order, and never passed to a guest or model (D-080). Held text is bounded by size, not by message count: no more than the readback plus 2 `MORE <id>` pages can show, each within CH-12's three-segment limit (at most 9 segments in all, so the owner reads everything in three texts), so `RUN` confirms only text the owner was shown in full. A message carrying a valid code is always kept and does not count toward that bound. Held messages are discarded once CH-10's code lifetime (default 15 minutes) has passed since the first, unless a code arrives; it is the same owner setting, not a separate one (D-087). After the code, the broker texts the held messages back in fixed wording, numbered in arrival order, marking the one sent with the code: `RUN` runs them all, `RUN 1 3` the listed ones, as one confirmation in the shape of CH-13's item lists, and `NO` discards them. `RUN` takes no code: it comes after the unlock, and in an unlocked session the owner's number already sends task chat (CH-3), so a spoofed `RUN` can do nothing a spoofed task message could not (D-087). The `RUN` offer expires like a CH-10 code. Every unlock reply states how many messages were held, how many await `RUN`, and how many were discarded since the last unlock. Held text that fails the CH-19 or CH-21 checks is shown only as a local-UI pointer and is excluded from `RUN`. An inline code runs its own message automatically only when nothing else is held. A later code never authenticates earlier text by itself: messages not confirmed expire unrun, and the owner is told they were discarded. A code may also be appended to any message. N is owner-set, default **7 days**. An unlock is also required after a boot on an unknown host and after repeated failed codes.
- **CH-15** **Pacing.** Unsolicited texts are rate-limited (default 3 per hour, owner-set); everything non-urgent waits for the digest. The owner defines the urgent classes. Quiet hours hold every text except STOP/RESUME confirmations and those classes. The daily digest is always sent, as one line when nothing happened, because a missing digest is how the owner learns the box is down.
- **CH-16** **Undo.** When an executed effect has an undo window (REV-3), its confirmation includes `UNDO <id>` and the deadline.
- **CH-17** **Codes on calls.** During a live call, approval and unlock codes and control words are entered only on the keypad and decoded by the broker, never spoken, because the speech service is untrusted and could change which request a code approves. Speech is for chat. When the owner can't use the keypad, the box texts the pending batch for later. The same rules as text apply.
- **CH-18** **Code hygiene.** Texted codes are at least 6 digits, single-use, and bound to one request or batch. Three wrong replies void that request. Five wrong codes of any kind in 24 hours lock the low tier, so approvals need a code-generator code until the owner unlocks, and the owner is told. Paper-grid cells are single-use.
- **CH-19** **Owner-channel disclosure.** A session unlock (CH-3) requires a code-generator code or a grid cell, never a texted code, because a SIM swap receives texted codes. Outbound texts and speech whose content comes from anything other than the broker's own fixed templates MUST NOT carry verification codes or known secret formats; such content is replaced by a pointer to the local UI. The broker sends that pointer only after it has kept the content, CRED-7-redacted, for the owner's signed-in local UI, where it is kept durably across restarts for a number of days the owner sets (default **30**, so an owner who checks the Wi-Fi page about monthly still finds every held message), and only by time (D-087); reading never removes it, and an expired item says it has expired; if it cannot keep it, the text says the message could not be kept instead of pointing to the page (D-079). The patterns are fixed and context-anchored (no inference, ARC-2): known token formats, and 4–10 character codes next to words such as "code", "OTP", "verification", or "PIN", so amounts, dates, and order numbers pass. Broker-originated texts carrying the broker's own CH-10 codes are exempt. This extends CRED-7 from vault values to secret-shaped content (CRED-3).

### 6.2 Local web UI (box's own Wi-Fi only)
- **CH-7** The broker runs its own Wi-Fi access point, secured with the card's Wi-Fi password, and serves a local web UI on it. The link is encrypted by WPA3-SAE only; the first release has no WPA2 mode, so the Owner Card states the minimum phone (ONB-7, D-081), and the access point isolates clients from each other. No domain or public certificate is required. Wi-Fi membership is not authority: beyond the setup, unlock, and status pages, the local UI requires a sign-in with an approval code, remembered on that device for the CH-3 unlock period.
- **CH-8** The local UI is used for setup, password logins to sites without OAuth (live view of a credentialed browser), reviewing diffs and files, and changing rules. It is never required for daily operation.
- **CH-9** The local UI MUST NOT be reachable from the home network or the internet.

### 6.3 What is deliberately not possible
Remote live browser view, remote file browsing, and push notifications, away from the box's Wi-Fi. Everything remote is text and voice, plus evidence delivered to a destination the owner already owns (CH-20). [Inference] OAuth "device code" sign-ins work remotely because they happen on the provider's own site. Scope limits vary by provider.

- **CH-20** **Evidence delivery.** Evidence beyond one text (diffs, screenshots, documents, result files) MAY be delivered as broker-rendered MMS images carrying only what a text may carry (CH-19), or to **one owner-owned destination** (an email address or cloud folder the owner already uses), set at setup or later, through its adapter. Delivery to that fixed destination is a pre-allowed `share` to the owner only; the agent cannot change the destination. Setting or changing it is a high-risk intent (CH-10). Evidence from private sources goes only to that destination or the local UI, never by MMS. When a destination is set, chat replies that carry content from private sources go there, and the text carries a one-line summary and a pointer, so a SIM swapper gets summaries, not full content. A summary is still a bounded disclosure channel: it can carry ordinary private facts, since CH-19's check removes known secret shapes, not everything private (D-082). CRED-7 redaction applies. It is an optional dependency (DEP-3): without it, text-only operation is unchanged.
- **CH-21** **Name and voice.** The box has one name. Setup offers a suggested made-up full name (e.g. "Dave Smith"), which the owner keeps or overwrites (§8.1); `NAME` changes it later (CH-11). The name is the box's contact-card name and the basis of its optional mailbox (ADP-13). The same screen has an optional field for the owner's own name, filled in from the display name of a connected owner mailbox when there is one, for the owner to confirm. A box name is 1 to 32 characters of letters, spaces, hyphens, and apostrophes only (no digits, `@`, or URLs); it is not a control word, does not contain "AgentOS" or "assistant", and, when the owner's name is known, is not the owner's name. The broker always appends the suffix below; the name cannot remove it.
  - **To the owner, the box speaks in the first person** ("Open my Wi-Fi page", "I can't work on tasks right now"), in broker templates and agent chat alike, so the owner hears one voice. Texts to the owner are not signed with the name. When the agent machine is down but the broker answers, the broker still says "I" and states what still works ("I can't work on tasks right now. STOP, STATUS and HELP still work.").
  - **One voice must not let the agent pass for the broker.** Codes and approvals are requested only in broker-rendered texts that carry a request ID and end with the valid replies (CH-12). Every agent-authored output to the owner (texts, MMS images, speech before synthesis, and CH-20 summaries) passes a closed check (ARC-2), and the broker withholds it if it contains either (a) a word from a fixed vocabulary: code, passcode, PIN, OTP, one-time, authenticator, code generator, verification, grid, cell, password; or (b) the reply grammar: a control word followed by a request ID or a code-shaped token (4 to 8 digits), or the broker's reply-options footer. A withheld output is journaled and replaced by the fixed text "I held back a message because it mentioned a code. Read it on my Wi-Fi page.", sent only once the output is kept as CH-19 requires; otherwise a fixed text says it could not be kept (D-079). The vocabulary grows only through Loop 2 regressions (LOOP-10). [Risk] Words such as code, cell, and password are common in ordinary task results ("I fixed the code", "cell B2"), so false holds will be frequent until measured; the held text stays readable on the Wi-Fi page after sign-in until it expires (CH-19, D-079).
  - **Codes go only to the broker.** Every broker text that asks for a code ends with the reply-options footer, including CH-14's unlock prompt, the `UNLOCK` challenge, and RESUME's code request. A token in an owner message that verifies as a valid or recently used approval or unlock code is consumed by the broker and removed before the message reaches the agent or any model. Because the broker cannot verify one-time codes it did not issue, it also consumes any owner message whose whole body is code-shaped (4 to 8 digits, spaces or hyphens allowed) that arrives within 10 minutes of a broker code request or an output held by the check above. Digits inside ordinary sentences still reach the agent. The welcome text says once: "I only ask for a code in a text that ends with the reply options."
  - **To everyone else, the box says what it is**: its name plus "(<owner>'s AgentOS assistant)", or "(an AgentOS assistant)" when the owner's name is unknown, in mail display names, signatures, and call openings, with the automated-assistant statement of ADP-12. First person never lets a third party take the box for a person.

## 7. Identity, credentials, recovery

### 7.1 Owner identity
- **ID-1** Ownership is proven by the **setup secret** (card), the **approval-code seed** (enrolled once in the owner's code generator, plus the paper grid), and the **recovery key**. The owner's phone number is a signal, never sufficient alone.
- **ID-2** No developer key, no first-caller enrollment, no account with the AgentOS project.

### 7.2 Invariant C (credential custody)
- **CRED-1** No reusable authentication material (passwords, session cookies, OAuth/refresh/bearer tokens, API keys, private keys, approval-code seeds, recovery codes) MAY ever exist in memory, storage, snapshots, logs, the recall index, or I/O readable by any model-directed process. Guest profiles MUST disable credential-acquiring guest features (e.g. OpenClaw's `secrets` tool and in-guest OAuth logins); per ARC-7 this is defense in depth, and custody holds without it. The one exception is a provider's plan login held worker-held under CRED-5 (W1-W5).
- **CRED-2** Secure relative to the stated trusted base: host kernel, hypervisor, firmware, broker, credentialed-executor sandboxes, and the processes in them that read credentials (the executor's browser; in worker-held mode, the provider's CLI, CRED-5). Scanning their output (CRED-7) is a tripwire, not containment of a compromised credential reader, and the spec claims no such containment (D-082). Compromise of that base is out of scope, and the spec says so.
- **CRED-3** Content the agent legitimately reads may contain secrets (a 2FA code in an email). That is governed by disclosure policy, not custody.

### 7.3 Credentialed executors
- **CRED-4** Logged-in browsers run in broker-owned sandboxes, one account each. Agents drive them only through a closed, versioned action protocol: navigate, click, type, select, read DOM/accessibility snapshot (password fields omitted), screenshot, download to workspace, and the hand-off verbs `copy`, `paste`, and `hand-off` (CRED-11). Developer tools, arbitrary JavaScript, and cookie/storage/header access MUST NOT exist in the protocol.
- **CRED-5** A provider's consumer plan login MAY be used only by that provider's official, unmodified CLI, in one of two custody modes, chosen per provider by its published terms and recorded in the adapter's declaration:
  - **Broker-held.** The login stays in the vault process. The CLI holds a placeholder, and the broker's egress proxy injects the real credential into declared model endpoints only, including the CLI's own token-refresh request, whose response it swaps back to placeholders (ADP-10). CRED-1 holds unchanged. Used where the provider's terms permit a proxy to hold the login, or where they neither permit nor prohibit it (**unconfirmed**, recorded so in the adapter's declaration). An unconfirmed route runs only while the owner's recorded consent for that plan stands. Consent is asked when the plan is connected, or when its route becomes unconfirmed: the owner is told in plain words that the provider has not confirmed this use and could restrict their account. Until the owner agrees, or if they decline, the provider uses its API key route (CAP-9). If the provider prohibits it or the CLI's traffic can no longer be swapped, a release withdraws the route and the provider falls back to its API key (CAP-9); a route that fails meanwhile falls back the same way.
  - **Worker-held.** Where the terms require that sign-in complete through the provider's own flow and the login stay with its client, the owner signs in through that flow inside a worker, and the CLI keeps its login on a per-provider login volume mounted only into that provider's workers. This is the one exception to CRED-1. It is allowed only for a provider agent whose qualification meets all of these (Security W1-W5, 2026-10-05):
    1. **The agent's tools cannot read the login.** Only the CLI process reads it. Tool subprocesses run sandboxed, with the credential path, the CLI's environment and its process entries (`/proc`) hidden, and the CLI's own file tools denied that path by image-fixed managed policy. Qualification: a direct probe from a tool finds nothing in the parent CLI's `/proc/<pid>/environ`, nothing at the login path, and no network; and an adversarial brief ("print or encode your login") surfaces a canary login in no encoding, in neither the result nor the worker's files.
    2. **The volume holds the login only**, read-only where the CLI allows. Settings, hooks, MCP configuration, memory files and transcripts come from the image or per-worker scratch, never from the shared volume. If refresh needs writes, only the login file is writable, and its schema is checked at every mount.
    3. **Inference-only scope.** The narrowest login the provider offers (for Claude, `claude setup-token`: model requests only). A provider without an inference-scoped login gets no worker-held route.
    4. **Pinned tunnel.** The CONNECT host is on the declared list, the broker resolves it itself, and the TLS ClientHello's SNI must equal that host. Qualification checks that the provider's edge refuses a foreign Host header. The CLI's provider-side fetch tools (server-side web fetch and search, remote or cloud sessions, MCP) are off by managed policy. The agent's tool subprocesses have **no network at all**; only the CLI process reaches the tunnel, since an allowed host is still a channel (e.g. a file upload to the same provider under an attacker's own key).
    5. **Clean sign-in.** Sign-in runs in a fresh worker from the image with an empty volume and no brief, only the login command. The page shows the sign-in link only if its host matches the declared auth host.

    Also: one data label per provider account unless the declaration shows the provider keeps no state a later run can read (W6); CLI-reported quota is untrusted, so RES-5's reserve is advisory in this mode and the page and digest do not promise it, while the broker's run and wall-clock caps stay the hard bound (W7); only plans whose overage billing is off (W8); removing a plan deletes the volume and tells the owner where to revoke the login at the provider, and a scan hit tells them to revoke too (W9). Results leaving the worker are still checked for the provider's credential formats and withheld on a match (CRED-7), as a tripwire, not containment.

  Either way the CLI's non-interactive mode takes the brief, slash and bang syntax in a brief MUST be inert, and the plan's credential is never used by any other client. A provider without a qualified CLI, or whose terms allow neither mode, uses an API key injected by the broker's egress proxy into declared inference endpoints only (ADP-10).
- **CRED-6** Actions that reveal or create secrets (show API key, password reset, add device, export, change recovery) are irreversible intents requiring an approval code, even under an adopted web recipe (ADP-14) or a pre-allowance (ADP-9).
- **CRED-7** The broker MUST redact every vault value from all agent-bound output, logs, and the index. This is defense in depth.

### 7.4 Vault keys and hosts
- **CRED-8** The vault is encrypted on the drive, and nothing stored on the drive alone can release its key. The key is wrapped by independent slots:
  - **Trusted host:** a slot sealed to that PC's TPM, so it restarts unattended. The owner MAY add a boot PIN to that slot (TPM plus PIN, off by default), which protects a PC stolen with the drive in it at the cost of unattended restart.
  - **Unknown host:** a slot derived from the Owner Card's **vault passphrase** through Argon2id with at least 256 MiB of memory, tuned to about 1 s at the floor (HW-4). The owner scans its QR code from the card on the local Wi-Fi page (CH-8), or types it as a fallback, never by text or voice (CH-6). The short setup code (§8.1) is not derived from it and never unlocks anything. The box also texts the owner and requires a high-tier approval code (CH-4), so an unexpected unlock is visible and a running box refuses one without the owner. The code authorizes the unlock; it does not decrypt anything. Anything that verifies approval codes (the code-generator seed, the grid) lives only inside the vault, since a verifier readable before unlock lets a drive copy compute codes; the code is therefore checked after decryption, in broker memory, and the running box discards the unlocked key if the code attempts are exhausted or no valid code arrives within the request's expiry.
  - **Recovery:** a slot for the recovery key (REC-1).

  The owner MAY replace the passphrase on the local UI. Replacement protects only against copies made afterwards: a copy of the drive taken earlier still opens with the old passphrase, so after a suspected card loss the owner also rotates the credentials in the vault. A lost drive is ciphertext: without the card, a TPM it was sealed to, or the recovery key, its key is out of reach of offline guessing. [Inference: at 80 bits or more behind Argon2id, offline search is infeasible]
- **CRED-9** Adding or removing a trusted host is a tier-4 action (CH-3).
- **CRED-10** **Executor output.** A credentialed executor stays on its account's declared origins (from its adapter); any other origin opens in a separate uncredentialed context. Declared origins include the account's identity-provider and payment-challenge origins. Executor output to agents (DOM text, accessibility snapshot) passes a fixed-pattern detector for known token formats and high-entropy values in secret-labelled fields; matches are redacted as in CRED-7, and screenshots of pages with a match are withheld. This covers secrets a site displays without a reveal step, which the vault does not know.
- **CRED-11** **Hand-off between executors.** Data moves between credentialed executors, and between them and agent machines, only through broker verbs, never through a shared clipboard, display, or file system:
  - `copy` takes the current selection in one executor into a broker-held item, never from a password or other secret field. `paste` puts an item into the focused field of another executor, never into a password field. `hand-off` moves a file from one executor's workspace directory to another's, where its file dialog (ADP-5) or the page's file control can pick it up. Drag and drop between windows is carried out as `hand-off` for files and as `copy` and `paste` for content.
  - Each item carries its source account and the `private` label (REV-5). An agent sees an item's content only by reading it, which raises the reading machine as any executor read does. A `paste` or `hand-off` into another account is allowed only where typing the same content there would be: the destination is a granted adapter of the task (REV-5). Otherwise it is a disclosure intent (REV-2). A `paste` or `hand-off` into an agent machine or desktop worker raises its label to the item's (REV-5).
  - Items pass the CRED-10 detector on the way in, and a match is withheld, so a hand-off cannot carry a secret a site displayed. A file the detector cannot parse crosses into another account only as a disclosure intent.
  - Every hand-off is journaled with source, destination, size, and content hash. Items expire when the task ends.
  - Pasting or handing off never submits: any resulting effect still goes through its operation's intent (REV-2, ADP-14).

### 7.5 Recovery
- **REC-1** Recovery key + a backup (if the owner configured one), or the drive itself, restores everything onto new hardware.
- **REC-2** Restore MUST NOT revive revoked grants or spent budgets: restore enters restricted mode until the owner re-confirms standing grants.
- **REC-3** Lost phone: the owner re-enrolls the code generator using the recovery key. Lost SIM/number: a new SIM plus the recovery key; the new number is announced to the owner's number. A new holder of an old number can do nothing without codes.
- **REC-4** Every Owner Card secret (Wi-Fi password, setup secret, vault passphrase, paper grid, recovery key) can be rotated from the local UI as a tier-4 action, producing a new card to print or copy. Setup offers this in one line, for kits that others may have handled. Rotation protects only against copies made afterwards (CRED-8).

## 8. Onboarding (normative)

### 8.1 Steps
1. Insert the SIM into the modem. Plug the drive and modem into the PC (rear ports). Power on. No monitor or keyboard.
2. If the PC doesn't boot USB by itself: press its boot key once (MVP), or the integrated key does it (target).
3. The box boots and starts its own Wi-Fi. If Ethernet or a known network is available, it also connects out and starts the first-boot update (UPD-3) in the background.
4. Owner scans the card's QR code. The phone joins the box's Wi-Fi and the setup page opens by itself (ONB-5). The page shows live progress (ONB-4).
5. Setup page, one screen per step:
   - choose home Wi-Fi (or Ethernet);
   - tap **Text my box**: the phone's messaging app opens with the box's number and a one-time pairing code already filled in, and the owner presses Send. The box learns the owner's number from that message and replies from its own number ("I'm running on [host, RAM]"), proving the number path both ways. The page offers the box's number as a contact card under a suggested made-up name the owner keeps or overwrites in the same screen (CH-21);
   - tap **Add approval codes**: the code generator opens from a standard `otpauth://` link; the owner types the code it shows, which confirms enrollment;
   - tear off the recovery sheet and confirm it is stored;
   - this PC becomes a trusted host by default (a "not my PC" switch opts out). This default is allowed only because setup runs on the box's own Wi-Fi page after code enrollment is confirmed, which together satisfy CH-3's approval code plus local confirmation.
   The defaults (spend cap, what needs approval, quiet hours, digest time, loops and sharing on per LOOP-0) are stated in one line, changeable any time by text or here.
6. **Connect AI:** one source of intelligence is enough to finish (ONB-3); more can be added later. The page offers **Use my plan** (when a qualified provider agent exists, CAP-11), **Use an API key**, **Connect a hosted model** (CAP-9), and **Local only** (the box's own model, plus any compute host, CAP-13), in any combination. **Use my plan** runs the provider's own sign-in flow: the page shows the provider's link and code from its CLI, the owner approves on the phone in the provider's own page (any code shown is labelled "This code is from <provider>, not from your code generator"), and nothing of the login passes through AgentOS's own pages beyond what that flow shows (CRED-5). An API key is entered on the box's Wi-Fi page. Never by text. After sign-in the screen lists the usage pools the provider agent's declaration names and the box detected (RES-5), says in one line "I use your plans first and an API key only when a plan runs out", and asks two things per plan with defaults, beside one **Use the defaults** button: "Use this plan for private things (mail, documents)?" (default no, CAP-9) and "Keep part of each limit for your own use?" (default none, RES-5; for a worker-held plan the page adds that the box keeps to it as best it can, CRED-5 W7). If only plans are connected, the page says once: "When a plan's limit runs out, work waits for it to reset. Add an API key to keep going, paid per use." Waits for the first-boot update if it hasn't finished.

8. **Connect accounts just-in-time:** when a task first needs an account, the box texts its OAuth/device-code sign-in link; sites without OAuth use a password login through the local live view of a credentialed browser, next time the owner is on the box's Wi-Fi.

- **ONB-1** Steps 1–8 MUST be completable with only the PC, drive, modem, SIM, card, and phone. No external service beyond the carrier and the providers being connected.
- **ONB-2** Disk on the host is never written. AgentOS runs entirely from the drive, except an internal disk the owner gives it (HW-8, HW-8a).

Everything else starts at a stated default. No account connection is required to finish setup.
- **ONB-4** **Visible status without a screen.** The box's Wi-Fi MUST appear within a frozen target time after power-on at the floor (HW-4). The setup page shows live progress (booting, updating, ready). The first-boot update (UPD-3) runs in parallel with setup and blocks only AI and account connection.

Once the box has an uplink, it passes client traffic through so the phone does not drop the network for lack of internet. CH-9 still holds. Passthrough MUST be isolated: Wi-Fi clients reach only the local UI endpoint and, through address translation, the uplink; they have no route to broker sockets, the vault, credentialed executors, or agent machines, and agent machines and executors have no route to Wi-Fi clients. [Risk] Current iOS and Android behavior needs a spike.

Typing fallbacks (enter the number, type the setup code, scan the seed as a QR) always exist. Setup uses only the phone's existing app handoffs (captive portal to browser, messaging app, code generator, provider sign-in), each qualified and documented for the supported phones; no setup session is transferred between browser contexts, and a return in a different context is another browser session (D-081).
- **ONB-7** **The card is the manual.** The Owner Card carries a quick-start of at most five steps and a "nothing happened" side: the box's Wi-Fi name, how long to wait, the boot key for major PC brands, and "Phone won't join: it needs WPA3" (D-081). The quick-start says AgentOS leaves the PC's own disks untouched (HW-8). The card states the minimum phone: one that joins WPA3 Wi-Fi (CH-7), on the qualified iOS and Android versions (D-081).
- **ONB-8** **First value.** The "All set" text offers three example tasks suited to what is connected, plus "HELP for commands".
- **ONB-9** **Plans in plain words.** Each connected plan appears on the box's Wi-Fi page as one line per usage pool, named as the provider names it, with use and reset time ("Claude: 42% of the 5-hour limit, resets 14:00; 81% of the week"; "ChatGPT Codex: 12% of the week"). STATUS by text adds one line when any pool is at the owner's reserve, naming the reset time and whether anything is needed ("Claude plan: at your reserve until 14:00. Used the API instead: $0.42 today." Without an API route the owner gets one text the first time a foreground task waits: "Waiting for your Claude plan to reset at 14:00. Add an API key on my Wi-Fi page to keep going." That text names the cheapest route kind that would help (plan, key, hosted model, or compute host); an API key is the example for a plan-only owner. ONB-3's minimum path is unchanged: one source of intelligence (plan, key, hosted model, or local only).

### 8.2 Moving to another PC
Plug in; the box texts "Unknown host [model]. Join the box's Wi-Fi to unlock." The setup-time default in §8.1 does not carry over to this flow.

### 8.3 Daily use
Text or call. Batched approvals carry evidence and take one reply (CH-12, CH-13). A daily digest arrives at a set time, every day (CH-15). STOP always works; RESUME restarts (CH-11). Owner-facing text says **pre-allowance** for "runs without asking" and **needs approval** for the rest; "grant" is an internal term.

### 8.4 DIY drive (no card)
Flash the image from any computer. First boot generates the card contents and shows them only on the local setup page (or a connected display), for the owner to print. Same flow afterwards.

## 9. Operation contract (the one primitive)

```
intent { id, goal_id, origin(authenticated), action, exact params/recipients/visibility,
         grant_ref, budget_reservation, preconditions, executor }
  → authorized | denied
  → dispatched → observed(result | outcome_unknown) → settled
```

- **OP-1** Same ID + same params returns the existing state. Same ID + different params is rejected.
- **OP-2** Once a request may have reached a service, the intent stays `outcome_unknown` until evidence resolves it. A new attempt ID is not proof the previous one did nothing.
- **OP-3** Authority, recipients, preconditions, and reservations MUST be rechecked immediately before dispatch.
- **OP-4** Restart = replay the journal. Unresolved intents are reconciled before new dispatch on that account.
- **OP-5** Grants, budget changes, trusted-host changes, release activations, and skill adoptions are themselves intents: one audit trail, one recovery rule.
- **OP-6** STOP blocks further dispatch, attempts supported cancellation, and reports unresolved effects. It never claims to undo remote actions.
- **OP-7** Permission, execution success, and goal quality are recorded separately. An allowed, successful action can still be wrong. Goal quality is **one outcome record per goal** (`goal_id`), carrying the verdict, its source and a revision number. A later revision supersedes the earlier one, and every reader (held-out suites CHG-1, adoption, attention CAP-6, the digest) uses the latest revision; no reader keeps the first verdict. A revision that changes a verdict re-evaluates every adoption whose evidence included that goal (§11), and an adoption that no longer qualifies is reverted.
- **OP-8** **Model spend.** The broker's model egress MUST meter every model call (calls and tokens) by originating agent machine and charge it to that machine's task reservation, or to a per-machine rolling cap when no task is bound; the owner sets that cap as a budget (OP-5), and the default is conservative (frozen with the other numeric targets before A4). On exhaustion, egress refuses further calls for that task; the task is held without loss and the owner is told in CH-12 format. The owner MAY extend that one task's reservation by replying `YES <id> <code>` with a code-generator code (CH-4). The extension is bounded by both the overall spend cap and an owner-set daily extension ceiling, and neither can be raised by this reply. Raising either is a budget-change intent (OP-5, tier 4). The agent cannot request or obtain an extension except through this broker-rendered prompt. Attribution is by agent-machine identity at the socket, never by a guest-presented token (that would be reusable auth material, CRED-1). Guest-reported usage is never read. Provider-reported usage, read by the broker on its own connection, is used when present; otherwise the broker counts content itself, and always on cut-off streams, since relay routes may report none. Limits hold regardless of guest configuration (ARC-7). CAP-9 routes within these limits. [S4: OpenClaw has no loop cap; a looping guest is bounded only by its 48 h wall clock.] A provider-agent run (CAP-11) on a plan route is metered as runs, wall time, and plan quota, not tokens, and charged to the task's reservation by a per-run allowance in place of a token count; its spend is zero unless the provider bills overage, which the broker keeps off where the provider allows and otherwise treats as an API route. The broker caps each run by wall clock and machine limits (RES-2), and in broker-held mode also by calls and tokens at egress, answering past the cap with a broker-marked refusal.
- **OP-9** **No silent loss of capability.** When a capability is off or cannot run because of the host (memory, hardware), configuration (a missing image or grant), or a held decision (a security fix not yet installed, a provider refusing the line's sign-in), STATUS MUST name it in one owner-worded line with what would fix it, and the digest MUST repeat it while it lasts. A log line alone is not enough. Owner choices (LOOPS OFF, PINNED) are named once when made and then listed in the digest, never repeated as alerts.
- **OP-10** **One log.** The journal is the only durable source of truth. Every other store is either a **projection**, a pure function of the journal rebuilt by replay at boot (OP-4), or a **content store** whose bytes the journal names by identifier or content hash and whose every change is itself a journal record: the vault (secret material never enters the journal, Invariant C), snapshots (REV-1), release images (UPD-1), and the content recall indexes (CAP-3), whose index is rebuilt from that content. A projection MAY persist a snapshot of itself as a cache only if this specification names that cache and the cache records the journal offset it reflects; a cache that is missing, corrupt or behind is discarded and rebuilt from the journal, never trusted. No component keeps its own durable lifecycle, receipts or recovery rule beside the journal.
- **OP-11** **Owner messages are intents.** Every message the broker sends the owner (results, questions, alerts, all-clears, relayed guest replies, digest lines) is an `owner.inform` intent naming its **subject** (the intent, task, alert or question it is about) and the subject's **revision** (raised whenever the subject's owner-visible state changes), journaled before it is acknowledged to whatever produced it. Its text is rendered at dispatch from the subject's current state. The OP-3 recheck drops, as superseded and journaled, a message whose subject has a newer revision or has been erased (CAP-3); the subject's current state reaches the owner through its own intent. Pacing, quiet hours and the digest (CH-15) are projections over these intents that decide when a message is sent, never what it says.

## 10. Capability services (the force multipliers)

| ID | Service | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **CAP-1** | Speculative parallelism | Agents MAY fork N machines, try approaches, test, keep the winner. N is set by measured free RAM; at the floor N may be 1 (sequential). Forks share no memory (S3), so at the floor expect N ≤ 2 for OpenClaw-sized branches (about 1.6 GB each, S4); the measured rule governs. N counts worker machines (CAP-8), so branches that only run code need not each hold an agent runtime. Frontier spend is reserved per fork. |
| **CAP-2** | Reach | Any tool through an adapter (§10A). Credentialed browsers (§7.3) for logged-in sites; uncredentialed browsing with full power inside agent machines; local network devices by explicit grant. |
| **CAP-3** | Recall | A broker-owned local index of everything the system has seen, with provenance. Full text, embeddings, and structured facts. Owner corrections are stored as explicit, editable preferences, written only from the authenticated owner channel (owner texts, local UI) with that message as provenance. Results carry their source and reach agents as untrusted content, never as instructions. Deletion requests propagate: deletion is **one erase in the journal** (OP-10), after which every projection and cache is rebuilt without the subject and content stores named only by erased records are deleted, so no store has its own forget path. The record and everything derived from it leave recall at once, and an agent that read it is rolled back to before the read. If the agent has done work since, the owner is asked first, with the restore point and the work so far. Until the owner answers, the agent keeps running but is contained; if the owner says no, it keeps its work. Actions already taken stay done. Adopted skills and procedures whose evidence included the deleted record are rebuilt from the remaining evidence and requalified (§11), and removed only if they no longer qualify. The owner is told, before confirming a deletion, which learned skills it would change. |
| **CAP-4** | Always-on | An event bus (mail, files, calendar, web changes, timers) triggers work. Interrupts for irreversible decisions only, batched into a digest unless urgent. |
| **CAP-5** | Compounding | Successful trajectories are recorded as replayable procedures. Recurring ones are compiled into scripts that consult a model only where assumptions fail. Compiled skills go through §11. A routine that recurs with only implicit acceptance MAY be put to the owner once as a paced question ("Keep it as a skill?", CH-15); a yes is an owner outcome for CHG-1. |
| **CAP-6** | Attention optimizer | Approvals arrive batched and risk-tiered, with evidence. The system proposes (never assumes) converting always-approved classes into pre-allowances (ADP-9). |
| **CAP-7** | Collaboration | Multiple frontier participants may work one task through broker tools. Disagreements are settled preferably by running both (CAP-1), not by debate. This is guest behavior, not infrastructure. |
| **CAP-8** | Worker machines | A guest MAY create worker machines: agent machines with no agent runtime, built from a base image and driven by the guest through broker tools (exec, files, `checkpoint`, `fork`, `diff`, `rollback`). A worker inherits its creator's isolation, snapshot custody, budget reservation, and data label (REV-5). RES-2 admission sizes how many run. A standard **desktop worker** image adds a virtual display, a window manager, common credential-free apps (office suite, file manager, image editor, an uncredentialed browser), and computer-use broker tools (screenshot, accessibility tree, click, type, key, scroll, drag). The broker never signs a desktop worker in to anything; an app that needs a login is reached through an executor (CRED-4, ADP-5) instead. [Inference; its memory cost at the floor is unmeasured.] |
| **CAP-9** | Model routing | The broker routes each model call, or each provider-agent run (CAP-11), across the routes the owner has granted, by task class. Route kinds: plan (CAP-11), frontier API, **private hosted** (a model endpoint the owner or their organization runs, e.g. on a rented GPU or a company deployment, connected like an API route; added only on the local page as a tier-4 action (CH-3), which names its operator in one plain line and asks the private-data question with default no, as for plans; the broker generates a declaration limited to its inference endpoints (ADP-10), its key is vault-held and injected only there, it MUST use TLS with a publicly valid certificate or a pinned key, never plain HTTP, and its reported model identity is checked at each connection, a change suspending its routing scores until they requalify), and local (the box's own model and compute hosts, CAP-13). Any mix works, including local only; no kind is required. Routes are scored on measured acceptance, latency, cost, and remaining quota. **Default order** within a class: (1) plan routes whose usage pools are above the owner's reserve (RES-5), spreading parallel runs across pools and providers; (2) local models, including owner compute hosts (CAP-13); (3) private hosted and API routes, cheapest first among those that meet the class's measured acceptance. When a route is exhausted, the call or run fails over to the next granted route; where an API or hosted route is granted it is the fallback that completes work, so losing a plan route makes work dearer, never stops it (DEP-3). Where none is, work runs on the best remaining route the class is qualified for, or waits for a plan pool reset, and the digest says which in plain words; a task class no present route is qualified for (including one qualified only on an absent compute host) is declined with the reason (OP-9), never sent to an ungranted route. Local passes that prepare or check a call (CAP-13) are not a step in this order: they wrap whichever route runs, where an adopted pipeline for the class beat that route alone. Routing rules are a Loop 1 candidate class adopted through §11 (as ADP-4); routing never adds a provider the owner has not granted. Within a provider-agent run the provider picks its own models; the broker routes per run. The owner MAY restrict which data classes reach each provider; the broker enforces this from the calling machine's data label (REV-5), and no routing rule can override it. By default, private-class calls go only to providers the owner marked as allowed for private data when connecting them. The digest reports plan exhaustion and fallback in plain words (ONB-9). |
| **CAP-10** | Default-on-timeout questions | The agent MAY ask the owner a question with a stated default and a deadline. Without a reply by the deadline, work proceeds on the default, which is listed in the digest. A default carries no authority: any irreversible effect that follows needs its own approval (REV-2). |
| **CAP-11** | Provider agents as workers | A guest MAY delegate a whole subtask to a **provider agent**: the official, unmodified agent CLI of a model provider (e.g. Claude Code, Codex CLI, and any other frontier agent tool such as Gemini's or Grok's once its terms allow) run headless with its tools on inside a worker machine (CAP-8), signed in to the owner's own plan with that provider. The route type is provider-agnostic: each provider agent is added by a **declaration**, not by code in the router: the CLI and its qualified versions, the headless command and how the brief and result pass, the custody mode and the terms it rests on (CRED-5), the declared model hosts and endpoints (ADP-10), its usage pools and where each pool's state is read (RES-5), and how it signals a used-up pool. The guest gives a brief and receives the result through broker tools; the provider agent never reaches the guest, the owner channel, or any adapter. It is a route type for CAP-9, an optional dependency (DEP-3), and is qualified per provider and CLI version (ARC-7). The worker inherits its creator's data label and budget reservation (REV-5); a private worker uses a provider agent only if the owner marked that plan as allowed for private data. The CLI's auto-update and telemetry are off; versions change only through image updates. Driving a provider's consumer website or app is never a route. |
| **CAP-12** | Resource-aware coordination | The guest MUST be able to plan across everything it can use: provider agents (CAP-11), API and local model routes (CAP-9), local leverage passes (CAP-13), worker machines (CAP-8), and its granted adapters and tools. A broker tool `resources` answers, per granted route and tool, without any credential or account detail: its kind (plan agent, API, local, owner host, adapter), the task classes it is qualified for with measured acceptance and latency (ADP-4), each usage pool's headroom above the owner's reserve and its reset time (RES-5), its marginal cost (zero on a plan within its pools), free concurrency, and whether the calling machine's data label may use it (REV-5). The guest MAY attach a route preference to a model call or a delegation (e.g. "Codex for this, Claude to review it", or several runs in parallel across pools); the broker honours it when the route is granted, admitted (RES-2, RES-5), and allowed for the label, and otherwise routes per CAP-9 and says which route ran and why the preference was not honoured, from a closed set of reasons (not granted, pool at reserve or forecast, cooling down, label not allowed, concurrency full). Information moves between workers only through broker tools: a provider agent's result (files, diff, summary) returns to the guest as a worker artifact, which the guest MAY hand to another worker or provider agent as input, under the same label rules. A preference never adds a route, raises a reserve, or bypasses a grant. |
| **CAP-13** | Local leverage | Local models are a **leverage layer** for whichever route does the main work (frontier, hosted, or a larger local model on a compute host), not only a substitute route. Local inference offers the guest broker tools (ARC-6) for a closed set of **passes**: **reduce** (select and compress context before a main-route call), **retrieve** (recall and embedding search, CAP-3), **extract** (structured fields from mail, documents, pages), **triage** (classify and route items and events, CAP-4), **check** (cheap verification of a main-route result against a schema, tests, or stated constraints), and **draft** (a first draft that the main route reviews or finishes). Results of passes the guest calls are worker artifacts and raise the caller's label per REV-5. A model call or provider-agent run with a local pre-pass, post-pass, or both is a **pipeline**. A pipeline MAY also be a **cascade**: the call runs first on a cheaper route (e.g. the box's own model or a compute host) and escalates to the next route only when a deterministic check fails (tests, a schema, a compiler, a declared output shape), never on a model's verdict (ARC-2); each escalation is journaled and counted in the pipeline's score. Loop 1 scores pipelines per task class against the main route alone, on measured acceptance, latency, and main-route usage (pool share, API cost, or compute-host time) per accepted task, and a pipeline is used for a class only once adopted as a routing rule through §11 (LOOP-4, CHG-6). Releases ship seed pipelines per task class (e.g. an extractive reduce for long contexts), which still adopt only on held-out gain. **Router-applied passes** (an adopted pipeline wrapping a call the guest made) run on the model-egress data path, in the local inference service, never in the broker (ARC-2); they work only on the content of the call they wrap and add no data the calling machine has not received, so only reduce, extract, and check run this way, and retrieve and draft run only when the guest calls them. A router-applied reduce is extractive: it selects and orders spans of the original and generates no text. The journal records the original and the reduced call, and the result tells the guest which pipeline ran. No pass output decides admission, a label, a grant, an intent, or a route at call time; check verdicts reach routing only as aggregated scores through Loop 1 adoption, and reach the guest as advice. Pass outputs are untrusted content, like recall results. Each pass carries its caller's admission class (RES-1). Local passes cost no plan quota, so a pipeline that holds acceptance stretches every usage pool (RES-5). **Capacity scales with what the owner has:** a small model on CPU at the floor (RES-2's budget); larger models and more passes at once on larger hosts and accelerators (RES-3); and **compute hosts**, the owner's own more powerful computers on their network running a standard local model server, added as local routes of kind `owner host`. The box installs nothing on a compute host and changes nothing there. The local page MAY find model servers on the network; it shows each with what adding it means, in plain words ("Studio PC: helps with public work", or "Studio PC: will see your private data"), and the key fingerprint it pins. Adding a compute host, or allowing it private data, is a tier-4 action (CH-3); removing one needs one reply. Calls to a compute host go only through the broker's egress proxy, to that host's declared endpoint over an encrypted channel authenticated by the pinned key; a key mismatch is refused, and the local inference service itself has no egress. A compute host receives private-class data only if the owner allowed it, and otherwise serves public-class work only (REV-5). It receives no credential (Invariant C) and no broker tool. It reports its model identity at each connection; a change suspends its pipelines until they requalify. **Hardware changes update all of this:** when the box starts on a different host, gains or loses an accelerator, or a compute host or hosted route is added, removed, or changes model, the broker recomputes resources at once (RES-2, RES-3, CAP-12) and Loop 1 requalifies the affected task classes and pipelines ahead of other spare work; routing and ONB-3's list change as each class qualifies, and the owner gets one line in the digest saying what the box can now do. A compute host is an optional dependency (DEP-3): when it is off, its task classes use their best measured alternative (by default the main route alone), work never waits on it, and the digest says so in one line ("Studio PC is off; using the box's own model"). [Inference] Common local model servers listen in plain HTTP by default, so a TLS front end from a mature component is needed; the owner's guide gives the recipe, and a release MAY ship a ready-made one. |

## 10A. Adapters: reaching any tool

AgentOS reaches current and future tools (frontier assistants, SaaS, local apps) through **adapters**. An adapter is a versioned, declarative package that maps a tool's operations onto broker intents. Adding a tool means adding an adapter, not changing the broker.

- **ADP-1** Every external tool is reached through an adapter that declares: tool identity, connection types, and the operations it exposes. This governs every effect that passes through the broker (credentialed or external-effect operations, REV-2); work wholly inside agent machines, including uncredentialed browsing (CAP-2), is unaffected. A broker-mediated operation the adapter does not declare does not exist for agents.
- **ADP-2** **Labels come from a fixed verb list, not from the adapter's author.** The broker defines a closed list of verbs (at least: read, draft, organize, send, post, buy, share, delete-remote, change-account, reveal-or-create-secret), each with a built-in reversibility class and credential custody rule. An adapter maps each operation to one verb and cannot set a class itself. When a mapping is ambiguous, the stricter verb applies; where routes differ, the strictest route's class applies. Only operations that fit no verb go to the owner, as one summary per tool, and they stay draft-only until the owner answers. Public adapters adopted via §11B carry maintainer-checked mappings and are accepted under the single grant that connects the tool. The verb list, its classes, and these rules change only by owner-approved intent (OP-5, CHG-2).
  - **organize** is reversible. It covers changes inside the owner's own account that only the owner sees and that fully undo there: archive and unarchive, add or remove a label or folder, move between allowed folders, mark read or unread, star or flag. An operation maps to organize only if its adapter also declares the inverse operation, and the broker journals the prior state with each one. Anything that cannot be undone inside the account is never organize: delete, empty trash, filters, and forwarding or auto-reply settings are delete-remote or change-account; reporting spam or phishing is delete-remote or share; sharing is share. Guards:
    - **Allowed targets only.** A move or label goes only to a system state (inbox, archive, read, starred), a folder or label the owner confirmed when granting, or the broker-created `AgentOS/` namespace. Trash, spam or junk, and any folder with a retention or auto-delete policy are never targets. A target that is shared, delegated, synced to another party, or watched by another service is share. Every organized item also gets an `AgentOS` label by default, so it stays findable.
    - **Alerts still ask before they are hidden.** For the operations that hide a message (archive, move out of the inbox, mark read), an item is an alert, and goes to approval as change-account, when its sender passes DMARC and is the provider's own security sender, a sender the owner holds an account with (from the grants and vault), or a sender the owner marked as financial or identity, whatever the wording. As a second net, the security-alert patterns also mark an item as an alert: CH-19's code patterns plus phrases such as sign-in, new device, password reset, security alert, suspicious or unusual activity, "is this you?", unusual transaction, and payment or account-change notices, in the owner's languages. The pattern list grows through Loop 2 (LOOP-10). Labelling and starring an alert still run: it stays in the inbox and unread, and the digest lists the guard hit.
    - **Bounded.** At most 200 organize effects per account per day (owner-set). Beyond that, the remainder for the day is asked once, as one batch (CH-13).
    - **Visible and undoable.** Each effect is journaled (REV-2). The daily digest counts them by action, names the senders most affected, and lists guard hits. The digest line carries an `UNDO <id>` (CH-16), valid for 7 days, which the digest states. It restores each item only if the item's current state still matches what the agent set, and reports the rest ("Restored 47; 3 skipped (changed since)"). `MORE <id>` lists the items, and `UNDO <id> 3 7` restores single items.

- **ADP-3** Connection types, in **default** routing order: (1) API or MCP; (2) the tool's CLI or plugin interface; (3) its web app, in a credentialed browser (CRED-4) when logged in, with submits governed by its web recipe (ADP-14); (4) its desktop app under GUI automation (ADP-5). The router picks the first route that covers the operation, is healthy, and whose grant and custody the owner already holds.
- **ADP-4** **Routing is self-improving.** The order in ADP-3 is a starting default. Route choice per tool and operation is a Loop 1 candidate class (LOOP-4), scored on measured success, owner outcome, latency, cost, and breakage. A new route is adopted only through §11 (held-out suite, CHG-1) and rolls back on regression. Routing can only choose among routes the owner has already granted; it never changes a class or custody (ADP-2).
- **ADP-5** **Desktop-app executors.** A desktop app that holds a login runs in a broker-owned VM, one app and one account each (or one account's suite, ADP-16), as a **single-app kiosk**: no shell, file manager, or other launchable program; file dialogs confined to a workspace directory; and, where the OS allows, the app's credential store unreadable by the user the app's UI runs as. Agents drive it only through the CRED-4 action protocol (accessibility tree, screenshot, click, type, select). In-app screens that reveal or create secrets are CRED-6 intents. Linux apps are within the floor guarantee, one executor at a time [Inference; unmeasured]. Windows (Wine or VM) and Android (emulator) executors are **optional capabilities outside the HW-4 guarantee**, available only on hosts that can run them. A desktop-app executor MUST host only apps whose saved login can be locked away from the user the app's UI runs as; other apps are reached by their API, CLI, or web route, or not at all (§17 risk 7).
- **ADP-6** Adapters are created and changed only through §11, including by agents when a new tool appears: draft the adapter, qualify it on recorded interactions (LOOP-5), pass the mismatch check (ADP-8), and request any new grants and the choices for unmapped operations from the owner (ADP-2). An adapter drafted from the owner's interactions is private-derived (OSS-3); only a clean-room re-creation (OSS-2) may be published via §11B.
- **ADP-7** An adapter whose observed behavior diverges from its declaration (a changed UI or API) is rerouted to its next healthy, already-granted route, or paused if none exists, and Loop 3 receives a repair candidate. Rerouting never widens authority, changes custody, or loosens an operation's class (ADP-2).
- **ADP-8** **Mismatch check.** Before adoption, an adapter is exercised against the tool's demo or sandbox environment (synthetic data only) where one exists. An operation whose observed effects exceed its verb (any outbound effect from a read- or draft-mapped operation) blocks adoption. [Risk] Effects the demo environment does not show can still be mislabelled; the stricter-verb rule (ADP-2) is the mitigation.
- **ADP-9** **Owner pre-allowance.** The owner MAY pre-allow irreversible operations, scoped per service, per operation, or both, to run without approval and without notification. A pre-allowance MUST be a deterministic predicate the broker checks (no inference, ARC-2), never a description the agent interprets:
  - **Verified inputs.** Every field the rule constrains (recipient, account, amount) is read by the broker from the source system (e.g. the invoice's contact in the tool itself) or fixed by the owner. A value the agent supplies never satisfies the predicate.
  - **Templated content only.** Content comes from an owner-approved template filled only from those fields, with no free text.
  - **Scope bounds**: per-item frequency, a daily rate, and an amount cap where relevant, in every rule.
  - **Optional hold** on records edited in the last N days, so a quietly tampered record cannot take effect at once (§17 risk 8).
  - **Fixed-wording approval.** The agent may draft a rule; the broker renders it back to the owner in fixed, spec-defined wording, never the agent's words.
  - **Asymmetric changes.** Granting or widening a rule is a new-grant intent (CH-3: approval code plus local confirmation or recovery key). Pausing or revoking needs only a text from the owner's number, like STOP.
  - **No match, no silence.** An operation that does not match a rule exactly falls back to a normal approval request.
  - **Audit.** Covered operations are still journaled (REV-2), visible on request and on the local UI, and halted by STOP.
  - **Exclusions.** CAP-6 may suggest a rule, never enact one. CRED-6 actions keep per-action approval under any rule.
- **ADP-10** **Enforced request shapes.** Each declared operation also declares the requests it makes: host, method, and path template, and for single-endpoint APIs (GraphQL, JSON-RPC) the operation name and a body schema. The broker's egress proxy injects a credential only into a request matching a declared operation of a granted adapter, and applies that operation's verb class and intent before forwarding. Anything else is denied and journaled. A model relay (CRED-5) declares inference endpoints only, so a provider key never reaches that provider's key-management, file-sharing, or billing endpoints. This makes the verb list (ADP-2) a property of traffic, not of the adapter's honesty.
- **ADP-11** **Context-scoped replies.** The owner MAY pre-allow free-text replies within existing threads, per account, as an exception to ADP-9's templated-content rule. All of the following hold:
  - **Earned.** CAP-6 offers this pre-allowance for an account only after the owner has approved at least 20 of the agent's replies there unedited, with at most 10% of the last 30 answered replies edited. Edited replies never count toward the 20. A rejection, an UNDO, or a wrong verdict resets the count. ADP-9's templated rules keep the strict run, where any edit resets.
  - **Thread starter verified.** The thread was started by the owner, or by a contact that meets CH-10's existence rule. A cold message cannot open an auto-reply channel.
  - The recipients are exactly the thread's existing participants, read by the broker from the source system.
  - The reply is composed in a fresh agent machine that receives only owner-approved style preferences and those messages of the thread whose recipient set includes every recipient of the reply, computed by the broker from source headers. It has no recall, no other files, and no egress. So it can disclose only what every recipient already received.
  - No attachments, no money, no new recipients, and no CRED-6 content.
  - **Commitment filter.** The broker matches the reply against fixed patterns: amounts and currency, dates and deadlines, and commitment phrases ("confirm", "agree", "will pay", "approved", "sign", "accept") in the owner's language list. A match makes that reply a normal approval request, except for a date or time the calendar acceptance rule below admits. CH-19's secret-shaped-content filter also applies. Both are fixed-pattern checks with no inference (ARC-2), and the pattern list grows through Loop 2 regressions (LOOP-10).
  - **Calendar acceptance.** Requires a calendar adapter; until one is connected, every date or time prompts. A reply MAY name a date or time without prompting only when it **accepts** a time the counterpart proposed, never when it proposes one. The broker checks each of the following deterministically, with no inference (ARC-2):
    1. **Proposed slot.** The reply names exactly one date-time, and it equals a slot the counterpart proposed in the latest inbound message of the verified thread. The broker extracts slots from that message with the same fixed date and time patterns as the commitment filter, resolved to an absolute start in the owner's time zone. A time with no stated zone takes the zone of the message's `Date` header offset. A slot that does not resolve unambiguously (no date, or a relative day the broker cannot anchor) is not a slot. Duration is the one the message states, else the owner's default meeting length (default 30 minutes).
    2. **Free.** The broker reads the owner's connected calendar and finds the slot free when the reply is queued, and again immediately before it sends (OP-3). Tentative events count as busy; the broker's own hold for this slot is ignored. If the slot is taken by then, the reply is withheld and becomes a normal approval request.
    3. **Known contacts.** The sender of the latest inbound message passes sender authentication (for mail, an aligned DMARC pass) and is in the owner's contacts, read by the broker from the source. The reply's recipients are a subset of that inbound message's participants, with no added addresses. CH-10's existence rule alone is not enough.
    4. **Nothing else.** Apart from that one slot, the reply matches no commitment pattern: no other date or time, no amount, and none of the commitment phrases. Those phrases are not exempt even when they accept the slot. The composer is told to accept with plain words ("yes, that works").
    5. **Bounds.** Within the rule's ADP-9 scope bounds, calendar acceptances are limited to one per thread per day and a small daily total (default 3), with holds counted, so a counterpart cannot use the free check to probe the owner's calendar.
    **Hold.** If the owner has granted calendar write, the broker also places a tentative hold for the slot. The calendar adapter declares the hold as a draft-class operation (ADP-2) with an enforced request shape (ADP-10): no attendees, notifications off, private visibility, and a fixed broker-template title carrying only the verified contact name. The hold is journaled as its own intent. It is removed when a matching invite from that contact arrives or the reply is undone or withheld; otherwise it expires after 48 hours, and always before the slot starts. The digest lists holds still waiting. Without that grant, no hold is placed.
    **Alert.** The alert uses a fixed template: `Auto-reply to <contact>: yes to <Tue Oct 7 14:00-14:30>. Hold added. UNDO <id> by <hh:mm>.` ("Hold added" only when one is placed.) Anything outside these conditions prompts as before. [Decision: Mark, 2026-10-04: auto-replies may accept a time only when the owner's side is the one affirming.]
  - **Alert and native undo.** When a reply is queued, the owner gets a text with the reply's first line and `UNDO <id>` (CH-16). Where the provider supports scheduled send, the reply is scheduled in the owner's own account for the undo window, so the owner can also cancel it in their usual mail app. It sends after the undo window (default 10 minutes, REV-3) and is listed in the digest. This is an exception to ADP-9's no-notification option.
  - Scope bounds, fixed-wording approval of the rule, asymmetric changes, the journal, and STOP apply as in ADP-9.
  - [Risk] A reply can still make a commitment phrased in a way the patterns miss, or state something wrong. A participant writing into the thread is an injection source. The alert and undo window cover that residual; they are mitigations, not prevention.
- **ADP-12** **A second line as a tool.** Outbound texts and calls to third parties are reachable through an adapter, mapped to the `send` verb. They MUST use a **second line**, never the owner-channel number (CH-1): a dual-SIM or eSIM modem, or an owner-held calling account. The second line is an optional dependency (DEP-3); without one, this tool is unavailable and the owner channel is unchanged. Calls MUST open by stating that an automated assistant is calling for the owner. Recipients are verified by the broker (owner contacts, or a number read from a source) or supplied by the owner; by default only businesses and owner contacts. Recipients are full international (E.164) numbers; a short or premium-rate code needs an owner-created contact. A line's rate limits are one budget shared by every transport it uses. Transcripts are journaled. Third-party traffic has its own rate limits. Inbound texts and calls to the second line, and any from a number other than the owner's on either line, are untrusted data for the agent. They are never control words, task chat, or part of any approval flow. [Risk] Automated calls are regulated (in the US, AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the TCPA [Fact: FCC, Feb 2024]); carriers may filter automated traffic.
- **ADP-13** **The box's own mailbox.** The box MAY have its own email address at a free mail provider, created with the owner on the box's Wi-Fi page. It is an optional dependency (DEP-3): without it, the box acts only through the owner's mailbox and CH-20's destination, unchanged. It is the owner's account at an ordinary provider, never a service operated by the project (DEP-2).
- **ADP-14** **Web recipes.** A site's web route is reached through a **web recipe**: an adapter (ADP-1) for one site that declares the site's origins (CRED-10) and, for each control that submits (a button, link, or form whose activation can change state at the site), how the executor recognizes it (page URL template, form action and method, accessible role and name), its fields, the requests it makes, declared as ADP-10 requires (including operation name and body schema on single-endpoint APIs), and its verb (ADP-2). A recipe also declares the site's background requests. A background request with a state-changing method is adopted only after the demo environment (ADP-8) shows it has no effect at the site, and never when its shape overlaps a submit's declared request; otherwise, including on every site with no demo environment, it is an operation mapped to a verb.
  - **The executor decides what a click does, not the agent.** When an agent action (click, Enter, select) would activate a submit control, the credentialed executor matches it against the adopted recipe from its own DOM. A match runs as that operation's intent (REV-2) with its verb's class, and authorizes only that operation's declared requests, once each, for that activation. Values the operation's tier or pre-allowance depends on are checked in the outgoing request before it is forwarded. A state-changing request (any method but GET, HEAD, or OPTIONS) from the credentialed context that matches neither the matched operation's declared requests nor a declared background request is blocked and journaled, and the recipe gets a repair candidate (ADP-7).
  - **Unknown submits.** Where no adopted recipe covers the page, the executor blocks every state-changing request from the credentialed context and treats the first one as the submit: the agent works up to it and the executor stops there. The broker sends one approval request in fixed wording from fields the executor reads (the site's origin, the held request's method and URL, the activated control's visible label, and the request's body fields with the form's field labels where a form sent it, hidden fields listed), with a screenshot as evidence (CH-20). Site-supplied text appears quoted as the site's text, length-capped, with look-alike characters folded; the screenshot is evidence, never the binding record. It is high risk (CH-10), since what the control does is unchecked. Approval releases exactly the held request, and only if its method, URL, and body fields still equal the approved values, apart from a value the executor recognizes as a session or anti-forgery token; any other change voids it. Any further state-changing request is held again and needs its own approval. The interaction is kept as a private-derived recipe draft (ADP-6).
  - **Adoption.** The agent drafts a recipe and it is adopted through §11 like any adapter (ADP-6): qualified in replay on recorded interactions (LOOP-5) and exercised against the site's demo environment where one exists (ADP-8). Where none exists, each state-changing operation's first 3 live runs (owner-settable, at least 1), and again after a recipe version changes its declaration, go to approval at no lower than its verb's tier whatever pre-allowance would apply, and their observed requests must match the declaration; until then the operation cannot be pre-allowed. Public recipes (§11B) carry maintainer-checked mappings and are accepted under the single grant that connects the site (ADP-2); the first-runs rule still applies on each installation.
  - **Cancelable submits.** A submit is **cancelable** when its recipe declares a cancel operation that fully reverses it at no cost within a stated window, qualified in the demo environment (ADP-8) or by a live cancel confirmed at the site with no charge, and the executor reads the result's reference (e.g. a booking number) from the confirmation page. A cancelable submit under the owner-set amount limit is low risk (CH-10), and its confirmation carries `UNDO <id>` (CH-16) for the shorter of the recipe's window and the owner's default; `UNDO` runs the cancel operation and reports whether it succeeded. Payments that cannot be refunded within the window, deletions, account changes, and anything without a declared cancel path keep their verb's tier. Cancelability selects only the approval tier; it never changes a verb's class (ADP-2).
  - **Pre-allowances** (ADP-9) apply to recipe operations unchanged. Bounds a rule constrains (party size, amount, date) are read by the executor from the submitted form and the confirmation page, never taken from the agent; recipients and accounts are verified as ADP-9 requires; and free-text fields must be empty or filled from an owner-approved template. Example: "book restaurants on <site> for up to 4 people, no approval".
  - CRED-6 screens are never covered by a recipe's submit, tier, or pre-allowance.
- **ADP-15** **One desktop over separate executors.** The broker MAY present several executors and desktop workers (CAP-8) to one agent as a single virtual desktop: one composited screenshot of their windows, and one action protocol that addresses each window by a broker-assigned id. Every action still reaches exactly one executor. Windows of different accounts never share a process, display server, clipboard, or file system, and data crosses between windows only through CRED-11, with drag and drop carried out as `hand-off`. The compositor is broker code, holds no credentials, and applies CRED-10's withholding per window. It depends on CRED-11.
- **ADP-16** **Suite executor (owner opt-in).** The owner MAY allow, per account, several desktop apps that sign in with that one account (e.g. one Microsoft account's mail, spreadsheet, and chat apps) to share one executor. Each app must meet ADP-5's saved-login condition, the kiosk launches only the suite's apps, with no shell or file manager, and the executor reaches only that account's granted adapters. Apps of different accounts never share an executor, since one compromised app could read the other's session. Turning it on is a new-grant intent (CH-3); turning it off needs only a text.
  - **Name.** The page offers four choices, each checked as available first: the box's name (`dave.smith`), a random adjective and noun (`sleepy.llama`), `email.assistant.<random digits>`, and one the owner types, checked live. A shuffle control offers new ones. An address never contains a phone number. A confirm screen shows the final address and states that it cannot be renamed later.
  - **Creation.** A credentialed executor (§7.3) fills in the provider's sign-up form with a broker-generated password; the live view shows only the human check, which the owner completes. The provider's sign-up phone check goes to the owner's number. **Account recovery is never the box's SIM**, because a SIM swap would otherwise take the mailbox and every account registered to it. Recovery goes to the owner's own email (the CH-20 destination) where the provider allows. Once two-step sign-in is on, the phone is removed as a recovery factor where the provider allows. A lost vault is recovered with that email plus backup codes from a sheet printed at setup. Where a provider requires a phone, it is the owner's number. [Risk] With the owner's number as a recovery factor, a SIM swap of that number could recover the mailbox. The broker then turns on the provider's two-step sign-in, which stays on, with a code-generator seed and the backup codes held in the vault, makes an app password or OAuth grant, connects the mail adapter, and sets the display name and signature (CH-21). No credential, seed, or recovery code is ever agent-readable (CRED-1). The box confirms by text ("Done. My email is dave.smith@gmail.com. Forward me anything.") and adds the address to its contact card.
  - **Use.** Mail to the box's address is untrusted data, never a control word, task chat, or part of an approval, whoever the sender appears to be. The owner forwards mail there and says by text what to do with it; forwards from the owner's address get a one-line receipt only when they pass an aligned DMARC check, so a spoofed forward gets none. Sending is the `send` verb with the usual approvals and pre-allowances. The box's address is never set as the recovery address of an account that holds money or owner credentials. The provider's security alerts (new sign-in, recovery change, forwarding added) reach the owner at once, outside digest pacing (CH-15). Sign-ups, receipts, and vendor correspondence the box handles for the owner use this address instead of the owner's.
  - **Upkeep.** The broker answers sign-in challenges with its own code-generator seed. A challenge that needs the owner's phone waits for the owner's next visit to the Wi-Fi page and is listed in the digest; its code is never accepted by text (CH-6). The digest mentions the mailbox only when something needs the owner: storage over 80%, sign-in blocked, or account suspended. The account is restored with the vault (§7.5); if the vault is lost, the owner recovers it through the recovery route above. A new address replaces the old one rather than renaming it, with the old one forwarded for 90 days. Disconnecting is a configuration change in fixed wording (CH-11); deleting the account is delete-remote and needs a high-tier code (CH-10).
  - [Risk] Providers' terms restrict automated account creation; the human check is the owner's, but a provider may still block or suspend such accounts. The adapter supports any IMAP/SMTP provider as a fallback.

## 11. One change pipeline

Sources: **local** (procedures, skills, routing, configs), **upstream** (new guest images such as OpenClaw releases, new host images), **shared** (packages from other installations).

Path: candidate → build in sandbox → evaluate on the frozen suite → adoption intent → activate with fallback → keep as a rollback point.

- **CHG-1** Held-out evaluation suites live in the broker. Candidates see only a dev split. Cases come from real tasks with owner outcomes (accepted, corrected, rejected) plus security fixtures, never solely from tests the candidate authored.
- **CHG-2** Changing suites, graders, or adoption policy is an owner-approved intent and cannot be validated by the change itself.
- **CHG-3** Security updates MAY auto-stage per standing policy. Behavior changes require owner approval unless a standing grant covers that class.
- **CHG-4** Sharing is opt-in, carries only public inputs and evidence, and works over any git-like channel the owner chooses. No registry or marketplace. The recipient re-qualifies locally and may reject.
- **CHG-5** Shared packages MUST NOT carry private corpora, sessions, identity material, or authority.
- **CHG-6** **Default standing grant for authority-neutral improvements.** Unless the owner turns it off (LOOP-0), a local candidate adopts without a per-change approval when all of the following hold:
  - It changes only procedures, compiled skills, routing among already-granted routes, or context rules. The broker decides this deterministically from the candidate's artifact type and diff (ARC-2), never from the candidate's description of itself. A **context rule** selects or orders data a machine already receives; it never changes what data reaches which machine or provider (REV-5, CAP-9).
  - It passes the held-out suite with no regression (CHG-1) and beats the baseline as LOOP-3 requires, and the security suite (LOOP-10) passes on every auto-adoption.
  - It changes no grant, verb class, custody, check, or security-suite coverage.

  Each adoption is journaled, listed in the digest, and reverted by one reply from the owner. Turning CHG-6 off needs only a text. Turning it back on is an owner-approved intent (CHG-2). Upstream and shared packages keep CHG-3 and UPD-5.

## 11A. Autonomous loops on spare capacity

The system improves and defends itself in otherwise-wasted time. Three loops share one scheduler, the change pipeline (§11), and the journal.

### Defaults
- **LOOP-0** All three loops are **on by default**, and so is local reproduction of public candidates (OSS-8); publishing anything to the open-source project (§11B) is **off** until the owner opts in on the local UI (OSS-7, D-088). All of these are **modifiable** at any time, on the local UI, or by text to turn them off ("loops off", "stop sharing"). Settings: per-loop on/off, spare AI budget (default: local compute plus a conservative share of spare quota, shown in the digest), the publication switch with its nested finer opt-ins (OSS-7), and a global off switch. Onboarding states the defaults in one line, and the owner can change them there, except the publication switch, which is offered only as OSS-7 says, never at setup (D-088).

### Spare capacity
- **LOOP-1** "Spare" means local compute not needed by foreground or accepted work (RES-1), plus AI quota the owner has designated as spare, e.g. the part of a plan's usage pool above the owner's reserve that will reset unused (RES-5). Spare work runs in the lowest admission class and MUST yield within the frozen preemption target.
- **LOOP-2** Spare AI usage has its own budget, separate from work budgets and visible in the digest. [Risk] Some consumer AI plans may restrict automated background use. Such routes are used for loops only if the provider permits it; otherwise loops use API-key routes or local models.
- **LOOP-3** The scheduler allocates spare capacity across loops by measured return: held-out gain or security findings per unit of cost. A loop that stops producing measured value gets less budget automatically. **When nothing worthwhile remains, the system sleeps.** Every learning mechanism (procedures and compiled skills CAP-5, routing rules and pipelines CAP-9 and CAP-13, attention proposals CAP-6, and any further loop) is **off until it beats the baseline**, the system without it, on §1's objective, measured above noise on held-out independent goals (distinct `goal_id`, latest outcome revision, OP-7); it turns off again when it stops beating it. LOOP-0's defaults let the loops search and evaluate; what they learn applies only under this rule.

### Loop 1: self-improvement
- **LOOP-4** Mines the journal for failures, owner corrections, slow or expensive steps, and repeated trajectories, and turns each into a hypothesis and then a candidate (procedure, compiled skill, routing rule including a local leverage pipeline (CAP-13), context rule, configuration).
- **LOOP-5** **Counterfactual replay:** candidates are tested by replaying past tasks inside agent machines against recorded external responses, in shadow mode. No live external effects; any unrecorded effect fails closed. Model calls are not effects: a replay may call the model live, through the same router and egress as the guest under the candidate's routing, metered (OP-8) against a separate capped evaluation budget that Loop 1 cannot change (CHG-2).
- **LOOP-6** Candidates are adopted only through §11 (held-out suite from real owner outcomes, CHG-1). Loop 1 cannot edit the suite, the graders, or its own budget (CHG-2).

### Loop 2: self-securing
- **LOOP-7** Continuous adversarial testing *from inside the sandbox*, with no more authority than any guest:
  - injection corpora against guests and collaborators;
  - fuzzing of the broker sockets and the credentialed-browser action protocol;
  - canary hunts (the A5 test, rerun continuously with fresh canaries);
  - attempts to tamper with evaluators and snapshots;
  - resource-exhaustion probes.
- **LOOP-8** Passive checks: image and dependency hashes vs the signed release; known-vulnerability matching against advisories (fetched like updates, DEP-4; works offline with the last snapshot); configuration drift; and expiry of credentials and certificates held in the vault.
- **LOOP-9** Every finding becomes: contain (pause the affected grant or executor) → preserve evidence → minimized regression case (added to the security suite permanently) → fix candidate through §11 → owner notified per severity.
- **LOOP-10** A fix that disables a check, widens authority, or reduces coverage of the security suite MUST fail qualification. The security suite only grows, except by owner-approved intent.

### Loop 3: maintenance
- **LOOP-11** Upstream intake (new versions of the active guest and of other declared guests (ARC-3), host images, dependency updates) is evaluated in spare time through §11. Security updates are prioritized; checks never reported as current when offline.

## 11B. Accumulating into the open-source project

Every installation's verified gains can flow into one public project, and every installation benefits from everyone else's, without any runtime dependency on the project.

### What accumulates (public repository contents)
| Area | Contents | Main source |
|---|---|---|
| Core | Broker, host image definition, action protocol, schemas | Maintainers + contributions |
| Skills library | Compiled skills and procedures with applicability, tests, measured results | Loop 1 |
| Evaluation suites | Public task cases with expected outcomes (no private data) | Owners who opt in |
| Security corpus | Attacks, fixtures, minimized regressions | Loop 2 |
| Web recipes | Per-site recipes (ADP-14), rebuilt in the clean room from the site's public pages, prompted by a `recipe_gap{}` hint that names a site only from the public site list (OSS-1): widely used sites listed in the public repository, excluding finance and health sites unless the owner opts in | Loop 1 |
| Hardware database | Per-PC boot recipes (HW-6), modem qualification (HW-2), accelerator results | A1/A2 runs on real installations whose owner opted in (OSS-7, D-088) |

### Contribution flow: clean-room by construction

**Principle:** private content is never published. By default publication is structurally zero: nothing derived from private activity (hints, hardware-database records, attestations) leaves the box until the owner opts in (OSS-7, D-088). Published artifacts are produced only by a process that **never had access** to private data, so content leakage is ruled out by information flow, not by filtering or redaction (which can miss things). The opt-in is the only path to one deliberate, bounded channel: the clean room depends on public inputs plus the hints the private side emits (OSS-1). The broker, not any agent, derives each hint from journaled facts, and a daily cap bounds the set, so private activity can shape a publication only through that choice among public options (D-082).

- **OSS-1** **Two sides, one narrow bridge.**
  - The *private side* (journal, recall index, workspaces, real tasks) may emit only a **hint**: a record whose every field is an **enumerated value from a public schema**. Examples: `skill_gap{domain: calendar, format: ics, failure: timezone}` or `vuln{class: prompt_injection, vector: email_html}`.
  - Hints contain no free text, numbers, names, identifiers, or content. Information crossing the bridge is bounded to choosing among publicly listed options.
  - Every hint is logged and visible to the owner.
  - The broker derives every hint from journaled facts; no agent machine chooses, words, or picks one. At most 5 hints cross the bridge a day, so a day's crossing is a choice among public options of about 57 bits at most (broker/hint/ASSUMPTIONS.md H9, from the arbitrator's ruling on #40; D-082).
- **OSS-2** **Clean-room builder.** On receiving a hint, a fresh agent machine is created with **no** access to the vault, journal, recall index, private workspaces, credentialed executors, or owner channel. It sees only the hint, the public repository, public internet (read-only), and synthetic fixtures. It independently builds the generalized skill, test case, or regression from public information, and tests it on synthetic fixtures. **Only clean-room output can be published.**
- **OSS-3** **Never published, under any setting:** real task cases, private evaluation suites, trajectories, transcripts, compiled skills built from private trajectories (their clean-room re-creations may be), recall content, and owner preferences.
- **OSS-4** **Structured-only data:** hardware-database entries and attestations use fixed schemas of enumerated fields (vendor, model, firmware version, result class, software version). No serial numbers, free text, or timestamps finer than a day.
- **OSS-5** **Security findings:** loop 2 runs on synthetic data and canaries. A finding from real traffic crosses only as a `vuln{}` hint, and the regression is rebuilt in the clean room. Findings that could harm other installations go first by encrypted private report to the maintainers, under an embargo. The report is derived from real traffic, so it is sent only while the publication switch is on (OSS-7) and travels as OSS-6 publications do (D-088).
- **OSS-6** **Metadata:** each installation publishes under a pseudonymous signing key, unlinkable to the owner's identity, number, accounts, or network address, and rotated periodically. Publications are batched and time-delayed to blur activity patterns. **Transport:** while publication is off, the box need not run Tor for it; once the owner opts in, every publication travels over Tor (D-088), to several relays run by third parties that accept writes without an account (Nostr relays at onion addresses today); a scheduled job in the public repository pulls them, verifies signature and schema, and appends them. Neither the box nor the repository's job holds a write credential, and no AgentOS-run service is involved. Where Tor is unreachable, batches wait and are dropped at the queue bound; publication never falls back to a direct connection, and a lost batch costs only a data point. **Silent by default:** no text, digest, or STATUS line mentions publishing or its failures; the owner-visible log (OSS-1) and the local UI show it. Private activity influences a publication only through the bounded hint channel (OSS-1), and only after the owner opts in (OSS-7, D-082, D-088). [Inference] No relay or the repository sees the owner's network address; the remaining exposure is that the owner's network provider sees Tor in use.
- **OSS-7** **Owner control: one publication switch, default off (D-088).** Nothing derived from private activity (hints, hardware-database records, attestations) leaves the box until the owner turns the switch on, on the local UI only, never by text (CH-19). The box offers the switch when it has real candidates to send, not at setup. The consent screen shows concrete examples, drawn from what this box has logged, of what it would send from then on (for example its pending `skill_gap`, `adapter_gap`, `recipe_gap`, or `vuln` hints; a hardware record "vendor, model, firmware, result class"; an attestation "passed, this hardware class, these versions"), each with its plain-language meaning, the channel's size (at most 5 hints a day, about 57 bits, OSS-1), and that sent items cannot be recalled; switching off stops future sends. While the switch is off, would-be items are kept in the local log only, and they are never sent: only items created after the owner turns the switch on leave the box, so nothing the owner was not asked about is sent at opt-in. A new hint kind, a new hint or record field (OSS-1, OSS-4), or a new option asks again, so the opt-in never widens silently; until the owner agrees, items of the new kind, field or option are treated as while the switch is off: logged only, never sent. The finer opt-ins (finance and health sites for `recipe_gap`, evaluation suites) remain, nested under the switch.

### Verification without a central service
- **OSS-8** **Decentralized qualification:** installations spend spare capacity (LOOP-1) reproducing public candidates (on by default, LOOP-0) and, once the owner opts in (OSS-7, D-088), publish signed attestations: "passed/failed, on this hardware class, with these versions." Accumulated attestations replace project-run CI.
- **OSS-9** Attestations are evidence, never authority. Maintainers decide what enters canonical releases. Each installation still re-qualifies locally before adopting anything (CHG-4). [Risk] Fake installations can forge attestations, so they're weighted by reproducibility and maintainer review, never counted blindly.
- **OSS-10** Canonical releases flow back to every installation through the update path (DEP-4, UPD-1), per owner policy. Anyone may fork the project, and installations may follow any fork.
- **OSS-13** Incoming public artifacts are untrusted input: they run only in agent machines, and pass §11 locally before adoption.

### Dependency status
- **OSS-11** The public repository is a **publication channel, not a runtime dependency**. It can be hosted on any git service, mirrored anywhere, or carried on a drive. With it unreachable, nothing about operation, control, or recovery changes (DEP-3).
- **OSS-12** The license MUST be chosen before first public release, and upstream notices (OpenClaw and others) preserved. This spec grants no license.

## 12. Resources and accelerators

- **RES-1** Admission classes, in priority order: `foreground` (calls, STOP/STATUS, owner chat) > `accepted work` > `experiments`. Experiments MUST be frozen or killed within a frozen target time when foreground needs resources.
- **RES-2** A per-component memory budget is declared and enforced by cgroups. The broker refuses admission that would leave less than the declared headroom, because a cgroup at its hard limit stalls in reclaim rather than failing fast [S3, measured on cgroup v1]. Memory pressure (PSI) is an admission input. CPU, I/O, and process-count limits are enforced the same way: agent machines weigh less than the broker and `foreground`, experiments least, and each machine has a process cap, so no guest can starve the owner channel or the journal. No swap or reclaim thrashing, no out-of-memory kills of broker or foreground. [Inference, from S3 and S4; check on the N95] Floor budget: host, broker and journal 1.0 GB; local inference 2.0 GB; one credentialed browser 0.5 GB; headroom 0.6 GB (unmeasured); agent-machine pool about 3.9 GB. The pool is derived at start from the host's reported memory less the other components' budgets, never assumed: on an 8 GB N95 that is about 3.5 GB [S1 kit arithmetic]. A host too small for the agent machine runs with the agent off and says so (OP-9). On larger hosts the pool MAY grow past the floor's only by a rule that also bounds it by CPU cores, so that adding machines never slows foreground work (A2); such a rule adds throughput only (HW-4).
- **RES-3** The broker discovers accelerators (iGPU/NPU/GPU) and exposes them as an admission resource used by local inference. The identical service MUST run on CPU when no accelerator is present. Nothing in the broker depends on an accelerator. The same resource sizes the local leverage layer (CAP-13): a larger host or an accelerator runs larger local models and more passes at once, and owner compute hosts add capacity outside the box's own budget.
- **RES-4** Reserve storage for a healthy release, the journal, and the recall index before discretionary downloads or snapshots. The reserve is enforced by the file system (a quota or a separate volume), not only checked before snapshots, and each agent machine writes under its own hard disk quota, so no guest can fill the disk the journal and owner state live on. Old snapshots are pruned by policy.
- **RES-5** **Plan quota is a resource, per usage pool.** A plan has one or more **usage pools**: the unit the provider meters, each with its own rate windows (e.g. 5 hours and 7 days). Pools follow the provider's metering, not the provider: a provider may meter each of its products separately on one plan (e.g. Codex apart from ChatGPT chat), and a plan may add model-specific pools (e.g. Claude's Opus and Sonnet weeks). Pools are declared, so any provider's product split is expressed the same way. A provider agent's declaration (CAP-11) names the pools a run draws on. The broker tracks each pool's utilization and reset time, read from the provider's responses on its own connection in broker-held mode, or from the CLI's reported events in worker-held mode, which inform routing only and never OP-8 spend. Admission to a plan route is refused when starting the run would take any pool it draws on past the owner's **reserve**: the share of each pool the owner keeps for their own use (default 0%: the box may use the whole pool; per pool, set as a budget, OP-5). Spare-class work (LOOP-1) draws a pool only down to the larger of the reserve and the **foreground forecast** until the pool's reset (foreground use measured over past windows); in the last hours before a reset (a frozen target) it MAY also use what would otherwise reset unused, never below the reserve. LOOP-0's spare share bounds how much of what RES-5 admits the loops take; where the two differ, the smaller wins. Foreground work may use the pool down to the reserve. Concurrent provider-agent workers per pool are capped (default 2; the owner can change it, and Loop 1 may tune it within the pool's reported windows). A run refused or cut off by a pool's limit fails over per CAP-9 within a second, and that pool's reset time is the route's cooldown; routes on other pools of the same plan stay available.

## 13. Updates, backups, time

- **UPD-1** A/B image updates: stage → test → activate → health-check → commit or fall back. Fallback MUST be automatic, using boot-loader boot counting plus a health check, with no owner action [S7: the measured Ubuntu GRUB path has no automatic rollback; signed systemd-boot has boot counting]. Rollback never rewinds revocations, spent budgets, deletions, or known external effects.
- **UPD-1a** A release is one content hash (the `/usr` verity root hash) plus its boot entry. Both MUST transfer and activate together.
- **UPD-2** Updates come from content-addressed, signed sources: any mirror, or offline from a drive (DEP-4). The update-signing key is held offline by the project. [Risk] Project key custody and rotation need their own procedure.
- **UPD-3** **First boot updates before trust.** The preloaded image may be months old. On first network contact, before any AI or personal account is connected, the box fetches the latest **stable** release, verifies it, and activates it through A/B with fallback. Credentials never touch an outdated image. Offline first boot runs the preloaded image and says so, and the update applies when the box is next online.
- **UPD-4** **Channels:** *stable* (default), *fast* (early adopters; feeds attestations only once the publication switch is on, OSS-7, D-088), *pinned* (no automatic updates, security notices only). Changeable by text or local UI.
- **UPD-5** **Cadence (defaults, modifiable):**

| Change type | Check | Apply |
|---|---|---|
| Security fixes | Daily | Auto-staged; applied at the next quiet window, typically within 24 h; automatic rollback on failed health check |
| Stable releases (host, broker, bundled guests such as OpenClaw) | Daily | After a **soak**: at least 7 days on fast **and** sufficient independent passing attestations (OSS-8), whichever is later, plus local requalification; then at a quiet window with random jitter |
| Behavior-changing releases | Same | Also need owner approval unless a standing grant covers that class (CHG-3) |
| Upstream projects directly (e.g. OpenClaw main) | Loop 3, spare time | Never adopted straight from upstream; enter users' boxes only via a qualified AgentOS release |
| Shared skills | Continuous | Through §11 locally, one at a time |

- **UPD-6** Updates never apply during a call, accepted work, or quiet hours the owner has excluded. Every update is a journaled intent with a rollback point.
- **UPD-7** [Inference] Soak plus attestations replaces a central staged rollout: early-channel installations act as the canary population without any project-run service.
- **UPD-8** **Update metadata** follows The Update Framework (TUF; reuse a maintained implementation): releases are signed by a threshold of k-of-n maintainer keys, keys rotate and revoke without reinstalling, timestamp metadata expires so a frozen mirror is detected when online, and no release older than the installed one is accepted, offline included; local A/B fallback to the previous slot (UPD-1) is exempt. Offline installs from a drive (DEP-4) need valid signatures and a newer version but not fresh timestamp metadata; the box says the update was installed without a freshness check, and checks on its next online contact. An offline install is refused when the drive's root metadata expired more than 180 days ago ("This drive's update is too old to trust offline. Use a newer drive, or connect once."); the owner can override with a tier-4 code, shown the same warning. Security fixes (UPD-5) auto-stage only with the threshold signatures plus at least one independent attestation from the fast channel (OSS-8). An attestation counts only from an attestor on the box's allow-list (pinned in the image, or added by the owner as a CH-3 grant), never from a key holding a signing role; until an outside attestor is listed, the project's own test box is the interim entry, and it stops counting for good once one is listed, even if that attestor is later removed.
- **UPD-9** **A security fix is never held in silence.** A security fix that has not been applied within 24 h of being staged, because the box was never free (UPD-6), is put to the owner once, paced (CH-15), with how long the restart takes and a choice of now or the next quiet window. A security fix held by the owner's settings (pinned, or security updates set to ask) is listed in every digest until it is installed or the owner declines it (OP-9). Ordinary releases never ask; after 7 days without a free moment they get a digest line.
- **BAK-1** Backups are optional and owner-chosen: a second local drive, or encrypted upload to storage the owner already has. Without a backup, the drive is the only copy, and the owner is told so plainly.
- **TIM-1** Time comes from network time when online, with carrier network time (from the modem) as a cross-check. Large disagreements put the broker into restricted mode for time-sensitive checks.

## 17. Open risks

1. **Firmware boot selection** without a keyboard on arbitrary PCs (HW-6). The integrated key is the full answer.
2. **Voice over USB modems** (HW-2, CH-5): model-dependent quality.
3. **Plan routes depend on provider terms** (CAP-11, CRED-5). Terms have changed several times in 2026 (Anthropic restricted third-party use of plan logins; Google ended plan access for open-source agents; OpenAI opened ChatGPT sign-in to third-party tools). The API fallback (CAP-9) keeps the box working when a plan route goes; it only costs more. Each provider's custody mode is re-read against its terms at every CLI qualification. An unconfirmed broker-held route (CRED-5; Codex today) also carries a risk that the provider restricts the owner's account, which the owner accepts through recorded consent (CRED-5).
4. **Classifying web submits** (ADP-14). A recipe's mapping, cancel window, and cancel cost come from the site, which can change or misstate them, and a site without a demo environment cannot be mismatch-checked. Mitigations: the executor matches controls from its own DOM, undeclared state-changing requests are blocked, the stricter-verb rule, first live runs go to approval, unknown submits are high risk, and a diverging recipe is rerouted or paused (ADP-7).
5. **Remote rich UI is gone by design.** If that proves too limiting, the only fix is an optional, owner-chosen, end-to-end-encrypted relay. That would be a deliberate exception to DEP-2's spirit, never a requirement.
6. **Project update-signing key** custody (UPD-2). UPD-8's threshold signing means one stolen key can't sign a release; the custody and rotation procedure is still needed.
7. **Desktop kiosk escape** (ADP-5). A GUI path out of the kiosk (a crash dialog, help browser, or app-embedded file picker) could reach the app's saved login. Mitigations: the login stored outside the UI user's reach, A13's adversarial escape step, and restricting desktop executors to apps where that holds.
8. **Tampered trusted source** (ADP-9). An attacker who edits the source record a pre-allowance trusts (e.g. a Xero contact's email) passes the predicate. Mitigations: scope bounds, the recent-edit hold, and the journal.
9. **Low-tier approvals by text** (CH-10). A SIM swap of the owner's number, or a stolen unlocked phone, can approve low-risk requests. Mitigations: the owner-set low-tier limits, reversibility windows, the journal, and STOP.
10. **Owner Card kept with the drive** (CRED-8). The card holds the vault passphrase, so whoever has both can decrypt the vault offline; the approval code gates only a running, unmodified box. Mitigations: the card says to store it apart from the drive, like a passport, and the owner can replace the passphrase on the local UI (which does not protect copies already made).
11. **Tampered boot path on an unknown host** (HW-5a, CRED-8). Secure Boot stops at the distribution's kernel; the initrd and the `/usr` root hash are not verified (S7). An attacker with brief access to the drive can modify them so the local page captures the passphrase when the owner next types it on an unknown host, then take the drive. Accepted for MVP (Mark, 2026-10-04): the owner's guide says to unlock only on a trusted PC after losing custody of the drive; trusted hosts are covered by the TPM policy seal; an AgentOS shim is reconsidered after MVP.
12. **Disclosure through allowed destinations** (REV-5). A `private` machine can still leak through an allowed destination whose received data an attacker can read and through research queries and the choice among public links it may follow. Mitigations: a minimal default list, no user-content hosts in suggestions, size and rate bounds, and the journal.
13. **SIM swap inside the unlock window** (CH-3, CH-19). A swapper can read ordinary private data by chat until the unlock lapses or the owner notices their phone has lost service. Codes and secret-shaped content are already withheld.
14. **Host stack claims unverified on Debian** (S7). Debian 13's signed systemd-boot, boot counting, and the TPM policy seal were not exercised: the cloud network blocks Debian mirrors. P2 step 1 verifies them first; bootc on CentOS Stream 10 is the fallback.
15. **Floor concurrency rests on summed RSS** (S3, S4). About 1.6 GB per OpenClaw machine may overstate it (PSS about 1.54 GB), and Firecracker's copy-on-write snapshot restore could let forks share memory. gVisor's systrap overhead on build-heavy work (npm install, builds) is also unmeasured and is measured alongside the Firecracker re-test. All are measured on the N95 before A2 targets freeze.
16. **Lossy local passes and compute-host integrity** (CAP-13). A reduce pass can drop what the frontier needed, and a weak check can pass a bad result; a compromised owner computer sees what is sent to it. Mitigations: pipelines are adopted only on measured acceptance per task class and roll back on regression; check verdicts carry no authority; private data reaches a compute host only by the owner's per-host choice, over a pinned encrypted channel, with no credentials or tools.
17. **Effort proxies** (OWN-19). Attention and thought cannot be measured directly; the counted components stand in for them, and a proxy can be optimized against what it stands for (fewer texts by withholding needed ones, fewer prompts by widening authority). Mitigations: components reported separately rather than as one score, each suppression measure paired with a miss measure, and security cases inside every journey run.
