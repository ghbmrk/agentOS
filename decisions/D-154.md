# D-154: The keeper boundary, in software first

Date 2026-10-24. Source: Mark, "Okay let's do that" in Questions 8 (cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuK3bePcHnZxeeysskvezfDjU, 2026-10-24 00:01:30Z), approving the staged keeper path in cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuK4xqwe6jaPbbRLC6vwGVRvt and cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuK6WYJzNd1aSpESSYchpYRpv. Design: change 10 of the ideal-backwards review (project file `answers/2026-10-24-hardware-local-diffs.md`, section A).

**Decision.** The parts that hold authority form one **keeper domain**: the kernel's verdict and key state, the log key and head, the sealed store, the unlock step, and the API and token clerks (mail over IMAP or the Gmail API, the model clerk, OAuth token clerks, each with its exit). Until the keeper prototype (#2943) it runs on the M6 Ultra as one isolated VM or one confined group of processes, keeping its internal per-uid split. Everything else (models, guests, the planner and reader, voice, the owner page, browsers) is the **main side** and reaches the keeper only across the boundary. Moving the keeper to its own board must then be a deployment change, not a rewrite. Builders and reviewers hold every brief and PR that touches the keeper to these rules:

1. **Typed frames only.** Crossing the boundary is a closed, versioned, size-bounded call table per side (today's `sockets` seams and the clerk wire). No shared files or state directories, shared memory, fd passing, environment or signals cross it. Fd passing inside the keeper (the unlock step's hand-off) is fine.
2. **Side identity comes from the link.** Which side a caller is comes from the listener the deployment supplies: a peer uid on one host today, a VM socket or a dedicated link later. Frames carry no uid, and no kernel or clerk logic reads one.
3. **Keeper clerks end TLS themselves.** A keeper clerk opens TLS to its origin through an injected dialer (today `netguard`'s `DialContext`), so a relay of ciphertext through the main side can replace the dialer. No credential is injected, and no TLS ends, outside the keeper.
4. **Nothing of the main side links into the keeper.** No keeper process links inference, guests, browsers, the reader, voice or the owner page's server. A keeper clerk parses only what its own check needs and passes other content out as bytes (#2937, #2892).
5. **Own clock and randomness.** The keeper takes no time or entropy from the main side (the kernel's sockets already run every call at the kernel process's own clock).
6. **The TPM is one slot kind.** Nothing outside `clerk/seal` and the unlock step assumes the store's host factor is a TPM, so a secure-element slot can be added beside tpm, passphrase and recovery. The TPM pins (STO-pins) stay behind the unlock step.
7. **Log I/O through the kernel's log only.** No other code reads or writes the log file, so its bulk bytes can later live on the main side while the keeper keeps the key, the head and the append signature.

**Browser custody tier.** Browser-session clerks (CLK-5) run on the main side, outside the keeper, as a named weaker tier: a main-side compromise reaches their sessions. Each receives only its own clerk's secrets, over a frame. STATUS and the graph naming which accounts are in this tier is a SPEC edit for after the prototype.

**Not decided here.** The SPEC edits (KRN-2, HW-9, HW-16, STO-2, STO-3, CH-1, a new HW-17), the keeper board and the owner-line modem's move. All follow the prototype's measurements (#2943). Mark buys nothing until its brief names the board.
