# D-150: Gmail through the API, on the household's own client

Date 2026-10-23. Source: Mark, tap "All 22" on the trust-nothing card in Decisions 7 (cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuK3EfYFiaBoCsqjAh4vzYGxt, 2026-10-23 18:34:54Z), and his 15:33Z message in the drafting thread cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuKPbPErx7W73Lihpj5bMmw2d ruling out a Google project that spans households.

**Decision.**
1. Gmail goes through the Gmail API, with the narrowest grant for the declared use (SPEC CLK-9): `gmail.metadata` to watch, `gmail.readonly` to archive, `gmail.modify` to sort or trim. Turning on `gmail.modify` tells the owner the box can then send as them.
2. The client is a Google OAuth client of the installed-app type that each household registers for itself. The project never runs one (DEP-2). The owner registers it by hand, once, from a guided page on their own device. The box registering it in its own Google account (TN-gmail-register, #2868) is deferred as `class:later` until a second household is planned (D-149 item 9).
3. It stays unverified under Google's personal-use exception (fewer than 100 users), so every consent shows Google's "unverified app" warning. A logo is not set, since one reportedly starts brand review; publishing *In production* is a checked step, since a client left in *Testing* loses its token after 7 days.
4. Consent happens on the owner's phone and is returned by paste on the owner page. There is no one-tap return: that needs a web client with an HTTPS redirect under a public domain, which means a project domain spanning households (ruled out) or one per household.
5. The owner never signs in to Google inside the box. IMAP with an app password stays the fallback, labelled *Full power* (DEP-3).
6. The client ID and secret are sealed with the token in the mail clerk (CLK-1); RFC 8252 §8.5 says such secrets are not confidential, and none is in the image, CI or the repository.

**Open.** Whether Google's device flow excludes Gmail scopes is unverified (Google's pages could not be fetched from the drafting session); the spec connects Gmail at home either way.
