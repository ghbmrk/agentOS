# D-271: Root-program lint is an allow-list (#4493, KRN-2)

Date 2026-11-02. Source: Security reject on PR #4559 (comment 6573272343), fourth round: the argv-text model leaks through working-directory spellings, shipped links and uncounted wrappers. Standing rule: structure first.

**Decision.** The root-side lint in `broker/kernel/privfile` (`TestRootSidePathOpsGoThroughPrivfile`, #4493) stops modelling argv text. Instead an allow-list names every program a root `.service` or `.socket` unit may run: each entry is an absolute path with a reason, in the manner of `root_programs.txt` for udev. A program not on the list fails the lint. Shells and runners (`sh`, `bash`, `env`, `flock`, `bwrap`, wrappers that exec a further program) are refused unless listed with a reason. A listed program is checked once, as itself; its arguments are not parsed for paths.

**Why.** A deny-list over argument spellings has false passes by construction (`cd` spellings, links under `image/`, `..` folding, uncounted helpers). An allow-list fails closed: a new spelling or wrapper is simply not on the list. It is also simpler, and no lens gets worse.

**Review.** The reviewer checks that the list is the only way a root unit's program is admitted, that no listed entry is a shell or runner without a stated reason, and that a link or relative path in `ExecStart` is refused rather than resolved.

**Auto-decided** by the coordinator (no lens worse); no Mark card.
