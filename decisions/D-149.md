## Decision

1. **Objective.** Maximise leverage, subject to the owner's control of four assets (money, identity, data and physical safety), at the least owner effort, against frontier attacks and against any one provider.
2. **Control.** An asset is under the owner's control when only the owner, or a bounded grant from the owner, acts on it; the owner sees every act from a vantage the actor does not control; the owner can stop or undo an act in time; and no single provider can cut the owner off from it.
3. **Three principles.** (a) Nothing stealable works away from home: every credential, address and copy is tied to the owner's key, one device, one site or one use, or encrypted to the owner's key. (b) Every root of control (the keys that authorize, the address that recovers accounts, the main copy of data, each device's admin) sits at home wherever an option exists, and every provider is replaceable. (c) New capability arrives as data (a declaration, a recipe, a table row, a graph fact) on existing mechanisms; a package that adds a mechanism names the one it retires, or the measurement that shows it is needed. Brief writers apply this package by package (plan update 18:35Z, answering Mark's "Should get simpler as it gets more complete/powerful").
4. **Ranking.** Structural measures first, deterministic detection second, probabilistic signals last; no probabilistic signal stands alone.
5. **Assumptions.** Every copy of every credential leaks eventually. Any mechanism now thought hyper-secure can fail, and a provider can decide against the owner or be made to. A provider's incentives can differ from the owner's while it works normally, and a device can sense and send what its maker wants. The scope is the owner's four assets and the providers behind them, not only the box.
6. **The model has four parts**, and every new rule and package sits in one:
   - **One graph** of the owner's accounts, credentials, providers, devices, keys and people, with failure domains and AND/OR edges. Every list the box produces (thief cut sets, cut-off sets, rotation lists, unset brakes, correlated safeguards, roots not yet home) is a query on it. It is advisory and holds no authority.
   - **One invariant**: no single failure domain takes any of the four beyond an effect that is bounded, passes a delay a witness in another failure domain sees, and can be stopped by the owner; no single provider can cut the owner off; roots sit at home where an option exists. Data sits outside the delay rule, since a read cannot be undone; its bound is what a provider still holds, end-to-end encryption and decoys.
   - **One loop**: the AI proposes; the graph scores; the clerks and the kernel enforce. The box narrows on its own only through controls that cannot widen; anything wider waits for the owner.
   - **One scoreboard**: STATUS opens with the weakest asset and one next action, and shows for each asset the failures a thief needs and the providers that can cut the owner off.
7. **The four rules of the final pass stay**, each inside one part, bound by OWN-7 and OWN-8 (about two taps a year plus a PIN): *Narrow* (hold the least, behind the far side's fence), *Two* (no single compromise yields money or identity), *Witnessed from outside* (detection is a set difference between the log and an independent record), *Ends* (every trust lapses, on a schedule or at once).
8. **KRN-2** becomes: compromise of the trusted base is out of scope for prevention; its containment and recovery are in scope.
9. **Promotion (D-086).** 21 of the plan's 22 packages are `class:release` rows, tested by A22 in G3, and start in the plan's order: six before the first slice (A21) passes (the three Gmail rows, the reference model, no-code-from-writable-storage and the CI vulnerability check), the other 15 after it. The 22nd, the box registering the household's Gmail client itself (TN-gmail-register), is deferred as `class:later` until a second household is planned: the owner does Google's console steps once, guided by the owner page (Mark, 2026-10-23 18:47:11Z, cmsg_01R91wiieBzMSvPKFsAJVvuKFDyXJVLDkpr3vZrgckhx9D: "Yes do these recs. Fewer more powerful mechanisms that scale to infinite accounts and devices and interactions"). The spec lands in two PRs: the first carries what the six need (CLK-9, UPD-10, EVL-1's reference model, EVL-5 and the Gmail sign-in, with A22's first clauses); the second carries the rest (CLK-10 to CLK-13, KRN-10, KRN-11, REC-5, BAK-2, DEV-13, the KEY, CH, REC, LOG and BAK changes, the rest of A22, the §19 risks, and *reads from home*: once the archive exists, tasks read archived mail and files only from it, in the log's content store (LOG-1), and provider logins only sync, watch and act) once A21 passes.

## What it stops

| Compromise | Under this model |
|---|---|
| A provider token copied off the box | Its first visible unexplained effect cuts it off; a narrow credential never changes the doors. |
| The owner's mailbox taken over | A text within about a minute from a door change or the box's grant dying; once the fixes are made, no single compromise yields it. |
| The box's card number leaked | Caught at the first charge the box did not make; a credit card keeps it disputable. |
| The core running someone else's code | It cannot delete mail permanently, change doors through a narrow login, or raise a limit; implants last at most a week (UPD-10); it ends with a recovery that needs no compromise date. |
| A passkey enrolled during a compromise | Cut at restore; what it granted is listed and dropped. |
| Erases forged by a compromised core | Held sealed at restore until the owner confirms. |
| The page CA key copied | Replaced at a compromised restore, with the new fingerprint texted. |
| Backups recorded off the box once X25519 falls | Still sealed by ML-KEM-768. |

## What it does not stop

- **Silent use** (Mark, 15:48Z). A stolen password, session or token used only to read sets off no alert and needs no reset. The model's answer is to make the stolen thing worth little: narrow grants, trimmed windows, per-site addresses, one-time cards.
- A session stolen from the owner's own device by malware stays open until it expires, and only its provider can see it.
- A provider reading what it holds.
- An instant, irreversible transfer inside a limit.
- The box sending mail as the owner, since Gmail's sorting grant also sends.
- A fully compromised core using its full-power logins until a provider's own alert or a copy of the log's head shows it, since the witness runs on the core.
- Everything failing at once.
- Money's root stays with the bank: it can be bounded, spread and made reversible, not brought home. The phone number stays with the carrier, and cloud-only devices with their makers.
