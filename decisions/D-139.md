**Decision.** The trusted core is rebuilt around one primitive:
- capability keys, signed and narrowable but never widenable, whose caveats carry spend, bounds, expiry, flow labels and the hold window;
- one holder (the clerk), which is the only exit and alone holds passwords and keys;
- one append-only log, with the kernel a pure function of it, so simulation, replay and audit share one code path.

Everything else is untrusted, the AI included. The planner is blind by default.

The migration runs in six steps:
0. Freeze: this decision and docs/migration/cases.md.
1. One spec PR: every requirement maps to the key, the clerk or the log, and PLAN, BOARD, LANES and OPERATING are rewritten in the same PR.
2. The kernel, with simulation from its first commit.
3. A first slice, "Sort my inbox and unsubscribe from and delete the spam" (Mark 02:47Z, cmsg_01R63S2yrPkq32thy8WQCuvDGrA4qoiA2gbRXoJtHKP23E), run in QEMU against a synthetic mailbox with fake endpoints and seeded injection emails. Its only writes are labels and archive to sort the inbox (both reversible), moving mail to trash, and an RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (the List-Unsubscribe-Post header) for DKIM-passing legitimate bulk senders, all through the clerk; the unsubscribe also waits out the hold window and the novelty check. Permanent delete is forbidden. Spam is trashed, never unsubscribed, because unsubscribing confirms the address is live and calls an endpoint the attacker chose, and links in a message body are never followed. It replaces the energy and thermostat slice, which needs hardware and is high risk.
4. Ports by directory. Each port deletes the old code it replaces.
5. Deleting the old core.

**Frozen.** No new build starts on the old core. The old core is the broker packages journal, grants, vault, recall, recalltool, egress, holder, checker, projection, replay, durable and sim, plus their wiring in agentosd. That covers the SIM-* rows, D-095 store work, the credential-split later items, the SR3 follow-ups, GR-reissue-deadline and EGRESS-rule-1. Their open PRs and issues close as superseded once docs/migration/cases.md holds what they proved. The old code stays on main until step 4 or 5 deletes it, and no one hardens it in the meantime.

**Supersedes.**
- D-095: the journal-centred simplification and briefs/SIM.md. One log, intents, one outcome and forget-once are carried into the kernel as cases.
- D-103: short-secret hashes kept in egress. The clerk holds secrets.
- D-078: the vault signing key, with egress minting per-request tokens. Signing becomes a clerk capability.
- D-065, D-071 and D-076: the forget-log HMAC chain, its picker and its decoys. The restore-after-forget behaviours they protect are cases for the log.

**Not superseded.**
- D-096 and D-097 (one log, pull-first, key erasure) and D-037, D-038 and D-116 state requirements, not mechanisms. The spec PR re-homes them.
- Hardware, image, modem and Wi-Fi work, CI fixes, the Codex lane, #2518 and the STOP defect fixes continue.

**Who does what during the migration.** This supersedes D-099's "no lane restriction between them" from step 1 until step 5 deletes the old core; the two subscriptions stay equal peers otherwise, and D-099 applies in full again after step 5. The spec PR, which owns LANES, sets the step-4 detail.
- Steps 1 to 3: subscription A (the one that ran this freeze) runs the freeze, the spec PR, the kernel and the first slice. Its spec session owns SPEC.md, PLAN, BOARD, LANES and docs/OPERATING.md until the spec PR merges.
- Steps 1 to 3: subscription B (the peer) runs only the hardware track and the eval set.
- The eval set is the first slice's acceptance cases written as runnable tests against the kernel's public interface: the docs/migration/cases.md cases the slice touches, plus the slice's own synthetic-mailbox, fake-endpoint and injection-email cases. It lives under evals/slice-inbox/, which no kernel PR touches (evals/review/ is the separate reviewer eval runner), so the two never overlap.
- In step 4, ports split by directory: A the trusted side, B the untrusted side, the Codex lane the adapters and evals.
