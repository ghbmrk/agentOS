**Threat check.** Assets:
- the deletion invariant: deleting a source deletes every note a lineage made after reading it (K2b, R6), and nothing is derived from a deleted item while its tombstone stands;
- the broker's availability: recall search, the deletion hooks and the daily prune, which all share the index and provenance locks;
- the broker's memory and disk under a hostile guest.

Adversaries:
- a hostile guest calling `recall_search` and `recall_note` at the rate limit (burst 30, one per second, up to 100 results per search);
- a guest that also controls several lineages (worker machines);
- concurrency between guest calls, the deletion hooks and the prune.

**Findings**
- **S-R1 release** (upgrades LATER RECALL-prov-L1; invariant CAP-3, one guest cannot deny another lineage's recall).
  - Problem: `Check` refuses every lineage's notes once the sum of all lineages' reads exceeds `ReadsAll` (200,000), but `Given` never bounds the reads. A search returns up to 100 items.
  - Failure path: a guest with about ten lineages that each read 20,000 distinct items (or one lineage reading 200,000 on a large index) makes `recall_note` fail for every lineage on the box, until the owner takes those lineages back. At the rate limit this takes well under a day.
  - Fix: count a lineage's reads only against its own cap at note time, or bound the read set in `Given` (RECALL-prov-L1) so that no lineage holds more than `Reads`, and test that one lineage over its reads cannot refuse another lineage's note.
- **S-L1 later** (`recheck`). A failed `Rewrite` leaves `base` unchanged, so every later write tries the full rewrite again while the disk keeps failing. It is bounded by the failure itself and costs I/O only.

Findings: blocker 0; release 1; later 1.
