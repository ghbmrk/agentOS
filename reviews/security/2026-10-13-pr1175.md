Threat model. The assets are:
- the typed current vault passphrase (the proof) and this PC's boot PIN, both held by the vault process between begin and commit;
- the new card's values, held in agentos-localui's display;
- the old and new recovery key and passphrase slots on the drive;
- the code-generator seed.

The adversaries are a device on the box's AP, a cross-site page in the owner's browser, a dropped phone or a lost reply in the middle of a commit, a crash or restart, a concurrent or late request, and a compromised agentosd (ARC-2).

**The unknown-outcome choice: a commit with no reply, or a 5xx, counts as in effect, and the owner keeps both cards.** This is the safer of the two mappings, and it cannot leave a revoked card usable for longer:

- What the vault enforces does not depend on the page's mapping. A card part is revoked exactly when `Commit` rewrote it, and the page's text cannot bring an old factor back. The owner channel gets "the Owner Card was replaced" or the part-way note only when the vault really wrote, so the owner can tell which case happened.
- The other mapping, `ErrDiscard`, would say "nothing changed" after a commit that in fact rewrote the passphrase or recovery slot. An owner who then threw away the new card would be locked out of the drive. That is the R1 failure path.
- If nothing was in fact written (for example, the socket refused the dial), the old card stays fully valid. The in-effect text tells the owner to start a new card now. No rotation-owed marker is set, because nothing was written.
- The page's cancel after an unknown outcome is safe. It waits on `rotation.mu` behind any commit still running, so it only drops a rotation that is still waiting, such as one the vault kept after a 400.
- The display erases its copy. That loses no factor, because the confirm proved the owner holds a copy, as in the #1090 F1 re-sign.

The residual cost is UX only: the text says part of the card "is already in effect" when it only may be, and the done notes and new code-generator link are lost (UX record U-R1, U-l1).

## Findings

- **S-R1 (release; REC-4 and the #1090 R1 invariant that 410 means nothing changed; A1).** `/rotate/commit` and `/rotate/cancel` carry no rotation identity, so a request the page has given up on still acts on whichever `Pending` is waiting when it gets `rotation.mu`. A review probe on the real handler (not committed) showed both halves. Begin A (wifi, grid), then begin B (wifi, grid), then a commit carrying A's values: the vault answers 200 and stores A's Wi-Fi password under rotation B. Then begin C and a stale cancel: C's commit gets 410.
  - Failure path. A commit's client gives up (the `UnlockClient` timeout is 60 s) while the request waits on `rotation.mu`. The page shows "in effect" and the owner starts B. A's queued commit then lands on B. B's confirm gets 410, and the display says nothing changed, while A's values are in effect.
  - Severity. The owner was told after A to keep that card, so no factor is lost. It needs the vault process to hold `rotation.mu` or `custody.mu` for more than about 60 s, so likelihood is low.
  - Fix. Begin returns a random rotation ID, commit and cancel carry it, and an ID that does not match answers 410 without touching the waiting `Pending`.
  - Same follow-up. When a begin gets no answer, the page should cancel. Today that `Pending` and its passphrase wait out the 30-minute timer. This is inside R2's bound, but it is cheap to close.
- The unknown outcome has no real-socket test. That is a test-depth point, carried in the L3 record as L-l2, not a security finding: the mapping is shown above.
