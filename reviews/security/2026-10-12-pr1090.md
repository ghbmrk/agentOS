Threat model: the assets are the new and old recovery key, the new vault passphrase, and the typed proof passphrase. The adversaries are a device on the box's AP, a cross-site page in the owner's browser, a crash or restart, and a concurrent request.

- **F1 (blocker, defect; HW-1, REC-4).** The in-effect display can never be confirmed, because the real `Pending` is `done` after a partial write. It keeps the new key in memory and serves it to signed-in phones until restart. A probe confirmed this. Fix it as the L3 F1 says.
- **F2 (blocker, defect; REC-4).** ASSUMPTIONS R14 says `Commit` maps errors to `card.ErrDiscard`/`ErrInEffect`, but only the test adapter does. Correct R14 and move the mapping into the wire (R1).
- **R1 (release, CTRL-card-rot-wire; A1 REC-4).** The wire's provisioner maps the lapse to `ErrDiscard` and a partial write to `ErrInEffect`, with a test at the socket op. Failure path: a cancel after a partial write erases the only copy of the key the drive uses.
- **R2 (release, CTRL-card-rot-wire; CRED-1).** A page cancel calls `Pending.Cancel` through a display discard hook or a cancel op, and an unused `Pending` lapses on a timer. Failure path: the typed passphrase stays in vault-process memory with no time limit.
- **l1 (later, `recheck`).** New values are generated outside the vault process, and `checkNext` checks shape only.
- **l2 (later).** Unrotated generated secrets linger as strings until GC (C13; IMG-nodump).

Mutation: 13 of 13 mutants were killed (the list is in the L3 comment). UX cost of the fixes: none, and F1 removes a dead end. Potency cost: none.
