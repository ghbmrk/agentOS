## Potency
- **Normal operation is not slowed.** If no exec was cancelled, `Kill` only does a map lookup under a mutex and waits for nothing. On Rollback or Rebuild with an exec in flight, `Kill` now runs the one `runsc kill --pid` before the sandbox kill instead of in parallel with it. That adds one runsc invocation, not 5 s. The 5 s cap is only reached when `runsc kill --pid` itself hangs.
- **Preempt is unchanged when cgroups are on, which is the production config.** In that case `Manager.kill` empties the cgroup and does not call `Runtime.Kill`.
- **Later P1: the wait ignores Kill's ctx.** `waitPendingKills` blocks for up to 5 s whatever `ctx` allows. `stopRuntime` gives `Kill` `KillTimeout`, which defaults to 10 s, so 5 s is left for kill and delete. If `KillTimeout` were set at or below about 5 s, a hung `kill --pid` would use up the whole budget. kill and delete would then fail on an expired ctx, and the machine would stay Running, not known to be dead. No config sets it that low today.
- **Later P2: a wedged sandbox costs 5 s more.** When `runsc kill --pid` hangs, Rollback, Rebuild, Stop and Preempt without cgroups now wait the full 5 s before the whole-sandbox kill. Before this PR, that kill ran at once. Rollback and Rebuild are the recovery steps for exactly that case. The cost is bounded and acceptable, but it is a regression worth recording.
