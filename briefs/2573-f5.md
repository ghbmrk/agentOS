# 2573-f5: one browser approval covers the task's later submits on its site (#2676 part b)

Board row: #2676 (class:release, tier A, lane:primary). Source: Mark's D-147 tap (card cmsg_01R63S2yrPkq32thy8WQCuvDVqJDxwC3n6imvwEqZc2cTb) and CLK-3 "One approval per task" as merged in #2677 and amended by #2709 (D-148: an unreadable price rides the tap). It builds on #2693 (2573-f6) and on the kernel's task (PR #2703, KRN-task). It also takes #2689 item 3, the VRB-1 screen check. Usage estimate: 90k tokens.

**Why (facts).** After #2693, every submit needs the owner's approval of that exact request, so a multi-page flow needs one tap per page, not one per task.

## Requirements (from SPEC.md)
- **CLK-3, one approval per task:** the owner's approval, signed over the held request, the task and its site, covers the task's later state-changing requests on that site; what asks again; one STATUS line per covered request.
- **KRN-5:** the kernel binds the task into the key; a task approval ends with its task.
- **VRB-1:** a screen that reveals or creates a secret is approved per step.
- **KRN-8:** each request is sent at most once.

## Design
`Clerk.Do` takes a post on the exact approval (#2693), or else on `kernel.TaskApproved` for the task its key serves, handing the gate that approval's site and held request. The gate sends the held request on its site; any other request asks again on another site or on a secret screen; an unreadable price does not hold it (D-148). A covered submit's result carries its approval and a fixed STATUS line.

## Package IDs
- **BRS-14 One tap per task.** In one task, Continue then Place order are each sent once under one tap, each with its STATUS line.
- **BRS-15 Only its task.** The same flow in another task, under no task, or after the task ends sends nothing.
- **BRS-16 What asks again.** Another site and a secret screen hold the request with the approval of that exact request; the held request itself is sent.
- **BRS-17 The site.** The registrable domain on the public suffix list, its private section included; an IP address or `localhost` is its own host.
